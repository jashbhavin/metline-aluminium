6061 Aluminium Wire & Drawn Rod

6061 aluminium wire is drawn from the most widely used heat-treatable Al-Mg-Si alloy, giving about twice the strength of 1100 or 3003 wire with good corrosion resistance. It is the standard stock for structural rivets, cold-headed pins and fasteners, and formed or machined small parts. Metline supplies 6061 wire and rod in coils and straight lengths, in soft and heat-treated tempers, certified to ASTM, AMS and EN standards for customers worldwide.

Overview

What is 6061 aluminium wire?

6061 wire is cold-drawn from extruded or rolled redraw rod through a series of dies until it reaches the finished diameter. Because 6061 is heat-treatable, the same wire can be delivered soft for heading and forming, or solution heat-treated and aged to T6 for strength. That flexibility is the main reason it is chosen over the non-heat-treatable 1xxx, 3xxx and 5xxx wire alloys.

The alloy contains roughly 1 % magnesium and 0.6 % silicon, which form magnesium silicide (Mg2Si) during ageing, plus small amounts of copper for strength and chromium for grain control and resistance to stress corrosion. In wire form this gives a typical tensile strength of about 310 MPa in T6, good atmospheric and fresh-water corrosion resistance, and a surface that anodises cleanly.

Most 6061 wire ends up as rivets, pins and cold-headed parts, where it is valued for a predictable shear strength and a forgiving heading response in the softer tempers. Drawn 6061 rod in the upper diameter range is also fed into automatic lathes and screw machines for small turned components, although free-machining grades such as 2011 or 6262 are faster where the part does not need 6061's corrosion resistance.

Important: 6061 is not a welding filler

  • 6061 wire is not used as MIG or TIG filler: deposited 6061 metal is prone to hot cracking
  • To weld 6061 parts, use ER4043 (crack-resistant, heat-treatable joints) or ER5356 (stronger fillets, better anodised colour match)
  • Buy 6061 wire for rivets, cold heading, pins, wire forms and machining, not for welding

Tempers

Which 6061 wire temper should you order?

Temper choice for wire is mainly a question of when the part is shaped. Heading, bending and coiling are easiest before the final heat treatment; strength comes afterwards.

6061-O

Fully annealed and as ductile as 6061 gets. Choose O for complex cold heading, tight wire forms and deep upsetting, then solution treat and age the finished parts to T6.

6061-H13

A light draw after annealing, typical of rivet and cold-heading stock under ASTM B316. The slight work hardening gives straighter wire that feeds well through headers while keeping enough ductility to form a head.

6061-T4

Solution heat-treated and naturally aged. Rivets can be driven in T4 and will gain some strength with time; artificial ageing then brings them to T6.

6061-T6

Solution treated and artificially aged for full strength. The normal temper for pins, dowels, shafts, machined parts and rivets that are driven cold in the hardened condition.

6061-T89 / T94

Heat treated, then cold drawn (and in T89, aged) to push strength above T6. Used where a small diameter must carry more load, at the cost of lower ductility.

Not sure?

Tell us how the wire will be shaped and what the finished part must carry. We will recommend a temper and confirm which standard it can be certified to.

Working with 6061 wire

Heading, forming, heat treatment and finishing

Cold heading

Head in O, H13 or freshly quenched (W) condition for the most demanding upsets. T6 wire heads well for simple, shallow heads. Keep header dies polished and use a suitable lubricant to avoid pick-up.

Riveting

6061 rivets are usually driven cold in T6, or driven in T4 and aged. They suit 6xxx and 5xxx structures because the similar chemistry avoids galvanic problems seen with steel fasteners.

Bending and coiling

T6 wire bends to about 1.5 – 2 × diameter without cracking; O temper can be bent flat on itself. For springs or tight forms, shape soft and heat-treat after.

Heat treatment

Solution treat at about 530 °C, quench rapidly in water, then age at about 160 – 175 °C for 8 – 18 hours to reach T6. Quench delay must be short in fine wire, which cools and loses temperature quickly.

Machining

Drawn T6 rod turns cleanly with sharp carbide or high-speed steel tools. Chips are longer than with free-machining grades, so use chip breakers on automatic lathes.

Anodising and joining

Takes clear and coloured anodic coatings well. When 6061 parts are welded, use ER4043 or ER5356 filler; the heat-affected zone drops to roughly T4 strength unless re-treated.

Applications

Where 6061 aluminium wire is used

  • Rivets: solid and semi-tubular rivets for boats, truck and trailer bodies, ladders, scaffolding and aluminium structures
  • Cold-headed fasteners: pins, screws, studs and special fasteners where aluminium-to-aluminium joints avoid galvanic corrosion
  • Pins and dowels: hinge pins, clevis pins and locating dowels cut from straightened T6 wire
  • Wire forms: handles, clips, hooks, racks and frames bent from soft wire and aged afterwards
  • Turned parts: small fittings, spacers and connectors machined from drawn rod
  • Transport and marine: lightweight fixings for vehicle bodies, deck hardware and fittings above the waterline
  • Sports and leisure: fishing tackle, camping equipment and bicycle accessories

For overhead power conductors, the electrical grade 6201 is used instead: it offers higher conductivity (about 52.5 % IACS) with similar strength, whereas 6061 is chosen for mechanical duty.

FAQ

6061 aluminium (aluminum) wire: frequently asked questions

Can I use 6061 wire as MIG or TIG welding filler?

No. 6061 weld metal is highly prone to solidification cracking. Weld 6061 parts with ER4043 for crack resistance or ER5356 for higher fillet strength and a better colour match after anodising.

What temper of 6061 wire is best for cold heading rivets?

O or H13 for complex or deep heads, followed by heat treatment of the finished parts. Simple rivets can be headed from T4 and aged, or driven cold in T6 where the head shape is shallow.

How strong is 6061-T6 wire?

Typically about 310 MPa tensile, 276 MPa yield and 207 MPa shear. ASTM B211 specifies minimums of 290 MPa tensile and 240 MPa yield for T6 rod and wire.

How much does 6061 aluminium wire weigh?

Weight in kg per 1000 m is about 2.12 × d², with d in millimetres. A 3 mm wire weighs roughly 19.1 kg per kilometre, and a 6 mm wire about 76.3 kg per kilometre.

Is 6061 wire suitable for electrical conductors?

It conducts about 43 % IACS, so it can carry current in small bonding and grounding parts, but overhead conductors use 1350 or 6201, which offer higher conductivity at a similar or lower cost.

What is the difference between 6061 wire and 6061 rod?

Mainly diameter and production route. Wire is the small-diameter, cold-drawn product supplied in coils; rod is larger and supplied straight. ASTM B211 covers both, with tighter tolerances on drawn sizes.

Do you ship 6061 wire internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies customers worldwide from Mumbai, with export packing, mill certificates and shipping by sea or air on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
