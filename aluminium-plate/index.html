Overview

What counts as aluminium plate

Aluminium plate is flat rolled aluminium that is thicker than sheet. Most specifications draw the line at roughly 6 mm (0.25 in): below that the product is sheet, above it the product is plate. The extra thickness changes how the material is made, stocked and used. Plate is hot rolled from a cast ingot, often stretched after heat treatment to relieve internal stress, and is usually bought to be machined, welded into structures or cut into heavy components rather than formed.

That is why choosing plate is mostly a question of three things: the alloy, which sets strength, corrosion resistance and weldability; the temper, which sets how the metal has been strengthened and how stable it will be when you machine it; and the thickness and size, which decide how much material you buy and how much you remove.

Sheet or plate?

  • Sheet roughly 0.2–6 mm. Bent, pressed, rolled and formed.
  • Plate roughly 6 mm and above. Machined, welded and used for structural or heavy-duty parts.
  • The exact break point depends on the standard you buy to, so state the standard on your enquiry.

Tempers

What the temper code tells you

The letters and numbers after the alloy describe how the plate was strengthened. Getting the temper right matters as much as the alloy, especially for parts that will be heavily machined.

O and H111

Annealed or only lightly strain hardened. Softest condition with the best formability; used where the plate will be bent or rolled, or where toughness matters more than strength.

H32, H116 and H321

Strain hardened and stabilised tempers for 5xxx alloys. H116 and H321 are tested for resistance to exfoliation and intergranular corrosion, which is why shipbuilders specify them.

T6

Solution heat treated and artificially aged for full strength in 2xxx, 6xxx and 7xxx alloys.

T651

T6 plus controlled stretching to relieve residual stress. The plate stays flat and stable when you machine deep pockets, so it is the standard choice for machined parts, tooling and fixtures.

T351

Naturally aged and stress relieved by stretching. The usual supply condition for 2024 plate.

T7351

Over-aged 7xxx temper that gives up a little strength for much better resistance to stress corrosion cracking, used in critical aerospace parts.

Applications

Where aluminium plate is used

Marine and shipbuilding

Hulls, decks, superstructures and offshore equipment in 5083 and 5086, where seawater resistance and weldability come first.

Transport and vehicles

Trailer chassis, tipper bodies, rail car structures and tanker shells that need strength with less weight.

Aerospace and defence

Machined frames, ribs and fittings in 2024 and 7075, bought with full traceability and certification.

Moulds, tooling and machinery

Blow and injection moulds, jigs, fixtures and machine base plates in 6061 and 7075, where flatness and machinability save hours on the shop floor.

Construction and architecture

Structural brackets, cladding sub-frames, bridge decks and walkways that must resist weather for decades.

Process and electrical equipment

Chemical tanks, cryogenic vessels and conductive parts in pure and 5xxx alloys.

FAQ

Aluminium plate questions

What thickness is considered aluminium plate?

Generally from about 6 mm (0.25 in) upwards. Thinner material is classed as sheet. The exact boundary depends on the standard, so state the standard you are buying to.

Which aluminium plate is best for marine use?

5083 is the usual first choice for hulls and decks because it combines high strength with excellent seawater resistance and good weldability. 5086 and 5052 are also widely used. For immersed or wetted structures, specify a marine temper such as H116 or H321.

What is the difference between 6061 and 7075 plate?

7075-T651 is roughly twice as strong in yield as 6061-T651 and is preferred for highly loaded machined parts and tooling. 6061 is easier to weld, more corrosion resistant and costs less, which makes it the better all-round choice for frames, fixtures and general engineering.

Why choose T651 instead of T6?

T651 plate has been stretched after heat treatment to relieve internal stress. It stays flat when material is machined away unevenly, which reduces distortion and rework on precision parts.

Can 5083 plate be heat treated for more strength?

No. 5083 is a non-heat-treatable alloy. Its strength comes from magnesium in solution and from strain hardening, which is why it is supplied in O, H111, H116 and H321 tempers.

Do you supply cut-to-size plate with mill certificates?

Yes. Tell us the sizes and the certification you need when you enquire, and we will confirm availability, lead time and documentation with our quote.

Need aluminium plate for your next project?

Send us the alloy, temper, sizes and quantity. Our Mumbai team will reply with availability, lead time and a competitive quote.
