5059 Aluminium Plate & Sheet

5059 is a high-magnesium Al-Mg-Mn-Zn-Zr alloy that is roughly 10 – 25 % stronger than 5083 in the same temper, before and after welding, while keeping marine-grade corrosion resistance. Shipbuilders use it to thin down hull and deck plating, and defence builders use it as armour. Metline supplies 5059 sheet and plate in O, H111, H116, H321, H131 and H136 to ASTM B209, ASTM B928 and MIL-DTL-46027, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is 5059 aluminium?

5059 was developed in Europe in the 1990s as a stronger successor to 5083 for welded marine structures. It raises magnesium to 5.0 – 6.0 % and adds zinc (0.40 – 0.90 %) and zirconium (0.05 – 0.25 %) to the familiar Al-Mg-Mn base.

Each addition has a purpose. The extra magnesium gives more solid-solution strength. Zirconium forms very fine Al3Zr dispersoids that resist recrystallisation, so the heat-affected zone next to a weld keeps more of its strength than in 5083. Zinc helps the alloy resist the grain-boundary precipitation that makes high-magnesium alloys prone to intergranular and exfoliation corrosion. The result, in the marine tempers, is plate with yield strength around 25 % above 5083-H116 and welded joints with a correspondingly higher allowable stress.

For designers, that headroom can be spent in two ways: reduce plate thickness and save weight (common in fast ferries, patrol craft and superyachts), or keep the thickness and gain a margin for fatigue and impact. In armoured vehicles, the strain-hardened H131 temper gives ballistic performance at lower areal density than 5083 armour plate. Tank and tanker builders use the soft O and H111 tempers for shells and heads that must be rolled and formed.

Where 5059 earns its higher price

  • Higher as-welded strength than 5083, so welded structures can be thinner
  • Marine tempers (H116, H321) tested for exfoliation and intergranular corrosion to ASTM B928
  • Armour tempers qualified to MIL-DTL-46027 for military vehicle hulls and add-on armour
  • Good toughness at low temperature, useful for cryogenic and LNG-related equipment

Tempers

5059 tempers and what they are for

H116

Strain hardened with controlled processing to meet the exfoliation (ASTM G66) and intergranular (ASTM G67) corrosion tests in ASTM B928. The default for hull, deck and superstructure plating in seawater.

H321

Strain hardened and thermally stabilised, also corrosion-tested to B928. Interchangeable with H116 in most class rules; choose whichever the drawing or shipyard specifies.

O / H111

Annealed or lightly strain hardened. Highest elongation for rolling cylindrical tank shells, dishing heads and pressing. Used in road tankers and cryogenic vessels.

H112

As-fabricated plate with properties from hot rolling. Also common for 5059 bar and extrusions.

H131

Heavily strain hardened armour temper defined in MIL-DTL-46027 for military vehicle hulls, floors and add-on armour panels.

H136

Armour temper with a slightly different strength and ductility balance for thicker armour sections.

Working with 5059

Welding, forming and corrosion control

Welding

MIG and TIG weld well. ER5183 is the usual filler; ER5556 or ER5087 (Zr-bearing) give higher as-welded strength. Keep heat input moderate and interpass temperatures low to preserve heat-affected-zone strength.

As-welded strength

The fusion zone and HAZ are weaker than the parent H116/H321 plate, as with every 5xxx alloy, but the welded strength of 5059 is clearly higher than 5083. Use the welded design values from your class rules.

Forming

O and H111 roll and dish well. H116 and H321 form to moderate radii; avoid heating to aid forming, as it can change the corrosion-tested condition.

Service temperature

Like all alloys above about 3 % Mg, 5059 should not be used for sustained service above roughly 65 °C, where sensitisation can lead to intergranular corrosion and stress corrosion cracking.

Cutting

Plasma, waterjet and saw cut cleanly. Remove plasma-affected edges before welding critical joints.

Finishing

Can be painted after blasting and priming; antifouling systems must be compatible with aluminium (no copper-based paints in direct contact).

Applications

Where 5059 plate is used

  • Fast vessels: hull bottoms, side shell and decks of ferries, patrol boats and crew transfer vessels, where thinner plate saves weight
  • Yachts: highly loaded structure in large aluminium sailing and motor yachts
  • Offshore: gangways, helidecks and topside modules where weight matters
  • Defence vehicles: welded hulls, floors and add-on armour panels in H131/H136
  • Road tankers: shells, heads and baffles in O/H111 for fuel and chemical transport
  • Cryogenic and aerospace: welded tanks and test structures needing strength at low temperature

A typical fast-ferry application replaces 8 mm 5083-H116 bottom plating with thinner 5059-H116 after recalculating scantlings to the class rules, saving weight across the whole hull. The saving depends entirely on the rule-based design, so treat any figure as project-specific.

FAQ

5059 aluminium (aluminum) plate: frequently asked questions

How much stronger is 5059 than 5083?

In the marine tempers, minimum yield is about 270 MPa for 5059 against 215 MPa for 5083 in medium thicknesses, roughly 25 % higher. Minimum tensile strength rises from about 305 to 370 MPa. The welded strength is also higher, which is what usually lets designers reduce thickness.

What filler wire should be used to weld 5059?

ER5183 is standard. Zirconium-bearing fillers such as ER5087 or higher-strength ER5556 can raise as-welded strength. Always follow the welding procedure qualified for the class society or code you build to.

Can 5059 be used above 65 °C?

Not for sustained service. Like 5083 and 5086, it can become sensitised above about 65 °C, making it susceptible to intergranular corrosion and stress corrosion cracking. For hot service, use 5454 or 5052.

Is 5059 approved by classification societies?

Yes, 5059 in H116 and H321 is covered by the rules of the major classification societies when produced and tested under their requirements. Material for a classed vessel needs society certification, so tell us the society before ordering.

What is the difference between H116 and H321 in 5059?

Both are corrosion-tested marine tempers under ASTM B928. H116 is reached by controlled strain hardening; H321 adds a thermal stabilising step. Properties are very similar and most rules accept either.

Is 5059 used for armour?

Yes. 5059-H131 and H136 are covered by MIL-DTL-46027 alongside 5083 and 5456 and are used for welded military vehicle hulls and add-on armour.
