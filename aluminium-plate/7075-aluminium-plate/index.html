7075 T6, T651 and T7351 Aluminium Sheet and Plate

7075 is the classic high-strength aluminium alloy: in T6 and T651 it reaches about 570 MPa tensile strength, close to many structural steels at one third of the weight. It machines superbly but is not suited to fusion welding, and the over-aged T7351 temper is used where stress-corrosion resistance matters. Metline supplies 7075 sheet and plate in T6, T651 and T7351, certified to ASTM, AMS and EN, worldwide.

Overview

What is 7075 aluminium plate?

7075 combines about 5.6 % zinc, 2.5 % magnesium and 1.6 % copper, with chromium for grain control. After solution treatment and ageing, fine zinc-magnesium precipitates give it one of the highest strengths of any commercial aluminium alloy.

Introduced in the 1940s for aircraft structure, 7075 is still used for wing and fuselage fittings, but today much of its plate goes into moulds, tooling, jigs, high-performance sports goods and precision machined parts, because it machines cleanly and holds tight tolerances.

Its limits are just as important: it is not weldable by conventional fusion processes, its corrosion resistance is lower than 5xxx and 6xxx alloys, and peak-aged T6 can be sensitive to stress corrosion in the short-transverse direction. The T73 family of tempers addresses that last point at the cost of about 10 – 15 % strength.

Tempers

7075-T6 vs T651 vs T7351

7075-T6

Peak-aged. Normal for sheet up to about 6 mm. Maximum strength.

7075-T651

Peak-aged and stretched to relieve stress. Standard for plate that will be machined: moulds, tooling, fittings.

7075-T7351

Stretched and over-aged. About 12 % lower strength than T651 but far better resistance to stress corrosion cracking; specified for aerospace fittings under sustained load.

7075-O

Annealed for forming; heat-treat to T6 afterwards.

Working with 7075

Machining, joining and finishing

Machining

Excellent: short chips, fine finish and tight tolerances. T651 stays flat when heavily machined.

Welding

Not recommended for fusion welding: welds crack and lose strength. Join by fastening, adhesive bonding or friction-stir welding.

Corrosion

Fair. Protect with anodising, chromate-free conversion coatings or paint; use T7351 where stress corrosion is a risk.

Anodising

Hard and protective anodising both work well; colour is slightly darker than 6061.

Applications

Where 7075 plate is used

  • Aerospace: wing and fuselage fittings, frames, ribs and landing-gear parts
  • Tooling: blow moulds, injection moulds, jigs and fixtures
  • Defence: high-strength machined components
  • Sport: climbing hardware, bicycle components and firearms parts
  • Industry: high-load gears, shafts and robot arms machined from plate

FAQ

7075 aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQ

What is the difference between 7075-T6 and T651?

Same strength; T651 plate is stretched after quenching to relieve stress, so it stays flat when machined.

When should I use 7075-T7351?

When the part carries sustained tensile load in a corrosive environment, especially across the short-transverse direction. T7351 resists stress corrosion cracking far better than T651.

Can 7075 be welded?

Not reliably by MIG or TIG; welds crack and lose strength. Use mechanical fastening or friction-stir welding.

How strong is 7075 compared with steel?

7075-T6 has a typical tensile strength of about 572 MPa, comparable with many structural steels, at about one third of the density.

What does 7075 plate weigh?

2.81 kg per m² per millimetre. A 1250 × 2500 × 20 mm plate weighs about 176 kg.
