6013 Aluminium Sheet & Plate

6013 is a copper-bearing Al-Mg-Si alloy developed to give aircraft and vehicle designers 2024-class strength with the formability, weldability and corrosion resistance of the 6xxx family. Sheet is normally bought in T4 for stretch forming and aged to T6 after forming; plate is supplied in T651 for machining. Metline supplies 6013 to AMS and ASTM specifications, with full certification, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is 6013 aluminium?

6013 was introduced in the 1980s as an aerospace sheet alloy. Compared with 6061 it carries more silicon, much more copper (0.6 – 1.1 %) and a manganese addition. Together these lift the strength of the precipitation-hardened alloy well above 6061 while keeping the processing advantages of the 6xxx series.

Its headline claim, published when it was registered, is that 6013-T6 sheet offers about 12 % more strength than Alclad 2024-T3, the classic fuselage skin material, at slightly lower density. Unlike 2024, it does not need a pure-aluminium cladding layer for corrosion protection, it can be fusion-welded and laser-welded, and it can be stretch-formed in T4 and then artificially aged without a second solution heat treatment.

That combination is what makes 6013 interesting beyond aerospace. Automotive engineers use it for body panels and highly stressed formed parts, bicycle makers use it for tubes and rims, and machinists use 6013-T651 plate when 6061 is not quite strong enough but 7075 would bring corrosion and welding penalties.

Where 6013 earns its place

  • Strength close to 2024-T3 sheet without Alclad cladding
  • Good stretch formability in T4, then age to T6 after forming
  • Weldable by MIG, TIG and laser, unlike most 2xxx and 7xxx alloys
  • Corrosion resistance and anodising response similar to 6061

Tempers

6013-T4 vs T6 vs T651

6013-T4 (AMS 4347)

Solution heat-treated and naturally aged. Formability is comparable with 2024-O and better in stretch forming. Unlike 2024-W, it stays formable for months at room temperature, so no refrigeration of formed blanks is needed.

6013-T6 (AMS 4216)

Artificially aged to peak strength. Yield strength, fatigue and toughness are in the same class as 2024-T3, with better corrosion resistance and a small density advantage.

T4 to T6 after forming

Parts are stretch-formed in T4 and then given an artificial ageing treatment to reach T6. Because no second solution treatment and quench are needed, distortion of the formed part is small.

6013-T651 plate

Solution treated, stretched to relieve quench stress and aged. Used for machined fittings, moulds and precision parts that need more strength than 6061-T651.

Pre-strain sensitivity

Light strains of roughly 2 – 13 % followed by solution treatment can cause grain growth. Plan forming so that heavily formed areas are not re-solution-treated.

Choosing

Order T4 for anything that will be stretch-formed or bent; order T6 for flat or lightly formed parts; order T651 for plate that will be machined.

Working with 6013

Forming, welding, machining and finishing

Stretch forming

T4 sheet stretch-forms well and keeps that ability after long storage. Form, then artificially age to T6. Avoid forming in T6 except for gentle curvature.

Welding

Weldable by MIG, TIG, resistance and laser welding, which is unusual for an alloy of this strength. 4xxx fillers such as 4043 reduce hot-cracking risk. Expect strength loss in the heat-affected zone, as with all heat-treated 6xxx alloys.

Machining

T6 and T651 machine well, producing short chips and a good finish. Practice is similar to 6061: sharp carbide, high speeds, flood coolant.

Corrosion

Corrosion resistance is close to 6061 and much better than bare 2024, so 6013 skins normally do not need cladding. The copper content means it is slightly less resistant than copper-free 6xxx alloys in aggressive environments.

Finishing

Anodises, paints and takes conversion coatings well. Chromic, sulphuric and hard anodising are all used.

Temperature

Like other aged 6xxx alloys, prolonged service above roughly 150 °C over-ages the alloy and reduces strength.

Applications

Where 6013 is used

Aerospace and MRO

  • Fuselage skin panels and stretch-formed skins
  • Engine cowlings and nacelle parts
  • Leading- and trailing-edge parts
  • Helicopter structure and formed brackets
  • Interior structure and secondary fittings

Automotive, sport and industry

  • Car body panels, doors and bonnets
  • Bicycle tubes, rims and handlebars
  • Brake and hydraulic valve housings
  • Precision machined parts, fixtures and moulds from T651 plate
  • Electronics casings and heat-dissipating housings

A typical example: an aircraft repair shop replacing a stretch-formed skin panel orders 6013-T4 sheet to AMS 4347, forms the panel on a stretch press, ages it to T6 and anodises it, avoiding both the refrigeration that 2024-W blanks need and the cladding that 2024 sheet carries.

6013 is not a substitute for 7075 when maximum static strength is needed: 7075-T6 is roughly 50 % stronger. Its niche is the gap between 6061 and the 2xxx/7xxx aerospace alloys, where weldability, formability and corrosion resistance matter as much as strength. Against bare 2024-T3, 6013-T6 has similar yield strength, lower tensile strength and better corrosion behaviour; against Alclad 2024-T3, it is stronger.

FAQ

6013 aluminium (aluminum) sheet and plate: FAQ

What is 6013 aluminium used for?

Aircraft fuselage skins, cowlings and formed structure; car body panels; bicycle tubes and rims; brake and hydraulic housings; and machined fixtures and moulds from T651 plate.

Is 6013 stronger than 6061?

Yes. In T6, 6013 typically reaches about 340 – 360 MPa yield strength against about 276 MPa for 6061-T6, thanks to its higher copper and silicon content.

Is 6013 as strong as 7075?

No. 7075-T6 is considerably stronger (typical yield about 505 MPa). 6013 is chosen over 7075 when welding, forming or corrosion resistance matter more than peak strength.

How does 6013 compare with 2024?

6013-T6 has yield strength similar to 2024-T3 and about 12 % more strength than Alclad 2024-T3, with lower density, better corrosion resistance and the ability to be fusion-welded.

Can 6013-T4 be aged to T6 after forming?

Yes. That is the intended route: form in T4, then artificially age to T6. No second solution heat treatment is needed, which limits distortion.

Which specification should I order to?

AMS 4347 for T4 sheet, AMS 4216 for T6 sheet and ASTM B209 for plate such as T651, unless your drawing names a customer specification.
