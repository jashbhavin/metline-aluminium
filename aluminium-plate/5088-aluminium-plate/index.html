EN AW-5088 Aluminium Plate

EN AW-5088 (AlMg5Mn0.4) is a high-magnesium aluminium plate developed for welded road and rail tankers carrying dangerous goods, and for cryogenic transport vessels. It is stronger than 5083 in the soft O/H111 condition, which lets tank builders use thinner shells and carry more payload. Metline supplies 5088 plate and coil in O and H111 to EN 14286 and EN 485, worldwide.

Overview

What is EN AW-5088?

5088 is a European tank alloy registered in the early 2000s. It combines about 5 % magnesium with a modest manganese content, a controlled zinc addition and up to 0.15 % zirconium. The aim is a plate that is soft enough to roll into tank shells and press into heads, yet stronger than 5083 or 5182 in that same soft condition.

Tank shells are designed against the strength of the material in the annealed or H111 state, because forming and welding remove most of any cold work. A higher O-temper strength therefore translates directly into thinner walls and lower tare weight. For a road tanker running at a fixed gross vehicle weight, every kilogram saved in the shell is a kilogram of extra payload on every trip.

Zirconium raises the recrystallisation temperature and refines the grain in the heat-affected zone, which helps welded joints keep their properties. Like other Al-Mg alloys, 5088 has no ductile-to-brittle transition, so it stays tough at the very low temperatures met in liquefied gas service.

Why tank builders specify 5088

  • Higher O/H111 strength than 5083 and 5182, enabling thinner, lighter tank shells
  • Covered by EN 14286, the standard for weldable rolled products for dangerous-goods tanks
  • Good toughness down to cryogenic temperatures
  • Forms and welds well in O and H111, the tempers in which shells are rolled and heads pressed

Tempers

Tempers: O and H111

O

Fully annealed. Maximum ductility for pressing dished heads, flanging and tight forming.

H111

Annealed and then slightly strain hardened by levelling or stretching. Flatter plate with marginally higher proof strength; the usual temper for shell plate.

Why not H116 or H321?

5088 is designed around soft-temper strength for tanks. If you need a corrosion-tested marine temper, look at 5083 or 5059 instead.

Design & fabrication

Design and fabrication notes

Welding

MIG with ER5183 is typical for tank shells; follow a procedure qualified to the tank code. Keep heat input controlled to protect the heat-affected zone.

Forming heads

Press or spin heads in O temper; check springback, which is higher than for 5754 because of the higher strength.

Service temperature

EN 12392 limits Al-Mg alloys with more than about 3 % Mg in sustained service at elevated temperature (around 65 – 75 °C depending on the standard). Hot-product tanks such as bitumen use 5454 instead.

Cargo compatibility

Confirm compatibility of the plate with each product to be carried, including cleaning agents. 5088 is not intended for food-contact tanks.

Cryogenic service

Toughness and strength are retained at low temperature, making 5088 suitable for outer and inner structures of cryogenic transport vessels.

Inspection

Specify flatness and thickness tolerances that suit automatic welding lines; request ultrasonic testing if your code requires it.

Applications

Where EN AW-5088 is used

  • Road tankers: shells, heads and baffles for petrol, diesel and liquid hydrocarbons under ADR
  • Rail tank cars: lightweight barrels for fuel and chemical transport under RID
  • Cryogenic containers: shells and cover plates for LIN, LOX, LAR and LNG transport vessels
  • Pressure equipment: buffer tanks and vessels at ambient or low temperature
  • Special vehicles: body-mounted tanks where weight saving increases payload

FAQ

EN AW-5088 aluminium plate: frequently asked questions

What is the advantage of 5088 over 5083 for tankers?

Higher strength in the soft O/H111 condition, which is the basis for tank shell design. That allows thinner shells, lower tare weight and more payload, while weldability and toughness remain good.

Can 5088 be used for food or drinking water tanks?

It is not intended for food contact. Food tanks normally use alloys and surface treatments specifically approved for that purpose.

What is the maximum service temperature for 5088?

Like other alloys with more than about 3 % magnesium, 5088 should not be used in sustained service above roughly 65 – 75 °C (see EN 12392), because of the risk of sensitisation. Use 5454 for hot products.

Which filler wire suits 5088?

ER5183 is the usual choice for MIG welding tank shells; confirm with your qualified welding procedure.

Is 5088 suitable for cryogenic service?

Yes. As an Al-Mg alloy it keeps its toughness at cryogenic temperatures and is used for liquefied gas transport vessels.

Is there an ASTM equivalent of EN AW-5088?

5088 is registered with the Aluminum Association (UNS A95088) but is mainly used and standardised in Europe. Most North American tank designs use 5083, 5086, 5454 or 5182 instead.
