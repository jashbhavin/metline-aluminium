5A05 Aluminium Plate & Sheet

5A05 is an aluminium-magnesium alloy with about 5 % magnesium, registered in the Chinese GB/T system and practically identical to the Russian grade AMg5. It gives plate and sheet that are strong for a non-heat-treatable alloy, weld well and resist seawater and fuel. Metline supplies 5A05 in O and H112 tempers, cut to size and certified, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is 5A05 aluminium plate?

5A05 belongs to the 5xxx family, where magnesium in solid solution provides the strength. With 4.8 – 5.5 % magnesium and a small manganese addition it sits between 5052 and 5A06 in strength, and it cannot be hardened by heat treatment: strength comes from the alloy content and, in H tempers, from rolling.

The grade is registered in GB/T 3190 and has its origin in the Soviet AMg5 alloy, which is why it appears on drawings from Chinese, Russian and CIS design offices for welded tanks, pipework and marine structure. In annealed condition it has a tensile strength of about 275 MPa, roughly 40 % higher than 5052-O, while keeping enough ductility for rolling, bending and deep forming.

Buyers usually meet 5A05 in two situations: a drawing or legacy specification names it directly, or a designer needs a weldable Al-Mg plate with more strength than 5052 but is working to GB or GOST documents rather than ASTM. In both cases the alloy is supplied as rolled sheet (O temper) or hot-rolled plate (H112), and it is often cross-checked against 5083 and 5056 when Western equivalents are needed.

Why engineers choose 5A05

  • Higher annealed strength than 5052, with comparable weldability
  • Very good resistance to seawater, marine atmosphere and many fuels and oils
  • Good fatigue performance, which suits pressure-cycled tanks and pipe
  • Specified directly in GB and GOST documents, so no conversion is needed for those projects

Tempers

5A05 tempers and when to use them

5A05-O

Fully annealed. Maximum ductility for bending, rolling into cylinders, spinning and drawing. The usual condition for sheet up to about 4.5 mm used in tanks and formed parts.

5A05-H112

As hot-rolled with slight strain hardening and tested mechanical properties. The standard condition for plate from about 4.5 mm upward, for welded structures and machined parts.

5A05-H32 / H34 (on request)

Strain-hardened and stabilised tempers can be produced to order where a higher yield strength is needed in thin sheet. Confirm properties against the drawing before ordering.

Because 5xxx alloys with more than about 3 % magnesium can become sensitive to intergranular corrosion after long exposure above roughly 65 °C, 5A05 is not recommended for continuous service at elevated temperature. For hot service, a lower-magnesium alloy such as 5454 is the usual choice.

Working with 5A05

Welding, forming, machining and corrosion

Welding

Welds well by TIG and MIG. Use a 5xxx filler with matching or higher magnesium (5356, 5183 or the GB equivalent SAlMg5) to keep joint strength and corrosion resistance. Welds in O-temper plate retain almost the full base-metal strength.

Forming

O-temper sheet bends tightly and rolls into cylinders easily. H112 plate forms satisfactorily with larger radii. Expect more springback than with 5052 because of the higher yield strength.

Machining

Like most soft Al-Mg alloys, 5A05 machines with long, gummy chips. Use sharp, polished carbide tools with high rake and good chip evacuation. For heavily machined parts, 5083 or a heat-treatable 6xxx plate usually gives a better result.

Corrosion

Very good resistance to seawater, marine air and many hydrocarbons, which is why it is used for fuel and liquid tanks. Avoid prolonged service above about 65 °C.

Finishing

Can be anodised for protection; the film is clear to slightly grey. Polishes well. Paint systems for marine aluminium apply directly after suitable pre-treatment.

Cryogenic use

Like other Al-Mg alloys it keeps or gains toughness at low temperature, so it can be considered for cold-service vessels after design review.

Applications

Where 5A05 aluminium plate is used

  • Fuel and liquid tanks: aircraft and vehicle fuel tanks, oil and water tanks, chemical containers
  • Welded pipe and ducting: fuel lines, pipework and welded tube made from strip and sheet
  • Marine structure: superstructure plating, bulkheads and fittings for small craft built to GB or GOST drawings
  • Transport: tanker bodies, rail car panels and vehicle components where weight and corrosion matter
  • Medium-load structural parts: brackets, frames and covers that need ductility and corrosion resistance
  • Rivet and fastener stock: the related 5A05 wire grade is used for rivets matching 5A05 structures

FAQ

5A05 aluminium (aluminum) plate: frequently asked questions

What is the equivalent of 5A05 aluminium?

The Russian AMg5 (1550) is practically identical. There is no exact ASTM or EN equivalent; 5056 is closest in chemistry and 5083 is the usual plate substitute, subject to the designer's approval.

Can 5A05 be heat treated to increase strength?

No. It is a non-heat-treatable alloy. Strength comes from magnesium in solid solution and from cold work in H tempers. Heat treatment is only used to anneal it to the O temper.

Is 5A05 good for marine use?

Yes. Its corrosion resistance in seawater is very good and it welds well, so it is used for marine structure built to GB or GOST drawings. For classed vessels, 5083 or 5086 with class approval is usually specified.

Is 5A05 easy to machine?

Only moderately. It is soft and gummy, giving long chips. Sharp, high-rake tools help, but parts that need extensive machining are better made from 5083 or 6061-T651.

What does 5A05 plate weigh?

About 2.65 kg per m² per millimetre of thickness. A 1500 × 3000 × 10 mm plate weighs roughly 119 kg.

Do you export 5A05 plate?

Yes. Metline ships 5A05 sheet and plate worldwide from Mumbai with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and seaworthy packing, on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
