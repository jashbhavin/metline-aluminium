4047 Aluminium Sheet, Plate & Coil

4047 is an aluminium-silicon alloy with 11 – 13 % silicon, close to the eutectic composition, so it melts at about 577 – 582 °C, well below most aluminium base metals. It is used as braze cladding, filler foil and shims, and as welding filler. Metline supplies 4047 sheet, plate, strip and foil cut to your sizes, certified to the specification you name, worldwide.

Overview

What is 4047 aluminium?

Silicon lowers the melting point of aluminium, reaching a minimum at the eutectic of about 12.6 % silicon and 577 °C. 4047, at 11 – 13 % silicon, sits almost exactly there: it melts over a very narrow range and flows freely when molten. That makes it an ideal filler for joining aluminium parts that melt at 600 – 660 °C.

In sheet and strip form, 4047 has three main jobs. It is roll-bonded as a thin cladding layer onto a 3xxx core to make brazing sheet for heat exchangers; it is cut into foil, shims and preforms that are placed in joints before furnace brazing; and it is used as a filler alloy (ER4047) for welding where low shrinkage and good fluidity reduce cracking.

As a standalone structural material 4047 is not attractive: it is not heat-treatable, its strength is modest and the high silicon makes it brittle in thicker sections and abrasive to machine. Buyers therefore choose 4047 sheet for what it does at brazing temperature, not for its room-temperature properties.

Why 4047 is used for brazing

  • Lowest melting range of the common Al-Si brazing alloys
  • High fluidity fills long, narrow joints and fillets
  • Wide gap between filler and base-metal melting reduces the risk of melting the parts
  • Compatible with CAB (flux) and vacuum brazing processes

Brazing base metals must have a solidus comfortably above the brazing temperature. 1xxx, 3xxx (such as 3003) and low-magnesium 6xxx alloys (such as 6063 and 6951) are routinely brazed with 4047; high-magnesium 5xxx alloys and most 2xxx and 7xxx alloys are not, because they either melt too low or form oxides that flux cannot remove.

Practical guidance

Using 4047 in brazing

  1. CleanDegrease all parts; remove heavy oxide by etching or mechanical cleaning.
  2. Fit upKeep joint clearances small, typically about 0.05 – 0.25 mm, so capillary action draws filler into the joint.
  3. Flux or vacuumIn controlled-atmosphere brazing, apply non-corrosive fluoride flux; for vacuum brazing use a magnesium-bearing filler rather than standard 4047.
  4. HeatRaise parts uniformly to about 590 – 605 °C and hold briefly; avoid overheating, which causes erosion of thin base metals.
  5. InspectCheck fillet formation and leak-test heat exchangers.

FAQ

4047 aluminium (aluminum) sheet: frequently asked questions

What is 4047 aluminium used for?

Mainly brazing: as cladding on brazing sheet, as filler foil, shims and preforms, and as ER4047 welding filler.

What is the melting point of 4047?

About 577 °C solidus and 582 °C liquidus. Brazing is usually done at about 590 – 605 °C.

What is the difference between 4047 and 4045?

4045 has about 9 – 11 % silicon and a wider melting range; 4047 has 11 – 13 % silicon, melts lower and flows more freely. Both are used as braze cladding.

Can 4047 be used structurally?

It is not a good structural alloy: strength is modest and it cannot be heat-treated. Its value is as a filler.

Is 4047 suitable for vacuum brazing?

Standard 4047 is used mainly with flux (CAB). Vacuum brazing normally uses magnesium-bearing Al-Si fillers.
