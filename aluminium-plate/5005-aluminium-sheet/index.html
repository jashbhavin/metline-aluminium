5005 Aluminium Sheet

5005 is a low-magnesium aluminium sheet alloy chosen when appearance after anodising matters as much as strength. It gives an even, clear anodic finish that blends with 6063 extrusions, forms easily and resists weathering. Overview

What is 5005 aluminium sheet?

5005 is the leanest of the common 5xxx alloys: it contains only about 0.5 – 1.1 % magnesium, roughly a third of the magnesium in 5052. That small addition makes it noticeably stronger than 1100 or 3003 while keeping the clean, bright metal that anodisers prefer.

Because 5005 carries very little iron-bearing or manganese-bearing intermetallic material, its anodic film forms evenly and stays clear. Manganese-rich 3003 tends to give a slightly yellow or cloudy tone after anodising; 5005 does not, which is why architects specify it for cladding panels, soffits, column covers and trims that must match anodised 6063 window and curtain-wall sections on the same elevation.

The alloy cannot be heat-treated. Its strength comes from the magnesium in solid solution and from cold rolling, expressed in the H tempers. In H32 and H34 it bends cleanly, takes roll-forming and folding without cracking, and stays flat on large panels. It also welds well with standard 5xxx filler wire, although welded areas show a colour change after anodising and are usually kept out of sight.

Why designers choose 5005

  • Uniform, clear anodised appearance that matches 6063 extrusions
  • Stronger than 1100 and 3003 at a similar cost level
  • Good formability in H32 and H34 for folding, roll-forming and pressing
  • Good resistance to urban, industrial and coastal atmospheres

Tempers

Choosing the right 5005 temper

5005-H34

Strain hardened and stabilised to half-hard. The usual temper for anodised cladding and composite-panel skins: stiff enough to stay flat on large panels, ductile enough for folded returns.

5005-H32

Quarter-hard and stabilised. Easier to fold and roll-form than H34; used for cassette panels with tight returns and for trims.

5005-H14 / H24

Half-hard, either cold-rolled direct (H14) or over-rolled and partially annealed (H24). H24 gives better bendability than H14 at similar strength, which helps tight folds.

5005-O

Fully annealed for deep drawing, spinning and pressings such as lighting reflector housings and cookware lids.

Anodising quality

A controlled-chemistry, controlled-surface variant for colour-critical work. Ask for it, and order all panels for one facade from the same production lot.

Pre-anodised coil

If you do not want to anodise after fabrication, see our anodised sheet range, where 5005 is a common base metal.

Tolerances & inspection

Tolerances, surface quality and inspection

Thickness, width, length and flatness follow ASTM B209 (ANSI H35.2) or EN 485-4 for cold-rolled sheet. For facade work the surface matters more than the dimensional class: rolling lines, scratches and handling marks show through an anodic film rather than being hidden by it.

  • Specify which face is the visible face and whether PVC film is needed on it
  • Ask for sheets from one cast or production lot for each elevation to keep the colour consistent
  • Agree a reference anodised sample before the full order is processed
  • Check flatness requirements for large composite or cassette panels
  • Every lot carries an EN 10204 3.1 certificate with chemistry and tensile results
Working with 5005

Forming, joining and finishing 5005

Bending

In H32 an inside bend radius of about 0.5 – 1 × thickness is usually achievable for thin sheet; H34 needs slightly more. Bend across the rolling direction where possible.

Anodising

Anodise after forming. Clear and bronze/black two-step colours are common. Pre-treat consistently: etch time strongly affects the final gloss.

Welding

TIG and MIG weld well with ER5356 or ER4043. Welds and heat-affected zones look different after anodising, so plan joints on hidden faces.

Painting

Takes PVDF and polyester coil and powder coatings after chromate-free conversion treatment.

Machining

Soft and gummy in O temper; harder tempers cut better with sharp, polished tooling and lubricant.

Corrosion

Good resistance in most atmospheres. Isolate from copper and carbon steel and avoid alkaline mortar contact on site.

Applications

Where 5005 sheet is used

  • Architecture: anodised cladding, soffits, fascias, column covers, louvres and interior wall panels
  • Composite panels: skins for aluminium composite and honeycomb panels
  • Electronics: device housings, laptop and appliance trims where a fine anodised finish is wanted
  • Lighting: reflector bodies and luminaire housings
  • Transport interiors: rail and bus interior linings, trims and signage
  • Consumer goods: cookware, utensils and furniture components
  • Electrical: conductor and busbar uses where moderate strength and good conductivity are both needed

FAQ

5005 aluminium (aluminum) sheet: frequently asked questions

Why is 5005 preferred to 3003 for anodising?

3003 contains about 1 – 1.5 % manganese, which forms particles that give the anodic film a cloudy or yellowish tone. 5005 has very little manganese, so its film stays clear and matches anodised 6063 extrusions far more closely.

What is the difference between 5005-H32 and H34?

Both are strain hardened and stabilised. H32 is quarter-hard (about 115 – 160 MPa tensile) and bends more easily; H34 is half-hard (about 140 – 180 MPa) and stays flatter on large panels.

Is 5005 suitable for marine environments?

It performs well in coastal atmospheres, especially when anodised. For parts in direct splash or immersion, 5052, 5754 or 5083 offer more strength and better long-term seawater resistance.

Can 5005 be heat-treated to make it stronger?

No. 5005 is non-heat-treatable; strength is set by cold work. Heating only softens it towards the O temper.

How much does a 5005 sheet weigh?

A 1220 × 2440 × 3 mm sheet weighs about 24.1 kg, and a 1250 × 2500 × 2 mm sheet about 16.9 kg, based on 2.70 g/cm³.

What is the European designation of 5005?

EN AW-5005, chemical symbol AlMg1(B), Werkstoff 3.3315. EN AW-5005A is a close variant with slightly different limits.
