1050 Aluminium Sheet & Plate

1050 is commercially pure aluminium with at least 99.5 % Al, valued for very high formability, excellent corrosion resistance and high thermal and electrical conductivity rather than strength. Sheet and plate in 1050 and its close European variant 1050A are used for chemical equipment, reflectors, signage, heat exchangers and deep-drawn parts. Overview

What is 1050 aluminium?

1050 belongs to the 1xxx series, where the last two digits give the minimum aluminium content above 99 %: 1050 means 99.50 % Al. With almost no alloying elements, it is soft, extremely ductile and highly resistant to corrosion, and it conducts heat and electricity better than any alloyed grade except higher-purity 1060, 1070 and 1350.

Strength comes only from cold work. Annealed 1050-O is about as soft as aluminium gets; full-hard H18 is roughly twice as strong but still far below 3003 or 5052. For that reason, 1050 is chosen when the part must be deep drawn, spun, bent flat or anodised to a bright finish, or when conductivity and chemical resistance matter more than load-bearing capacity.

In Europe and much of Asia the standard variant is EN AW-1050A, which differs slightly in its zinc and titanium limits; the two are interchangeable for almost every use. In North America 1050 is less common, and buyers usually specify 1060 or 1100 under ASTM B209 for similar applications.

Why buyers choose 1050

  • Excellent formability for deep drawing, spinning and tight bends
  • Very good resistance to atmospheric, fresh-water and many chemical environments
  • High thermal and electrical conductivity, around 61 – 62 % IACS
  • Bright, even surface that anodises and polishes well

Working with 1050

Forming, welding and finishing 1050

Forming

Outstanding: 1050-O can be bent flat on itself, deep drawn and spun. H14 and H24 still bend tightly; H24 has better elongation than H14 at the same strength.

Welding

Welds very easily by TIG, MIG, resistance and friction stir; use 1100 or 4043 filler, or 1050/1070 filler for conductivity and colour match.

Machining

Soft and gummy; use sharp, polished, high-rake tools and lubricant to avoid built-up edge.

Anodising

Gives a clear, bright and uniform anodic film; widely used for decorative and reflective anodised products.

Corrosion

Excellent resistance to atmosphere, water and many chemicals, including nitric acid and some food products.

Brazing and soldering

Readily brazed and can be soldered with suitable fluxes.

Applications

Where 1050 aluminium sheet and plate is used

  • Chemical and food: tanks, process vessels and liners where purity and corrosion resistance matter
  • Lighting: lamp reflectors and light fittings
  • Building: flashing, cladding, insulation jacketing and roofing
  • Signage and printing: signs, name plates and lithographic plate substrate
  • Thermal and electrical: heat-exchanger fins, heat spreaders, cable shielding and transformer strip
  • Formed products: deep-drawn and spun parts, cookware, circles for utensils

FAQ

1050 aluminium (aluminum) sheet and plate: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 1050 and 1050A?

Both are 99.5 % minimum aluminium. 1050A is the European registration and allows slightly more zinc (0.07 % vs 0.05 %) and titanium (0.05 % vs 0.03 %), with no separate vanadium limit. In practice they are interchangeable.

Is 1050 aluminium strong?

No, it is one of the softest aluminium grades: about 75 MPa tensile when annealed and around 155 MPa when full hard. Choose it for formability, conductivity and corrosion resistance, not for structural loads.

Which 1050 temper should I order for bending?

O or H111 for the tightest bends and deep drawing; H24 for a good balance of stiffness and bendability; H14 when flatness and a little more stiffness matter and bends are moderate.

Can 1050 aluminium be anodised?

Yes. Its high purity gives a clear, bright and uniform anodised finish, which is why it is widely used for reflectors and decorative anodised parts.

What is the US equivalent of 1050 aluminium?

1050 is a registered Aluminum Association grade (UNS A91050), but North American mills more commonly supply 1060 or 1100 sheet to ASTM B209 for similar uses.

How much does a 1050 aluminium sheet weigh?

About 2.705 kg per m² per millimetre. A 1220 × 2440 × 1 mm sheet weighs about 8.1 kg and a 1500 × 3000 × 3 mm sheet about 36.5 kg.
