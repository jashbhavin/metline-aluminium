5086 Aluminium Sheet & Plate

5086 is a marine-grade Al-Mg alloy with about 4 % magnesium: slightly less strong than 5083 but easier to form, with the same excellent resistance to seawater and good welded strength. It is a favourite for boat hulls, workboats and tanks, especially in North America. Metline supplies 5086 sheet, plate and tread plate in O, H32, H34, H112 and H116 to ASTM B928, ASTM B209 and EN 485, worldwide.

Overview

What is 5086 aluminium?

5086 sits between 5052 and 5083 in the 5xxx family. With 3.5 – 4.5 % magnesium, 0.2 – 0.7 % manganese and a small chromium addition, it is non-heat-treatable and gains its strength from solid solution and cold work.

The practical difference from 5083 is formability. Lower manganese and slightly lower magnesium make 5086 a little softer, so it bends more readily and is easier to roll into curved hull panels, while its seawater resistance is essentially the same. Many North American boatbuilders and workboat yards standardise on 5086-H116 for hull bottoms and sides for exactly that reason, with 5083 used where higher strength is needed.

Like 5083, 5086 welds well with ER5356 or ER5183 filler, retains good strength in the welded condition and performs well at low temperatures. It shares the same limitation: sustained service above about 65 °C can sensitise the alloy, so it is not used for hot tanks or near engines without careful assessment.

Why choose 5086

  • Excellent resistance to seawater and marine atmospheres
  • Easier to form than 5083 at only a small cost in strength
  • H116 temper corrosion-tested to ASTM B928 for hull plating
  • Available as plain plate, sheet and slip-resistant tread plate

Tempers

5086 tempers explained

H116

Strain hardened under special process control and tested for exfoliation and intergranular corrosion per ASTM B928. The correct temper for hull and deck plating in seawater.

H32

Quarter-hard and stabilised. Same strength level as H116 but without the marine corrosion test; widely used for tanks, trailers and general fabrication.

H34

Half-hard and stabilised. Higher strength for flat panels and tread plate; slightly less formable.

O

Annealed for heavy forming, spinning and deep drawing.

H112

As-rolled plate, used for thick sections, machined parts and flanges.

Tread plate

5086 diamond, five-bar and similar patterned plate in H32/H34 gives slip-resistant decks with marine corrosion resistance.

Tolerances & inspection

Tolerances and inspection

  • Dimensions and flatness to ASTM B209 / ANSI H35.2 or EN 485-3 / 485-4
  • H116 corrosion testing (ASTM G66 and G67) reported where B928 is specified
  • EN 10204 3.1 certificate with chemistry and tensile results for each lot
  • Class society or third-party inspection when specified on the order
  • Pattern height and base thickness checked on tread plate
Working with 5086

Welding, forming and finishing

Welding

MIG and TIG weld readily. ER5356 is the most common filler; ER5183 gives slightly higher weld strength. Welded strength is close to the O-temper level.

Forming

Bends more easily than 5083. In H116/H32, inside radii of about 1 – 2 × thickness are typical for plate up to 6 mm.

Machining

Fair; soft and gummy in O. Use sharp, polished tools with high rake and coolant.

Painting

Blast or etch, prime and coat. Use aluminium-compatible antifouling below the waterline.

Galvanic contact

Isolate from stainless steel, copper and bronze fittings with insulating washers and sealants.

Temperature

Avoid sustained service above 65 °C; tough and strong at cryogenic temperatures.

Applications

Where 5086 is used

  • Boatbuilding: hull bottoms and sides, decks and transoms of workboats, fishing boats, pontoon boats and yachts
  • Marine structures: gangways, floating docks, pontoons and offshore access systems
  • Tanks: fuel and water tanks, cryogenic vessels and storage tanks
  • Transport: dump bodies, trailers and rail cars
  • Decking: tread plate for vessel decks, steps and ramps
  • Defence: naval superstructure components and support equipment

FAQ

5086 aluminium (aluminum) sheet and plate: FAQs

Is 5086 or 5083 better for a boat hull?

Both are excellent. 5083 is about 10 % stronger (minimum yield 215 against 195 MPa in H116), while 5086 forms more easily. Many smaller craft use 5086; larger welded hulls more often use 5083. The design rules you build to decide the final choice.

What is 5086-H116?

A strain-hardened temper produced under special control and tested for exfoliation and intergranular corrosion under ASTM B928. It is the temper to specify for plating in contact with seawater.

Can 5086 be welded?

Yes. It welds readily by MIG and TIG with ER5356 or ER5183 filler and keeps good strength in the welded zone, close to the annealed level.

Is 5086 suitable for hot fuel or engine areas?

Not for sustained service above about 65 °C, because it can become sensitised. 5454 or 5052 is preferred for hot service.

How much does a 1/4 in 5086 plate weigh?

A 48 × 96 × 0.25 in plate weighs about 111 lb (50 kg); a 60 × 240 in plate of the same thickness about 346 lb.

What replaced QQ-A-250/7?

The federal specification was cancelled in favour of ASTM B209 for general sheet and plate, with ASTM B928 covering marine tempers such as H116.
