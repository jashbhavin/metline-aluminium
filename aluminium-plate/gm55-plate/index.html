High-Strength Formable Aluminium Sheet: a GM55 Alternative

GM55 is a proprietary high-magnesium aluminium sheet developed in Japan for electronics housings, offering roughly one and a half times the strength of 5052. Metline supplies high-magnesium 5xxx sheet (about 5.6 – 6.0 % Mg) in thin gauges and O to H18 tempers as an alternative for stamped midframes, support plates and device chassis, matched to your property targets and shipped worldwide.

Overview

What is GM55, and what is the alternative?

GM55 is a trade name for a high-strength, non-heat-treatable Al-Mg sheet originally developed by Sumitomo Light Metal for casings of cameras, optical drives, flat-panel back covers and similar devices. Its selling point was strength close to cold-rolled steel sheet in an aluminium product that could still be stamped and anodised.

The strength comes from a high magnesium content, well above the 2.2 – 2.8 % of 5052. Magnesium in solid solution strengthens aluminium directly and also raises its work-hardening rate, so each step of cold rolling adds more strength than it would in a leaner alloy. No heat treatment is involved, which means parts keep their properties after forming and there is no risk of distortion from quenching.

Because GM55 is a proprietary product, buyers who need a second source, a different gauge or a shorter supply chain look for a sheet with equivalent chemistry and properties. Metline supplies high-magnesium 5xxx sheet for that purpose. We do not sell the trademarked GM55 product itself; we match your drawing's mechanical and forming requirements with a comparable alloy and temper, confirmed by test results.

Why designers choose high-magnesium sheet

  • About 40 – 60 % more strength than 5052 in comparable tempers, allowing thinner walls
  • No heat treatment after forming, so stamped parts stay flat and stable
  • Good corrosion resistance and an anodisable surface
  • Wide temper range, from fully soft O for deep forming to full-hard H18 for flat supports

Formability

Stamping and forming high-magnesium sheet

Electronic parts are blanked, bent, flanged and coined at high speed. The table gives starting guidance; confirm with a trial on production tooling, because die clearance, lubrication and grain direction all change the result.

TemperForming roomTypical use
OVery good: deep draws and tight bendsDrawn housings, complex formed covers
H32Good: moderate draws, bends around 1 × tFormed brackets and chassis with flanges
H34Moderate: bends around 1.5 – 2 × tSupport plates with simple bends
H36 / H38Limited: gentle bends, mostly flat partsMidframe plates, stiffeners
H18Low: flat or lightly formed onlyFlat high-strength shims and plates

Springback

Higher strength means more springback than 5052. Allow extra over-bend or a restrike operation in the tool design.

Stretcher-strain marks

Fully soft high-Mg sheet can show Lüders lines after forming. For cosmetic parts, use a slightly cold-worked temper or a light temper-pass to suppress them.

Die wear

The harder tempers are more abrasive than 1xxx or 3xxx sheet. Coated or carbide punches extend life on long runs.

Bend direction

Bend across the rolling direction where possible; bends parallel to it crack sooner in hard tempers.

For thin parts that are stamped rather than machined, high-magnesium sheet sits in a useful gap: stronger than 5052, with no heat treatment step, and cheaper to process than a machined 6xxx or 7xxx frame. When the part is CNC-machined from thicker stock, 6013, 6061 or 7075 usually gives a better result.

Finishing

Anodising and surface quality

High-magnesium sheet anodises well in natural silver and dyed colours, including black. Magnesium-rich alloys can produce a slightly warmer tone than very pure alloys such as 5252, so for visible cosmetic parts we recommend anodising a trial batch from production material and agreeing colour limits before series production.

Brushed, blasted and matte textures can be applied before anodising. Specify cosmetic grades, permissible defects and inspection distance on the drawing, because surface acceptance for consumer electronics is stricter than general engineering practice.

Al-Mg alloys with more than about 3 % Mg can become sensitised if held for long periods at 65 – 200 °C, which reduces resistance to intergranular corrosion. This is rarely a concern in consumer electronics but should be considered for parts near heat sources.

FAQ

GM55 alternative aluminium (aluminum) sheet: FAQs

Is this sheet the same as GM55?

No. GM55 is a proprietary product of its owner. We supply a high-magnesium 5xxx sheet with comparable chemistry and properties and confirm the match against your property targets with test data.

How much stronger is it than 5052?

Roughly 40 – 60 % in comparable tempers. For example, H34 sheet tests at about 340 – 350 MPa tensile against about 260 MPa for 5052-H34.

Which temper should I use for stamped midframe plates?

H34 to H38 for flat or simply bent plates, H32 when flanges and tighter bends are needed, and O for drawn shapes. Run a die trial before fixing the temper.

Can it be anodised for cosmetic parts?

Yes, in silver, black and dyed colours. Approve the colour on a trial batch, because high-Mg alloys can give a slightly warmer tone than very pure alloys.

What thicknesses are available?

Typically 0.10 – 1.00 mm, in sheet, with slit coil subject to confirmation.

What should my RFQ include?

Target properties or the material being replaced, temper, thickness, width, length or coil size, forming steps, finish, quantity, inspection criteria and delivery destination.

Looking for a second source to GM55?

Send your datasheet and drawings. We will propose a matching high-strength sheet and arrange test data.
