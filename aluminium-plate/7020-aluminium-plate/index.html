7020 Aluminium Plate

7020 (EN AW-7020, AlZn4.5Mg1) is the European weldable 7xxx structural alloy. It is stronger than 6082, it can be fusion-welded where 7075 cannot, and its heat-affected zone regains much of its strength by natural ageing after welding. That makes 7020-T6 and T651 plate a standard choice for rail vehicles, truck and trailer frames, cranes and protective structures. Overview

What is 7020 aluminium plate?

7020 contains about 4.5 % zinc and 1.2 % magnesium, with chromium and zirconium for grain control, and almost no copper. In the 7xxx family, the copper-free Al-Zn-Mg alloys are the ones that can be fusion-welded without hot cracking and without permanent loss of most of their strength.

The metallurgical reason is the natural ageing behaviour of Al-Zn-Mg. Welding heat dissolves the strengthening precipitates near the weld, but because the alloy is not very quench-sensitive, the heat-affected zone cools fast enough to keep zinc and magnesium in solution. Over the following weeks at room temperature, fine precipitates re-form and the zone regains a substantial part of its original strength. 6xxx alloys such as 6082 do not recover in this way.

Designers therefore use 7020 where a welded structure must be lighter than 6082 allows: rail car underframes, bogie parts, crane booms, military vehicle structures and heavy trailer chassis. The alloy is also used unwelded, as machined plate, where a little more strength than 6082 is needed without the corrosion sensitivity and cost of 7075.

Why engineers specify 7020

  • Around 15 – 20 % more proof strength than 6082-T6 in plate
  • Fusion-weldable, with natural recovery of the heat-affected zone
  • Fits European drawings directly as EN AW-7020 / AlZn4.5Mg1 / 3.4335
  • Stress-relieved T651 plate stays flat during heavy machining

Tempers

7020-T6 vs 7020-T651

7020-T6

Solution heat-treated and artificially aged. Normal for sheet and thin plate used flat or welded.

7020-T651

As T6, plus stretching after quenching to relieve residual stress. Standard for plate that will be machined, cut into large blanks or welded into precise assemblies.

After welding

The heat-affected zone recovers by natural ageing over roughly 30 – 90 days. A post-weld artificial ageing cycle can speed this up where the structure allows it.

Working with 7020

Welding, machining and surface treatment

Welding

MIG and TIG with 5356, 5183 or 5556 filler. Avoid 4xxx fillers, which give weak, brittle welds in Al-Zn-Mg. Clean oxide and degrease thoroughly; keep heat input moderate to limit the width of the softened zone.

Weld design

Place welds away from the highest stresses, avoid welds that leave sustained tensile stress across the short-transverse direction, and remove sharp notches at weld toes to support fatigue life.

Stress corrosion

Al-Zn-Mg alloys can be sensitive to stress corrosion cracking in weld zones with high residual stress. Correct filler choice, joint design and, where required, post-weld ageing or peening reduce the risk.

Machining

Good machinability, with crisp chips. T651 limits distortion; for very large pockets, rough machine, rest, then finish.

Cutting

Saw, waterjet and plasma cutting are all used. Plasma leaves a heat-affected edge that is usually machined back.

Surface treatment

Anodising, painting and powder coating give good protection. Zinc content can give a slightly uneven anodised colour, so it suits protective rather than decorative finishes.

Applications

Typical applications of 7020 plate

Rail vehicles

Underframes, bogie components, brackets and welded body sections where 7020 saves weight over 6082 while staying weldable.

Trucks and trailers

Chassis members, tipper bodies and special-vehicle frames that must carry high payloads.

Protective structures

Military vehicle hulls, crash structures and protective panels; projects normally specify their own test and certification requirements.

Lifting equipment

Crane booms, access-platform arms and lifting beams, where lighter structures increase reach and payload.

Bridges and platforms

Portable bridges, industrial platforms and support frames assembled by welding.

Sport and frames

Bicycle components and lightweight frames, usually from 7020 tube and plate together.

A typical example: a rail car builder replacing 6082-T6 floor members with 7020-T651 can reduce plate thickness by around 15 % for the same proof stress, while keeping the same welding procedure family with 5xxx filler.

FAQ

7020 aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQ

What is 7020 aluminium plate?

A weldable high-strength Al-Zn-Mg plate, designated EN AW-7020 / AlZn4.5Mg1 / 3.4335, used for welded and machined structures in rail, vehicle, lifting and defence applications.

Can 7020 be welded?

Yes. It is one of the weldable 7xxx alloys. Use 5xxx filler such as 5356 or 5183. The heat-affected zone recovers much of its strength through natural ageing after welding.

Is 7020 stronger than 6082?

Yes. 7020-T651 has a minimum proof stress of about 280 MPa in plate up to 40 mm, against 240 – 255 MPa for 6082-T651.

7020 or 7075 for a welded structure?

7020. 7075 is far stronger but is not suitable for fusion welding, while 7020 is designed to be welded.

What is the difference between 7020-T6 and T651?

Both are solution treated and artificially aged. T651 is also stretched to relieve residual stress, so it distorts less when machined.

Can 7020 be anodised?

Yes, for protection. Because of its zinc content the colour may be uneven, so it is better for protective than decorative finishes.

What affects the price of 7020 plate?

The aluminium price, thickness, plate size, temper, tolerances, cutting, quantity, packing and delivery terms.
