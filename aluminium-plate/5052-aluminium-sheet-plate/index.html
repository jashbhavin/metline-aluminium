5052 Aluminium Sheet & Plate

5052 is the strongest of the commonly stocked non-heat-treatable sheet alloys that still bends easily, and it resists seawater and fuels very well. That combination makes 5052-H32 the default sheet for tanks, enclosures, boats and vehicle bodies. Metline supplies 5052 sheet, plate and coil in O, H32, H34 and harder tempers to ASTM B209 and EN 485, cut to size and shipped worldwide.

Overview

What is 5052 aluminium?

5052 contains about 2.5 % magnesium and a small chromium addition. Magnesium in solid solution gives strength and seawater resistance; chromium refines the grain and improves resistance to stress corrosion. Strength is then raised by cold rolling into the H3x tempers.

In the H32 temper, 5052 offers roughly 50 % more tensile strength than 3003-H14 while still forming tight bends without cracking. It welds readily with no meaningful loss of strength in the O condition, and it does not suffer the sensitisation problem that affects higher-magnesium alloys in hot service, because its magnesium content is below about 3 %.

Those properties explain why 5052 appears in so many different industries: fuel tanks and pressure vessels, electronic enclosures, truck and trailer bodies, boat hulls under about 10 m, road signs, appliance panels and the honeycomb core used in sandwich panels. It is one of the most widely stocked aluminium sheet grades in the world, which keeps availability high.

Key strengths of 5052

  • High fatigue strength for a non-heat-treatable alloy, useful in vibrating structures
  • Very good resistance to seawater, marine atmospheres and many fuels and chemicals
  • Excellent formability in O and H32; good weldability by MIG, TIG and resistance welding
  • Suitable for service temperatures above 65 °C, unlike 5083 and 5086

Tempers

Which 5052 temper should you order?

5052-H32

Quarter-hard, stabilised. The standard temper for general fabrication: brakes cleanly to tight radii, welds well and is strong enough for tanks, boxes and body panels.

5052-H34

Half-hard, stabilised. Higher strength and stiffness for flat panels, covers and signage, with a slightly larger bend radius.

5052-O

Annealed. Used for deep drawing, spinning and heavy forming such as tank heads and complex pressings.

5052-H36 / H38

Three-quarter and full-hard. For springy parts, fins and strip that will not be formed sharply.

5052-H112

As-fabricated plate above about 6 mm, with properties from hot rolling; used where the plate is machined or welded rather than formed.

5052-H111

Slightly strain hardened after annealing; behaves much like O temper with marginally higher strength.

Tolerances & inspection

Tolerances and inspection

Unless the order states otherwise, 5052 is supplied to the standard tolerance tables of ASTM B209 (ANSI H35.2) or EN 485-4 (cold-rolled sheet) and EN 485-3 (hot-rolled plate). Cut-to-size blanks can be held to about ±0.5 mm.

  • Thickness, width, length, squareness and flatness checked against the order standard
  • Visual inspection for scratches, roll marks and corrosion stains
  • Tensile test results and cast analysis on the EN 10204 3.1 certificate
  • Optional third-party inspection or witness testing
Working with 5052

Forming, welding, machining and finishing

Bending

5052-H32 sheet up to about 3 mm typically bends to an inside radius of 0.5 – 1 × thickness; H34 needs about 1 – 1.5 × t. Use O temper for very tight forming.

Welding

MIG and TIG weld easily. ER5356 is the usual filler; ER5554 is preferred for service above 65 °C. The weld zone falls to about O-temper strength.

Machining

Gummy in soft tempers. Use sharp, polished, high-rake tools, high speeds and plenty of lubricant; harder tempers machine better.

Anodising

Anodises well with a slightly yellow tint compared with 5005; good for protective and coloured finishes.

Painting

Takes powder coat and liquid paint after suitable conversion coating.

Corrosion

Very good in seawater and marine air. Isolate from steel and copper fasteners with washers and sealant.

Applications

Where 5052 sheet and plate are used

  • Tanks: fuel tanks, hydraulic reservoirs, water tanks and road tanker parts
  • Marine: small boat hulls, decks, bulkheads, fuel tanks and fittings
  • Transport: truck bodies, trailer side panels, bus and rail interiors
  • Electronics: enclosures, chassis, cabinets and heat-dissipating panels
  • Building: roofing, wall panels, signs and street furniture
  • Appliances: refrigerator liners, cooker hoods and kitchen equipment
  • Honeycomb: 5052 foil for aerospace and architectural honeycomb cores
  • Pressure vessels: low-pressure vessels and heat exchanger shells

FAQ

5052 aluminium (aluminum) sheet and plate: FAQs

Is 5052 aluminium marine grade?

Yes. 5052 resists seawater and marine atmospheres very well and is widely used for small boats, fuel tanks and fittings. For large welded hulls, 5083 or 5086 in H116 or H321 is normally specified because those tempers are tested for exfoliation resistance.

What is the difference between 5052-H32 and 5052-H34?

H32 is quarter-hard (typical 228 MPa tensile) and bends more easily; H34 is half-hard (typical 262 MPa) and is stiffer. Choose H32 for formed parts and H34 for flat panels needing more strength.

Can 5052 be heat-treated?

No. It is strengthened only by cold work. Heating to about 345 °C anneals it to the O temper.

Is 5052 stronger than 6061?

No. 6061-T6 has a typical yield of 276 MPa against 193 MPa for 5052-H32. But 5052 bends and welds without the strength loss that 6061 suffers in the heat-affected zone.

How much does a 5052 sheet weigh?

At 2.68 g/cm³, a 1220 × 2440 × 2 mm sheet weighs about 16.0 kg and a 1250 × 2500 × 3 mm sheet about 25.1 kg.

What is the European equivalent of 5052?

EN AW-5052 (AlMg2.5, Werkstoff 3.3523). 5754 (AlMg3) is the more common European stock grade and is often accepted as an alternative after engineering review.
