Aluminium Forged Plate & Hand-Forged Blanks

Aluminium forged plate is a flat, rectangular hand forging: a cast ingot worked between open dies until its grain structure is refined and its properties are more uniform than those of very thick rolled plate. Metline supplies forged plate and near-net blanks in 7075, 7050, 6061, 2014, 2024 and 5083 to ASTM B247 and AMS specifications, with ultrasonic inspection and mill certificates, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is aluminium forged plate?

Forged plate is made by heating an aluminium ingot or billet and compressing it repeatedly between flat or simple dies on a hydraulic press, rotating and upsetting it so that the metal is worked in more than one direction. The result is a slab or block with closed internal porosity, a refined grain structure and properties that vary less between the length, width and thickness directions than in a comparable thick rolled plate.

Rolling works the metal mainly in one plane, so very thick rolled plate can show a marked difference between longitudinal and short-transverse properties, and the centre of the plate receives less deformation than the surface. Forging lets the producer control the reduction in each direction, which is why heavy, highly stressed parts such as aircraft bulkhead fittings, landing-gear supports, press platens, mould bases and pressure-vessel flanges are often machined from forged rather than rolled stock.

In standards terms, forged plate is a type of hand forging (open-die forging), covered in North America by ASTM B247 and by alloy-specific SAE AMS documents. It is different from "forged-look" or "tooling plate" products, which are rolled or cast and then machined. When a drawing simply says "forged plate", confirm whether the designer needs a true forging or just a thick, stress-relieved plate; the cost difference is significant.

When forged plate makes sense

  • Section thicknesses beyond the practical range of rolled plate, or where through-thickness properties must be guaranteed
  • Parts loaded in the short-transverse direction, where rolled plate is weakest
  • Fatigue-critical components that benefit from closed porosity and refined grain
  • Near-net blanks that save machining time and material on large parts

The decision is rarely about tensile strength alone. For many parts below about 150 mm thick, stretched rolled plate such as 7075-T7351 or 7050-T7451 meets every requirement at lower cost. Forged plate earns its price when the part is very thick, needs guaranteed properties in all three directions, or has a stepped shape that would waste a large share of a rolled slab.

Alloys

Alloys and tempers for forged plate

Forgings use their own temper designations. A "2" in the second digit after T (for example T652 or T7352) means the forging was compressed by about 1 – 5 % after quenching to relieve residual stress, the forging equivalent of the stretching that gives rolled plate its T651 temper. Order the forging temper, not the plate temper.

Rolled-plate standards such as ASTM B209 or AMS 4045 (7075-T651 plate) do not cover forgings. For forged plate, the governing documents are ASTM B247 and the AMS specification for the alloy and forging type.

A key point for buyers: the advantage of forged plate is uniformity and soundness, not a higher headline strength. A 7075 forging and a thin 7075-T651 plate have similar longitudinal strength. The difference appears in the centre of a 200 mm section and in the short-transverse direction, where the forging retains more of its properties.

Quality

Inspection and certification

  • Ultrasonic testing: full-volume scanning to ASTM B594 or AMS-STD-2154; specify the acceptance class (for example A or AA) on the order.
  • Mechanical testing: tensile tests in the directions your specification calls for, including short-transverse where required.
  • Conductivity and hardness: used to verify heat treatment, particularly for 7xxx over-aged tempers.
  • Grain flow and macro-etch: available on request for qualification lots.
  • Documents: EN 10204 3.1 certificate with chemistry, mechanical results and heat-treatment lot; UT report; third-party inspection on request.

Marine

5083 and 6061 fittings, shaft supports and heavy deck hardware.

Semiconductor and vacuum

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) forged plate: FAQs

Is forged plate stronger than rolled plate?

Not in the headline longitudinal strength. Its advantages are more uniform properties through the thickness, better short-transverse values and closed internal porosity, which matter most in very thick or fatigue-critical parts.

What does the temper T652 mean?

Solution heat-treated, stress-relieved by compressing the forging about 1 – 5 %, then artificially aged. It is the forging counterpart of the T651 temper used for stretched rolled plate.

Which standard covers aluminium forged plate?

ASTM B247 covers die forgings, hand forgings and rolled rings; aerospace orders usually add an alloy-specific AMS specification. Rolled-plate standards such as ASTM B209 do not apply.

Can forged plate be ultrasonically tested?

Yes. Full-volume UT to ASTM B594 or AMS-STD-2154 is normal for aerospace and mould-making forgings. Specify the acceptance class on your purchase order.

When should I use rolled plate instead?

When the section is within normal plate thickness and loads are mainly in the plane of the plate. Stretched 7075-T7351, 7050-T7451 or 6061-T651 plate is cheaper and has tighter dimensional tolerances.

Do you ship forged blanks internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies forged plate worldwide with export packing, certificates and shipping by sea or air.
