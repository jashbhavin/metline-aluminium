Overview

What is 3003 aluminium sheet?

3003 adds about 1.2 % manganese and a little copper to commercially pure aluminium. Manganese forms fine particles that strengthen the metal and refine its grain without hurting corrosion resistance much, so 3003 forms almost as easily as 1100 but is noticeably stiffer and stronger in every temper.

It is non-heat-treatable; strength comes from cold work. Half-hard 3003-H14 is the standard stock temper for general sheet metal work, while O temper is used for deep drawing and spinning, and H16 and H18 for flat, dent-resistant parts.

Typical uses include fuel and chemical tanks, heat-exchanger and radiator components, cooking utensils, roofing and siding, trailer and truck panels, ductwork, signs and tread plate. In brazed heat exchangers, 3003 is the usual core alloy beneath an Al-Si braze cladding such as 4343 or 4045.

Why 3003 is so widely used

  • Moderate strength with excellent formability
  • Very good resistance to atmospheric and chemical corrosion
  • Welds and brazes easily
  • Low cost and available in every common sheet format

Tempers

Choosing a 3003 temper

O

Fully annealed: deep drawing, spinning, severe bends and stretch forming.

H14

Half hard: the common stock temper for brake-press bending, panels, ducts and tanks.

H24

Half hard but partially annealed: similar strength to H14 with better elongation, preferred for tighter bends and roll forming.

H16 / H18

Hard tempers for flat panels, fins and parts that must resist denting.

H112 plate

As-rolled plate for thicker components, tanks and tread plate.

Tread plate

3003-H22 or H24 diamond and five-bar plate for floors, steps and toolboxes (ASTM B632).

Fabrication

Forming, welding and finishing 3003

Forming

O and H24 allow tight bends (0 – 1 × t); H14 about 1 × t for thin sheet. Excellent for spinning and deep drawing in O temper.

Welding and brazing

Easily welded with ER1100 or ER4043 filler, and readily brazed, which is why 3003 is the standard heat-exchanger core.

Machining

Gummy in soft tempers; use sharp, high-rake tools and lubricant.

Finishing

Accepts paint and coil coatings well. Anodised 3003 has a slightly grey tone and is not ideal for decorative bright finishes; choose 5005 for architectural anodising.

Applications

Applications of 3003 sheet and plate

  • Tanks and vessels: fuel tanks, chemical and food storage, pressure vessels to ASME SB-209
  • Heat transfer: radiator and condenser components, brazing sheet cores, fin stock
  • Building: roofing, siding, gutters, ducting and insulation jacketing
  • Transport: trailer and truck panels, toolboxes, tread plate floors
  • Household: cookware, refrigerator liners, kitchen equipment
  • Signs and general fabrication: sign blanks, enclosures and covers

Need more strength with similar corrosion resistance? 3004 and 5052 are the usual next steps. For a cheaper, slightly weaker painted sheet, 3105 is common.

FAQ

3003 aluminium (aluminum) sheet: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 3003-H14 and H24?

Both are half hard with similar tensile strength. H14 is strain-hardened only; H24 is strain-hardened then partially annealed, giving lower yield and better elongation, which helps with tight bends.

Is 3003 stronger than 1100?

Yes, by roughly 20 % in the same temper, with similar formability and slightly lower conductivity.

3003 or 5052: which should I use?

5052 is about twice as strong and better in seawater; 3003 is cheaper and easier to form. Use 5052 for marine and structural parts, 3003 for general sheet metal.

Can 3003 be welded?

Yes, easily, by TIG or MIG with ER1100 or ER4043 filler, and it brazes well.

Is 3003 good for anodising?

It anodises for protection, but the film is slightly grey and less uniform than on 5005 or 6063, so it is not the first choice for decorative anodising.

Do you supply 3003 tread plate?

Yes, in diamond and five-bar patterns in common thicknesses, cut to size.
