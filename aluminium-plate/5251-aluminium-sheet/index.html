5251 Aluminium Sheet & Plate

5251 is a 2 % magnesium aluminium alloy with medium strength, very good formability and high corrosion resistance, including in marine environments. It is a long-established British and European sheet grade for boats, vehicle panels, pressings and containers. Metline supplies 5251 sheet and plate in O, H22, H24 and H26 to EN 485, worldwide.

Overview

What is 5251 aluminium?

5251 contains 1.7 – 2.4 % magnesium and 0.1 – 0.5 % manganese. It is non-heat-treatable, work hardens quickly and welds readily. In the UK it replaced the old BS 1470 grade NS4, and it remains a common stock grade there and in Commonwealth markets.

The alloy sits between 5005 and 5754: stronger than 5005 or 3003, slightly weaker than 5052 or 5754, and very easy to form. In the H22 temper it folds and presses cleanly while keeping enough stiffness for vehicle panels, boat superstructures and enclosures. It also anodises well, which makes it useful where a protective or decorative finish is needed on a formed part.

Because its magnesium content is low, 5251 does not suffer the sensitisation that limits 5083 and 5086 above 65 °C, so it can be used for heat-transfer equipment and boiler casings as well as marine work.

Why choose 5251

  • Very good formability and quick work hardening
  • High corrosion resistance, especially in marine atmospheres
  • Readily weldable; suitable for elevated-temperature service
  • Good anodising response for protective and decorative finishes

Tempers

Choosing a 5251 temper

H22

Quarter-hard, partially annealed. The most common temper: good for folding, panel work and boat building.

H24

Half-hard, partially annealed. More strength for flat panels and covers with moderate bends.

H26

Three-quarter-hard. For stiff panels, chequer and flat parts with little forming.

O

Annealed for deep drawing, spinning and heavy pressings.

The H2x tempers are rolled harder than needed and then partially annealed back. Compared with H1x tempers of the same strength, they bend more easily, which is why H22 rather than H12 is the normal fabrication temper.

Working with 5251

Fabrication and finishing

Bending

H22 sheet typically bends to an inside radius of about 0.5 – 1 × thickness; H24 needs a little more.

Welding

MIG and TIG with ER5356 or ER5183 filler; weld zone softens towards O temper.

Anodising

Very good response for both decorative and protective anodic films.

Machining

Fair; use sharp tools and lubricant to avoid built-up edge.

Corrosion

High resistance in marine and industrial atmospheres; isolate from copper and steel.

Temperature

Suitable for elevated-temperature service, unlike higher-magnesium alloys.

Applications

Where 5251 is used

  • Marine: small boats, superstructures, marine fittings and panelling
  • Vehicles: body panels, van linings and trailer sides
  • Pressings: panels, covers and formed housings
  • Process equipment: silos, containers, heat-transfer devices and boiler casings
  • Furniture: tubing and formed components
  • Aircraft: secondary non-structural parts, ducting and fairings

FAQ

5251 aluminium (aluminum) sheet: frequently asked questions

What is 5251 aluminium used for?

Boat building, marine panelling, vehicle body panels, pressings, containers, silos and heat-transfer equipment: anywhere a formable, weldable sheet with good corrosion resistance is needed.

Is 5251 marine grade?

It has high resistance to seawater and marine atmospheres and is widely used on small boats. For large welded hulls, higher-strength 5083 or 5086 is normally specified.

What is the old British name for 5251?

NS4 under the withdrawn BS 1470; H22 material was formerly described as NS4 H3.

What is the difference between 5251-H22 and H24?

H22 is quarter-hard (190 – 230 MPa), H24 half-hard (210 – 250 MPa). H22 bends more easily; H24 is stiffer.

Is there an ASTM equivalent of 5251?

5251 is mainly a European grade. In ASTM-based markets, 5052 is the closest commonly stocked alternative.
