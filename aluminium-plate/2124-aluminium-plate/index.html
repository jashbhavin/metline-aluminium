Overview

What is 2124 aluminium plate?

2124 has the same copper, magnesium and manganese content as 2024 but much tighter limits on iron (0.30 % maximum instead of 0.50 %) and silicon (0.20 % instead of 0.50 %). Those impurities form coarse, brittle intermetallic particles; reducing them makes thick plate tougher, more ductile in the short-transverse direction and more consistent through the section.

The alloy was developed for aircraft parts machined from thick plate, such as wing spars and ribs, bulkheads, fuselage frames and landing-gear support structure, where 2024 plate in heavy gauges would show low through-thickness elongation and fracture toughness. It is normally supplied in the T851 temper (stretched and artificially aged), which gives high yield strength, good resistance to stress-corrosion cracking and stable properties at moderately elevated temperatures.

Because 2124 is made specifically for critical aerospace use, its purchase specifications normally include guaranteed minimum fracture toughness, ultrasonic inspection of the full plate and full lot traceability. For non-critical machined parts, 2024-T351 or 2024-T851 is usually more economical.

Why aerospace engineers specify 2124

  • Higher plane-strain fracture toughness than 2024 in thick sections
  • Better short-transverse ductility and stress-corrosion resistance in T851
  • Good strength retention at moderately elevated temperatures (around 100 – 150 °C)
  • Stretched tempers stay flat and stable when most of the plate is machined away

Thick plate properties depend strongly on thickness and test direction. The longitudinal (L) direction is strongest; the short-transverse (ST) direction, through the thickness, is weakest and matters most for parts loaded across the plate. Tempers

2124-T851, T351 and O compared

2124-T851

Solution heat-treated, stretched 1.5 – 3 % and artificially aged. The main aerospace temper: high yield strength, improved stress-corrosion resistance and best dimensional stability for heavy machining.

2124-T351

Solution heat-treated, stretched and naturally aged. Lower yield strength but higher elongation and toughness; chosen where damage tolerance outweighs yield strength, or where the part will be aged later.

2124-O

Annealed plate for forming or for heat treatment by the user. Rarely ordered in thick gauges.

The artificial ageing step that turns T351 into T851 raises yield strength by roughly a third and changes the precipitate structure along grain boundaries, which is what improves resistance to stress-corrosion cracking. The cost is lower elongation and somewhat lower toughness than T351, so specifications choose the temper part by part.

Metallurgy

Why low iron and silicon matter in thick plate

In a thin sheet, coarse constituent particles are small and well broken up by rolling, and they have little effect on toughness. In a 100 mm plate, the centre receives much less deformation and cools more slowly during quenching, so particles remain larger and properties through the thickness are lower. Cracks prefer to run along bands of these particles.

Cutting iron and silicon reduces the volume of constituent particles. The practical effects, reported in aerospace data since 2124 was introduced, are higher plane-strain fracture toughness (KIC), better short-transverse elongation and more uniform fatigue performance in thick sections. That is why many aircraft programmes call for 2124-T851 where 2024-T851 would otherwise be used in heavy gauges.

  • Better resistance to fast fracture from a flaw or fatigue crack
  • More reliable through-thickness properties for lugs and fittings loaded across the plate
  • Lower risk of rejection at ultrasonic inspection due to inclusions

The same principle explains other "high-purity" aerospace variants such as 7475 (from 7075) and 2524 (from 2024 sheet).

Quality

Inspection and certification of 2124 plate

Thick aerospace plate is accepted on more than tensile results. Typical requirements on a 2124 order are listed below; tell us which apply to your programme.

  • Chemistry: cast analysis confirming the low Fe and Si limits
  • Tensile tests: L, LT and, for thicker plate, ST directions at the locations defined in the specification
  • Fracture toughness: KIC testing to ASTM E399 where the specification guarantees minimum values
  • Ultrasonic inspection: to AMS-STD-2154, commonly class A or AA for critical parts
  • Electrical conductivity and hardness: as a check on correct heat treatment
  • Traceability: every piece marked with alloy, temper, lot and plate number, carried through cutting
Machining

Machining and processing thick 2124 plate

Machining

Machines well with carbide tooling at high speeds. Because up to 90 % of a plate may be removed, rough both sides symmetrically and finish in stages to keep residual-stress distortion to a minimum.

Orientation

Lay out parts so primary loads run in the L or LT direction. Keep lugs and attachment holes away from the plate mid-thickness when ST loading is high.

Joining

Not fusion welded. Parts are bolted or riveted into assemblies.

Protection

Finish with anodising (sulphuric or boric-sulphuric acid), conversion coating and primer as specified for the aircraft programme.

Buying advice

Common mistakes when buying 2124 plate

  1. Not stating the revisionAMS 4101 and customer specifications change over time. Quote the revision on the order so the certificate matches the drawing.
  2. Ignoring orientationA cut block without L, LT and ST marks cannot be laid out correctly. Ask for orientation to be transferred to every piece.
  3. Assuming 2024 data appliesDesign allowables for 2124 come from its own specification and handbook data; do not mix 2024 and 2124 values in stress calculations.
  4. Skipping ultrasonic classThick plate for rotating or primary structure normally needs class A or AA. Specify it at enquiry, because it affects which plates qualify.
  5. Over-specifyingFor thin plate or non-critical fixtures, 2024-T351 or T851 performs the same job at lower cost and with shorter supply times.
Applications

Applications of 2124 plate

  • Wing spars, ribs and bulkheads machined from thick plate
  • Fuselage frames, longerons and landing-gear support structure
  • Engine pylons and structure exposed to moderately elevated temperature
  • Military aircraft and missile structure
  • High-performance tooling and test fixtures where certified toughness is required

In each case the reason is the same: the part is machined from a thick section, carries high cyclic or static load, and must tolerate a flaw without sudden fracture. Where only one of those conditions applies, a more common grade is usually the better buy, and our team can help you compare the options before you order.

FAQ

2124 aluminium plate: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 2124 and 2024?

The alloying elements are the same, but 2124 limits iron to 0.30 % and silicon to 0.20 %. Fewer coarse particles give thick 2124 plate higher fracture toughness and better through-thickness ductility than 2024.

Why is 2124 usually supplied in T851?

T851 combines high yield strength with better stress-corrosion resistance and dimensional stability than naturally aged T351, which suits heavily machined aerospace parts.

What thickness is 2124 plate made in?

It is aimed at thick sections, usually about 25 – 150 mm (1 – 6 in). Thinner plate is normally ordered in 2024 because the toughness benefit is small.

Can 2124 replace 2024 on a drawing?

Technically 2124 meets or exceeds 2024 properties, but in aerospace a substitution must be approved by the design authority. For non-aerospace work it is usually acceptable.

Is 2124 plate weldable?

No, not by conventional fusion welding. It is machined and mechanically fastened.

Do you supply small cut pieces of 2124-T851?

Yes. We saw-cut pieces from full plates to your sizes, mark orientation and lot on each piece and supply a 3.1 certificate.
