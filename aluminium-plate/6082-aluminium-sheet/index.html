6082 T6 & T651 Aluminium Sheet and Plate

6082 is the standard heat-treatable structural aluminium alloy in Europe, the Middle East and much of Asia, with the highest strength of the common 6xxx grades. 6082-T651 plate machines well, welds readily and resists corrosion, making it the usual choice for machined parts, structural plates and welded frames built to EN standards. Overview

What is 6082 aluminium plate?

6082 is an Al-Si-Mg-Mn alloy. It carries more silicon than 6061 and a substantial manganese addition (0.4 – 1.0 %) instead of copper. Manganese controls grain structure and gives a fine-grained, tough plate, while the silicon excess pushes aged strength slightly above 6061 in thick sections.

The alloy replaced the British HE30/H30 and German AlMgSi1 grades and is now the reference structural 6xxx alloy in EN 1999 (Eurocode 9) design. It is supplied as sheet, plate, bar, tube and extrusions, which lets designers keep one alloy across a whole fabrication.

In practice, 6082-T651 is to European workshops what 6061-T651 is in North America: the default plate for jigs, fixtures, base plates, machine frames, vehicle parts and offshore and marine fittings. The two are close substitutes, but they are not identical, so a substitution should always be checked against the design standard.

Tempers

Which 6082 temper to order

6082-T651

Solution treated, stretched to relieve stress and aged. Standard for plate over about 6 mm that will be machined; stays flat as material is removed.

6082-T6

Same strength without stretching. Normal for sheet up to about 6 mm used flat or lightly formed.

6082-T4 / T451

Naturally aged; easier to form. Age to T6 after forming if strength is needed.

6082-O

Annealed for maximum formability. Re-heat-treat after forming to restore strength.

Working with 6082

Machining, welding, forming and finishing

Machining

Good in T6/T651, with short chips when tools are sharp. T651 minimises distortion in pocketed parts. Use carbide with high positive rake and flood coolant.

Welding

Readily welded by MIG and TIG with 4043, 5356 or 5183 filler. EN 1999-1-1 applies reduced strength in the heat-affected zone, typically to around half of the T6 proof stress, so welds are placed away from peak stresses.

Forming

Thin T6 sheet bends with inside radii of about 1.5 – 3 × thickness. For tight forming, use O or T4 and age afterwards.

Anodising

Gives a good protective anodised finish, though slightly less bright than 6063; the manganese can give a faint grey tint.

Corrosion

Good resistance to atmospheric and marine exposure; copper content is low, so it outperforms copper-bearing alloys in salt air.

Temperature

Like other aged 6xxx alloys, strength falls with prolonged exposure above about 150 °C.

Applications

Where 6082 plate is used

  • Engineering and automation: base plates, jigs, fixtures, machine frames and gantries
  • Transport: trailer and truck components, rail vehicle parts, crane and lifting structures
  • Construction: structural plates, bridge and walkway components, scaffolding fittings designed to EN 1999
  • Marine and offshore: brackets, gangways and topside fittings
  • Moulds and tooling: prototype moulds and low-volume tooling

FAQ

6082 aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQ

What is the difference between 6082-T6 and 6082-T651?

Both are solution treated and artificially aged. T651 is also stretched after quenching to relieve internal stress, so it stays flat when machined. T6 is normal for sheet; T651 for plate.

Is 6082 the same as 6061?

No, but they are close substitutes. 6082 uses manganese instead of copper and is slightly stronger in thick plate; 6061 anodises a little brighter and is more common in the Americas.

Is 6082 good for welding?

Yes. It welds well by MIG and TIG with 4043, 5356 or 5183 filler, but the heat-affected zone loses roughly half its proof strength, which design codes account for.

What is the old British name for 6082?

HE30 for extrusions and H30 / HS30 for other forms. Many drawings still use these names.

How much does 6082 plate weigh?

2.70 kg per m² per millimetre. A 1500 × 3000 × 10 mm plate weighs about 121.5 kg.
