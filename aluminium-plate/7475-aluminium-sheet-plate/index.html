Overview

What is 7475 aluminium?

7475 is an Al-Zn-Mg-Cu alloy with the same strengthening elements as 7075, but with iron capped at 0.12 % and silicon at 0.10 %, against 0.50 % and 0.40 % in 7075. Removing those impurities removes most of the coarse iron- and silicon-rich particles that act as starting points for cracks, which is where the gain in toughness comes from.

The alloy was developed for airframe structures designed on damage-tolerance principles, where the question is not only "how strong is the part?" but "how long will a crack take to grow, and how big can it get before the part fails?" In those designs, plane-strain fracture toughness (KIc) and fatigue crack growth rate govern skin thickness as much as tensile strength does. Published comparisons put 7475 well ahead of 7075 in the same temper, which is why it is used for wing skins, fuselage panels, bulkheads and spars on military and commercial aircraft.

Processing matters as much as chemistry. 7475 is rolled and solution heat-treated under tighter control than general-purpose 7xxx plate, and it is usually sold to aerospace material specifications rather than to general commercial standards. That makes it a specialist product: more expensive than 7075, available in a narrower range of sizes, and normally ordered with full traceability.

Why engineers specify 7475

  • Higher fracture toughness and slower fatigue crack growth than 7075 at similar strength
  • Over-aged tempers (T7351, T761, T7651) add strong resistance to stress-corrosion and exfoliation
  • Available as Alclad sheet for exterior skins that need galvanic protection
  • Good machinability and dimensional stability in stretched plate tempers

Tempers

Choosing a 7475 temper

7475 is sold in more tempers than most alloys because the airframer chooses a different balance of strength and corrosion resistance for each part. The table below summarises the options and the specifications they are usually bought to.

TemperFormTypical specificationWhat it gives you
T61Bare sheetAMS 4084Peak strength with good toughness; used where stress-corrosion risk is low
T651PlateAMS 4090Highest strength in plate; stretched for flatness and machining stability
T761Bare sheetAMS 4085Over-aged for exfoliation resistance with only a small strength loss
T7651PlateAMS 4089Plate equivalent of T761: good strength, good exfoliation and crack-growth resistance
T7351PlateAMS 4202Most over-aged: best stress-corrosion resistance, preferred for thick sections and short-transverse loads

When to choose T651

The part is a machined fitting or panel loaded mainly in the rolling plane, environment is controlled, and maximum strength-to-weight is the priority.

When to choose T7651 or T761

Wing and fuselage skins exposed to moisture and de-icing fluids, where exfoliation corrosion would be the life-limiting factor.

When to choose T7351

Thick plate machined into frames or bulkheads with significant short-transverse stress, where stress-corrosion cracking must be designed out.

Tolerances & quality

Tolerances, flatness and inspection

Aerospace sheet and plate is rejected for defects that would pass in general engineering. The checks below are the ones buyers most often specify; list any additional requirement in your enquiry so it is part of the quotation.

Typical quality requirements

Dimensional tolerances
ANSI H35.2 tables called up by ASTM B209 and the AMS specification; EN 485-3/-4 for EN orders
Flatness
Per the governing specification; stretched T651, T7651 and T7351 plate has low residual stress
Ultrasonic testing
Plate is commonly tested to AMS-STD-2154 (formerly MIL-STD-2154), class A or AA as specified
Conductivity and hardness
Eddy-current conductivity and hardness checks confirm temper, especially for over-aged material
Surface
Free of roll marks, pick-up, scratches, corrosion and water stains beyond the specification limits
Certification
Mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 with heat number, chemistry and mechanical results

Common rejection causes to guard against

  • Surface damage in handling: aerospace sheet should be interleaved and moved flat
  • Wrong temper substitution, for example T651 supplied where T7351 was required
  • Missing short-transverse properties or ultrasonic class on thick plate
  • Certificates that do not trace to the heat and lot actually shipped
Working with 7475

Machining, forming, joining and finishing

Machining

Machines much like 7075: short chips, good surface finish and fast metal removal with sharp carbide tooling and flood coolant. Stretched tempers keep pockets and thin webs flat after heavy machining.

Forming

Form in the O or freshly quenched (W) condition, then age to the final temper. Formability in T6x and T7x tempers is limited; use generous bend radii and check the specification for minimum radii.

Joining

Not considered fusion-weldable: like other copper-bearing 7xxx alloys it is prone to hot cracking. Assemblies are riveted, bolted or bonded. Friction-stir welding is used in some structures under qualified procedures.

Corrosion protection

Bare 7475 is normally protected by chromic, boric-sulphuric or sulphuric anodising plus primer. Alclad sheet relies on its cladding for protection on exterior skins.

Heat treatment

Solution treatment is followed by a rapid quench and controlled ageing. Over-ageing to T7x must follow the exact two-step cycle of the specification, so re-treatment is best left to qualified heat treaters.

Temperature limits

Like most 7xxx alloys, strength falls with prolonged exposure above about 120 °C. It is not chosen for hot sections; 2618 or 2219 are used there.

Applications

Where 7475 sheet and plate are used

  • Wing structure: upper and lower wing skins, spar webs and stiffened panels designed for damage tolerance
  • Fuselage: skins, bulkheads, frames and pressure-deck panels, often in Alclad sheet or T7351 plate
  • Military aircraft: structural panels where fatigue life under high load spectra governs design
  • Space and launch vehicles: machined panels and fittings that need toughness at low temperature
  • Defence equipment: high-strength structural parts and protective panels
  • Superplastic forming: fine-grain 7475 sheet has been used for complex superplastically formed aircraft parts
  • Precision industrial parts: highly loaded components where crack tolerance matters more than lowest cost

FAQ

7475 aluminium (aluminum) sheet and plate: FAQs

What is the difference between 7475 and 7075?

Both are Al-Zn-Mg-Cu alloys of similar strength, but 7475 has much lower iron and silicon limits (0.12 % and 0.10 % against 0.50 % and 0.40 %) and tighter processing. The result is higher fracture toughness and slower fatigue crack growth, at a higher price.

Which 7475 temper has the best stress-corrosion resistance?

T7351. It is the most over-aged temper and is used for thick plate and parts with short-transverse loading. T7651 and T761 sit between T7351 and the peak-strength T61/T651 tempers.

What is the density of 7475 aluminium?

About 2.81 g/cm³ (0.101 lb/in³). A 1 m² sheet weighs 2.81 kg for each millimetre of thickness, so a 1250 × 2500 × 10 mm plate weighs about 87.8 kg.

Can 7475 be welded?

It is not recommended for arc welding because of hot-cracking sensitivity and strength loss. Aircraft structures in 7475 are riveted, bolted or adhesively bonded; friction-stir welding is possible under qualified procedures.

What is Alclad 7475?

7475 sheet with a thin layer of high-purity, zinc-bearing aluminium bonded to each surface. The layer corrodes sacrificially and protects the core, which is why Alclad is used for exterior skins. Its strength is slightly lower than bare sheet.

Do you supply 7475 with aerospace certification?

Yes. We supply 7475 with EN 10204 3.1 mill certificates to the AMS or ASTM specification ordered. If your order requires a specific airframer approval, tell us at the enquiry stage so we can confirm a qualified source.
