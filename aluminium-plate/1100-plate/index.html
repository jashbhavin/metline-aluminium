Overview

What is 1100 aluminium sheet?

1100 is the workhorse of the commercially pure grades. Its 0.05 – 0.20 % copper gives a little extra strength and a finer response to cold work compared with 1050 or 1060, while silicon and iron together are capped at 0.95 %. It is non-heat-treatable, so strength is set by the degree of cold rolling.

In North America, 1100 is the default "pure aluminium" sheet. Fabricators use it for spun and deep-drawn parts, chemical and food-handling equipment, heat-exchanger fins, reflectors, nameplates and general sheet metal where corrosion resistance and an easy forming route matter more than load capacity. In annealed form it can be drawn into deep cups and spun into cookware and lamp shades without intermediate annealing.

The trade-off is low strength: even fully hard 1100-H18 reaches only about 165 MPa tensile, and the O temper about 90 MPa. If you need more stiffness with similar forming behaviour, 3003 (about 20 % stronger) is the usual next step; for real structural duty, move to 5052 or 6061.

Why buyers specify 1100

  • Excellent formability: deep drawing, spinning, stamping and tight bends
  • Very good corrosion resistance in the atmosphere, fresh water and many chemicals
  • Easy to weld and braze with common fillers
  • High thermal and electrical conductivity (about 57 – 59 % IACS)
  • Widely stocked to ASTM B209, so it is easy to source and certify

Choosing a temper

Which 1100 temper to order

1100-O

Fully soft. Order it for deep drawing, spinning, flanging and severe bending. It is the only sensible choice for spun cookware and deep cups.

1100-H12 / H22

Quarter hard. A little stiffer for moderate forming; H22 gives more elongation for the same strength.

1100-H14 / H24

Half hard. The most common stock temper for general fabrication, flat panels, fins and parts with simple bends.

1100-H16 / H18

Three-quarter and full hard. Use for flat parts that must resist denting, such as nameplates and reflector backs. Bend only with generous radii.

1100-H112

As rolled, for thicker plate where light machining or welding is the main operation and a guaranteed hardness level is not needed.

Not sure?

Tell us the operations (drawing ratio, bend radius, welding) and we will recommend the temper that runs cleanly on your line.

Fabrication

Working with 1100 aluminium

Forming and spinning

O temper tolerates a 0 × t bend radius and very deep draws. Allow for springback in hard tempers and for orange-peel texture if coarse-grained O material is stretched heavily.

Welding

TIG and MIG weld easily with ER1100 filler for colour match and conductivity, or ER4043 for better fluidity and crack resistance. Gas and resistance welding also work.

Machining

Soft and gummy. Use sharp, high-rake tools, high speeds and lubricant; harder tempers give a better finish.

Finishing

Anodises well for protection, though the copper content makes the film slightly less bright than on 1070 or 1085. Also accepts paint and lacquer.

Applications

Typical uses of 1100 sheet and plate

  • Spun and drawn ware: cookware, lamp shades, reflectors, pressure-cooker bodies
  • Chemical and food processing: storage tanks, linings, trays and equipment exposed to food or mild chemicals
  • Heat transfer: heat-exchanger fins and plates, where conductivity and formability are needed
  • Electronics: PCB drilling entry sheet, shielding and simple conductive parts
  • Signs and nameplates: etched, anodised and printed plates
  • Building: flashings, decorative trim and insulation jacketing

FAQ

1100 aluminium (aluminum) sheet: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 1100 and 1050 aluminium?

1100 is 99.00 % minimum aluminium with 0.05 – 0.20 % copper; 1050 is 99.50 % minimum with almost no copper. 1100 is slightly stronger, while 1050 has slightly better conductivity and corrosion resistance. Both are very formable.

Is 1100 aluminium food safe?

Commercially pure aluminium such as 1100 is widely used for cookware and food-processing equipment. Food-contact compliance depends on the regulation in your market (for example EN 602 in Europe), so state it on your enquiry.

What is 1100-H14 used for?

Half-hard 1100 is the common stock temper for general sheet metal, fins, panels and parts with simple bends, where a little stiffness helps but forming is still required.

Can 1100 aluminium be anodised?

Yes. It anodises well for protection and decoration, though the film is slightly less bright than on higher-purity grades.

How strong is 1100 compared with 3003?

In the same temper 3003 is roughly 20 % stronger because of its manganese content, with similar formability. 1100 has better conductivity.

Do you supply 1100 circles for spinning?

Yes. We supply cut circles and blanks in O and H-tempers to your diameter and thickness, packed for export.
