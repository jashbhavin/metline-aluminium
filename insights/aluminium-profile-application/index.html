12 Applications of Aluminium Profiles You May Not Expect

Most people picture window frames when they hear "aluminium profile", but extrusions now carry train bodies, EV battery packs, solar arrays, robot frames and flood barriers. What links them is the extrusion process itself: it puts metal exactly where the load path needs it, integrates channels, ribs and screw ports into one section, and does so in alloys that are light, corrosion-resistant and fully recyclable. Here are twelve sectors, the jobs profiles do in each, and the alloys engineers typically specify.

The common thread

Why extrusion keeps winning new applications

An extrusion die can produce almost any constant cross-section, hollow or open, with internal webs, cooling channels, snap-fit grooves and screw bosses built in. A single profile often replaces an assembly of bent sheet, welded tube and brackets, which cuts parts, joints and labour.

Three material facts explain the rest. Aluminium has roughly one third of the density of steel, so a well-designed section saves weight even where its walls must be thicker. Its natural oxide film protects it in most atmospheres, and anodising or powder coating extend that further. And it can be recycled repeatedly without losing properties, which matters as buyers start to report embodied carbon. The 6xxx alloys (6063, 6060, 6005A, 6061, 6082) dominate because they extrude easily, respond to simple press quenching and ageing, and weld and anodise well. The 7xxx and 2xxx alloys are used where strength comes first.

1 – 4 · Transport

Aerospace, rail, vehicles and refrigerated transport

1. Aerospace

Fuselage stringers, floor beams and seat tracks are extruded because the shape runs the full length of the part and can be machined locally. High-strength 2024 and 7xxx alloys are used in stress-relieved tempers such as T3511 and T73511; 7050 and 7150 offer better toughness and stress-corrosion resistance in thick sections. Aerospace extrusions are bought to AMS or EN aerospace specifications with full traceability.

2. Rail transit

Modern metro and high-speed car bodies are built from large, wide hollow extrusions running the length of the car, joined by friction stir welding. 6005A-T6 is a common choice because it extrudes in thin-walled hollow sections and welds well. The result is a stiff, light shell that reduces energy use and track wear.

3. EVs and commercial vehicles

Battery trays combine extruded side frames and multi-cavity floor sections with integral coolant channels. Crash management systems use 6xxx and 7xxx profiles that fold progressively in an impact. On trucks, buses and semi-trailers, extruded side rails, floor sections and tipper bodies cut tare weight and raise payload.

4. Refrigerated containers

Reefer floors use T-section extrusions that let cold air circulate beneath the cargo, while door frames and posts use profiles that stay strong at low temperature. Aluminium does not become brittle in the cold, unlike many steels, and resists the condensation-driven corrosion found inside reefers.

In transport, the argument for profiles is usually lifetime cost rather than material price. A lighter body carries more payload or extends range for every trip it makes, and aluminium structures need no rust protection. See our guide to aluminium for EV battery trays and the transportation industry page for alloy-by-part detail.

5 – 6 · Marine & defence

Ships, boats and defence equipment

5. Marine

Fast ferries, patrol boats, workboats and superstructures on steel ships use extruded deck panels, stiffeners and frames. Classification societies (DNV, ABS, Lloyd's Register) approve 6005A, 6061 and 6082 extrusions and 5083 shapes for hull and deck use, subject to their material rules. Lower topside weight improves stability and speed.

6. Defence

Light armoured vehicles, rapidly deployable shelters, bridging equipment and launch rails rely on profiles for stiffness at low weight. Shelter walls often combine extruded frames with sandwich panels; vehicle structures use weldable 6xxx and higher-strength 7xxx extrusions. Armour itself is generally rolled plate or composite rather than extrusion.

A practical point for marine buyers: 6xxx profiles perform well above the waterline and in splash zones, especially anodised or painted, while permanently immersed structure is usually 5xxx plate. Careful isolation from steel and stainless fasteners prevents galvanic corrosion. Our marine page covers class approvals in more depth.

7 – 8 · Energy & electronics

Power distribution, electronics cooling and photovoltaics

7. Power and electrical

Extruded busbars in 6101 and 1350 conduct current at about half the weight of copper for the same conductance. Cable tray systems, switchgear enclosures and battery-storage racks use 6063 profiles that resist corrosion and need no galvanising.

Electronics cooling (heat sinks)

Finned heat sinks for power electronics, LED lighting, telecom equipment and computers are among the highest-volume extrusions. 6063-T5 and 6060 combine good thermal conductivity (around 200 W/m·K for 6063-T5) with the ability to extrude thin, closely spaced fins.

8. Photovoltaic

Almost every crystalline solar module has an extruded aluminium frame, typically 6063 or 6005 in T5 or T6 temper, anodised for weather resistance. Ground-mount racking, rooftop rails and single-axis trackers add large volumes of structural profiles that must resist decades of outdoor exposure.

Solar and storage are now among the fastest-growing markets for extrusions. Designers pay close attention to wall thickness, because frame stiffness governs how well a module resists snow and wind loads, and to anodic film thickness, which governs durability in coastal and desert environments. Related reading: solar energy and heat sink extrusions.

9 – 12 · Everyday engineering

Robotics, healthcare, furniture and flood control

9. Robotics and automation

Modular T-slot profiles let engineers bolt together machine frames, guards and workstations without welding, and reconfigure them later. Linear axes and gantry beams use precision profiles; humanoid and collaborative robots use extruded and machined arm segments to keep moving mass low.

10. Healthcare and mobility aids

Walking frames, wheelchairs, patient hoists, hospital bed rails and rehabilitation equipment use 6061 and 6063 tube and profiles. Light weight makes equipment easier for patients and carers to handle, and anodised surfaces tolerate regular cleaning and disinfection.

11. Furniture and interiors

Office systems, kitchen cabinets, display units, wardrobe frames and handles are built from 6063 profiles, often anodised or powder-coated in custom colours. Aluminium is increasingly chosen for outdoor furniture because it does not rust or rot.

12. Flood control

Demountable flood barriers use interlocking extruded beams stacked between posts. They are light enough for crews to install by hand before a flood, corrosion-resistant during long storage and recyclable at the end of life.

These markets favour extrusions for the same reason: a single section can include fixing grooves, cable channels and decorative faces, reducing the number of parts a manufacturer must stock. For framing systems see T-slot profiles and our guide to aluminium for humanoid robots.

Selection advice

How to choose the right alloy and temper for a profile

AlloyTypical yield strength (T6 unless stated)StrengthsWatch-outs
6063about 170 MPa (T5: about 145 MPa)Best surface finish and anodising; complex thin sectionsModest strength; not for primary structure
6060about 150 MPaVery easy to extrude; good for decorative and thermal partsLower strength than 6063
6005Aabout 215 MPa (minimum, thin walls)Thin-walled hollow sections with structural strengthFewer stockists than 6061 or 6082
6061about 275 MPa typicalStrong, weldable, widely available worldwideHarder to extrude in complex thin hollows
6082about 260 MPa (minimum, by thickness)Highest-strength common 6xxx extrusion alloy; European defaultSurface finish less refined than 6063
7075about 500 MPa typical (T6511)Very high strengthPoor weldability; lower corrosion resistance; costly

Values are indicative for orientation only. Specified minimums depend on temper, wall thickness and standard (EN 755-2, ASTM B221); design to the certificate values for your order.

  • Start from function: structural, thermal, decorative or electrical
  • Pick the lowest-strength alloy that does the job; easier alloys allow thinner walls and better surfaces
  • Check joining: 6xxx alloys weld and friction-stir weld well; 7075 generally does not
  • Specify finish early, because anodising quality depends on alloy and press practice
  • Confirm tolerances: EN 755-9 or EN 12020 for precision profiles, ANSI H35.2 for ASTM orders

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) profile applications: FAQs

What is the most widely used alloy for aluminium profiles?

6063 is the most widely extruded alloy, used for windows, doors, furniture, heat sinks and solar frames because it gives an excellent surface and anodises well. Where more strength is needed, 6061, 6082 and 6005A are used.

Are aluminium profiles strong enough for structural use?

Yes. 6082-T6 and 6061-T6 profiles are used in vehicle frames, ship decks, bridges and rail car bodies. Structural design follows codes such as EN 1999 (Eurocode 9) or the Aluminum Design Manual, which account for reduced strength near welds.

Why are train bodies made from aluminium extrusions?

Long, wide hollow extrusions joined by friction stir welding make a stiff, light shell with few parts. The weight saving reduces energy consumption and track wear, and aluminium needs no corrosion protection over the life of the vehicle.

Which aluminium profile is used for solar panel frames?

Solar module frames are typically 6063 or 6005 in T5 or T6 temper, anodised to resist weathering. Racking rails and trackers use similar 6xxx alloys, often 6005A or 6061 for higher loads.

Can I get a custom aluminium profile made to my drawing?

Yes. Custom profiles require a die made to the cross-section. Send a drawing with alloy, temper, tolerances, finish and length, and the supplier will confirm feasibility, die arrangements and supply terms.
