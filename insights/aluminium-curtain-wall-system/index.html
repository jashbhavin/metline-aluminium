Aluminium Curtain Wall Systems: A Specifier's Guide

A curtain wall is a non-load-bearing façade hung from the building frame. It carries its own weight and the wind, keeps out air and water, and accommodates building movement, and almost every modern system relies on extruded aluminium to do it. This guide explains the main system types, what the aluminium profiles must achieve, how to specify alloys, tempers, finishes and thermal breaks, and how performance is tested, so fabricators and project buyers can source the right material.

Basics

What a curtain wall does

Unlike a load-bearing wall, a curtain wall supports nothing but itself. Brackets at each floor slab transfer its dead load and the wind load into the structure, while the frame and glazing form the weather barrier.

That sounds simple, but the façade must resist wind pressure and suction without excessive deflection, drain any water that gets past the outer seals, limit air leakage, control heat flow and condensation, survive decades of sunlight and pollution, and move with the building as floors deflect, the frame sways and temperatures swing. Aluminium extrusions are ideal for this because a single profile can integrate structural depth, glazing pockets, gasket grooves, drainage channels, thermal-break slots and screw ports.

Typical components are vertical mullions, horizontal transoms, pressure plates and cover caps (for captured systems), unit frames and stack joints (for unitised systems), anchors and brackets, gaskets and structural silicone, and infill of glass, metal panels or stone.

Appearance

Exposed, structurally glazed and semi-concealed frames

Exposed (captured) frame

A pressure plate screwed to the mullion clamps the glass, and a snap-on cover cap finishes the joint. Gives a visible grid, robust mechanical retention and simple glass replacement.

Structurally glazed (concealed)

Glass is bonded to a sub-frame with structural silicone, so the outside reads as uninterrupted glass. Requires strict control of silicone, surface preparation and adhesion testing.

Semi-concealed

Captured on two sides and silicone-bonded on the other two, giving vertical or horizontal emphasis. Widely used on commercial buildings.

Whichever style is chosen, the aluminium surfaces that receive structural silicone must have a finish and pre-treatment approved by the sealant manufacturer, and adhesion should be tested on production samples of the actual finish.

Materials

Specifying the aluminium profiles

Curtain wall profiles are extruded mainly from 6063, with 6060 common in Europe; both extrude complex thin-wall shapes with a fine surface and anodise uniformly. Where long spans or high wind loads need more strength, designers move to 6005A, 6061 or 6082 for mullions, or increase the section depth.

Alloy and temperTypical tensile strengthTypical yield strengthUse in curtain walls
6063-T5186 MPa145 MPaTransoms, cover caps, trims, lightly loaded members
6063-T6241 MPa214 MPaStandard mullions and unit frames
6061-T6310 MPa276 MPaLong-span mullions, brackets, high-load members
6005A-T6 / 6082-T6Higher than 6063-T6Higher than 6063-T6Heavily loaded mullions, anchors, fins

Typical values from Aluminum Association data. Design to the specified minimums of EN 755-2 or ASTM B221 for the temper and wall thickness, which appear on the mill certificate.

Stiffness often governs before strength. Because all these alloys share an elastic modulus of about 69 GPa, a stronger alloy does not reduce deflection; only a deeper or heavier section does. In North American practice, mullion deflection is often limited to span/175 for spans up to about 4.1 m, and span/240 + 6.35 mm above that; European projects use limits set under EN 13830 and the project specification.

Energy

Thermal breaks and condensation

Aluminium conducts heat very well, so a continuous metal path from inside to outside wastes energy and causes condensation on the room side in cold weather. Modern systems interrupt that path with a thermal break: glass-fibre-reinforced polyamide strips crimped between inner and outer profiles, insulating isolators beneath the pressure plate, or poured-and-debridged polyurethane in some designs. The thermal performance of the frame, together with the glazing, determines the façade U-value and condensation resistance.

New systems are proven on a full-scale mock-up in a test chamber before production. For infill panels, fire performance has become a central issue: many jurisdictions now require non-combustible or limited-combustibility materials (for example EN 13501-1 class A2-s1,d0) for panels and insulation on taller buildings, which rules out polyethylene-core composite panels.

Infill

Aluminium panels used with curtain walls

Solid aluminium sheet panels

Usually 2.5 – 4 mm 3003 or 5005 with PVDF finish; folded and stiffened. Non-combustible and easy to recycle.

Honeycomb panels

Aluminium skins bonded to aluminium honeycomb give very flat, stiff, light panels for large spans.

Composite panels

Thin aluminium skins on a mineral-filled core; choose fire-rated cores that meet local regulations.

Perforated and shaped sheet

Sunscreens, ventilation zones and decorative layers in front of the glazing.

Metline supplies the aluminium materials (extruded profiles, sheet, coil, honeycomb and composite panel materials) to system companies and façade fabricators. Design, fabrication and installation of the curtain wall remain with the façade contractor and engineer.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) curtain wall systems: FAQ

What is the difference between stick and unitised curtain walls?

A stick system is assembled piece by piece on site; a unitised system arrives as factory-assembled, glazed units that are lifted into place. Unitised systems cost more to develop but install faster with better quality control, which suits high-rise buildings.

Which aluminium alloy is used for curtain wall mullions?

Most mullions are 6063-T6 (or 6060 in Europe). For long spans or high wind loads, 6005A, 6061 or 6082 in T6 temper are used, or the section is made deeper.

Why do curtain walls need thermal breaks?

Aluminium conducts heat readily. Polyamide or polyurethane thermal breaks interrupt the metal path, reducing heat loss and preventing condensation on the interior frame.

Which finish lasts longest on a curtain wall?

Exterior anodising of 20 – 25 µm and multi-coat PVDF coatings both give long service lives. PVDF offers more colours with excellent fade resistance; anodising gives a metallic look and a very hard surface.

What standards apply to curtain walls in Europe?

EN 13830 is the product standard, supported by EN 12152/12153 (air), EN 12154/12155 (water), EN 13116/12179 (wind load) and EN 13501-1 for fire reaction of materials.

Does Metline supply complete curtain wall systems?

No. Metline supplies the aluminium materials: profiles to your dies or system drawings, sheet, coil and panel materials. System design, fabrication and installation are carried out by the façade company.
