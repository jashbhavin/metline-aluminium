Aluminium Materials for Heat Transfer: Automotive, HVAC and Industrial Cooling

Almost every modern car radiator, air-conditioning condenser, heat-pump coil and EV battery cold plate is made of brazed aluminium. This article explains the materials behind those parts: clad brazing sheet and strip, fin stock, welded and extruded tubes and the alloys used for headers and plates, and how to choose between them for automotive, HVAC, power-electronics and industrial cooling.

Context

Why aluminium dominates heat exchangers

Copper conducts heat better than aluminium, yet aluminium has displaced copper and brass from most automotive and many HVAC heat exchangers. The reason is system-level performance per kilogram and per unit cost, together with a joining process, controlled-atmosphere brazing, that assembles a complete core in one furnace pass.

Thermal conductivity (pure metal)about 237 W/m·Kabout 400 W/m·KCopper wins per volume; thin aluminium fins close most of the gap in practice
Density2.70 g/cm³8.96 g/cm³A copper part of the same size weighs about 3.3 times as much
Conductivity per unit massAbout twice copperBaselineAluminium moves more heat per kilogram of metal
JoiningBrazed whole cores (CAB, vacuum), welded tubesSoldered or brazed joints, more manualAluminium suits high-volume automated assembly

Commercial heat-exchanger alloys conduct less than pure aluminium; for example, 3003 is typically about 160 – 190 W/m·K depending on temper, and 6063 about 200 W/m·K.

Material 1

Brazing sheet and clad plate

Brazing sheet is a composite made by roll-bonding a thin layer of aluminium-silicon filler alloy onto a higher-melting core. In the furnace the clad layer melts at about 577 – 600 °C and flows by capillary action into every joint, while the core stays solid. One furnace cycle joins fins, tubes, headers and side plates into a single leak-tight core.

LayerCommon alloysPurpose
Filler (braze) clad4343 (about 7 % Si), 4045 (about 10 % Si), 4047 (about 12 % Si)Melts and forms the joints; higher silicon flows at lower temperature
Core3003, 3005, 3105; Cu- or Mg-modified "long-life" cores; 6xxx such as 6951 for strengthCarries pressure and load after brazing
Waterside or interliner clad7072 (about 1 % Zn) or Zn-bearing linersSacrificial layer that corrodes first and protects the core from pitting

Typical uses are battery cooling plates, plate-type and plate-fin heat exchangers, oil coolers and condenser side plates. In vacuum brazing, magnesium in the filler or core breaks down the oxide film instead of flux; in controlled-atmosphere brazing (CAB), a non-corrosive fluoride flux does that job, and core magnesium is kept low because it reacts with the flux. See brazing sheet specification and 6951 brazing sheet.

Material 2

Brazing clad strip and coil

The same clad structures supplied as narrow slit coil feed high-speed tube mills and progressive stamping lines. The strip is roll-formed and high-frequency welded into header and flat tubes, or stamped into plates, turbulators and end caps.

For tube mills, the key properties are tight thickness and clad-ratio tolerance, clean slit edges without burrs, consistent mechanical properties along the coil and good coil winding so that the mill runs without breaks. Coil weight and inner diameter must match the decoiler.

  • Single clad 4343/3003 or 4045/3105 for welded header tubes
  • Double clad 4343/3003/7072 or 4045/3003/7072 for tubes that need internal sacrificial protection
  • Zinc-enhanced variants for coastal and high-humidity environments

Related reading: brazing clad strip and coil and brazing sheet in automobiles.

Material 3

Fin stock: where most of the surface area is

Fins carry most of the air-side surface of a heat exchanger, so fin material is the largest share of core weight in many designs. Fin stock is very thin strip, usually around 0.06 – 0.2 mm, corrugated and louvred to disturb the boundary layer.

TypeTypical alloysWhere used
Bare fin1100, 3003, 3102, 8006, 8011Mechanically expanded tube-fin coils in HVAC
Clad fin4045/3003, 4343/3003 and modified coresBrazed cores where the fin carries the filler
Zinc-alloyed finAl-Mn-Zn alloys such as 7072-type additionsSacrificial fin that protects tubes from pitting
Coated finHydrophilic or epoxy-lacquered 8011 and 3003Air-conditioner evaporators, coastal condensers

Fins must resist sagging at brazing temperature, which is why fin alloys are designed with controlled recrystallisation behaviour, and they should be slightly anodic to the tube so that any corrosion happens in the fin rather than through the tube wall. See fin stock material and hydrophilic lacquered foil.

Material 4

Tubes for refrigerant and coolant

Tubes carry refrigerant, coolant or oil through the core. Each tube type suits a different design and pressure.

Tube typeHow it is madeTypical alloysMain uses
High-frequency welded (HFW) tubeClad strip roll-formed and welded; round, D-shaped, square or stepped3003 or 3105 core with 4045/4343 clad, optional 7072 linerCondenser headers, heat-pump manifolds, collectors
Microchannel (multi-port extruded, MPE) tubeExtruded with many small internal channels1xxx, 3003 and modified 3xxx alloys; zinc-arc-sprayed for protectionParallel-flow condensers, evaporators, gas coolers, cold plates

Microchannel tubes give high heat transfer per unit volume and low refrigerant charge, which is why they have spread from cars into residential and commercial HVAC. Their thin webs make them sensitive to corrosion, so zinc coating on the outside and a slightly anodic fin are the standard protection. For more detail see microchannel tube, folded vs extruded microchannel tube and condenser header pipe.

Material 5

Materials for headers, plates and stamped parts

Heat-exchanger makers stamp side plates, end caps, headers, tanks and cold-plate covers from clad or bare strip and sheet. The material supplier's job is to deliver strip that forms consistently and brazes reliably; the stamping itself is done by the component maker on progressive and transfer dies. Metline supplies the aluminium stock for these parts and can cut it to size or slit it to width; it does not make finished heat-exchanger components.

Side plates and frames

3003 or clad 3003 strip, often H14 or H24, with enough strength to hold the core during brazing.

Headers and tanks

Clad 3003 or long-life cores, formed and pierced to receive tubes. Tube slot quality decides joint integrity.

Cold plates

Clad 3003 or 6xxx brazing plate stamped with flow channels, plus 6061 or 6063 for machined or extruded plates.

End caps and covers

3003, 5052 or 6061 sheet for welded or brazed closures; 5052 where higher pressure resistance is needed.

Turbulators

Thin clad strip formed into offset or wavy inserts to raise internal heat transfer.

Heat sink bases

6063 and 6061 extrusions or plate for power electronics, often combined with brazed fins.

Joining

Brazing processes and what they demand of the material

The joining process decides the material. Most aluminium heat exchangers are brazed, and each brazing route sets limits on core chemistry, filler choice, cleanliness and part design.

ProcessHow it worksMaterial implicationsTypical products
Controlled-atmosphere brazing (CAB)Parts fluxed with a non-corrosive potassium fluoroaluminate flux and brazed in a nitrogen furnace at about 590 – 610 °CCore and clad magnesium must be low (roughly below 0.3 % in total contact with flux) or the flux is poisoned; flux residue stays on the partRadiators, condensers, evaporators, charge-air coolers, cold plates
Vacuum brazingParts brazed in a high-vacuum furnace; magnesium in the filler or core evaporates and disrupts the oxide filmMg-bearing fillers such as 4004 or 4104 type, or Mg in the core; very clean surfaces needed; no flux residuePlate-fin cores, aerospace and hydraulic coolers, high-purity cold plates
Flame and induction brazingLocal heating of individual joints with filler wire, rings or paste plus fluxBare tube alloys such as 3003 or 6063 with 4047 or Zn-Al fillers; flux removal may be neededField repairs, refrigerant line joints, tube-to-fitting joints
Dip (salt-bath) brazingAssembly immersed in a molten flux bathThorough post-braze cleaning required; now less commonComplex legacy assemblies, waveguides

A practical point that is often missed: the brazing cycle anneals the core. A 3003-H14 sheet comes out of the furnace close to O-temper strength, so designs must be checked against post-braze properties, not as-delivered values. Where higher post-braze strength is needed, designers use Mg-, Cu- or Si-modified cores or heat-treatable cores such as 6951, which can age-harden after brazing.

Filler-metal forms for local joints, such as brazing rods, rings, preforms and flux-cored wire, are covered on our brazing and welding materials pages.

Focus

A closer look: battery and power-electronics cold plates

Liquid cold plates are the fastest-growing use of aluminium heat-transfer material. A typical EV battery cold plate is two clad sheets, one stamped with serpentine or parallel channels and one flat, brazed together in a CAB furnace, with inlet and outlet ports brazed or welded on. Others use extruded multiport profiles closed with end caps, or friction-stir-welded plates for power electronics.

DesignMaterialStrengthsWatch points
Stamped and brazed plateClad 3003 or long-life core, 4343 or 4045 filler, sometimes 7072 interlinerLow cost at volume, flexible channel layout, thin and lightFlatness after brazing, channel collapse, clad erosion
Extruded multiport (harmonica) plate3003, 6063 or 6061 profilesVery consistent channels, high pressure capability, low tooling costFixed channel layout, end-cap joints
Machined or FSW plate6061-T6 or 6063 plate and extrusionHigh flatness for IGBT and SiC modules, complex pin-fin featuresHigher cost per part, longer cycle times

For battery plates, flatness and surface condition matter as much as heat transfer, because the plate is bonded to cells with thermal interface material. Specify flatness in the brazed condition, the coolant (usually water-glycol), operating pressure and burst requirement, and any electrical insulation coating. Durability

Designing against corrosion

Aluminium heat exchangers fail mainly by pitting through thin tube walls. The defence is a designed hierarchy of electrochemical potential: fins and outer zinc layers are made slightly more active, so they corrode first and protect the tube, and long-life cores form a protective dense band after brazing that slows pit growth.

  • Keep fins anodic to tubes by adding zinc to fins or zinc-coating microchannel tubes.
  • Use 7072 or zinc-bearing liners on coolant-side surfaces of radiator and cold-plate tubes.
  • Choose long-life cores for exposed condensers and coastal or road-salt service.
  • Avoid direct copper-aluminium contact in wet areas, and use correctly inhibited coolant.
  • Test realistically: SWAAT (ASTM G85 Annex A3) is more representative for brazed heat exchangers than neutral salt spray (ASTM B117).
Practical advice

How to specify heat-transfer aluminium

  1. Define the structureFor clad products, state each layer alloy, the clad ratio per side and its tolerance, total thickness and temper.
  2. State the brazing processCAB or vacuum, peak temperature and cycle, so that core magnesium and filler choice are compatible.
  3. Give dimensions and tolerancesWidth, thickness, coil ID/OD and weight for strip; section drawing and length for tube.
  4. Name the standardsFor example ASTM B209 or EN 485 for sheet, ASTM B221 for extrusions, ASTM B313 for welded tube, ASTM B210 and B491 for drawn and coiled tube, EN 573-3 for chemistry.
  5. Agree testingMechanical properties, clad ratio by metallography, post-braze properties, sag test for fin stock and corrosion testing where required.
  6. Plan packingEye-to-sky or eye-to-wall coils, moisture barrier and desiccant, because clad surfaces must stay clean and dry for brazing.
Outlook

Trends shaping heat-transfer aluminium

Several shifts are changing what heat-exchanger makers ask of their aluminium suppliers. Refrigerant changes are the first. The move to low-global-warming refrigerants such as R-290 (propane), R-1234yf and CO2 (R-744) favours compact microchannel cores with a small internal volume, and CO2 systems run at much higher pressures, which pushes demand for thicker-walled multiport tubes and stronger header materials.

Electrification is the second. An EV has more heat-transfer circuits than a combustion car: battery cooling, cabin heat pump, e-motor and inverter cooling, often linked through a multi-way valve. Each needs plates, tubes and fins with tight flatness and cleanliness. Data-centre liquid cooling and grid-scale energy storage add similar cold-plate demand outside the automotive sector.

The third is recycled content and carbon reporting. Customers increasingly ask for the recycled share and carbon footprint of their aluminium. Clad products are harder to recycle into the same alloys because of their mixed silicon and zinc layers, so alloy designers are developing cores and clads that tolerate more recycled input. Expect environmental declarations to become a normal part of heat-exchanger material specifications.

Our view

Common mistakes we see

  • Specifying "3003 brazing sheet" without the clad alloy or clad ratio, which leaves the most important parameter undefined.
  • Using a magnesium-bearing core in a CAB furnace, which poisons the flux and gives poor joints.
  • Choosing fin stock on price alone without checking sag resistance, causing collapsed fins after brazing.
  • Ignoring the potential difference between fin and tube, so the tube corrodes first.
  • Storing clad coils in humid warehouses, where surface oxidation and staining reduce braze flow.

FAQ

Aluminium heat-transfer materials: frequently asked questions

What is aluminum brazing sheet?

A roll-bonded composite of a core alloy (usually 3003 or a modified 3xxx) clad with an aluminium-silicon filler such as 4343 or 4045. The clad layer melts in the furnace and forms the joints of the heat exchanger.

What is the difference between 4343 and 4045 clad?

4343 contains about 7 % silicon and 4045 about 10 %. 4045 melts at a slightly lower temperature and flows more, while 4343 gives less erosion of thin cores. The choice depends on part geometry and furnace cycle.

Why is 7072 used on brazing sheet?

7072 contains about 1 % zinc, making it electrochemically more active than the core. It corrodes sacrificially and protects the core from pitting, typically on the coolant side.

Is aluminium as good as copper for heat exchangers?

Copper conducts better per volume, but aluminium conducts about twice as well per unit mass, costs less, and can be brazed into compact microchannel cores. For most automotive and many HVAC designs, aluminium gives the better overall result.

Can you supply clad strip slit to narrow widths?

Yes. Metline supplies brazing sheet, clad coil and fin stock, slit to the width your tube mill or stamping line needs, with mill test certificates.

Which test is best for heat-exchanger corrosion?

SWAAT (ASTM G85 Annex A3) is widely used for brazed aluminium heat exchangers because it is more representative of real service than neutral salt spray.
