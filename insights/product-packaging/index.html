How to Pack Aluminium Products to Avoid Shipping Damage

Most damage to aluminium in transit is not dramatic: it is water staining from condensation, rub marks between sheets, dented coil edges and bent profile ends. All of it is preventable with the right packing for the product form and the route. This guide explains the packing methods used for sheet, coil and strip, foil, tube and bar, building profiles, wire, forgings and powder, how to control moisture in a container, and what to check when the shipment arrives.

The problem

Four ways aluminium gets damaged in transit

DamageCauseHow packing prevents it
Water stain (white or grey staining)Condensation or trapped water between tightly packed surfaces forms hydrated oxide that cannot be polished offDry packing, sealed film wrap, desiccant, VCI paper and no temperature shock at loading
Rub and friction marksSheets or coil wraps moving against each other under vibrationInterleaving paper or film, oil, tight strapping, correct pallet size
Edge and end damageImpact during handling and lifting, steel straps cutting edgesCorner and edge protectors, end caps, wooden frames
DeformationUneven support, overloading, coils resting on their outer wrapsFlat stiff bases, saddles and core supports, load limits on stacking

Water staining is by far the most common claim, and it usually happens during the voyage rather than at loading. A container moving from a warm, humid port into cooler waters can drop below its dew point overnight; moisture then condenses on the cold metal. Where two aluminium surfaces sit tightly together, the water cannot evaporate, and it reacts with the surface to form stains within days.

Principles

Five principles that apply to every aluminium shipment

  1. Pack dry and at ambient temperatureNever pack metal that is colder than the surrounding air, and keep packing areas dry; wet timber is a common source of moisture.
  2. Seal against humidityWrap in polyethylene film or VCI paper, seal joints with tape, and add desiccant sized for the volume and voyage.
  3. Separate surfaces that must stay cleanUse interleaving paper, protective film or a light oil film between sheets, depending on the customer's process.
  4. Support the product, not the packagingCoils need cores or saddles; sheets need a flat, rigid base; long profiles need supports at regular intervals.
  5. Make it easy to handle correctlyForklift bearers, lifting marks, centre of gravity and "keep dry" markings reduce handling damage.

Timber packing for international shipment must comply with ISPM 15: heat-treated or fumigated wood carrying the IPPC mark, or engineered wood such as plywood, which is exempt.

Product form 1

Packing aluminium sheet and plate

Sheet and plate are packed flat on a skid or in a wooden case. The base must be stiffer than the sheet stack, and the stack must not overhang it. Interleaving and oiling are chosen with the customer, because some downstream processes, such as anodising, painting or adhesive bonding, are sensitive to oil or paper residues.

Surface optionBest forNotes

Dry without interleavingPlate and thick sheet with limited surface demandsLowest cost; only for short, dry routes
PVC or PE protective filmPolished, brushed, coated and anodised sheetProtects visible faces; specify UV-stable film if stored outdoors

Wooden case

Plywood or ISPM 15 timber case with base bearers, film wrap and corner protectors. Standard for export.

Skid (pallet) packing

Stack on a timber skid, wrapped in film and fibreboard, strapped over edge protectors. Suits regional trucking and robust plate.

Bare strapping

Plates strapped directly to bearers. Only for thick plate that will be machined, on short dry routes.

Product form 2

Packing coil and slit strip

Coils are heavy and their outer wraps and edges are easily damaged. The two basic orientations are eye-to-wall (axis horizontal) and eye-to-sky (axis vertical). Eye-to-wall coils sit in wooden saddles or on a core pipe supported at both ends so the outer wraps never carry the coil weight; eye-to-sky coils sit flat on a pallet, which protects the edges but requires the customer to tilt them for uncoiling.

Eye-to-wall on wooden saddlesCoil wrapped in film and paper, edge and bore protectors, strapped to a saddle palletWide coils for rolling, painting and stamping lines
Eye-to-wall with core suspensionA tube through the core rests on end supports so the coil hangs freeBright and coated coils where outer wraps must stay unmarked
Eye-to-sky on palletCoil laid flat, desiccant in the bore, film-wrapped and strapped through the coreNarrow slit strip, fin stock, transformer strip

Crisscross (cross-frame) palletCoils held between timber cross-frames bolted togetherHeavy narrow coils on long routes
Product form 3

Packing aluminium foil rolls

Foil is the most sensitive product: a single dent or a few water droplets can scrap many metres. Rolls are never laid on their outer surface. They are either suspended by their cores or stood on end with cushioned end faces.

  • Horizontal suspension in a case: the core rests in slotted end boards or plastic end caps so the roll hangs without touching the case.
  • Core-supported pallets: steel or timber supports engage the core ends; used for jumbo rolls going to converters.
  • Vertical packing: rolls stand on their ends on cushion boards, with board layers between tiers when double-stacked.
  • Moisture control: each roll is sealed in a polyethylene bag, with desiccant placed inside the core and soft cushions at the end faces.

For foil going to food, pharmaceutical or battery applications, packaging materials must also be clean and low-shedding, and roll labels should carry alloy, temper, thickness, width, length or weight and the lot number for traceability.

Packing tube, bar and extruded profiles

ProductCommon packingPoints to watch

Long profiles are easily bent if a bundle is lifted at one point. Bundles over about 6 m should be lifted with a spreader beam or forks set wide apart, and timber spacers must be placed directly above one another so the load passes straight down through the stack.

Product forms 5 – 7

Packing wire, forgings and aluminium powder

Wire and rod coils

Coils or spools in cartons or cases, often with a light oil or film for welding and electrical wire; welding wire must be sealed against moisture to prevent porosity in use.

Forgings and rings

Boxed individually with cushioning for machined forgings; large rings held between timber cross-frames bolted together; rough forgings may ship bare on pallets.

Aluminium powder

Sealed plastic liners inside drums or bags, then cased. Fine aluminium powder is regulated dangerous goods for transport (UN 1396 uncoated, UN 1309 coated) and must be packed, marked and declared accordingly.

Identification

Marking, labelling and shipping documents

Good packing is wasted if the receiving warehouse cannot identify what is inside without opening it. Every package should carry a durable label on at least two sides, and the same information should appear on the packing list so each package can be matched to its mill certificate.

Handling marks matter as much as identification. Pictograms to ISO 780, such as keep dry, this way up, centre of gravity and sling here, tell crane and forklift operators how to lift the package. For heavy coils and plate cases, writing the gross weight in large figures on the package helps prevent overloading of lifting gear.

Loading

Loading and securing in a container

  • Inspect the container: dry floor, no holes or leaks, doors sealing properly.
  • Place timber dunnage or bearers so forklifts can reach under packages; keep packages off any wet floor.
  • Distribute weight evenly along the container and keep the centre of gravity low and central.
  • Block and brace so nothing can slide; fill gaps with timber or airbags, and lash heavy coils to the lashing rings.
  • Add container desiccants for long sea voyages, especially across climate zones.
  • Follow the IMO/ILO/UNECE Code of Practice for Packing of Cargo Transport Units (CTU Code).

A full 20 ft container often reaches its weight limit before its volume limit with plate and coil, so loading plans are made by weight, while extrusions usually fill the volume first. Long profiles may need 40 ft containers or open-top and flat-rack units.

Receiving

What to check when aluminium arrives

  1. Before unloadingPhotograph the container, seal and packages; look for wet floor, torn wrap or crushed cases.
  2. Count and identifyCheck package numbers and labels against the packing list and certificate.
  3. Inspect surfacesOpen a sample of packages promptly; check for water stain, rub marks and edge damage.
  4. Store correctlyIndoors, off the floor, away from doors and condensation; let cold metal warm up wrapped before opening.
  5. Report quicklyNote damage on the delivery receipt and notify the seller and insurer within the agreed time.

FAQ

Packing aluminium for shipment: frequently asked questions

What causes water stains on aluminium after shipping?

Condensation inside the container or package. When warm, humid air meets cooler metal, water forms between tightly packed surfaces, cannot evaporate and reacts with the aluminium to form stains. Dry packing, sealed film, desiccant and avoiding temperature shock prevent it.

Should aluminium sheet be oiled for export?

Oiling plus paper interleaving gives the best protection on long sea routes. If the sheet will be anodised, painted or bonded, ask for dry packing with interleaving or protective film instead, so no oil residue affects the finish.

Is eye-to-wall or eye-to-sky better for aluminum coils?

Eye-to-wall suits wide coils that go straight onto an uncoiler; eye-to-sky protects the edges of narrow slit coils and is more stable on a pallet. The choice depends on coil width, weight and the customer's handling equipment.

Do wooden cases need ISPM 15 marking?

Yes, solid timber packing for international shipment must be heat-treated or fumigated and carry the IPPC mark. Plywood, OSB and other processed wood are exempt.

How should aluminium be stored after delivery?

Indoors, dry, off the floor and away from doors where condensation forms. Let cold metal reach room temperature before unwrapping, and use stock in first-in, first-out order.

Can Metline pack to my own specification?

Yes. Metline packs to the customer's specification when requested, including interleaving, film, labels and case dimensions, and confirms packing details on the quotation.
