How to Cut Aluminium Sheet and Plate: 7 Methods Compared

For thin sheet with straight cuts, a guillotine shear is fastest; for plate, a carbide-tipped saw; for shapes, fibre laser up to medium thickness and abrasive waterjet when the plate is thick or must not see heat. Hand tools work for one-off jobs in sheet up to a few millimetres. The table below matches each method to thickness, accuracy and edge quality, followed by practical tips for clean aluminium cuts.

Overview

Aluminium cutting methods at a glance

Aluminium is soft, conducts heat quickly and reflects light, so it cuts very differently from steel. Saws and shears go through it easily, but the metal can smear onto blades, and thermal methods must cope with its high conductivity and reflectivity.

Mechanical methods

Shearing and sawing: fast, cold and economical

A guillotine shear cuts sheet by driving an upper blade past a fixed lower blade. Because aluminium has a much lower shear strength than mild steel, a shear rated for a certain steel thickness will usually cut thicker aluminium. Set the blade clearance at roughly 5 – 10 % of sheet thickness: too tight and the blades wear quickly, too wide and the edge rolls over with a heavy burr. Shearing is the standard way that mills and service centres cut sheet from coil and produce rectangular blanks.

For plate, sawing is the workhorse. Use carbide-tipped blades designed for non-ferrous metals, typically with a triple-chip tooth grind and a neutral or negative hook angle, which stops the blade grabbing the soft metal. High blade speeds, a mist or wax lubricant and firm clamping prevent the aluminium welding onto the teeth. Vertical and horizontal plate saws cut large plates accurately into blanks with no heat input, so the temper of heat-treated alloys such as 6061-T651 or 7075-T651 is unaffected.

Thermal methods

Laser and plasma: shapes at speed

Older CO2 lasers struggled with aluminium because the metal reflects that wavelength. Modern fibre lasers are absorbed better and cut aluminium sheet quickly and accurately, which is why laser is now the default for profiled sheet parts. Use nitrogen as the assist gas for a bright, oxide-free edge that can be welded or painted without cleaning. Edge quality falls as thickness increases, so many shops switch to waterjet above about 15 – 20 mm when a smooth edge matters. Our laser cutting guide covers settings in detail.

Plasma cutting is fast and inexpensive for thick plate but leaves a rougher, slightly bevelled edge and a wider heat-affected zone. It suits structural parts that will be machined or welded afterwards, rather than visible or precision edges.

Cold precision

Waterjet and CNC routing: precision without heat

An abrasive waterjet erodes the metal with garnet carried in a very high-pressure water stream. There is no heat, so no heat-affected zone, no change in temper and no thermal distortion. That makes it the safest choice for thick plate, for heat-treated aerospace grades and for parts that will be anodised, where a heat-tinted edge would show. It is slower than laser on thin sheet and leaves a matt, slightly tapered edge on very thick sections.

CNC routers with single-flute carbide cutters cut aluminium sheet and aluminium composite panels accurately and leave a machined edge. For high-precision plate parts, sawing a blank and then milling the profile remains the most accurate route.

Workshop and site

Cutting aluminium sheet by hand

Aviation snips

For sheet up to about 1 mm. Choose left, right or straight-cut snips for curves. Leave a small allowance and file to the line.

Jigsaw

Use a fine-tooth metal blade and a slow to medium speed. Support the sheet close to the cut and apply a little cutting wax.

Circular saw

With a non-ferrous carbide blade it cuts sheet and thin plate cleanly. Clamp a straight edge as a guide and wear eye and hearing protection; aluminium chips are sharp and hot.

Score and snap

Thin soft sheet can be scored deeply several times and flexed along the line. Works only for straight cuts in O or H14 temper material.

Angle grinder

Use discs marked for aluminium or non-ferrous metals. General steel discs clog quickly and can shatter.

Nibbler

Electric or drill-mounted nibblers cut curves in sheet up to about 1.5 – 2 mm with no distortion.

Good practice

Tips for clean cuts in aluminium

  • Keep the protective PVC film on during cutting to prevent scratches, and remove it after deburring
  • Use lubricant or cutting wax on saws and drills to stop chips welding to the tool
  • Clamp thin sheet firmly to avoid vibration and chatter marks
  • Deburr every edge: aluminium burrs are sharp and can start cracks when the part is bent
  • Allow for kerf: about 0.1 – 0.3 mm on laser, about 1 mm on waterjet and 2 – 4 mm on saws
  • Collect swarf separately by alloy for recycling; clean aluminium scrap has good value

Alloy and temper matter too. Soft grades such as 1050 and 3003 in O temper tend to smear and burr, while harder tempers (H24, T6) shear and saw more crisply. Heat-treatable plate like 6061-T651 and 7075-T651 is best cut cold if the temper must be preserved right to the edge.

FAQ

Cutting aluminium (aluminum) sheet: frequently asked questions

What is the best way to cut aluminium sheet at home?

For sheet up to about 1 mm, aviation snips. For thicker sheet, a jigsaw with a fine metal blade or a circular saw with a non-ferrous carbide blade, with the sheet clamped and a straight edge as a guide.

Can you cut aluminum with a wood saw blade?

A carbide-tipped blade can cut aluminium, but blades made for non-ferrous metals, with a triple-chip grind and low or negative hook angle, are much safer and give a cleaner cut. Never use a blade that is not rated for the material.

Is laser or waterjet better for aluminium?

Laser is faster and more economical for sheet up to around 10 – 15 mm. Waterjet is better for thick plate, for heat-treated alloys and wherever a heat-affected edge is unacceptable.

Does cutting change the temper of aluminium?

Shearing, sawing, waterjet and machining do not. Laser and plasma create a narrow heat-affected zone at the edge, which can reduce local strength in heat-treated alloys; it is normally removed by machining if it matters.

How accurate is saw cutting for aluminium plate?

Modern plate saws typically hold about ±0.2 – 0.5 mm on length and width. Tighter tolerances need a milled edge.
