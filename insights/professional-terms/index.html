Aluminium Terminology: 110 Industry Terms Every Buyer Should Know

Aluminium has its own vocabulary, and a misread word on a purchase order can mean the wrong product arriving. This glossary explains the 110 terms you will meet most often in specifications, mill certificates and quotations, grouped from metal and alloy families through ingots, flat-rolled products, foil, tube, bar, wire, profiles, forgings, castings and composites.

Why it matters

Why precise aluminium terminology saves money

Our view: most order problems are not metallurgical, they are vocabulary problems. A buyer writes "rod" and means a 3 mm drawn wire; a drawing says "seamless" when a porthole-extruded tube would do at lower cost; a "sheet" request turns out to be 8 mm plate.

The terms below follow the meanings used in the European terminology standard EN 12258-1, the ISO 3134 series for light metals and the American National Standard ANSI H35.1/H35.2 published by the Aluminum Association. Where regional practice differs (Europe and North America draw the sheet-plate boundary in slightly different places, for example) we say so. Definitions are written in our own words; when a contract depends on a precise boundary, the standard named in the order governs.

Use the glossary in two ways: to read a specification you have been handed, and to write one that a supplier anywhere in the world will interpret the same way you do.

Three rules for writing clear aluminium enquiries

  • Give the alloy and temper together (6082-T6, not just "6082")
  • Name the product form with the standard that defines it (EN 755 bar, ASTM B221 extrusion, EN 485 plate)
  • State dimensions with tolerances, or name the tolerance standard, rather than relying on a word like "precision"
Terms 1 – 9

Metal, purity and alloy terms

No.TermMeaning
1Unalloyed aluminiumAluminium of at least 99.00 % purity; the wrought 1xxx series. Grades such as 1050, 1060 and 1100 sit here.
2Refined aluminiumAluminium of 99.90 % purity or higher, usually produced by three-layer electrolytic refining or segregation.
3High-purity aluminiumTypically 99.99 % (4N) to 99.999 % (5N) and above; used for sputtering targets, electrolytic capacitor foil and research.
4AlloyA metal made of a base element (the one present in the largest amount), deliberate alloying additions and residual impurities.
5Aluminium alloyAn alloy in which aluminium is the base metal and its content is below 99.00 %, or where specific elements exceed the limits for unalloyed metal.
6Alloying elementAn element added on purpose to give a property: Cu, Mn, Si, Mg and Zn define the series; Cr, Zr, Ti and others control grain structure.
7ImpurityAn element present without being intended, such as iron in most alloys or silicon outside the 4xxx and 6xxx series. Limits are set in the composition table.
8Master alloyA concentrated alloy of aluminium with a high content of one element (Al-Ti-B, Al-Sr, Al-Si, Al-Mn) added to a melt to adjust chemistry or refine grain.
9Alloying additiveA compacted tablet or flux-bonded form of an element (manganese, iron, chromium) that dissolves in molten aluminium at furnace temperature.
Terms 10 – 27

Wrought and cast alloy families

The first digit of a four-digit wrought designation names the main alloying element. Cast alloys use a different system (three digits and a decimal in the Aluminum Association scheme, such as A356.0), so the families are not numbered the same way.

No.TermMeaning and typical grades
10Wrought alloyAn alloy intended to be shaped by hot or cold plastic working: rolling, extrusion, forging, drawing.
11Al-Cu series (2xxx)Copper is the main addition. High strength, heat-treatable, modest corrosion resistance: 2011, 2014, 2024, 2219.
12Al-Mn series (3xxx)Manganese gives moderate strength and very good formability: 3003, 3004, 3105.
13Al-Si series (4xxx)Silicon lowers the melting point; used for brazing cladding and filler wire: 4043, 4045, 4047.
14Al-Mg series (5xxx)Magnesium gives strength by solid solution and cold work plus excellent seawater resistance: 5052, 5083, 5754.
15Al-Mg-Si series (6xxx)Magnesium silicide precipitates give heat-treatable strength with good extrudability: 6060, 6061, 6063, 6082.
16Al-Zn series (7xxx)Zinc, usually with Mg and Cu, gives the highest strength: 7075, 7050, 7020.
17Other series (8xxx)Alloys based on other elements such as iron and lithium: 8011 and 8079 foil, 8176 conductor, Al-Li grades.
18Casting alloyAn alloy designed to be poured or injected into a mould and used in the as-cast or heat-treated condition.
19Al-Cu casting alloysCopper-based castings with high strength at temperature (2xx.x in the AA system).
20Al-Si-Mg casting alloysHeat-treatable silicon-magnesium castings such as A356 / AlSi7Mg, widely used for wheels and structural parts.
21Al-Si casting alloysSilicon gives fluidity and low shrinkage; eutectic alloys like A413 / AlSi12 fill thin walls.
22Al-Mg casting alloysGood corrosion resistance and an anodised finish; harder to cast (5xx.x).
23Al-Zn casting alloysAge naturally at room temperature without solution treatment (7xx.x).
24Al-Ti casting alloysA family recognised in some national systems for castings with titanium as a key addition; uncommon in international trade.
25Other casting alloysGroups such as Al-Sn bearing alloys (8xx.x) and special compositions.
26Heat-treatable alloyGains strength by solution treatment, quenching and ageing (2xxx, 6xxx, 7xxx, most Al-Li). Tempers start with T.
27Non-heat-treatable alloyGains strength only by solid solution and cold work (1xxx, 3xxx, 5xxx). Tempers start with H or O.

Temper letters (O, H, T, W, F) are explained in our guide to aluminium tempers.

Terms 28 – 42

Molten metal, ingot and casthouse terms

No.TermMeaning
28Molten aluminiumLiquid metal tapped from reduction cells or melted in a furnace, before casting.
29Primary aluminium ingotIngot cast from smelter metal, sold by purity (P1020 = 99.7 % Al with Si ≤ 0.10 % and Fe ≤ 0.20 %).
30Recycled (secondary) ingotIngot produced by remelting scrap, refined and adjusted to a registered composition.
31IngotAny cast form meant for remelting or further working.
32Foundry ingotSmall notched ingot of a casting alloy, remelted by foundries and die-casters.
33Rolling ingot (slab)Large rectangular direct-chill (DC) cast block, scalped and rolled into plate, sheet and foil.
34Extrusion ingot (billet)Round DC-cast log, cut to length and pushed through an extrusion die.
35Forging ingotIngot or large billet with fine, sound structure intended for open-die and closed-die forging.
36Composite ingotA multi-layer ingot cast with two alloys bonded at the interface, used to make clad sheet without roll bonding.
37Round ingot / billetSolid circular ingot; the standard feedstock for extrusion and many forgings.
38Hollow ingotRound ingot cast with a central bore, used to extrude seamless tube on presses with a piercing mandrel.
39Rectangular ingotFlat ingot for rolling; the same thing as slab in common use.
40HeadThe top end of a DC-cast ingot (last metal to solidify), normally cropped before working.
41ButtThe starting (bottom) end of a DC-cast ingot, also cropped because its structure is uneven.
42Spray-formed ingotIngot built up by atomising molten metal onto a collector; gives very fine, uniform structure in highly alloyed grades.
Terms 43 – 62

Flat-rolled products: sheet, plate and strip

The sheet-plate boundary depends on the standard. In the Aluminum Association system, sheet runs from 0.006 in (0.15 mm) to 0.249 in, and plate starts at 0.250 in (6.35 mm). European practice places the line at 6 mm. Always state thickness in figures rather than relying on the word.

No.TermMeaning
43Sheet and plateFlat rolled products of rectangular section supplied in straight lengths.
44Thin sheetSheet at the lower end of the thickness range, normally cold rolled.
45Thick plateHeavy plate, typically from about 25 mm up to 200 mm and beyond; used for machined parts and tooling.
46Parent sheet / plateFull-size rolled product from which smaller pieces are cut.
47Hot-rolled sheet / plateFinished on the hot mill; plate thicker than a few millimetres is normally hot rolled.
48Cold-rolled sheetReduced at room temperature for better thickness tolerance, surface and, in H tempers, strength.
49Roll-cast (twin-roll cast) sheetMade from strip cast directly between two water-cooled rolls, skipping the hot mill.
50Alclad sheet / plateCore alloy with a thin metallurgically bonded layer of pure aluminium or 7072 that corrodes sacrificially to protect the core.
51Brazing sheetCore alloy (often 3003) clad with a 4xxx Al-Si layer that melts during brazing to join heat-exchanger parts.
52Corrugated sheetSheet formed into a wave or trapezoid profile for stiffness, for roofing and cladding.
53Embossed (stucco) sheetSheet given a shallow, irregular relief pattern by textured rolls; hides scratches.
54Patterned sheetSheet with a decorative rolled-in pattern, such as hammered or orange-peel finishes.
55Tread (raised-pattern) platePlate with raised diamond, five-bar or bar patterns for slip resistance.
56StripRolled product wound into a coil, or narrow product slit from a coil.
57Parent coilMill-width coil before slitting into narrower strip.
58Hot-rolled coilRe-roll coil coming off a hot tandem mill, feedstock for cold rolling.
59Cold-rolled stripCoil reduced to final gauge on a cold mill.
60Brazing stripClad brazing material in coil form, often slit narrow for tube and fin stock.
61Roll-cast stripContinuous twin-roll cast coil, common for foil stock in 8011 and 1235.
62Continuous cast-and-rolled stripStrip produced by casting a thin slab continuously (for example on a belt caster) and rolling it in line.
Terms 63 – 69

Foil terms and the "zero" naming system

Foil is flat-rolled product at the thin end of the range: up to 0.2 mm in European practice and below 0.006 in (0.15 mm) in the Aluminum Association system. The industry describes gauges by the number of zeros after the decimal point when thickness is written in millimetres.

No.TermThickness and use
63FoilRolled product at or below the foil limit, supplied in rolls or coils.
64Zero-free (heavy) foilAbout 0.1 – 0.2 mm (no zero after the point): containers, fin stock, lids.
65Single-zero foil0.01 to under 0.1 mm (e.g. 0.012, 0.040): household, pharmaceutical and cable foil.
66Double-zero foilUnder 0.01 mm (e.g. 0.0065): flexible packaging and cigarette foil, usually double-rolled.
67Embossed foilFoil passed through patterned rolls for grip, appearance or reduced contact area.
68One-side bright foilProduced by rolling two layers together; the faces against the rolls come out bright and the inner faces matte.
69Two-side bright foilRolled as a single layer so both faces are bright; usual for heavier gauges.

See our foil classification article for alloys, tempers and end uses by gauge.

Terms 70 – 80

Tube and pipe terms

No.TermMeaning
70TubeHollow wrought product of uniform wall, with round or other symmetrical cross-section, in straight lengths or coils.
71Seamless tubeTube with no longitudinal weld lines, extruded from hollow or pierced billet over a mandrel. Specified for pressure and critical service.
72Porthole (bridge-die) tubeTube extruded from solid billet through a die that splits and rewelds the metal, leaving solid-state seam welds. Economical for structural uses.
73Extruded tubeTube in its as-extruded state, whether seamless or porthole.
74Drawn tubeExtruded tube pulled through a die (often over a plug) for closer tolerances, thinner walls and better finish.
75Rolled tubeTube reduced by cold pilger rolling, used for thin walls and small diameters.
76Spun tubeTube or shell formed by spinning a blank on a mandrel.
77Forged and stamped tubeHollow part produced by forging or impact extrusion, such as cylinder bodies.
78Embossed tubeTube with a patterned or ribbed surface for grip or heat transfer.
79Variable-section tubeTube whose diameter or wall changes along its length (tapered, stepped or butted).
80Welded tubeStrip formed into a tube and seam-welded, usually by high-frequency welding; thin walls at low cost.

Terms 81 – 94

Rod, bar, wire and profile terms

No.TermMeaning
81Rod / barSolid product of uniform round, square, rectangular, hexagonal or octagonal section supplied straight. In North America "rod" is round and "bar" is any other solid shape.
82Extruded rodSolid bar in the as-extruded condition, usually to EN 755 or ASTM B221.
83Drawn (cold-finished) rodExtruded bar drawn through a die for tighter tolerance and a smoother surface, often to ASTM B211.
84Rolled rodBar or wire rod produced by hot rolling, typical for redraw stock.
85WireSolid product of small cross-section, often coiled; in North America under 0.375 in (9.5 mm) for round wire.
86Extruded wireWire produced directly by extrusion, used for brazing and welding feedstock.
87Drawn wireWire reduced to size by repeated cold drawing; the normal route for conductors and filler wire.
88Continuous cast-and-rolled wire rodRod cast and hot rolled in one continuous line (Properzi-type process), usually 9.5 mm conductor rod.
89Composite (bimetal) wireWire combining aluminium with another metal in one cross-section, such as copper-clad aluminium.
90Stranded wireSeveral wires twisted together, as in overhead conductors.
91Profile (section)Extruded product with a cross-section other than plain rod, bar, wire or tube.
92Hollow profileProfile containing one or more fully enclosed voids, made with porthole or bridge dies.
93Solid profileProfile with no enclosed void (angles, channels, tees). Semi-hollow profiles partly enclose a space.
94Micro-multiport profileThin flat tube with many small parallel channels, used in microchannel heat exchangers.

Practical advice

Six terms buyers most often confuse

Sheet vs plate

The boundary is 6 mm in Europe and 0.250 in (6.35 mm) under the Aluminum Association. A 6.35 mm product can be called either, so give the thickness in figures.

Rod vs bar vs wire

Rod is round, bar is any other solid section, and wire is small enough to coil. Specify diameter and whether you need straight lengths or coil.

Seamless vs porthole

Porthole tube has extrusion seam welds that are sound for most structural uses. Seamless is needed for high-pressure, critical or some drawn applications.

Alclad vs clad brazing sheet

Alclad protects against corrosion; brazing sheet carries a low-melting layer for joining. They are not interchangeable.

Foil vs thin sheet

A 0.2 mm product may be foil under EN and sheet under AA rules. Name the standard (EN 546 or ASTM B479 for foil).

Extrusion vs profile

Every profile is an extrusion, but extruded rod and tube are usually ordered under their own names and tolerance tables.

FAQ

Aluminium terminology: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between aluminium sheet and plate?

Only thickness. European standards call flat product thicker than 6 mm plate; the Aluminum Association uses 0.250 in (6.35 mm). Below that it is sheet, and below about 0.15 – 0.2 mm it is foil.

What does "1xxx" or "6xxx" mean?

It is the wrought alloy series. The first digit names the main alloying element: 1 for unalloyed aluminium, 2 copper, 3 manganese, 4 silicon, 5 magnesium, 6 magnesium plus silicon, 7 zinc and 8 other elements.

What is double-zero foil?

Foil thinner than 0.01 mm, written with two zeros after the decimal point (for example 0.0065 mm). It is usually rolled two layers at a time, which gives one bright and one matte face.

Is porthole tube weaker than seamless tube?

Correctly extruded porthole tube has sound seam welds and meets the same mechanical property minimums for most structural uses. Seamless tube is specified where internal pressure, critical safety or further drawing makes the seam a risk.

What is Alclad?

Sheet or plate with a thin bonded layer of pure aluminium or a zinc-bearing alloy on one or both sides. The layer corrodes preferentially and protects the high-strength core, which is why Alclad 2024 is used on aircraft skins.

Which standard defines aluminium terms?

EN 12258-1 in Europe, ISO 3134 internationally and the ANSI H35 series in North America. National systems such as GB/T 8005 and IS standards follow similar definitions.
