Using the calculator

How to use the fit calculator

  1. Enter the nominal sizeThe basic diameter shared by hole and shaft, in millimetres. ISO 286 groups sizes into ranges ("above 18 up to and including 30 mm"), so 25 mm and 30 mm give the same deviations.
  2. Choose the classesPick a preferred fit from the list, or set the hole letter and grade and the shaft letter and grade yourself. Capital letters are holes, lower case are shafts.
  3. Read the deviationsES and EI are the upper and lower deviations of the hole; es and ei are those of the shaft, in micrometres from the nominal size.
  4. Read the fitMaximum clearance is the largest hole minus the smallest shaft. Minimum clearance is the smallest hole minus the largest shaft; if it is negative, the parts interfere. The calculator classifies the result as clearance, transition or interference.

Supported: hole deviations C, D, E, F, G, H, JS, K, M, N, P, R, S, U and shaft deviations c to u, in IT grades 5 – 11, for nominal sizes up to 500 mm. Values are taken from the ISO 286-2 tables, including the Δ correction for K, M, N and P to U holes.

The system

How the ISO fit system works

ISO 286 describes every toleranced size with a letter and a number. The letter (the fundamental deviation) sets where the tolerance zone sits relative to the nominal size. The number (the IT grade) sets how wide the zone is. H7, for example, means a hole whose lower limit is exactly the nominal size and whose zone is IT7 wide: 0 / +21 µm at 25 mm.

Letters early in the alphabet put the shaft well below nominal (c, d, e, f, g give clearance against an H hole). The letter h puts the upper limit at nominal. js is centred on it. Letters from k onwards put the shaft above nominal, producing transition and then interference fits. Holes mirror this with capital letters.

Hole-basis system

The hole is H (lower deviation zero) and the shaft letter is varied to obtain the fit. This is the normal choice, because standard reamers, plug gauges and boring tools are made to H tolerances.

Shaft-basis system

The shaft is h and the hole letter varies. Used when one standard shaft, such as cold-drawn or ground bar, carries several parts with different fits.

IT grades

IT5 – IT7 are precision ground or finely machined; IT8 – IT9 are good turning and reaming; IT10 – IT11 are general machining and drawn stock.

Aluminium-specific advice

What changes when the part is aluminium

The ISO tables are material-neutral, but aluminium behaves differently from steel in three ways that directly affect fits.

Thermal expansion

Aluminium expands about 23 µm per metre per °C, roughly twice as much as steel. A steel bearing pressed into a 50 mm aluminium housing loses about 48 µm of interference if the assembly runs 80 °C above room temperature (50 mm × 12 µm/m·°C × 80 °C). Check fits at the extreme service temperatures, not only at 20 °C.

Lower stiffness and hardness

With about one third of the elastic modulus of steel, an aluminium hub yields and relaxes more under the same interference. Heavy press fits can gall or score the bore. Use shrink fitting (heating the aluminium part) or a lubricant, and consider a steel insert or bushing for high-load joints.

Coatings change size

Anodic films grow partly outward. A hard anodised coating 50 µm thick typically adds about 25 µm per surface, so a bore shrinks by about 50 µm on diameter. Machine to pre-anodise dimensions or mask fitted surfaces.

Measure and inspect at 20 °C, the ISO reference temperature. A 100 mm aluminium part measured at 30 °C reads about 23 µm larger than at 20 °C, which already exceeds a full IT6 band at that size.

Practical advice

Common mistakes when specifying fits

  • Tighter than needed: each IT grade step raises machining cost. Use H7 only where location really matters; H8 or H9 is fine for most clearances.
  • Ignoring temperature: aluminium-to-steel fits can loosen or seize across the operating range.
  • Forgetting coatings: anodising, plating or paint must be allowed for in the machined size.
  • Mixing systems: choose hole basis or shaft basis for the assembly and keep to it.
  • Specifying form loosely: a bore inside its size limits can still be oval or tapered; add roundness or cylindricity where the function needs it (ISO 1101).

FAQ

ISO 286 fits and tolerances: frequently asked questions

What does H7/g6 mean?

It is a hole-basis sliding fit. The hole is H7 (lower limit at nominal, IT7 wide) and the shaft is g6 (slightly below nominal, IT6 wide). At 25 mm the hole is 0 / +21 µm and the shaft −7 / −20 µm, giving 7 – 41 µm clearance.

What is the difference between ISO 286-1 and ISO 286-2?

Part 1 defines the system: tolerance grades, fundamental deviations and how fits are formed. Part 2 contains the tables of limit deviations for the commonly used hole and shaft classes.

Which fit should I use for a bearing in an aluminium housing?

Follow the bearing maker's recommendation for load type, then correct for aluminium's larger thermal expansion. Housings in aluminium are often specified one class tighter than in cast iron, or fitted with a steel sleeve, so the outer ring does not creep when warm.

Is the calculator valid above 500 mm?

No. ISO 286 has separate data for 500 – 3150 mm with different rules, and this tool covers up to 500 mm only.

Why is my JS tolerance shown with half micrometres?

JS and js zones are symmetrical, ±IT/2. Where the IT value is odd, the half value is not a whole micrometre. The standard permits rounding in some grades; the calculator shows the exact half value.

Can Metline supply bar and tube to an ISO tolerance class?

Yes. Metline supplies precision ground bar, drawn bar and precision drawn tube in a range of alloys, and can cut or machine to drawing, with mill test certificates.

Need stock for a precision fit?

Send the ISO class, alloy, size and quantity. Our team will suggest the right ground, drawn or extruded stock and quote delivery worldwide.
