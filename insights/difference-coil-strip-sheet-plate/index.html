Aluminium Coil vs Strip vs Sheet vs Plate

Coil, strip, sheet and plate are the same rolled aluminium in different shapes. Coil is wide sheet wound into a roll, strip is coil slit to narrow widths, sheet is flat material up to about 6 mm cut to length, and plate is anything thicker. Which one you buy depends on how your production line feeds material: continuous lines want coil or strip, while workshops and machine shops want flat sheet or plate.

Four forms

What each term means

All four products come from the same rolling route: a cast ingot is hot rolled, then usually cold rolled. The difference is how the material is finished and delivered, which affects price, handling, minimum quantities and the equipment needed to use it.

Coil

Flat-rolled sheet wound into a roll after cold rolling, in full mill width or trimmed width. Typical thickness about 0.2 – 6 mm. It is the starting point for most sheet and strip, and the natural feed for roll-forming, stamping, coating and slitting lines.

Strip

Coil slit lengthwise into narrow widths, from a few millimetres to a few hundred. Wound into pancake coils or traverse-wound (oscillated) reels for long continuous runs. Used for transformer winding, fin stock, cable wrap, trims and stamped parts.

Sheet

Coil uncoiled, levelled and cut to length into flat rectangles, or rolled flat to size. Thickness up to 6 mm (EN) or 0.249 in (ASTM). Sold by the sheet or pack, in standard sizes such as 1000 × 2000, 1220 × 2440 and 1500 × 3000 mm.

Plate

Flat material over 6 mm (EN) or from 0.250 in (ASTM), hot rolled and supplied as cut plates. Heat-treatable grades are usually stretched (T651) for flatness. Machined, welded or used structurally.

Ranges are typical market offerings, not limits of any standard. Inner diameter, outer diameter and coil weight are agreed on the order to suit the customer's decoiler.

There is no formal width at which "coil" becomes "strip". In trade, material slit to narrow widths for a specific process is called strip, while wide material is called coil or sheet in coil. When you order, quote the exact width and slit tolerance rather than relying on the name.

Buying decision

When to buy coil or strip instead of sheet

Buy coil or strip when you run a continuous process: roll forming of roofing and gutters, progressive stamping, coating or laminating, slitting into your own widths, or winding. Coil has less edge scrap and lower handling cost per tonne, and you can cut exactly the length each job needs.

Buy sheet or plate when you work in batches on a press brake, laser, router or CNC machine, when volumes are small, or when you have no decoiling equipment. Sheet costs a little more per tonne because of the extra levelling and cutting, but there is no capital outlay for coil handling.

Logistics

How each form is packed and shipped

FormTypical export packingHandling notes
CoilEye to wall or eye to sky on wooden skids, wrapped in film and moisture-resistant paper with desiccant, steel or PET strappingLift through the eye with a C-hook or padded mandrel; never with chains on the outer wraps
StripSeveral slit coils per pallet with separators, or reels in casesKeep coils banded until mounted to prevent telescoping
SheetPacks on wooden pallets or in cases, interleaved paper or PVC film, edge protectorsLift packs, not single sheets; avoid sliding sheets over each other
PlateBundles on heavy skids or in wooden cases, plate-to-plate separatorsUse vacuum lifters or slings with protection; heavy plates need dedicated handling

Whatever the form, water staining is the biggest transit risk for bare aluminium. Condensation trapped between tightly packed sheets or coil wraps causes white or grey stains that do not wash off. Keep material dry, let it reach workshop temperature before unpacking, and store it off the floor under cover.

Pricing

What drives price differences between the forms

  • Processing steps: each extra step after coil (slitting, levelling, cutting to length) adds conversion cost
  • Thickness: thin gauges need more rolling passes and cost more per kilogram than medium gauges
  • Heat treatment and stretching: T651 plate costs more than as-rolled H112 plate
  • Width and tolerance: very narrow strip, tight slit tolerance and burr-free edges add cost
  • Surface and packing: PVC film, interleaving and seaworthy cases add cost but prevent rejects

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) coil, strip, sheet and plate: FAQ

Is aluminium coil cheaper than sheet?

Usually slightly cheaper per tonne, because it skips levelling and cutting to length. The saving only helps if you have equipment to decoil and cut it.

What is the difference between aluminium coil and strip?

Strip is coil slit to narrow widths for a specific process. Both are continuous, wound products; the name mostly reflects width and end use.

What inner diameter should I order for aluminium coil?

The one that fits your decoiler mandrel. Common sizes are 150, 300, 405, 505 and 508 mm for coil and 76 – 400 mm for narrow strip.

Can plate be supplied in coil form?

No. Plate is too thick to wind and is always supplied flat. Heavy sheet up to about 6 mm can be coiled, depending on alloy, temper and width.

How do I calculate the length of material in a coil?

Divide coil weight by (thickness × width × 2.70 g/cm³) in consistent units. Our coil weight calculator does this automatically.
