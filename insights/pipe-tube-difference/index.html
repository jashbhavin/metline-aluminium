Aluminium Pipe vs Tube: What Is the Difference?

Aluminium pipe and aluminium tube can be made of the same alloy on the same press, yet they are specified, measured and used differently. Pipe is ordered by nominal pipe size (NPS) and schedule because it is built to carry fluids at a rated pressure and fit standard fittings. Tube is ordered by its actual outside diameter and wall thickness because it is used where exact dimensions matter: structures, machinery, heat exchangers and precision parts.

Definitions

Pipe and tube in one sentence each

Aluminium pipe is a round hollow product made to standard NPS outside diameters and schedule wall thicknesses (for example 2" Sch 40), intended mainly for conveying liquids and gases and for joining with standard flanges, threads or welded fittings. Its nominal size is a name, not a measurement: a 2" pipe has an outside diameter of 60.3 mm.

Aluminium tube is a hollow product, round or shaped (square, rectangular, oval, hexagonal), specified by its actual outside dimensions and wall thickness, or by OD and ID, with tolerances chosen for the application. A 50 × 3 mm tube really is 50 mm across.

The distinction is about specification, not quality. Both can be seamless or welded, extruded or drawn, in 6061, 6063, 3003, 5083 and other alloys.

Two popular myths are worth clearing up. Aluminium pipe is not weaker than tube: strength depends on alloy, temper and wall, not on the name. And aluminium pipe is not "hot rolled"; in practice both products are extruded, and tube may then be cold drawn to tighter sizes.

Sizing

How NPS and schedule work, with aluminium weights

For sizes up to NPS 12, the nominal size is smaller than the real outside diameter; from NPS 14 upwards, OD in inches equals the NPS. The schedule sets the wall: as the schedule number rises, the wall gets thicker and the inside diameter smaller, while the OD stays the same so the pipe still fits standard fittings. Up to NPS 10, Sch 40 and STD are identical, as are Sch 80 and XS up to NPS 8.

For pressure service, check whether the pipe must be seamless. Porthole-die extruded tube contains longitudinal weld seams formed inside the die; it is fine for structures and many low-pressure uses, but codes for pressure piping and gas systems usually call for seamless material to ASTM B241.

Practical advice

Should you order pipe or tube?

Order pipe when

The line carries fluid or gas, must mate with standard fittings or flanges, or a piping code specifies NPS and schedule (process lines, compressed air, irrigation, bus pipe).

Order tube when

Exact OD or ID matters: frames, handrails, cylinders, shafts, heat-exchanger tubes, telescopic parts or anything machined to fit.

Order drawn tube when

You need tight tolerances, a fine surface or thin walls: hydraulic and pneumatic tube, instrument tube, precision sleeves.

Ten points to put on every pipe or tube enquiry

  • Alloy and temper (e.g. 6061-T6, 6063-T52)
  • Seamless, porthole extruded, welded, or welded and drawn
  • Governing standard (ASTM B241, B221, EN 755)
  • Size: NPS and schedule, or OD × wall (or OD × ID)
  • Length, and whether random or exact cut
  • Surface: mill finish, anodised, polished
  • Straightness, ovality and concentricity requirements
  • Cleanliness for gas, vacuum or food service
  • Quantity and delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF, DAP)
  • Certification: EN 10204 3.1 mill certificate, third-party inspection

FAQ

Aluminium pipe vs tube: frequently asked questions

Is a 2 inch aluminum pipe 2 inches in diameter?

No. NPS 2 pipe has an outside diameter of 2.375 in (60.3 mm). The inside diameter depends on the schedule: about 52.5 mm for Sch 40 and 49.2 mm for Sch 80.

Is aluminium pipe stronger than tube?

Neither is inherently stronger. Strength depends on alloy, temper and wall thickness; a 6061-T6 tube and a 6061-T6 pipe of the same wall have the same material strength.

What is the most common alloy for aluminium pipe?

6061-T6 for structural and pressure pipe and 6063-T6 for general and architectural pipe, both commonly supplied to ASTM B241 or B429. 3003 and 5083 are used for specific process and marine duties.

Can aluminium tube be used for fluids?

Yes. Heat exchangers, hydraulic lines and fuel lines all use aluminium tube. It is specified by OD and wall and joined with compression fittings, brazing or welding rather than standard pipe fittings.

What does Sch 40 mean for aluminium pipe?

It is a wall-thickness designation from ASME B36.10M. For each NPS it gives a fixed wall, for example 3.91 mm on NPS 2 and 6.02 mm on NPS 4; the same schedule tables are used for aluminium and steel pipe.

Need aluminium pipe or tube?

Send the alloy, temper, NPS and schedule or OD and wall, length, quantity and standard. Metline will quote certified pipe or tube delivered worldwide.
