2024 vs 7075 Aluminium: 10 Differences That Decide the Choice

7075 is the stronger alloy: in T6/T651 its typical yield strength is about 500 MPa against roughly 345 MPa for 2024-T3. 2024 wins on fatigue resistance, fracture toughness and strength at moderately elevated temperatures, which is why it remains the classic fuselage and lower-wing alloy. Choose 7075 for stiff, highly stressed, compression-loaded or machined parts, and 2024 where cyclic tension and damage tolerance govern the design.

The chemistry explains the behaviour. In 2024, copper and magnesium form Al2CuMg (S-phase) precipitates that give high strength with good toughness, and these remain stable to higher temperatures. In 7075, zinc and magnesium form MgZn2 (η and η′) precipitates, which are more potent strengtheners but coarsen more quickly when heated. Chromium in 7075 controls grain structure and improves resistance to stress-corrosion cracking. The high copper content of 2024 is also why it corrodes more readily than lower-copper alloys.

A note on numbers you will see elsewhere: some comparison pages quote 483 MPa as the yield strength of 2024. That figure is the typical tensile strength of 2024-T3; the yield strength is about 345 MPa. Only the artificially aged T8 tempers bring 2024 yield strength close to 450 MPa, at the cost of much lower elongation.

Differences 3 to 5

Fatigue, toughness and temperature: where 2024 earns its place

If 7075 is stronger, why do aircraft still use 2024? Because static strength is rarely what limits an airframe. Fuselage skins and lower wing skins are loaded in tension every flight, so cracks can start at rivet holes and grow cycle by cycle. 2024-T3 grows fatigue cracks more slowly and tolerates a longer crack before fracture than 7075-T6. That damage tolerance gives longer inspection intervals and a safer structure.

Upper wing skins and stringers, by contrast, are loaded mainly in compression, where high yield strength prevents buckling and fatigue cracking is less of a concern. That is classic 7075 (and now 7055, 7150 and 7050) territory. Heavy forgings and fittings that are highly stressed but must also resist stress-corrosion are made in 7075-T73 or 7050-T7451.

Temperature is the third factor. The copper-based precipitates in 2024 are more stable, so 2024 holds its strength better during prolonged exposure at around 100 – 150 °C. 7075 overages faster at these temperatures. For parts near engines or in hot environments, 2024 (or 2618 and 2219 for higher temperatures) is the safer choice.

Difference 6

Corrosion and stress-corrosion cracking

Neither alloy is corrosion resistant in the way 5xxx or 6xxx alloys are. 2024 is susceptible to pitting and intergranular corrosion because of its high copper; 7075-T6 is prone to exfoliation and stress-corrosion cracking (SCC), particularly when stressed in the short-transverse direction of thick plate or forgings.

Alclad sheet

Both alloys are rolled with a thin cladding of pure aluminium (Alclad 2024, Alclad 7075, or 7072 cladding) that sacrificially protects the core in sheet products.

Over-aged tempers

7075-T73 and T76 trade some strength for high resistance to SCC and exfoliation. T73 is the normal choice for thick forgings and fittings.

Surface treatments

Anodising (sulphuric or chromic-replacement processes), conversion coating, primers and sealants protect bare parts in service.

Selection

Which should you choose?

  1. Identify the governing loadMostly static or compressive and stiffness-driven: lean to 7075. Repeated tension with fatigue-critical holes or joints: lean to 2024.
  2. Check temperatureSustained service above about 100 °C favours 2024; above about 150 °C consider 2618 or 2219.
  3. Check environmentMoist, salty or stressed thick sections: specify 7075-T73 / T7351 or Alclad sheet, and protect with anodising or coatings.
  4. Consider manufacturingHeavy machining from plate or bar: 7075-T651 machines exceptionally well. Riveted sheet assemblies: Alclad 2024-T3 is the traditional choice.
  5. Confirm the specificationAerospace parts must follow the drawing specification exactly; do not substitute one alloy for the other without engineering approval.

FAQ

2024 vs 7075 aluminium (aluminum): frequently asked questions

Which is stronger, 2024 or 7075?

7075. In T6/T651, its typical yield strength is about 500 MPa and tensile about 570 MPa, compared with about 345 MPa yield and 483 MPa tensile for 2024-T3.

Why is 2024 used for aircraft fuselages if 7075 is stronger?

Fuselage skins are loaded in repeated tension. 2024-T3 has better fatigue crack-growth resistance and fracture toughness, so cracks grow more slowly and are found before they become critical.

Can 2024 or 7075 be welded?

Neither is suitable for conventional arc welding of structures because of hot cracking and loss of strength. Friction-stir welding works with qualified procedures; otherwise use rivets or fasteners.

Which machines better, 2024 or 7075?

Both machine well. 7075-T651 is usually preferred for complex CNC parts because it gives an excellent surface finish and holds tight tolerances.

Is 7075 more corrosion resistant than 2024?

Generally a little better in general corrosion, but 7075-T6 is susceptible to stress-corrosion cracking. Use 7075-T73 / T7351 or protective coatings where corrosion and sustained stress combine.

Can I substitute 7075 for 2024 on a drawing?

Not without engineering approval. The alloys differ in fatigue, toughness and temperature behaviour, so a stronger alloy is not automatically a safer one.
