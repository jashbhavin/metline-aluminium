6061 vs 7075 Aluminium: Choosing for Plate, Forgings and Profiles

7075 is roughly 80 % stronger than 6061 in their peak tempers, but it cannot be fusion welded, corrodes more easily and costs considerably more. 6061 is the default for welded and general structures; 7075 earns its place in bolted or machined parts where weight and strength dominate. This comparison looks at the choice across product forms, with tempers, standards and the mistakes we see most often in enquiries.

Two different jobs

Why 6061 and 7075 are compared so often

6061 and 7075 sit at opposite ends of the commonly stocked heat-treatable range. 6061 (Al-Mg-Si) is the general-purpose structural alloy: moderate strength, easy to weld, form, extrude and anodise. 7075 (Al-Zn-Mg-Cu) was developed for aircraft structures and is one of the strongest aluminium alloys in general supply.

Engineers usually arrive at this comparison from one of two directions. Either a 6061 part is too heavy or too weak and someone suggests "just make it in 7075", or a 7075 part is being questioned on cost and someone asks whether 6061 would do. Both questions have the same answer: it depends on how the part is joined, what environment it sees, which product form it comes from and whether strength or stiffness is the real limit.

For a points-based summary aimed at CNC bar buyers, see our companion article 6061 vs 7075 for CNC bar. This page goes further into tempers, product forms, stress-corrosion behaviour and structural design.

In 6061 the strengthening phase is Mg2Si. In 7075 it is a family of zinc-magnesium (MgZn2-type) precipitates, present in a much larger volume fraction, with copper adding further strength. More precipitate means more strength, but the same chemistry makes 7075 hot-short during welding and more electrochemically active in corrosion.

Two numbers deserve attention. First, stiffness is almost identical. A 7075 part bends by nearly the same amount as a 6061 part of the same shape under the same load, so if the design is deflection-limited, switching alloy gains very little. Second, specific strength (yield divided by density) is about 179 kN·m/kg for 7075-T6 against 102 for 6061-T6; that is where the weight savings come from, but only in strength-limited parts.

Tempers

The temper choice that matters most with 7075

6061 buyers rarely think beyond T6 and T651. 7075 buyers must, because peak-aged 7075-T6 is susceptible to stress-corrosion cracking (SCC) when loaded in the short-transverse direction, and to exfoliation corrosion in aggressive environments.

7075-T6 / T651

Peak strength. Fine for small machined parts, tooling, moulds and sports goods. Avoid where sustained tensile stress acts through the thickness of thick plate or forgings in humid or salty service.

7075-T73 / T7351

Over-aged for maximum SCC resistance. About 10 – 15 % lower strength than T6, but far more reliable in thick sections and forgings. T7351 is the stretched plate version.

7075-T76 / T7651

Intermediate over-ageing, mainly for exfoliation resistance in sheet and plate, with strength between T6 and T73.

6061-T6 / T651

Peak-aged and stretched; no comparable SCC concern in normal service, which is one of the reasons it is the default general alloy.

6061-T4 / T451

Naturally aged. Used where forming follows, with ageing to T6 afterwards.

6061-T6511 / 7075-T6511

Stretched and straightened extrusions. The same suffix logic applies to both alloys.

When 7075 thick sections need both high strength and SCC resistance, many aircraft designs move to 7050-T7451, which keeps strength better in thick plate. That comparison has its own article.

Extrusion is where the gap is widest. 6061 extrudes readily into hollow, multi-void profiles and thin walls. 7075 needs much higher pressure and slower speeds and is generally limited to solid or simple shapes, so a complex 6061 profile often cannot be "upgraded" to 7075 without redesign.

Joining

Welding: the deciding factor in many designs

6061 is routinely MIG and TIG welded with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. Its heat-affected zone loses strength, falling towards T4 level, but the joint is sound and predictable, and codes such as AWS D1.2 and EN 1999 give design values for it.

7075 is generally regarded as unweldable by conventional fusion welding. Its high zinc and copper content gives a wide solidification range, so welds and the partially melted zone crack, and what does not crack has poor strength and corrosion behaviour. 7075 structures are bolted, riveted or adhesively bonded instead. Friction stir welding can join 7075 successfully, but it is a specialist process, not a workshop repair option.

Design consequence

  • If the drawing shows weld symbols, 7075 is almost certainly the wrong alloy
  • If a 6061 welded frame is too weak, first look at section shape, joint location or a stronger 6xxx alloy such as 6082
  • For bolted joints in 7075, consider bearing strength, fastener material (galvanic pairing) and hole finish for fatigue
Durability

Corrosion, anodising and fatigue

6061 has good general corrosion resistance and needs no special temper to avoid SCC. 7075 is noticeably more susceptible to pitting and, in T6, to SCC and exfoliation. Aircraft use clad 7075 sheet (a thin pure-aluminium skin) or protective anodising and primers. For outdoor and marine use, 7075 should always be protected, and the T73 or T76 tempers should be considered.

Both anodise well. 6061 gives a clear, uniform film and takes dyes consistently; 7075 anodises to a slightly darker or yellowish tone and is commonly hard anodised for wear surfaces. For fatigue, 7075 has the higher endurance strength in smooth specimens, but it is more notch-sensitive, so its advantage shrinks around holes, threads and fillets unless surfaces are well finished.

Shop floor and budget

Machinability, heat and cost

Both alloys machine very well in peak tempers. 7075 is harder, forms small chips and gives a slightly finer surface; 6061 is a little more forgiving on tools. Thick plate in either alloy should be bought stress-relieved (T651 or T7351) to keep parts flat.

Service temperature limits are similar in principle: both over-age above about 120 – 150 °C over long periods. 7075 also has a lower solidus (about 477 °C), which matters for brazing and for heat input in any process.

On cost, 7075 is typically priced well above 6061 per kilogram, and the gap widens for aerospace-certified plate and forgings. It is also about 4 % denser, so the same part weighs more in 7075 unless the section is reduced. Use 7075 where its strength lets you take material away; otherwise you pay more for a heavier part.

Mistakes we see in enquiries

  • Ordering 7075-T6 for a welded assembly
  • Specifying 7075-T6 for thick forgings in outdoor service without considering T73
  • Upgrading to 7075 to reduce deflection, which it barely does
  • Mixing 6061 and 7075 parts without accounting for their slightly different anodised colour

FAQ

6061 vs 7075 aluminum: frequently asked questions

How much stronger is 7075 than 6061?

In peak tempers, 7075-T6 has a typical yield strength of about 503 MPa against 276 MPa for 6061-T6, roughly 80 % higher. In the SCC-resistant T73 temper the margin falls to around 55 – 60 %.

Is 7075 stiffer than 6061?

Only slightly. The elastic modulus is about 71.7 GPa for 7075 and 68.9 GPa for 6061, a 4 % difference. A part that deflects too much in 6061 will deflect almost as much in 7075.

Can 7075 aluminium be welded?

Not by conventional MIG or TIG welding; the welds crack and lose strength. Join 7075 with fasteners or adhesives, or use friction stir welding where available.

Which temper of 7075 should I order for forgings?

For forgings and thick sections under sustained stress, T73 (or T7352 for stress-relieved hand forgings) gives far better stress-corrosion resistance than T6, at some cost in strength.

Is 7075 heavier than 6061?

Yes, slightly: 2.81 g/cm³ against 2.70 g/cm³. 7075 saves weight only when its higher strength allows a thinner or smaller section.

Which is better for anodising?

Both anodise well. 6061 gives a clearer, more uniform colour; 7075 tends towards a slightly darker or yellowish tone and is frequently hard anodised.
