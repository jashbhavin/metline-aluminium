Welding 6061 Aluminium: Process, Filler and Strength Loss

6061 welds well by TIG, MIG and friction stir welding, but only with a filler metal (4043 or 5356 in most cases) and only if you design for the softened heat-affected zone. In the T6 temper, a fusion weld cuts local tensile strength roughly in half, so the real engineering question is not "can it be welded?" but "where do the welds go and what strength can they be credited with?"

The metallurgy

Three things that make 6061 different from steel

6061 gets its T6 strength from fine Mg2Si precipitates formed during artificial ageing. Welding heat undoes that work next to the weld, and the alloy's chemistry makes the weld metal itself crack-prone unless filler is added.

Hot cracking

At around 1 % Mg2Si the weld metal sits near peak solidification-crack sensitivity. Autogenous (no-filler) welds and low-dilution joints often crack. Filler shifts the weld chemistry out of the danger zone.

HAZ softening

Precipitates dissolve or coarsen in the heat-affected zone. Without re-heat-treatment, the zone settles at a strength between T4 and annealed, well below T6.

Oxide and hydrogen

The tough aluminium oxide film melts at about 2,050 °C, far above the metal, and moisture or oil releases hydrogen that causes porosity. Cleaning and AC or pulsed arc control are essential.

None of these stops 6061 from being one of the most-welded aluminium alloys in the world. Bicycle frames, truck bodies, boat fittings, structural extrusions and machine frames are routinely welded in 6061; the successful ones are designed around the points above.

The as-welded yield strength falls even more steeply than tensile strength, to well under half of the T6 base value. Use the welded yield figure from the code your project follows rather than an estimate.

How wide is the soft zone? The US Aluminum Design Manual treats metal within 25 mm (1 in) of the weld centreline as weld-affected. Eurocode 9 (EN 1999-1-1) defines a HAZ width that grows with thickness and heat input, from about 20 mm for thin MIG welds to 40 mm for thick sections, with larger values for TIG, and applies reduction factors to the design strength inside it. Either way, a weld across the most highly stressed section of a member is the classic design error. Move welds to low-stress locations, use thicker material locally, or design the member for the reduced strength.

Design rules of thumb

  • Place welds parallel to the main stress, not across it, wherever possible
  • Keep welds away from bending maxima and fatigue-critical details
  • Use friction stir welding on long panel seams when the strength loss must be minimised
  • For fillet-welded brackets, size the weld on the filler's shear strength (5356 is much stronger than 4043)

Shielding gas is normally pure argon (99.99 % or better). Argon-helium mixtures add heat and penetration on thick sections and are common for DC TIG and heavy MIG work.

Filler selection

Which filler wire for 6061?

There is no 6061 filler: a weld deposit of 6061 composition would crack. The choice is between silicon-based and magnesium-based fillers.

Filler (AWS A5.10)Choose it whenWatch out for
ER4043 (AlSi5)Crack resistance matters most: thin sections, high restraint, square butts, parts that will be paintedLower fillet shear strength; grey colour after anodising
ER5356 (AlMg5)Fillet-weld strength, ductility and anodised appearance matterMore crack-sensitive on 6061 at high dilution; avoid sustained service above about 65 °C

Shop practice

Preparation and technique that prevent defects

  1. DegreaseRemove oil and marking ink with a solvent such as acetone before any brushing.
  2. Remove oxideBrush with a clean stainless-steel brush used only for aluminium, or scrape; weld within a few hours.
  3. Fit upUse bevels and root gaps that let filler enter the joint; avoid square butts in thick material if cracking is a risk.
  4. Control heatPreheat is not normally needed. Keep preheat and interpass temperature low (codes commonly cap it around 120 °C for 6xxx alloys) to limit HAZ softening.
  5. Weld fastUse enough current to weld quickly; slow, cold welding lengthens the time the HAZ spends hot and causes lack of fusion.
  6. Fill cratersUse crater-fill or run-off tabs; crater cracks are a frequent cause of rejection.

Qualify procedures and welders to AWS D1.2 or ISO 15614-2 and ISO 9606-2, and specify acceptance levels for porosity and other imperfections with ISO 10042 where European rules apply.

Brazing

Can 6061 be brazed?

Only with care. The solidus of 6061 is about 582 °C, while common aluminium-silicon braze alloys such as 4047 melt at roughly 577 – 582 °C. With virtually no temperature margin, furnace brazing 6061 risks melting or eroding the parent metal. That is why brazed heat exchangers use cores with higher solidus temperatures, such as 3003 or 6951, clad with 4343 or 4045 braze alloy.

Where a 6061 part must be brazed, lower-melting fillers (zinc-aluminium or special aluminium-silicon-copper alloys) and close temperature control are used, and the parts are re-aged afterwards if strength matters. For multi-joint thermal assemblies, specify a brazing-sheet system from the start rather than adapting 6061.

Where welded 6061 is used

Typical welded 6061 applications

  • Transport: trailer and truck-body frames, EV battery trays (often friction stir welded), bus structures
  • Cycling and sport: TIG-welded bicycle frames, usually heat-treated after welding
  • Marine: deck fittings, towers, rails and brackets above the waterline
  • Structures: walkways, platforms, ladders and handrails designed to welded strengths
  • Machinery: welded frames and fixtures that are machined after welding
  • Pressure and vacuum: chambers and vessels built to code with qualified procedures

FAQ

Welding 6061 aluminum: frequently asked questions

Can you weld 6061-T6 without filler?

Not reliably. Autogenous welds in 6061 are very prone to solidification cracking. Use 4043, 5356 or another suitable filler so the weld chemistry moves out of the crack-sensitive range.

How strong is a welded 6061-T6 joint?

Design codes credit an as-welded groove weld in 6061-T6 with a minimum tensile strength of 165 MPa (24 ksi), compared with about 310 MPa typical for the unwelded T6 base metal.

Is 4043 or 5356 better for 6061?

4043 is more crack-resistant and easier to weld; 5356 gives stronger fillet welds and a better colour match after anodising. Both are accepted for 6061.

Can welded 6061 be heat-treated back to T6?

Yes, by full solution treatment, quenching and ageing. The base metal regains close to T6 properties, but distortion must be managed, and the weld metal's response depends on the filler (4643 is designed for this).

Should I preheat 6061 before welding?

Usually not. Light preheat can help on thick, cold sections, but keep it low (around 120 °C maximum is a common limit) because extra heat widens the softened zone.

Why is my 6061 weld porous?

Almost always hydrogen from moisture, oil or hydrated oxide on the wire or base metal, or poor gas shielding. Clean and dry the parts and wire, check gas flow and protect the arc from drafts.
