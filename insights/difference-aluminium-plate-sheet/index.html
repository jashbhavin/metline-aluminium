Aluminium Plate vs Sheet: What Is the Difference?

The difference is thickness. Under ASTM B209, aluminium sheet runs from 0.006 in to 0.249 in (about 0.15 – 6.3 mm) and plate starts at 0.250 in (6.35 mm); European standards draw the line at 6 mm. That one boundary brings different rolling routes, tempers, tolerances and uses, which is what buyers really need to understand.

Standards

How standards define sheet and plate

"Sheet" and "plate" are formal product definitions, not just trade words. They decide which tolerance table, mechanical property table and temper options apply to your order.

ProductASTM B209 / ANSI H35.2 (US)EN 485 / EN 12258-1 (Europe)Typical use of the term in trade
FoilUnder 0.006 in (0.15 mm)Up to 0.20 mmPackaging, fin stock, insulation
Sheet0.006 – 0.249 in (0.15 – 6.32 mm)Over 0.20 mm up to 6 mmThin sheet 0.2 – 2 mm; "heavy sheet" 2 – 6 mm
Plate0.250 in (6.35 mm) and overOver 6 mmMedium plate 6 – 25 mm; thick plate 25 – 200 mm and above

The boundaries do not quite agree, so a 6.35 mm (¼ in) product is plate in the US and still plate in Europe, while a 6.0 mm product is sheet under ASTM and the top of the sheet range under EN. When a thickness sits near the line, state the standard on your order so the correct tolerance and property tables are applied.

Production route

How plate and sheet are produced

Both start as a direct-chill (DC) cast ingot, often 400 – 600 mm thick, which is scalped and homogenised before hot rolling on a reversing mill.

Plate usually leaves the hot mill at its final thickness. Heat-treatable plate such as 6061, 7075 or 2024 is then solution heat-treated, quenched, stretched by about 1.5 – 3 % to relieve quench stresses (the "51" in T651) and aged. Non-heat-treatable plate such as 5083 is supplied as-rolled (H112) or in stabilised tempers (H116, H321).

Sheet continues further: the hot-rolled band is coiled and cold rolled down to gauge, with intermediate anneals as needed, before being levelled and cut to length or supplied as coil. Thin sheet for common grades can also come from twin-roll continuously cast (CC) stock, which is economical for foil and general-purpose sheet but less suited to demanding anodising or deep-drawing uses than DC material.

Applications

Grades and applications of each form

Typical sheet grades: 1050, 1060, 1100, 3003, 3004, 3105, 5005, 5052, 5754, 6061 and 6082. Sheet is formed, bent, deep drawn, spun or stamped, so formability and surface quality matter most. Uses include building cladding and roofing, signage, vehicle body panels, appliance housings, heat exchangers, cookware and packaging stock.

Typical plate grades: 5083, 5086, 6061, 6082, 7075, 7050 and 2024. Plate is machined, welded or used as a load-carrying structure, so strength, flatness and through-thickness properties matter most. Uses include marine hulls and decks, moulds and tooling, machine bases, aircraft structures, tanks and pressure vessels.

Quality

Tolerances and flatness differ too

Sheet tolerances are tight in absolute terms, often a few hundredths of a millimetre on thickness, because cold rolling gives close gauge control. Plate tolerances are wider in absolute terms but similar as a percentage of thickness. Flatness is the bigger issue for plate: a thick plate that is not stress-relieved can bow when machined, which is why T651 or precision-milled plate is specified for machined parts.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) plate vs sheet: FAQ

At what thickness does aluminium sheet become plate?

Under ASTM B209, at 0.250 in (6.35 mm). Under European EN standards, products over 6 mm are plate. Below about 0.15 – 0.2 mm the product is foil.

Is 1/4 inch aluminum sheet or plate?

Plate. ASTM defines plate as 0.250 in and thicker, so 1/4 in (6.35 mm) is the thinnest plate.

Why does plate cost more per kilogram than some sheet?

Often it does not; thin sheet carries more rolling cost per kilogram. Plate costs more when it is heat-treated and stretched (T651), ultrasonically tested, or made in high-strength grades such as 7075.

Can I bend aluminium plate?

Thinner plate in softer grades such as 5083-H111 can be bent with generous radii on heavy press brakes. Heat-treated plate such as 6061-T651 or 7075-T651 has limited bendability and is normally machined instead.

Which is better for machining, sheet or plate?

Plate in a stress-relieved temper such as T651, or cast tooling plate, because it stays flat as material is removed.
