Top 10 Aluminium Companies in the World and What Each Is Known For

The global aluminium industry is dominated by a small number of integrated groups that mine bauxite, refine alumina and smelt metal, alongside specialist rolling and extrusion companies that turn that metal into sheet, plate and profiles. This overview profiles ten internationally important companies, explains what each is known for, and shows buyers how producers, converters and suppliers fit together.

Context

How the global aluminium industry is structured

Rankings of "top aluminium companies" vary widely because they measure different things: primary metal tonnage, revenue, rolled-product shipments or market value. Our list is not a strict tonnage league table. It profiles ten companies that matter to international buyers because of their scale, their technology or their influence on standards, sustainability and supply.

According to International Aluminium Institute statistics, world primary aluminium output was about 74 million tonnes in 2025, and China produced roughly 60 % of it. Much of that output is consumed within the Chinese domestic market, so volume rankings and international relevance are not the same thing. The companies below are those most visible in cross-border trade, standards development and downstream products.

Aluminium production has four main stages. Bauxite ore is mined; the Bayer process refines it into alumina (about two tonnes of alumina make one tonne of metal); the Hall-Héroult electrolytic process smelts alumina into liquid aluminium; and casthouses, rolling mills, extrusion plants and forges turn that metal into the products engineers buy. Recycling adds a fifth loop, using around 5 % of the energy of primary production.

Profiles

Profiles: Rio Tinto, Alcoa, Rusal, EGA and Hydro

1. Rio Tinto (UK / Australia)

A diversified miner with one of the largest aluminium businesses outside China. Its smelters are concentrated in Canada and rely heavily on hydropower, giving low-carbon primary metal. Rio Tinto is a partner with Alcoa in ELYSIS, a joint venture developing inert-anode smelting that emits oxygen instead of CO2. Products include slab, billet and foundry alloys.

2. Alcoa (United States)

The company whose predecessor commercialised the Hall-Héroult process in the late nineteenth century. Today Alcoa focuses on bauxite, alumina and primary aluminium, with refineries and smelters in the Americas, Australia and Europe. Its alumina business is among the largest in the world, and it markets low-carbon metal under its Sustana brand.

3. Rusal (Russia)

One of the largest primary producers outside China, with most smelting capacity in Siberia powered by hydroelectricity. Rusal produces primary aluminium, alloys, alumina, foil and powders. International buyers should check current sanctions and trade restrictions in their jurisdiction before sourcing Russian metal.

4. Emirates Global Aluminium, EGA (UAE)

A large producer operating smelters at Jebel Ali and Al Taweelah, plus bauxite mining in Guinea. EGA developed its own reduction-cell technology, licenses it internationally, and launched CelestiAL, aluminium smelted using solar power. It also supplies high-purity metal, billet and foundry alloys.

5. Norsk Hydro (Norway)

Integrated from bauxite in Brazil to extrusions across Europe and North America. Hydro is known for low-carbon products: REDUXA primary metal at 4.0 kg CO2e per kg of aluminium or less, and CIRCAL extrusion billet with a high share of post-consumer scrap. Its extrusion division is one of the largest profile producers in the world.

Profiles

Profiles: Hindalco and Novelis, Vedanta, Century, UACJ and Constellium

6. Hindalco Industries and Novelis (India / global)

Hindalco, part of the Aditya Birla Group, is an integrated Indian producer of alumina, primary aluminium, extrusions, flat-rolled products and foil. Its subsidiary Novelis, acquired in 2007, is the world's largest producer of flat-rolled aluminium and the largest recycler of used beverage cans, supplying can sheet and automotive sheet worldwide.

7. Vedanta Aluminium (India)

The largest primary aluminium producer in India, with smelters at Jharsuguda (Odisha) and through BALCO in Chhattisgarh. Products include ingot, billet, wire rod and rolled products for Indian and export markets, with significant investment in captive alumina refining.

8. Century Aluminum (United States / Iceland)

A primary-only producer with smelters in the United States and the Grundartangi smelter in Iceland, which runs on renewable geothermal and hydropower. It supplies standard ingot and value-added primary products such as billet and foundry alloys.

9. UACJ Corporation (Japan)

Formed in 2013 by the merger of Furukawa-Sky Aluminum and Sumitomo Light Metal Industries. UACJ is one of the largest aluminium rolling companies in the world, supplying can stock, automotive and heat-exchanger materials, foil, extrusions and precision products, with major operations in Japan, Thailand and the United States.

10. Constellium (France / global)

A specialist in high-value rolled and extruded products: aerospace plate and sheet, including aluminium-lithium alloys, automotive body sheet and crash-management structures, and packaging. Constellium works closely with aircraft and car makers on alloy development.

Trends

Five trends shaping the big producers

Low-carbon metal

Smelting emissions depend mainly on the power source. Hydro, geothermal and solar-powered smelters produce metal with a fraction of the footprint of coal-powered output, and buyers increasingly ask for verified product carbon footprints.

Inert anodes

Technologies such as ELYSIS aim to remove the direct CO2 emitted when carbon anodes are consumed in the cell.

Recycling

Post-consumer scrap is now a strategic raw material. Alloy-sorting technology lets more scrap return into demanding products like automotive sheet.

Electrification

EV battery enclosures, busbars, cables and lightweight bodies are a major growth market for rolled and extruded products.

Trade policy

Tariffs, sanctions and carbon border adjustments (such as the EU CBAM) increasingly shape where metal flows and what documentation buyers need.

Buyer advice

What these companies mean for an aluminium buyer

Most engineers and fabricators do not buy directly from a smelter. Primary producers sell ingot, slab and billet in large lots to converters; converters sell coil, plate, extrusions and forgings in mill quantities with long lead times; and suppliers and service centres break bulk, cut to size, certify and ship. Our view is that the supplier's job is to make that chain invisible: you specify grade, temper, size, standard and destination, and receive certified material traceable to the producing mill.

  1. Specify by standard, not brandOrder 6061-T651 to ASTM B209 or EN AW-5083 H111 to EN 485; any qualified mill can then supply.
  2. Ask for traceabilityAn EN 10204 3.1 certificate should identify the producing mill, cast or lot number and test results.
  3. State carbon or origin requirementsIf you need low-carbon metal, recycled content or a particular origin, say so at the enquiry stage.
  4. Check trade complianceConfirm sanctions, tariffs and customs documentation for your destination before ordering.

FAQ

Global aluminium companies: frequently asked questions

Which country produces the most aluminium?

China, by a wide margin: roughly 60 % of world primary output in 2025 according to International Aluminium Institute data. India, Russia, Canada and the UAE are the next largest producers.

What is the difference between a primary producer and an aluminium supplier?

A primary producer smelts metal from alumina and casts ingot, slab or billet. Converters roll, extrude or forge it into products. A supplier such as Metline sources finished products from qualified mills, processes them to size and ships them with certification.

What is low-carbon aluminium?

Primary aluminium smelted with renewable electricity, or metal with high recycled content, giving a much lower CO2 footprint per kilogram. Producers market it under brands and verify it with product carbon footprints.

Is aluminium from different producers the same?

Material made to the same alloy, temper and standard must meet the same chemical and mechanical limits, so it is interchangeable for design purposes. Differences are in surface quality, tolerances within the standard, carbon footprint and service.

Which aluminium company makes the most rolled products?

Novelis, a Hindalco subsidiary, is widely reported as the largest producer of flat-rolled aluminium and the largest recycler of used beverage cans.

Can Metline supply aluminium from a specific producer?

We supply to standard and can state origin and mill on the certificate. If your project requires a particular origin or low-carbon metal, include it in your enquiry and we will confirm availability.
