13 Properties of Aluminium and the Applications They Make Possible

Aluminium is a third of the weight of steel or copper, can be alloyed and heat-treated to steel-like strength, conducts heat and electricity well, resists corrosion through its own oxide film and stays tough at cryogenic temperatures. Each of these properties maps to specific industries, and the useful question for an engineer is which property is really driving the choice, because that decides the alloy.

A recurring design point: aluminium is about one-third the stiffness of steel. Equal-stiffness aluminium parts need deeper sections, which is why extrusions are so useful: material can be placed exactly where bending stiffness is needed.

Properties 1 – 3

Low weight, high specific strength and easy processing

1. Low density

At 2.70 g/cm³ aluminium weighs roughly 34 % as much as steel and 30 % as much as copper. Lower mass cuts fuel or energy use in anything that moves, reduces handling and freight cost and lightens building loads. Used in aircraft, rail cars, trucks, ships, bridges decks and containers.

2. Can be strengthened

Pure aluminium is soft (tensile around 90 MPa annealed). Alloying with Cu, Mg, Si, Zn, Mn or Li, then cold working or heat treating, raises strength to over 500 MPa in 7075-T6. Strength-to-weight then rivals many alloy steels. Used in airframes, movable bridges, pressure vessels and high-performance sports goods.

3. Easy to form and machine

Aluminium can be cast by every common method, rolled to foil under 0.01 mm, drawn into fine wire, extruded into complex profiles and machined at very high spindle speeds. That makes it economical from prototype to mass production: optical frames, housings, structural sections and containers.

Properties 4 – 6

Appearance, corrosion and chemical resistance

4. Attractive, finishable surface

The natural silver-white surface accepts anodising, dyeing, powder coating, PVDF painting, polishing and brushing. Anodic films become part of the metal and do not peel. Used in façades, consumer electronics, furniture and decorative trim.

5. Corrosion resistance

A thin, self-healing oxide film forms instantly in air and protects the metal. 5xxx alloys such as 5083 and 5086 resist seawater well enough for hulls; 6xxx alloys serve decades outdoors. Copper-rich 2xxx and some 7xxx alloys need cladding or coatings.

6. Chemical resistance

The oxide film is stable roughly between pH 4.5 and 8.5, so aluminium handles many organic chemicals, fuels, nitric acid at certain concentrations and food products. Strong alkalis and hydrochloric acid attack it. Used for chemical tanks, food processing, brewing vessels and cryogenic storage.

Corrosion resistance depends on alloy and environment. In contact with copper, carbon steel or stainless steel in a wet electrolyte, aluminium becomes the anode and corrodes galvanically; isolate dissimilar metals with gaskets, coatings or sealants. Our corrosion selection guide covers this in detail.

Properties 7 – 8

Thermal and electrical conductivity, and reflectivity

7. Conducts heat and electricity

Electrical-conductor aluminium (1350) has about 62 % of copper's conductivity at about 30 % of the weight, so per kilogram it carries roughly twice the current. That is why overhead transmission lines, busbars and many cables are aluminium. Its thermal conductivity makes it the default for heat sinks, radiators, cookware and heat-exchanger fins.

8. Reflects light and heat

Polished or high-purity aluminium reflects more than 90 % of visible light and over 95 % of long-wave infrared. It is used for lighting reflectors, solar concentrators, insulating foil laminates, roofing that keeps buildings cooler and emergency blankets.

Alloying lowers conductivity, so match grade to purpose: 1350 and 6101 for conductors, 1050 and 6063 for heat-dissipating profiles, and 1085 or 1090 high-purity sheet for bright reflectors.

Properties 9 – 13

Non-magnetic, non-toxic, acoustic, cryogenic and nuclear properties

9. Non-magnetic and low-sparking

Aluminium does not respond to magnetic fields, so it is used for instrument housings, MRI room components, compass binnacles and electronic shielding enclosures. Aluminium striking aluminium does not throw hot sparks, but aluminium smeared onto rusty steel can produce thermite-type sparks; hazardous-area rules treat that combination with care.

10. Non-toxic

Aluminium is widely used in food and pharmaceutical contact: foil, cans, closures and blister packs. Foil gives a complete barrier to light, oxygen, moisture and bacteria.

11. Sound absorption

Solid aluminium is not an absorber by itself, but perforated aluminium panels backed with absorbent and aluminium foam panels are effective acoustic treatments for ceilings, noise barriers and machine enclosures.

12. Toughness at low temperature

Aluminium has no ductile-to-brittle transition. Strength and elongation of alloys such as 5083 actually increase as temperature falls, which is why it is used for LNG tanks, liquid oxygen and hydrogen vessels and cryogenic equipment.

13. Nuclear applications

Aluminium has a low thermal-neutron absorption cross-section (about 0.23 barn) and its activation products decay quickly. It has been used for research-reactor fuel cladding and structures, and boron-aluminium composites serve as neutron absorbers in spent-fuel storage.

Bonus: recyclability

Aluminium can be remelted repeatedly without losing its properties, using roughly 5 % of the energy needed to make primary metal. Most of the aluminium ever produced is still in use.

FAQ

Properties of aluminium (aluminum): frequently asked questions

How strong is aluminium compared with steel?

Common structural alloys such as 6061-T6 reach about 310 MPa tensile, similar to mild steel, at one-third of the weight. High-strength 7075-T6 exceeds 550 MPa. Aluminium is, however, about one-third as stiff, so sections must be deeper for the same deflection.

Is aluminium magnetic?

No. Aluminium is paramagnetic, so it is not attracted to a magnet in any practical sense. Moving magnets do induce eddy currents in it, which is the principle behind eddy-current brakes and scrap separators.

Why does aluminium not rust?

Rust is iron oxide. Aluminium forms its own thin aluminium-oxide film, which seals the surface and re-forms if scratched. It can still corrode by pitting in chlorides or galvanically next to other metals, so alloy choice and design detail matter.

What is the melting point of aluminium?

Pure aluminium melts at 660 °C. Alloys melt over a range; for example 6061 between about 582 and 652 °C. This low melting point is one reason aluminium is cheap to cast and recycle.

Is aluminium a good conductor of electricity?

Yes. EC-grade 1350 has about 62 % of the conductivity of copper but weighs about 30 % as much, so per unit mass it conducts about twice as well. That makes it standard for overhead lines and many busbars.

Can aluminium be used at cryogenic temperatures?

Yes. Unlike carbon steel, aluminium does not become brittle. Alloys such as 5083 gain strength and keep their toughness at liquefied-gas temperatures, so they are used for LNG tanks and cryogenic vessels.
