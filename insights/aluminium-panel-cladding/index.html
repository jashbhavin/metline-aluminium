Aluminium Panel Cladding: Choosing the Panel and the System

Aluminium panel cladding is an outer building skin made of aluminium panels fixed to a support frame, usually as a ventilated rainscreen. The two decisions that matter most are the panel type (solid sheet, composite, honeycomb, perforated or insulated) and the fixing system (rainscreen cassettes, interlocking planks, standing seam or large-format panels). This article explains both, with the fire and coating questions every specifier now has to answer.

Context

Why aluminium for cladding

Aluminium weighs about a third as much as steel for the same volume, does not rust, folds into crisp edges and takes durable PVDF, polyester-powder and anodised finishes in almost any colour. A 3 mm solid aluminium panel weighs about 8.1 kg/m², light enough for long spans on slender sub-frames.

It is also non-combustible as a metal, which matters more than ever since tighter facade fire rules came into force in many countries. The catch is that some aluminium-faced products contain combustible cores, so "aluminium cladding" is not automatically a safe or compliant facade. The panel build-up and the complete wall system determine the fire performance.

Metline supplies the aluminium for cladding: coil-coated and anodised sheet and coil, composite and honeycomb panels, perforated sheet and extruded sub-frame profiles, cut to size on request. Folding into cassettes, sub-frame design and installation are carried out by the facade fabricator and installer.

Our view: on tall or high-risk buildings, start from solid aluminium or honeycomb panels with mineral-free, all-metal cores and check national rules early. ACP is a good material where its core classification is accepted, but it should never be specified simply as "ACP" without stating the core and its fire class.

Fixing systems

How cladding panels are fixed

Ventilated rainscreen

Panels form an outer skin with an air cavity behind them. Water that passes the joints drains away and the cavity ventilates, keeping insulation dry. The most common system for aluminium cladding.

Hook-on cassettes

Folded trays with hooks or pins hang on vertical rails, giving concealed fixings, quick installation and individual panel removal.

Rivet or screw fixed

Flat panels fixed through the face with colour-matched rivets. Simple and economical, with visible fixings.

Interlocking / shiplap planks

Long planks with tongue-and-groove edges create linear facades with hidden fixings; tight edge tolerances are essential.

Standing seam

Long, narrow trays joined by seamed upstands; used on curved roofs and walls for a continuous, weathertight look.

Back-bolted large format

Honeycomb or composite panels fixed by undercut anchors or bonded studs, for very large, flat panels.

Engineering

Design considerations for aluminium cladding

Key design checks

Wind load
Panel thickness, folds, stiffeners and fixing spacing are set by the design wind pressure (for example EN 1991-1-4 or ASCE 7), with higher suction at corners and edges
Thermal movement
Aluminium expands about 0.024 mm per metre per °C; a 3 m panel moves around 3.5 mm over a 50 °C range, so fixings need slots and joints need space
Flatness
Large, thin solid panels can show "oil canning"; add stiffeners, increase thickness or use composite or honeycomb panels
Fire
Classify the panel and the system to EN 13501-1 or test to NFPA 285 or BS 8414, as local regulations require
Galvanic contact
Isolate aluminium from steel and copper with stainless fasteners and separation tapes
Drainage and ventilation
Provide cavity openings at the base and top, and fire barriers at floors where required

Colour consistency across a large facade comes from ordering the whole quantity, or clearly defined batches, from one coating run and matching folding direction, because the grain of metallic and mica finishes looks different when panels are rotated. Specify film thickness, gloss and colour tolerance, and keep protective film on until installation is complete, but remove it within the period the film supplier allows.

Checklist

What to include in a cladding material enquiry

  1. Panel type and build-upSolid, composite (with core type), honeycomb, perforated or insulated.
  2. Alloy, temper and thicknessFor example 3003-H24 at 3 mm for solid panels, or skin and core details for composite panels.
  3. FinishCoating system, colour code, gloss, film thickness and warranty expectation.
  4. Fire classificationThe class required by the project (for example A1, A2-s1,d0 or B-s1,d0) and the test evidence needed.
  5. Sizes and quantitiesSheet or panel sizes, nesting plan if available, and spare quantities for damage and future repairs.
  6. DeliveryDestination, packing for large panels, and the documentation needed for approval.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) panel cladding: frequently asked questions

What thickness is used for aluminium cladding panels?

Solid panels are usually 2 – 4 mm, with 3 mm common for rainscreen cassettes. Composite panels are typically 4 mm overall with about 0.5 mm skins. Wind load, panel size and flatness requirements decide the final thickness.

Is aluminium cladding fire safe?

Solid aluminium panels are non-combustible. Composite panels depend on their core: polyethylene cores are combustible and banned on many buildings, while mineral-filled FR and A2 cores perform far better. Fire performance must be proven for the panel and the complete system.

What is the difference between ACP and solid aluminium panels?

ACP has two thin aluminium skins on a core and is very flat and economical. Solid panels are a single thicker sheet, fully non-combustible, more robust against impact and easier to perforate and curve.

Why choose honeycomb panels over solid panels?

Honeycomb panels stay flat and stiff at large sizes with low weight, making them suitable for large-format panels and high wind loads where solid sheet would need many stiffeners.

How long do coated aluminium facades last?

With a PVDF or super-durable powder coating and correct maintenance, coated aluminium facades commonly last for decades; coating warranties depend on the system, climate and exposure.

Does Metline install cladding?

No. Metline supplies the aluminium materials: coated sheet and coil, composite and honeycomb panels, perforated sheet and extrusions. Fabrication and installation are done by facade specialists.
