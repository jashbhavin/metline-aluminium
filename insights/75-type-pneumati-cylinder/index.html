Pneumatic Cylinder Tube Sizes and Aluminium Profile Reference

Almost every pneumatic cylinder barrel in factory automation is an aluminium extrusion with a honed or drawn, hard-anodised bore. This reference sets out the main tube families, from plain round barrels to ISO 15552 square profiles, compact, guided, slide-table and gripper bodies, with section dimensions, calculated weights and the alloy, finish and tolerance points cylinder makers specify. Use it to identify a profile before you request a quote.

Overview

Why cylinder barrels are made from aluminium

A pneumatic cylinder barrel has three jobs: hold up to 10 bar of air without bulging, give the piston seal a smooth, hard, round surface to run on for millions of strokes, and carry the external features (sensor slots, tie-rod channels, mounting grooves) that let the cylinder be built into a machine.

Extruded aluminium does all three economically. One die produces a barrel with the bore, the outer square or round shape, the T-slots for reed or magnetic sensors and the holes for tie rods or screws, all in a single section. The 6xxx alloys used extrude to close tolerances, take a hard anodic coating that resists wear and corrosion, and weigh about a third as much as a steel barrel of the same size. Being non-magnetic, aluminium also lets the magnet on the piston operate position sensors mounted on the outside of the barrel.

Cylinder makers buy these barrels as precision tube in mill lengths, or cut to stroke length, and then fit the end caps, piston, rod, seals and cushioning themselves. Metline supplies the aluminium tube and profiles, cut and finished to drawing; the cylinders themselves are built by the cylinder manufacturer.

Four standards that shape the profiles

  • ISO 15552 (successor to ISO 6431 and VDMA 24562): standard cylinders with detachable mountings, 10 bar series, bores 32 – 320 mm
  • ISO 21287: compact cylinders, 10 bar series, bores 20 – 100 mm
  • ISO 6432: small round-body cylinders, bores 8 – 25 mm
  • These standards fix mounting and interface dimensions, not the internal barrel profile, so profile shapes still differ from one cylinder design to another
Specification

Alloy, bore finish and tolerances

Typical cylinder tube specification

Alloy and temper
6063-T5 for most standard barrels; 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 where higher strength or thinner walls are needed
Bore tolerance
Commonly H9 to H11, depending on the seal design and bore size
Bore roughness
Typically Ra 0.4 – 0.8 µm after cold drawing or honing, for long seal life
Bore surface
Hard anodised; outside clear, black or hard anodised, or powder coated
Straightness
Controlled by stretching and by precision drawing for round tubes
Section tolerance
Profile dimensions typically ±0.05 – 0.10 mm, depending on size
Lengths
Mill lengths up to about 6 m, or cut to stroke length with square, deburred ends
Working pressure
Designed for the usual 0.1 – 1.0 MPa (1 – 10 bar) pneumatic range

Values are typical of industry practice. The binding requirements are those on your profile drawing and purchase specification.

The bore is the critical surface. Round barrels are usually cold drawn over a mandrel to size and finish; square profiles with complex outer shapes are extruded slightly undersize and the bore is then honed or skived and roller burnished. A hard anodic coating is grown on the finished bore, which raises surface hardness far above that of the base alloy and gives the seal a low-friction, wear-resistant track. Coating thickness and hardness vary between cylinder designs, so specify them on the drawing rather than relying on a supplier default.

Because the anodic coating grows partly outward from the original surface, the bore diameter shrinks slightly during anodising. Cylinder makers allow for this when setting the pre-anodise bore size; the final bore tolerance applies after coating.

Family 3

ISO 15552 standard square cylinder profiles

Standard ISO 15552 cylinders use a square barrel whose four corners carry screw channels for the end caps, while the flats carry sensor slots. Because the standard fixes the mounting interface, the outer square (dimension A or B) is similar across designs, but the inner shape of the corner channels and slots differs. These are the profiles most often supplied as cut-to-length barrels for cylinder assembly lines.

Family 4

Guided, twin-rod and slide-table profiles

Guided cylinders add guide rods or linear bearings beside the piston to resist side loads and stop the tooling plate rotating. Their bodies are wider, multi-bore extrusions, often with two parallel bores (twin-rod) or a bore plus bearing channels. Straightness and parallelism between bores are the critical features, because misalignment causes binding.

Buying advice

How to specify a cylinder tube correctly

  1. Identify the profileSend the section drawing, or the series and bore, plus a photo of the cut end if you are matching an existing barrel. Series names are used only to identify a shape; always confirm dimensions against a drawing.
  2. State the bore requirementBore diameter and tolerance class after anodising, roughness (Ra or Rz), and whether the bore is to be drawn, honed or burnished.
  3. Define the finishBore and outside coating type, thickness and colour; whether the cut ends must be masked or left bare.
  4. Give length and quantityMill length or cut length with tolerance, end squareness, deburring, and the quantity per size.
  5. Agree inspectionCritical dimensions, bore checks with plug gauges or air gauging, coating thickness, and the EN 10204 3.1 certificate.
  6. Packing and deliveryIndividually sleeved tubes to protect the bore, export cases, and EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.

Common specification mistakes

  • Specifying the bore tolerance before anodising, then finding the coated bore undersize
  • Leaving roughness unspecified, which allows a finish that wears seals quickly
  • Ordering by series name alone when two designs share a name but differ in slot geometry
  • Forgetting that cut-to-length barrels need burr-free ends so seals are not damaged during assembly

FAQ

Aluminium pneumatic cylinder tubes: frequently asked questions

Which aluminium alloy is used for pneumatic cylinder tubes?

Most barrels are 6063-T5, which extrudes complex profiles to close tolerances and anodises well. 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 is chosen when the design needs higher strength, thinner walls or larger bores.

What bore tolerance do cylinder tubes have?

Typically ISO H9 to H11 on the bore after anodising, with roughness around Ra 0.4 – 0.8 µm. The exact class depends on the seal design, so it should be stated on the drawing.

Why is the bore hard anodised?

Hard anodising creates a dense aluminium oxide layer much harder than the base alloy. It resists wear from the piston seal, lowers friction and protects against corrosion from moist compressed air.

Is ISO 15552 the same as ISO 6431 and VDMA 24562?

ISO 15552 replaced ISO 6431 and VDMA 24562, keeping the same mounting interface so cylinders remain interchangeable. It defines mounting and accessory dimensions, not the internal profile of the barrel.

Can you supply aluminum cylinder tube cut to stroke length?

Yes. Tubes can be supplied in mill lengths or cut to length with square, deburred ends and protected bores, packed for export with an EN 10204 3.1 mill certificate.

Can a custom cylinder profile be made?

Yes. A die can be made to your section drawing for non-standard bores, additional sensor slots, air passages or mounting grooves. Send the drawing so extrudability and tolerances can be reviewed first.

Need cylinder barrels in a specific profile?

Send your drawing or series, bore, length, finish and quantity. Our team will confirm the profile, tolerances and a delivered price.
