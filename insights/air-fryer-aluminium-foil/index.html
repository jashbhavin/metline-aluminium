Aluminium Foil in an Air Fryer: Yes, With Four Rules

Yes, you can use aluminium foil in most air fryers. Foil will not melt or burn at air-fryer temperatures, and it catches drips and makes cleaning easier. The risks come from how it is placed: foil that blocks the airflow, blows into the heating element or wraps acidic food causes problems. Follow four simple rules and foil is a useful tool rather than a hazard.

The short answer

Why foil is safe at air-fryer temperatures

Household air fryers typically run between about 80 °C and 200 °C, with some models reaching around 230 °C. Aluminium melts at about 660 °C, so the foil itself is never close to its limit.

An air fryer is a small convection oven: a heating element sits above the food and a fan drives hot air down and around it at high speed. Crisping depends on that air reaching every surface of the food. Foil is a thin, flexible sheet of almost pure aluminium, usually 1235, 8011 or 8079 alloy in the fully soft O temper and roughly 9 to 25 micrometres thick. It conducts and reflects heat well and is chemically stable with most foods.

So the question is not whether foil survives the heat; it does. The question is whether it gets in the way of the air, and whether it contacts anything it should not. That leads to four practical rules.

The four rules

  • Foil goes in the basket, under the food, never loose in the empty fryer
  • Never cover the whole basket floor or block the drawer vents
  • Keep foil well clear of the heating element and fan
  • Do not use foil with strongly acidic or very salty foods
Good uses

When foil genuinely helps

Keeping juices in

A loose foil parcel around fish, chicken or vegetables holds moisture and flavour and stops juices dripping through the basket into the drawer.

Holding delicate food

A small foil tray with raised edges stops flaking fish, stuffed vegetables or small pieces falling through the basket grid.

Preventing sticking

Glazed, cheesy or marinated foods stick to bare baskets; a sheet of foil underneath protects the non-stick coating and saves scrubbing.

Batch cooking

Changing the foil between batches means the basket stays clean, so there is no need to stop and wash it between rounds.

Shielding edges

A small piece over the thin end of a fillet or the tips of chicken wings slows browning where it would otherwise burn.

Reheating

Loosely tented foil warms leftovers through without drying the surface, then can be removed for the last minutes to re-crisp.

The catches

Where foil works against you

  • Lost crispness. Wrapping chips, wings or breaded food traps steam. If crunch is the goal, leave the food uncovered or use foil only underneath.
  • Blocked airflow. A sheet that covers the entire basket floor stops air rising through the food, so cooking is slower and uneven. Leave a clear margin around the edges.
  • Foil in the drawer. Never line the bottom of the outer drawer. It can disturb the airflow the appliance is designed around and trap fat where it can overheat.
  • Flying foil. The fan is powerful. Light foil without food on it can lift into the element, where it may scorch, spark or damage the appliance. Never preheat with foil alone in the basket.
  • Acidic food. Tomato sauces, citrus marinades, vinegar dressings and heavily salted foods attack aluminium's protective oxide film, increasing the amount of aluminium that passes into food and sometimes leaving small holes in the foil.

Always check the appliance manual. A few manufacturers advise against foil in particular models, usually because of where the element or sensors sit. The manufacturer's instruction overrides general advice.

Food safety

Does cooking with foil add aluminium to food?

A small amount, and how much depends mainly on the food. Aluminium is naturally present in many foods and in drinking water, and the body excretes most of what is ingested. The European Food Safety Authority set a tolerable weekly intake of 1 mg of aluminium per kilogram of body weight in 2008; most of the intake for typical adults comes from food itself rather than from cookware or foil.

Migration from foil is low for neutral, dry and fatty foods, and rises with acidity, salt, temperature and contact time. That is why food-contact guidance in Europe and elsewhere advises against using uncoated aluminium for strongly acidic or salty foods and for long storage of such foods. In an air fryer, where cooking times are short, the practical rule is simple: use foil for meat, fish, vegetables and baked goods, and use parchment or a dish for anything acidic.

Step by step

How to use foil safely in an air fryer

  1. Check the manualConfirm the manufacturer allows foil in the basket of your model.
  2. Cut it smaller than the basketLeave a gap of at least a couple of centimetres around the edges so hot air can rise past the foil.
  3. Put food on top straight awayThe weight of the food holds the foil down. Shape the edges upward if you need a tray.
  4. Keep it lowMake sure no corner of the foil can reach the element when the basket is closed.
  5. Choose the right gaugeStandard household foil is fine for liners; heavy-duty foil holds tray shapes better and tears less.
  6. Replace when greasy or tornRemove used foil once the basket has cooled, and start each batch with a fresh piece.
Alternatives

Alternatives to foil

Perforated parchment liners

Pre-cut paper liners with holes let air flow while preventing sticking. Like foil, they must be weighed down by food.

Silicone baskets and trays

Reusable, non-stick and good for saucy dishes, though they block more airflow than a perforated liner.

Small oven-safe dishes

Metal or ceramic ramekins and tins suit cakes, eggs, gratins and acidic dishes.

Nothing at all

For maximum crispness, cook directly on a lightly oiled basket and clean it after.

Avoid wax paper: its coating is not designed for oven heat and can smoke or ignite.

Material

What food-grade aluminium foil is made of

Kitchen foil is rolled from high-purity aluminium or from lean alloys such as 8011 and 8079, which contain small additions of iron and silicon that give the thin foil better strength and pinhole resistance. It is rolled down to its final gauge and then annealed, which leaves it soft and free of rolling lubricant so that it is suitable for food contact. The bright side and the matt side come from the final rolling pass, where two sheets are rolled together; in cooking, either side can face the food.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) foil in an air fryer: FAQ

Can aluminium foil catch fire in an air fryer?

The foil will not melt or burn at air-fryer temperatures, but loose foil can be blown into the heating element, where it can scorch or spark. Keep food on top of it and keep it clear of the element.

Should the shiny or dull side of foil face the food?

It makes no practical difference. The two finishes come from the rolling process, and both conduct and reflect heat almost equally.

Is parchment paper better than foil in an air fryer?

Perforated parchment is better for acidic foods and wet batters and allows good airflow. Foil is better for shaping trays, wrapping food to keep it moist and shielding edges from over-browning.

Can I put foil in the bottom of the air fryer drawer?

No. Lining the outer drawer disturbs the airflow and can trap hot fat. Use foil only in the basket, under the food.

Can I use foil containers in an air fryer?

Small aluminium foil trays work well if they fit with room around them for air to circulate and do not touch the element.

What thickness of foil is best for air frying?

Standard household foil (around 10 – 16 µm) is fine for liners. Heavy-duty foil (around 18 – 25 µm) holds a tray shape better and is less likely to tear.
