6061 vs 6082 Aluminium: Differences and When to Substitute

6061 and 6082 are close cousins: both heat-treatable Al-Mg-Si alloys with similar density, stiffness and corrosion behaviour. 6082 is slightly stronger in most product forms and is the default structural alloy under EN standards; 6061 is the default under ASTM and AMS and machines to a slightly better finish. They are often interchangeable, but only after checking the certified minimums in the governing standard.

Our view

The ten differences that matter

Most "6061 vs 6082" questions are really substitution questions: a European drawing calls for 6082 and the stock available is 6061, or the reverse. Our view is that the swap is usually technically fine for general engineering, and rarely fine for certified structural, aerospace or pressure work without the designer's approval.

6082 uses excess silicon and a substantial manganese addition instead of copper. Manganese forms dispersoids that suppress recrystallisation, so extruded 6082 keeps a fine, fibrous grain and higher strength in thick sections. 6061 relies on copper and chromium for a similar effect. The low copper of 6082 is the reason it is sometimes favoured for marine and exposed structures.

Typical properties tell the same story: 6082-T6 usually tests around 340 MPa tensile and 300 – 310 MPa yield, against 310 MPa and 276 MPa for 6061-T6. Elongation is similar (roughly 10 – 12 %), and both have a density of 2.70 g/cm³ and a modulus close to 69 GPa. Equal stiffness means a substitution never changes deflection; it only changes the strength margin.

Some published comparisons show 6061 at 340 MPa and 6082 above 400 MPa. Those figures do not match standard data for the T6 temper, and we advise against designing to them.

Swapping alloys

When can one replace the other?

6082 instead of 6061: usually safe

In most forms 6082 meets or exceeds 6061 minimums, so it satisfies the strength intent. Check machined finish, anodised colour and whether the drawing or customer specification names an ASTM or AMS standard that only lists 6061.

6061 instead of 6082: check first

6061 bar and profile minimums are lower than 6082 above 20 mm. A part designed to 6082 minimums may not have the margin. Re-check stresses or ask the designer.

Never swap without approval

Aerospace (AMS), pressure equipment, class-approved marine parts, structural members designed to Eurocode 9 or the ADM, and any part with a qualified welding procedure tied to the alloy.

Indian standards add a third name to the mix: IS 733/IS 737 grades 64430 (traditionally called HE30) correspond to 6082 and 65032 (HE20) to 6061, which is useful when reading older Indian drawings.

When you request a substitution, put it in writing on the purchase order: the original alloy, the proposed alloy, the governing standard and the minimum properties the part needs. Ask for the mill certificate of the actual lot and compare its tested values with the original specification. That small step protects both buyer and supplier and leaves a clear audit trail for inspection.

Working the alloys

Welding, machining, corrosion and conductivity

Welding

Both weld with 4043 or 5356 filler and neither should be welded autogenously. Both lose a large share of T6 strength in the heat-affected zone; Eurocode 9 applies HAZ reduction factors to both.

Machining

Both machine very well in T6/T651. 6061 tends to give a slightly brighter finish; 6082's manganese adds a little tool wear. Differences are small next to tooling and coolant choices.

Corrosion

Both are good in atmospheric and mild marine service. 6082's very low copper is a small advantage; drainage, isolation from dissimilar metals and coatings matter more.

Electrical and thermal

Both are mid-range conductors, well below 6101 or 1350. 6061-T6 is about 43 % IACS; 6082 is usually a little lower. For busbars use a conductor alloy.

Anodising

Both anodise well. 6061 generally gives a slightly cleaner colour; 6082 can look greyer, especially in hard anodising.

Forming

Both have limited bendability in T6. Form in T4 or O and age afterwards where tight bends are needed.

FAQ

6061 vs 6082 aluminum: frequently asked questions

Is 6082 stronger than 6061?

Slightly, in most forms. EN 755-2 sets 6082-T6 bar over 20 mm at 310 MPa tensile and 260 MPa yield minimum, against 260 / 240 MPa for 6061-T6.

Can I use 6061 instead of 6082?

For general machined parts, usually. For structural parts designed to 6082 minimums, check the stresses first, because 6061 minimums are lower in thicker sections.

What is the US equivalent of 6082?

There is no direct ASTM workhorse equivalent; 6061 is the usual US substitute. 6082 is registered with the Aluminum Association (UNS A96082) but is rarely specified under ASTM.

Which is better for welding?

Both weld similarly with 4043 or 5356 filler and lose strength in the heat-affected zone. 6082 is common in welded European structures because Eurocode 9 data for it are well established.

Which machines better?

Both machine very well in T651. 6061 tends to give a slightly brighter finish and a little less tool wear.

Is HE30 the same as 6082?

HE30 is the older British/Indian name for the alloy now designated 6082 (IS 64430). HE20 corresponds to 6061.

Not sure whether 6061 or 6082 fits?

Send the drawing, standard and quantity. We will advise on substitution and quote either alloy with mill certificates.
