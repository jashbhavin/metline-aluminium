Black Anodized Aluminium: Process, Alloys and How to Specify It

Black anodized aluminium is aluminium with a grown oxide layer that has been coloured black, either by absorbing an organic dye into the pores or by depositing metal salts electrolytically, and then sealed. The colour sits inside the oxide rather than on top of it, so it cannot peel. How deep, even and fade-resistant the black is depends mostly on three choices: the alloy, the film thickness and the colouring method.

What it is

What black anodized aluminium actually is

Our view: "black anodised" is not one finish but at least three different processes that look similar on day one and behave very differently after a few years in sunlight. Name the method in your specification.

Anodising converts the surface of aluminium into aluminium oxide by electrolysis in an acid bath, usually sulphuric acid. The oxide forms as a dense barrier layer next to the metal with a thicker porous layer above it, millions of pores per square centimetre that are open until they are sealed. Those pores are what make colouring possible: dye molecules or metal particles are deposited inside them and then locked in by hydrothermal sealing.

Because the coating grows out of the metal itself, roughly a third of its thickness lies below the original surface and two-thirds above it. It is hard (much harder than the base aluminium), electrically insulating and integral with the substrate. A black anodised surface therefore resists chipping and peeling in a way that paint or powder coating cannot, although the film itself is thin and can be scratched through by hard objects.

Methods

Three ways to make anodised aluminium black

MethodHow the black is madeFade resistance outdoorsTypical use
Organic dye (adsorptive)Porous film is immersed in a black dye solution after anodising, then sealedVaries with dye; many blacks fade or shift colour in long UV exposureConsumer electronics, interior parts, machined components, optics
Electrolytic (two-step) colouringAfter anodising, AC current deposits tin, nickel or cobalt particles at the pore bases; long colouring times reach blackVery good; the standard for exterior architectureFaçades, window and curtain-wall profiles, exterior panels

For indoor products and machined parts, organic black dyes give the deepest, most neutral black at the lowest cost. For anything that will face the sun for years, ask for electrolytic colouring, or for a dye that has passed a light-fastness test such as ISO 2135 at the required rating. Hardcoat on many alloys is naturally dark grey to bronze, which helps the dye produce a dense black, but the shade varies more with alloy and thickness than decorative anodising does.

Process

The black anodising process step by step

  1. PretreatmentDegreasing, then alkaline etching for a matte satin look, or chemical/electrochemical brightening for a glossy black. Mechanical brushing or bead blasting before this sets the texture.
  2. DesmuttingAcid removal of the smut that etching leaves on the surface, especially on silicon- or copper-bearing alloys.
  3. AnodisingTypically sulphuric acid at around 18 – 22 °C for decorative work. Film for a deep dyed black is usually 15 – 25 µm; thinner films look grey or brown.
  4. ColouringImmersion in black dye (often warm, several minutes) or electrolytic colouring in a metal-salt bath until the target shade is reached against a limit sample.
  5. SealingHot-water or mid-temperature nickel acetate sealing closes the pores and locks in the colour; cold sealing is used on some lines. Sealing quality decides fade and stain resistance.
  6. InspectionColour against agreed limit samples, film thickness (eddy current), and seal tests such as dye-spot or mass-loss.

Dyed black films should be sealed promptly. Delays, warm rinse water or contamination before sealing can leach dye and leave a brownish or uneven black.

Alloys

Which aluminium alloys anodise best in black?

Alloying elements that do not anodise cleanly, mainly silicon, copper and iron-rich particles, make the film greyer, thinner or patchy. That matters more for black than for clear finishes, because any variation in film thickness changes how much dye the film absorbs.

AlloyBlack anodising resultBest for
5005 / 5657 (anodising quality sheet)Very even, deep black; excellent match between sheetsArchitectural panels, signage, appliance trims, decorative sheet
6063 / 6060 (anodising quality profiles)Very even black with good gloss controlWindow, door and façade profiles, frames, display systems
5052Good; slightly warmer tone than 5005Enclosures, panels, machined and formed parts
3003Fair to good; can look slightly grey and streakyGeneral sheet parts where appearance is secondary
6061Good, slightly greyer than 6063Machined parts, structural components, optical mounts
7075Good, dense black; batch-to-batch shade variesHigh-strength machined parts, sporting and defence goods
2024 and 2011Fair; thinner, less uniform film because of copperFunction first; accept shade variation
High-silicon cast alloysPoor; dark grey and blotchyConsider paint or powder coat instead

Mixing alloys in one visible assembly is the most common cause of complaints: a 6061 machined part next to a 6063 profile and a 5005 panel will never match perfectly, even from the same tank. If appearance matters, standardise alloys or accept and agree the shade differences in advance. Our guide on aluminium for anodised appearance parts covers alloy choice in more depth.

Good when well sealed; class 20 – 25 µm recommended for coastal and industrial exteriors

Electrical behaviour
The film is insulating; mask areas that need electrical contact or grounding
Thermal emissivity
High, typically around 0.8 – 0.9, which helps heat sinks radiate compared with bare aluminium
Heat exposure
Organic dyes can fade or shift colour at sustained elevated temperatures; check with the finisher for hot service
Optical
Low reflectance matte black is widely used in optical and camera housings to control stray light

Two practical cautions. Anodic film can craze (fine surface cracking) if the part is bent after anodising or heated well above about 80 – 100 °C, because the oxide expands far less than the metal. And anodising slightly changes dimensions: about half of the film thickness builds up on each surface, which matters on precision bores and threads.

Finishes

Matte, satin, brushed and gloss black finishes

Matte / satin black

Alkaline etch before anodising diffuses the surface and hides minor marks. The most common architectural and industrial black.

Brushed black

Linear brushing before anodising gives a directional grain that shows through the black film; popular for appliances, lifts and interiors.

Bead-blasted black

Uniform fine texture, very low gloss; widely used on electronic housings and machined consumer products.

Gloss / bright black

Chemical or electrochemical brightening on high-purity or bright-trim alloys gives a deep, reflective black.

Pre-anodised black coil and sheet

Continuously anodised on a coil line for very consistent colour over large areas, then cut and formed by the fabricator. Bending must respect the minimum radius for anodised material.

Black hardcoat

Functional dark finish for wear, defence and tooling; colour depends heavily on alloy and thickness.

Choose anodising when the metallic look, hardness and absence of an organic layer matter, and when alloys can be controlled. Choose powder or PVDF when exact colour matching across mixed alloys and parts is more important than metallic depth.

Applications

Where black anodized aluminium is used

  • Architecture: curtain-wall and window profiles, façade panels, soffits, sunshades and trims, usually with electrolytic colour for fade resistance
  • Electronics: laptop, audio, camera and instrument housings, front panels and heat-sink fins
  • Industrial equipment: machine guards, automation frames, control panels and enclosures
  • Optics and defence: lens barrels, sight mounts and optical benches where low reflectance matters
  • Interiors and display: signage, retail fixtures, lighting bodies, furniture frames and trims
  • Sports and consumer goods: bicycle components, torches, knife scales and accessories

FAQ

Black anodized aluminium: frequently asked questions

Does black anodized aluminium fade?

Electrolytically coloured black is very stable outdoors. Organic dye blacks can fade or turn brownish or purplish after long UV exposure or sustained heat, depending on the dye and the sealing. Specify electrolytic colour or a light-fast tested dye for exterior use.

Which alloy is best for black anodizing?

Anodising-quality 5005 sheet and 6063 or 6060 extrusions give the most uniform black. 6061 and 7075 anodise well for machined parts but look slightly different in shade; copper-rich 2024 is the hardest to make uniform.

How thick should a black anodized coating be?

For a deep dyed black indoors, about 15 – 25 µm. For exterior architectural work, 20 – 25 µm (AAMA 611 Class I or ISO 7599 class 20/25). Hardcoat black is usually 25 µm or more.

Can black anodized aluminum be bent after anodising?

Gently, with generous radii. Tight bends crack (craze) the oxide and show as grey lines. For formed parts, anodise after forming or use pre-anodised material graded for the bend radius.

Is black anodizing electrically conductive?

No. The oxide is an insulator, so areas needing electrical contact or grounding must be masked or machined after anodising.

Is black anodized better than black powder coating?

It is harder, cannot peel and keeps a metallic look, but colour depends on alloy. Powder coating hides alloy differences and is easier to match and repair. The right choice depends on appearance and service needs.
