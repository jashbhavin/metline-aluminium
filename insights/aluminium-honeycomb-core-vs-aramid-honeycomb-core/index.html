Aluminium Honeycomb Core vs Aramid Honeycomb Core

At the same density, aluminium and aramid honeycomb cores have similar compressive strength, but aluminium core is several times stiffer in shear, conducts heat and electricity, and usually costs less per panel. Aramid core is non-metallic, insulating, easier to form over curves and dominant in aircraft interiors. The right choice depends on what the sandwich panel must do, not on which core is "better".

Material 1

What is aluminium honeycomb core?

Aluminium honeycomb is made from thin aluminium foil, typically 3003, 5052 or 5056 alloy, printed with lines of adhesive, stacked, cured into a block and then expanded into hexagonal cells. The expanded block is sliced to the core thickness the panel needs. Foil gauge and cell size set the density, which usually ranges from about 30 to over 190 kg/m³.

Bonded between two face sheets, the core keeps the faces apart and carries shear, much like the web of an I-beam. Doubling the core thickness roughly multiplies panel bending stiffness several times for very little added weight, which is why honeycomb sandwich panels are used wherever stiffness per kilogram matters. For aerospace use the foil is often given a corrosion-resistant treatment such as phosphoric-acid anodising before bonding.

Material 2

What is aramid honeycomb core?

Aramid honeycomb is made from aramid-fibre paper (meta-aramid such as Nomex, or para-aramid such as Kevlar-based paper for higher-performance grades), formed into honeycomb and then dipped in phenolic or other resin to build up wall thickness and stiffness. The resin content largely sets its density, typically from about 24 to over 140 kg/m³.

Because it is non-metallic, it does not corrode, it insulates thermally and electrically, it is transparent to radar at suitable thickness, and it has good inherent fire, smoke and toxicity (FST) behaviour with phenolic resin. It also drapes and thermoforms more easily over compound curves. Metline supplies aluminium honeycomb; aramid core is discussed here as the main alternative buyers compare it with.

Data

Strength and stiffness at similar density

The most meaningful comparison is at the same cell size and density. The table uses typical values from published manufacturer data sheets for a 3.2 mm (1/8 in) cell core of about 48 – 50 kg/m³ (3 lb/ft³).

Typical values from manufacturer data sheets tested to ASTM C365 (compression) and ASTM C273 (plate shear). Values differ between producers and foil treatments; design allowables must come from the supplier's certified data.

Two conclusions follow. Compressive and shear strength are broadly similar, so for crushing and core shear failure the materials are comparable at equal weight. Shear stiffness is very different: aluminium core is roughly five to eight times stiffer in shear, so a panel with aluminium core deflects less and resists face-sheet buckling better. Where deflection limits govern, as in floors, platforms, facade panels and tooling, aluminium usually allows a thinner or lighter panel.

Physical behaviour

Note that the effective thermal conductivity of a honeycomb core is much lower than that of solid aluminium, because the foil occupies only a few per cent of the volume and the rest is air; it also differs between the through-thickness and in-plane directions. For heat-spreading panels, electronics enclosures and EMI vent panels, aluminium is the natural choice. For radomes, antenna covers, battery isolation and insulated interiors, aramid is.

Durability

Fire behaviour, corrosion and moisture

Fire and FST

Aluminium itself does not burn, but the panel's adhesive and faces do contribute. Aramid-phenolic core is a long-established choice for aircraft cabin panels qualified to FAR/CS 25.853. In every case, FST compliance is tested on the complete panel, not the core alone.

Corrosion

Aluminium core can corrode if moisture enters and the foil is untreated, especially near carbon-fibre faces or in marine service. Use 5052 or 5056 foil with corrosion-resistant treatment and seal edges and inserts.

Moisture

Aramid paper and phenolic resin absorb some moisture, which can affect properties and bonding. Store and dry core before bonding, and seal panel edges.

Temperature

Both are commonly used up to about 175 °C in service with suitable adhesives; the adhesive system often sets the real limit.

Impact

Aluminium core dents permanently and absorbs energy by crushing, useful in crash absorbers. Aramid core recovers partly from small impacts but can hide damage.

Recycling

Aluminium core and aluminium-faced panels are easier to recycle than resin-impregnated aramid.

Manufacturing

Cost, forming and fabrication

For large flat or gently curved panels, aluminium honeycomb is usually the more economical core, both per square metre and per unit of stiffness. Aramid core costs more, but it machines into complex contoured shapes, drapes over compound curves (especially in over-expanded or flexible cell configurations) and bonds well to composite prepreg skins in a single cure.

Aluminium core can also be formed: over-expanded and flexible-cell aluminium cores bend to single curvature, and blocks can be CNC machined to contour. Machining aluminium core needs sharp tools and good support to avoid tearing the foil; aramid needs dust control. Inserts, edge closures and potting are similar for both.

Design thinking

A worked example: why shear stiffness changes the panel

Consider a simply supported floor panel 1 m long with 0.5 mm aluminium faces and a 20 mm core of about 50 kg/m³, under a distributed load. Its deflection has two parts: bending of the faces, which depends on face thickness and core depth and is the same whichever core is used, and shear deformation of the core, which is inversely proportional to the core shear modulus.

With aluminium core, the shear part is usually a small fraction of the total, so the panel behaves almost like an ideal sandwich. Swap in an aramid core of the same density, with a shear modulus roughly one-seventh as high, and the shear part grows by the same factor. In thin-faced, short-span panels it can then become the larger share of the deflection. To get back to the original stiffness, the designer has to thicken the core or raise the core density, and both add cost or weight.

The reverse also holds. On long, slender panels where face bending dominates, the difference in core shear modulus matters much less, and the electrical, thermal, FST or forming advantages of aramid can decide the choice. A quick sandwich-beam calculation with both cores, done before material selection, makes the trade-off visible.

Selection

How to choose between them

Choose aluminium core when

  • Shear stiffness and low deflection drive the design
  • Panels are large, flat and cost-sensitive
  • Face sheets are aluminium or the panel must conduct heat or ground electrically
  • Energy absorption by controlled crushing is wanted
  • Recyclability matters

Choose aramid core when

  • The panel must insulate electrically or be radar-transparent
  • Aircraft interior FST requirements apply and a qualified system exists
  • Skins are composite prepreg and the part has compound curves
  • Corrosion risk is high and sealing cannot be guaranteed
  • Thermal insulation through the panel is required

Common mistakes: comparing cores at different densities, using only compressive strength (where they look similar) and ignoring shear modulus (where they differ most), and assuming any aramid panel automatically passes FST tests. Always compare at the same cell size and density, and qualify the complete panel.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) vs aramid honeycomb: frequently asked questions

Which is stronger, aluminum or aramid honeycomb?

At the same density and cell size, compressive and shear strengths are similar. Aluminium core is much stiffer in shear, typically five to eight times, so panels deflect less.

Is aramid honeycomb lighter than aluminium honeycomb?

Not inherently. Both come in a range of densities. Aramid is available in some very low densities, but at equal density the weight is the same and the comparison comes down to properties.

Is aramid always required where FST rules apply?

No. FST compliance is demonstrated on the complete panel, including faces and adhesive. Aramid-phenolic is common in aircraft interiors, but aluminium-cored panels are also used where the system is qualified.

Which core is better for heat dissipation?

Aluminium. Its foil walls conduct heat, while aramid core insulates.

Are curved parts always better with aramid core?

Aramid drapes compound curves more easily, but flexible-cell and over-expanded aluminium cores handle single curvature well, and aluminium blocks can be machined to contour.

Does Metline supply aramid honeycomb?

No. Metline supplies aluminium honeycomb core in 3003, 5052 and 5056 foil, as blocks, slices and panels. Aramid core is covered here only for comparison.
