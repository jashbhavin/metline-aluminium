Aluminium Alloy Tempers Explained: How to Read O, H, T, W and F Codes

The temper code after an alloy number tells you how the metal was processed and therefore how strong, formable and stable it is. F means as fabricated, O annealed, H strain hardened, W solution treated and unstable, and T heat treated to a stable condition; the digits that follow refine the route. Reading them correctly is as important as choosing the alloy: 6061-O and 6061-T6 differ in yield strength by a factor of about five.

The five letters

The five basic temper designations

Temper designations are internationally harmonised. The Aluminum Association system in ANSI H35.1 and the European EN 515 use the same letters and almost all the same digits, so 5083-H116 or 6082-T6 means the same thing in Houston, Hamburg or Mumbai.

LetterMeaningApplies toExample
FAs fabricated: no special control of strain hardening or heat treatment; no mechanical property limits for wrought productsAll alloys6063-F extrusion, 5083-F plate
OAnnealed: heated to give the lowest strength and maximum ductilityAll wrought alloys and some castings3003-O, 6061-O
HStrain hardened: strength raised by cold work, with or without partial annealing or stabilisingNon-heat-treatable alloys (1xxx, 3xxx, 5xxx, some 8xxx)5052-H32, 1050-H14
WSolution heat treated: unstable, because the alloy keeps ageing at room temperatureHeat-treatable alloys, as a transient state7075-W (used for forming before ageing)
TThermally treated to a stable temper other than F, O or H, with or without cold workHeat-treatable alloys (2xxx, 6xxx, 7xxx)6061-T6, 2024-T3, 7075-T73

F is not "free machining". It simply means the product left the mill without a controlled temper, so properties must be agreed separately or checked by test.

Strain-hardened tempers

H tempers: reading the two (or three) digits

The first digit after H says what happened after cold work. The second digit gives the degree of hardening on a scale where 8 is full hard, 4 is roughly halfway between O and full hard, and 2 is a quarter. The third digit, when present, marks a special variation.

CodeMeaning
H1xStrain hardened only, e.g. H12, H14, H16, H18
H2xStrain hardened beyond the target, then partially annealed back to it; slightly more ductile than the equivalent H1x
H3xStrain hardened and stabilised by low-temperature heating, used for Mg-bearing 5xxx alloys that would otherwise soften over time
H4xStrain hardened and partially annealed by the curing of an applied paint or lacquer (common in coil-coated sheet)
Hx2 / Hx4 / Hx6 / Hx8Quarter hard / half hard / three-quarter hard / full hard
Hx9Extra hard: tensile at least 10 MPa above the Hx8 minimum (e.g. 3104-H19 can stock, 8011-H19 foil)
H111Annealed, then given a small amount of strain (from straightening or stretching) less than H11; common for 5083 and 5754 plate
H112Slightly strain hardened by hot working, with specified minimum properties; common for extrusions and hot-rolled plate
H116Special 5xxx temper for marine plate with tested resistance to exfoliation and intergranular corrosion (ASTM B928)
H321Strain hardened and stabilised with controlled corrosion resistance; marine 5083 and 5086 plate

Practical reading: 5052-H32 is quarter-hard and stabilised, so it bends well and stays stable; 5052-H34 is stronger but needs larger bend radii; 1050-H24 is half-hard and partly annealed, so it forms more easily than 1050-H14 at similar strength.

"Solution heat treated" in the press sense matters: in T5 the extrusion is quenched at the press exit rather than reheated in a separate furnace, which is why 6063-T5 is cheaper but weaker than 6063-T6. Additional digits refine the T temper, as below.

Why stress relief matters

  • Quenching leaves high residual stress: compression at the surface, tension in the core
  • Machining one side releases it and the part bows; T651 and T6511 remove most of it
  • For machined plate and bar, always order the stretched temper unless the part is thin
Annealed and unstable tempers

O, O1 and W tempers

O

Fully annealed, lowest strength. Choose for deep drawing, spinning and severe bending, or as the starting point for heat treatment by the user.

O1

Heated at about the solution-treatment temperature and slowly cooled; mainly an ultrasonic-inspection-friendly condition for some forging stock.

National variants

Some national standards add codes such as O2 (thermo-mechanically processed for superplastic forming) and O3 (homogenised). Confirm their meaning against the standard named on the order.

W

The freshly quenched state. 2xxx and 7xxx parts are sometimes formed in W (or refrigerated to delay ageing) and then aged. W is followed by the natural-ageing time, e.g. W ½ h.

Selection

How to choose a temper: practical rules

If your part…ChooseAvoid
Is heavily machined from plateT651 (6061, 7075, 2024-T351)Unstretched T6 plate
Is bent tightly or deep drawnO, H111, H22/H32, or T4 then ageT6 or full-hard H18
Is a 5xxx plate in seawaterH116 or H321 to ASTM B928H32 or H34 for hull plate
Is a thick 7xxx part in a corrosive or stressed environmentT73, T7351, T74, T74517075-T6 in thick sections
Is an architectural extrusion6063-T5 or T6, 6060-T66F temper without properties
Carries current6101-T6/T61/T63, 1350-H19/H111High-strength tempers that lower conductivity
Will be weldedAny temper, but design for HAZ softening (6xxx drops to near T4)Relying on T6 strength at the weld

Two frequent errors: ordering "6061" without a temper and receiving F or O material; and specifying H32 when the contract calls for marine-certified H116. Always write alloy and temper together and name the product standard.

FAQ

Aluminium tempers: frequently asked questions

What does T6 mean in aluminium?

Solution heat treated, quenched and artificially aged to peak strength. 6061-T6, 6082-T6 and 7075-T6 are typical examples.

What is the difference between T6 and T651?

T651 is T6 material that has also been stretched about 1.5 – 3 % after quenching to relieve residual stress, so it stays flat during machining. Strength is the same.

What does H32 mean on 5052 aluminium (aluminum)?

Strain hardened to about quarter-hard and then stabilised by low-temperature heating, which stops Mg-bearing alloys softening over time. It is the most common temper for 5052 sheet.

Is H111 the same as O?

Almost. H111 is annealed material that has received a small amount of strain, for example from straightening, so its minimum properties are slightly higher than O. It is widely used for 5083 and 5754 plate.

What does F temper mean?

As fabricated, with no controlled strain hardening or heat treatment and no specified mechanical properties for wrought products. Order a defined temper if you need guaranteed strength.

Can non-heat-treatable alloys be given T tempers?

No. 1xxx, 3xxx and 5xxx alloys do not respond to precipitation hardening, so they are supplied in O or H tempers only.

Need help specifying a temper?

Send the alloy, form, size and application. We will confirm the right temper and quote certified material delivered worldwide.
