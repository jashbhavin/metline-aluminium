1060 Aluminium Slug

1060 aluminium slugs are annealed blanks of at least 99.6 % aluminium, sitting between 1050 and 1070 in purity and softness. They give excellent flow in impact extrusion with high conductivity for electrical and thermal parts. Metline supplies 1060 slugs punched or sawn to your diameter, thickness, shape and surface finish, with mill certification, worldwide.

Overview

Where 1060 fits among slug grades

The 1xxx slug grades differ mainly in purity. Each extra tenth of a per cent of aluminium makes the annealed metal slightly softer, more ductile and more conductive, and slightly more expensive.

1060 (99.6 % Al) is widely used in North America and Asia, where it is registered as A91060 and JIS A1060; in Europe the nearest common slug grades are EN AW-1050A and 1070A. In a press it behaves very close to 1050 with a marginally lower flow stress, and its slightly higher conductivity makes it a good fit for electrical housings, terminals and heat-dissipating parts.

Like all pure grades it cannot be strengthened by heat treatment. Finished impact-extruded parts gain strength from the cold work of the extrusion itself, which is why a thin wall made from a soft slug ends up noticeably harder than the slug was.

FAQ

1060 aluminium (aluminum) slugs: FAQs

Is 1060 better than 1050 for slugs?

The difference is small. 1060 is slightly purer, softer and more conductive; 1050 is slightly stronger and more widely used in Europe. Choose by the conductivity, strength and availability your part needs.

What is the European equivalent of 1060?

1060 is not a common EN slug grade; EN AW-1050A (99.5 %) or EN AW-1070A (99.7 %) is normally specified instead, depending on the ductility needed.

Can 1060 slugs be anodised after extrusion?

Yes. Pure aluminium anodises well, giving a clear, protective film; finished parts can also be lacquered or printed.

What temper are 1060 slugs supplied in?

Normally fully annealed (O). A specific hardness range can be agreed for your process.
