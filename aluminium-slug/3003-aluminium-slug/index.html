3003 Aluminium Slug

3003 aluminium slugs are annealed blanks of an aluminium-manganese alloy that is about 50 % stronger than pure aluminium while remaining very formable. They are chosen when an impact-extruded part such as a battery case, pressure-bearing can or automotive housing needs more strength than 1050 or 1070 can give. Metline supplies 3003 slugs punched or sawn to your size, shape and surface specification, worldwide.

Overview

Why choose 3003 slugs?

Manganese in solid solution and fine manganese-bearing particles raise the strength of 3003 without heat treatment. In the annealed condition it still has ample ductility for impact extrusion, and the part it makes is stiffer and more dent- and pressure-resistant than the same part in pure aluminium.

The cost is a higher flow stress, so 3003 needs more press force than 1070 and walls cannot be quite as thin for the same tooling. In return the designer can reduce wall thickness for a given strength, or meet burst and swelling limits that pure grades cannot. 3003 also keeps the excellent corrosion resistance of pure aluminium, is brazeable, and has good thermal conductivity.

Typical users are makers of cylindrical and prismatic battery cases, capacitor cans that must resist internal pressure, automotive receiver/dryers and filter housings, and technical aerosol containers.

Production & pressing

Production and press considerations

RouteHow it worksBest for
Punched from stripCast or rolled strip is cold rolled to thickness, slugs are blanked on high-speed presses, then annealed and finishedThin to medium slugs in high volume: aerosol, tube and capacitor slugs
Sawn from rod or barCast or extruded rod is sawn to length, then annealed and finishedThick slugs, large diameters and short runs; low scrap
  • Expect higher press loads than with 1070; check press capacity and tool stresses when switching grades
  • A well-blasted surface and even lubricant coverage matter more as flow stress rises
  • Rectangular and oval slugs for prismatic battery cases need close control of outline and flatness
  • Finished parts can be brazed, welded, anodised or lacquered

FAQ

3003 aluminium (aluminum) slugs: FAQs

How much stronger is 3003 than 1070?

In the annealed condition 3003 has a typical tensile strength of about 110 MPa against roughly 60 – 80 MPa for 1070, so parts can be stronger or thinner-walled.

Can 3003 slugs be impact extruded as easily as pure aluminium?

They extrude well but need higher press force, and very thin walls are harder to achieve. Good surface preparation and lubrication become more important.

Is 3003 heat-treatable?

No. It gains strength from manganese and from cold work, not from heat treatment. Finished parts are strengthened by the deformation of extrusion.

Why is 3003 used for battery cases?

It combines good formability for deep, often rectangular cases with higher strength to resist swelling, plus good corrosion resistance and weldability for sealing.

What certificate comes with 3003 slugs?

An EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate with chemical analysis and agreed test results, such as hardness and dimensions, for each lot.
