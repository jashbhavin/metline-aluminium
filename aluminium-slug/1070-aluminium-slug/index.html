1070 Aluminium Slug

1070 aluminium slugs are annealed blanks of at least 99.7 % aluminium, the softest and most ductile of the common slug grades. They make the thinnest walls and deepest parts in impact extrusion, from aerosol cans and collapsible tubes to battery and capacitor cases. Metline supplies 1070 and 1070A slugs in round, perforated, oval, rectangular and custom shapes, with controlled hardness and surface, worldwide.

Overview

Why 1070 is the reference slug alloy

In a typical aerosol-can press, a slug only a few millimetres thick becomes a can body with walls a fraction of a millimetre thick in a single stroke. Very few metals tolerate that much deformation at room temperature; annealed 99.7 % aluminium does it routinely.

The low level of iron, silicon and other elements in 1070 means fewer hard intermetallic particles and a lower work-hardening rate. The result is low flow stress, high elongation, fewer surface defects in thin walls, and lower press loads and tool wear than with 1050 or alloyed slugs. That is why 1070 (or the European 1070A) is the conventional alloy for aerosol cans and collapsible tubes.

The trade-off is strength. Parts in 1070 rely on the cold work of extrusion for stiffness and, for aerosol cans, on wall thickness and shape. When higher strength or burst pressure is needed, designers move to 1050, 3003 or heat-treatable 6061 slugs.

Benefits of 1070 slugs

  • Maximum ductility for thin walls and deep draws
  • Low and consistent hardness after annealing
  • High thermal and electrical conductivity for heat sinks and electrical cans
  • Clean surface that lacquers, prints and anodises well
  • Fully recyclable without loss of properties

Surface

Surface treatments

Tumbled

Rotated with media to remove burrs and oxide; the slightly roughened surface holds lubricant.

Shot-peened or blasted

An even matte texture that retains lubricant uniformly, preferred for demanding draws.

Sand-blasted

Cleans and roughens the surface, removing oxide and contaminants.

Mill finish

As punched or sawn and annealed; for less critical parts.

The press lubricant, commonly zinc stearate, is applied by the user (or on request) after surface treatment. A uniform surface texture is what keeps lubricant coverage, and therefore wall finish and tool life, consistent from slug to slug.

Packing

Packing for export

Slugs are commonly packed by net weight in cartons or bags (25 kg is a common unit), stacked on wooden pallets and stretch-wrapped against moisture and dirt. Each carton is labelled with product code, lot number and customer details, and carton or pallet sizes can be adapted to your handling equipment. Every shipment includes an EN 10204 3.1 certificate and packing list.

FAQ

1070 aluminium (aluminum) slugs: FAQs

Why is 1070 used for aerosol cans?

At 99.7 % purity and fully annealed, 1070 has the low flow stress and high ductility needed to extrude thin, tall can walls in one stroke, with a clean surface for lacquering and printing.

What is the difference between 1070 and 1070A?

1070A is the European EN 573-3 designation. Both require at least 99.7 % aluminium; the small differences in copper and zinc limits do not affect impact extrusion.

What hardness are 1070 slugs supplied at?

Annealed 1070 slugs are typically in the range of about 16 – 20 HB. A specific window can be agreed and certified for your process.

Which surface treatment should I choose?

Shot-peened or blasted slugs hold lubricant most evenly and suit demanding draws; tumbled slugs are a good general choice. Discuss your part and press with us.

How do I work out the slug weight?

Slug mass equals the mass of the finished part plus trim. For a solid round slug, mass in grams = 0.785 × d² × t × 2.70 / 1000, with d and t in mm.

When should I use 3003 instead of 1070?

When the finished part needs higher strength, for example pressure-bearing cans or battery cases that must resist swelling. 3003 needs higher press force.
