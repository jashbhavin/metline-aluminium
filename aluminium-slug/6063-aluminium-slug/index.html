6063 Aluminium Slugs

6063 aluminium slugs are annealed blanks that flow easily under an impact press and can then be aged for strength, which makes them a common choice for cold-forged heat sinks, LED and motor housings and decorative anodised parts. 6063 sits between soft 1xxx/3xxx slugs and the stronger 6061: easier on the tools, with good thermal conductivity and a bright anodised finish. Metline supplies 6063 slugs punched or sawn to drawing, worldwide.

Overview

Why use 6063 for impact extrusion?

6063 contains less magnesium and silicon than 6061 and almost no copper, chromium or manganese. That leaner chemistry gives it a lower flow stress in the annealed state, so it fills complex dies with less force, while still responding to ageing after forming.

Impact extrusion and cold forging turn a slug into a finished part in a fraction of a second. With 6063 the process can produce thin fins, deep cups and fine detail that would overload tooling in 6061, and the finished part can be aged to T5 or T6 for useful stiffness and hardness. Because 6063 is also one of the most thermally conductive heat-treatable alloys, it is widely used for forged pin-fin and plate-fin heat sinks, LED lamp bodies and electronic cooling parts.

The same low alloy content gives 6063 an even, bright surface after anodising, so it suits visible consumer parts such as torch bodies, pen and cosmetic cases and appliance trims. Where a part has to carry heavy loads or pressure, 6061 is the better slug; where the part is a simple can or tube, 1070 or 3003 slugs cost less and run faster.

Where 6063 slugs make sense

  • Complex shapes with thin walls or fins that need easy metal flow
  • Heat sinks and thermal parts where conductivity matters
  • Anodised, visible parts that need a uniform finish
  • Parts that need more strength than 1xxx or 3xxx, but not full 6061 strength

Slug forms

Slug shapes and cutting routes

Sawn round slugs

Cut from extruded 6063 rod; the usual route for thicker slugs and heat-sink blanks, giving a consistent structure and accurate weight.

Punched slugs

Blanked from plate or thick strip; economical for thinner slugs in long runs, followed by tumbling to remove burrs.

Holed slugs

With a punched or drilled centre for forward-extruded tubes and sleeves.

Square and rectangular slugs

For non-round housings, battery and electronic cases.

Preformed slugs

Upset, chamfered or domed blanks that start metal flow evenly in the die.

Discs for spinning and drawing

Thin 6063 circles for spun reflectors, lamp shades and decorative covers.

Preparation

Annealing, surface treatment and lubrication

Slug preparation is what lets 6063 flow into fine fins without tearing. A full anneal (typically about 415 °C followed by slow cooling) brings the slug to the O temper and evens out the grain. Coarse grain in a slug shows up later as an orange-peel surface on the part, so annealing cycles and the structure of the source bar both matter.

After annealing, slugs are tumbled or shot-blasted to remove burrs and give a matt surface that holds lubricant, then coated with a dry lubricant such as zinc stearate. Whether lubrication is done by the supplier or at the press is agreed per order. Prepared slugs are packed dry, because moisture or contamination degrades the lubricant film.

FAQ

6063 aluminium (aluminum) slugs: frequently asked questions

Is 6063 good for cold-forged heat sinks?

Yes. It flows well in the annealed state, so thin fins and pins form cleanly, and its thermal conductivity of roughly 200 – 218 W/m·K (depending on temper) is high for a heat-treatable alloy. Pure 1050 or 1070 conducts a little better but gives much softer parts.

What temper are 6063 slugs supplied in?

Normally O (fully annealed), which gives the lowest flow stress and a uniform grain. The finished part is then aged, or solution treated and aged, to T5 or T6.

How strong is a 6063 impact after heat treatment?

Typical T6 values are about 241 MPa tensile and 214 MPa yield strength, with around 73 HB hardness. T5 parts are lower, at roughly 186 MPa tensile.

Why does surface grain matter on slugs?

Coarse grain in the slug can show as orange peel on formed or anodised surfaces. Controlled annealing and fine-grained source bar keep the part surface smooth.

Can you supply lubricated slugs?

Yes, slugs can be supplied tumbled or shot-blasted and coated with a dry lubricant such as zinc stearate, or bare for lubrication at your press, by agreement.

Do you ship 6063 slugs internationally?

Yes. Metline exports slugs worldwide from Mumbai with mill certificates and sealed, moisture-protected packing.
