1050 Aluminium Slugs: Punched or Sawn

1050 aluminium slugs are discs or blanks of 99.5 % pure aluminium, soft-annealed so they flow readily under the punch in impact extrusion. They are the economical everyday choice for collapsible tubes, aerosol and bottle bodies, capacitor cans and small housings. Metline supplies 1050 and 1050A slugs, punched from strip or sawn from rod, to your size, shape and surface specification, worldwide.

Overview

Why 1050 is used for slugs

Impact extrusion turns a slug into a hollow part in one stroke: the punch strikes the slug in a die and the metal flows up around it (backward extrusion), out through an opening (forward extrusion), or both. Only very ductile, low-strength metal can make that flow without cracking, which is why commercially pure aluminium dominates.

1050 contains at least 99.5 % aluminium, with iron and silicon as the main impurities. Annealed, it combines low flow stress, high elongation and a low work-hardening rate, so it extrudes thin, tall walls with modest press force. It costs less than higher-purity 1070 and is a little stronger, which helps where the part must resist denting.

EN AW-1050A, the European variant, has nearly identical limits and is the usual European designation for these slugs. 1050 also has good corrosion resistance, high thermal and electrical conductivity, and takes lacquering, printing and anodising well.

Production & surface

How 1050 slugs are made and finished

RouteHow it worksBest for
Punched from stripCast or rolled strip is cold rolled to thickness, slugs are blanked on high-speed presses, then annealed and finishedThin to medium slugs in high volume: aerosol, tube and capacitor slugs
Sawn from rod or barCast or extruded rod is sawn to length, then annealed and finishedThick slugs, large diameters and short runs; low scrap

After cutting, slugs are annealed to a uniform soft condition, then tumbled or blasted. The roughened surface removes burrs and oxide and holds the press lubricant (commonly zinc stearate) evenly, which reduces friction and galling in the die. Dimensions, mass and hardness are sampled throughout production.

Applications

Applications of 1050 slugs

  • Collapsible tubes: toothpaste, pharmaceutical ointments, cosmetics, adhesives and food pastes
  • Aerosol and bottle bodies: personal care, household and technical sprays
  • Electrical: electrolytic capacitor cans and small battery cases
  • Industrial: cartridge and filter housings, shielding cans, small cylinders and caps

Choose 1070 for the deepest draws and thinnest walls, and 3003 when the finished part needs more strength.

FAQ

1050 aluminium (aluminum) slugs: FAQs

What is the difference between 1050 and 1050A slugs?

Both contain at least 99.5 % aluminium. 1050A is the European (EN 573-3) designation with slightly different limits on zinc and titanium; for impact extrusion they behave the same.

Why are 1050 slugs annealed?

Annealing makes the metal soft and uniform so it flows into thin walls without cracking, keeps press loads down and gives consistent wall thickness from slug to slug.

Should I choose 1050 or 1070 slugs?

1070 (99.7 % Al) is softer and more ductile, suited to the thinnest walls and deepest parts. 1050 is slightly stronger and lower in cost, and suits most tubes, cans and housings.

Are slugs supplied with lubricant?

Slugs are normally supplied tumbled or blasted and clean, ready for your lubrication step. Pre-lubricated slugs can be discussed on request.

How is slug size chosen?

From the volume of the finished part plus the trim allowance: slug mass = part mass + trim. Diameter matches the die cavity less a small clearance.
