3003 Cold Finished Aluminium Bar and Rod

3003 is an aluminium-manganese alloy that is strengthened only by cold work, not heat treatment. As bar and rod it offers about 20 % more strength than 1100 with the same easy forming, welding and brazing and very good corrosion resistance. Metline supplies 3003 round, square, hex and flat bar in annealed and strain-hardened tempers to ASTM B211, ASTM B221 and EN 754 / 755, shipped worldwide.

Overview

What is 3003 aluminium bar?

3003 is the most widely used aluminium-manganese alloy. About 1.2 % manganese, plus a small copper addition, raises its strength above commercially pure aluminium while keeping almost all of its ductility, corrosion resistance and weldability.

Because 3003 cannot be precipitation hardened, the strength of a bar depends entirely on how much cold work it received during drawing. Annealed (O) bar is soft and bends easily; half-hard H14 and full-hard H18 bar are stronger but less ductile. Choosing the right temper is therefore the main decision when buying 3003 rod.

Cold-finished bar is drawn through dies after extrusion, giving tighter diameter tolerances, a smoother surface and higher strength than as-extruded H112 bar. Typical uses include rivets and cold-headed parts, spun and formed components, heat-exchanger and HVAC fittings, brazed assemblies, chemical and food equipment parts, and electrical or decorative items where a soft, corrosion-resistant metal is wanted.

Why choose 3003 bar

  • Higher strength than 1100 with similar formability
  • Excellent weldability and brazeability; widely used in brazed heat-exchanger assemblies
  • Very good resistance to atmospheric, fresh-water and many chemical environments
  • Food-contact suitable and easy to anodise for protection

Tempers

3003 tempers explained

O

Fully annealed. Maximum ductility for bending, heading, spinning and flaring. Lowest strength.

H12 / H14

Quarter- and half-hard by cold drawing. H14 is the most common cold-finished bar temper: a balance of strength and formability.

H16 / H18

Three-quarter and full hard. Highest strength and the best machining finish, but limited bending.

H112

As extruded with a small amount of incidental work. Economical for larger sections where tight tolerances are not needed.

F

As fabricated, no property guarantees. Used as feedstock for further working.

Restoring ductility

Hard tempers can be annealed back to O at roughly 345 – 415 °C. Strength cannot be regained by heat treatment, only by more cold work.

Working with 3003

Forming, welding, brazing and machining

Forming and heading

Excellent cold formability in O and H12. Bend radii can be tight; full-hard H18 needs larger radii or annealing first.

Welding

Readily welded by TIG, MIG and resistance methods. 1100 or 4043 filler is common; the weld zone softens to near-O properties.

Brazing and soldering

One of the most brazeable alloys and a standard core alloy for brazed heat-exchanger components.

Machining

Soft and gummy in O; machine in H14 – H18 with sharp, polished, high-rake tools, high speeds and plenty of coolant to avoid built-up edge.

Anodising

Takes a protective anodic film well; the manganese gives a slightly grey tone, so 6063 or 5005 are preferred for bright decorative finishes.

Corrosion

Very good in air, fresh water and many chemicals. Use 5052 or 5083 where sea-water exposure is constant.

Quality

Tolerances and inspection

Cold-finished 3003 rod is held to the tighter diameter and straightness tolerances of ASTM B211 or EN 754-3; extruded bar follows ASTM B221 or EN 755-3. Ask for a specific tolerance class (for example h9 or h11) if your part relies on it.

  • Chemical analysis for every cast
  • Tensile testing to confirm temper, with results on the EN 10204 3.1 certificate
  • Diameter, ovality, straightness and length checks
  • Surface inspection for scratches, die lines and inclusions
  • Lot traceability marked on each bundle

Applications

Where 3003 bar and rod are used

  • HVAC and heat transfer: fittings, headers and brazed components
  • Fasteners: rivets and cold-headed parts that must stay ductile
  • Chemical and food: equipment parts, handles and fittings in contact with food or mild chemicals
  • Electrical: connectors, spacers and low-load conductors
  • Architecture and decoration: rails, trims and turned decorative pieces
  • General engineering: spun parts, pins and lightly loaded machined parts

FAQ

3003 aluminium (aluminum) bar: frequently asked questions

Can 3003 aluminium be heat treated to increase strength?

No. 3003 is non-heat-treatable; strength comes only from cold work. Heating will anneal it and reduce strength.

Which temper of 3003 rod should I order?

Order O for heavy bending or heading, H14 for a general balance of strength and formability, and H18 for the highest strength and best machined finish.

Is 3003 stronger than 1100?

Yes, by roughly 20 % in the same temper, thanks to its manganese content, with similar formability and corrosion resistance.

Is 3003 good for marine use?

It performs well in marine atmospheres, but for constant sea-water immersion 5052 or 5083 are better choices.

Can 3003 bar be welded and brazed?

Yes. It welds easily by TIG and MIG and is one of the most brazeable aluminium alloys.

What does a 25 mm 3003 round bar weigh?

About 1.34 kg per metre at a density of 2.73 g/cm³.
