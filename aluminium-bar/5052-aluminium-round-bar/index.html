5052 Aluminium Round Bar

5052 is an aluminium-magnesium alloy with about 2.5 % magnesium, the strongest of the common non-heat-treatable alloys used for general fabrication. As round bar and rod it combines good strength, high fatigue resistance, excellent weldability and very good resistance to sea water and marine air. Metline supplies 5052 round, hex and square bar, cold finished or extruded, to ASTM B211, ASTM B221 and EN 754 / 755, shipped worldwide.

Overview

What is 5052 aluminium round bar?

5052 sits in the middle of the 5xxx family: more magnesium than 5005 or 5050, less than 5754 or 5083. That balance makes it strong enough for many structural and machined parts while remaining easy to form, weld and anodise.

Magnesium strengthens the alloy in solid solution, and a small chromium addition controls grain structure and improves resistance to stress corrosion. Because 5052 is non-heat-treatable, its strength comes from cold work. Annealed (O) bar is soft and very ductile; H32 and H34 bar, drawn and then stabilised, are significantly stronger. With magnesium below 3 %, 5052 is not prone to the sensitisation that can affect higher-magnesium alloys after long exposure to heat.

As round bar, 5052 is chosen for parts that face moisture, salt or chemicals: marine fittings and hardware, fuel and hydraulic system components, fasteners and rivets, pressure-vessel parts, food-processing equipment and electronic chassis parts. Its fatigue strength is among the highest of the non-heat-treatable alloys, which suits parts subject to vibration.

Why choose 5052 bar

  • Highest strength of the commonly stocked non-heat-treatable alloys below 5083
  • Very good resistance to sea water, marine atmosphere and many chemicals
  • High fatigue strength for vibrating parts and fasteners
  • Readily welded with 5356 or 5554 filler; welds retain good strength
  • Anodises with a clear, protective film

Tempers

5052 tempers explained

O

Fully annealed. Best for heavy bending, heading and flaring; lowest strength.

H32

Strain hardened and stabilised to quarter hard. The most popular temper: good strength with useful formability.

H34

Strain hardened and stabilised to half hard. Higher strength for machined parts and fasteners.

H36 / H38

Three-quarter and full hard. Highest strength, limited bending; used for rod and wire where strength matters most.

H112

As extruded, with incidental work. Economical for larger bars where tight tolerances are not needed.

Why "stabilised"

The H3x tempers receive a low-temperature heat treatment after cold work so that Al-Mg alloys do not soften slowly at room temperature (age softening).

Working with 5052

Machining, welding, forming and finishing

Machining

Fair in O, good in H32 – H38. Use sharp, polished, high-rake carbide tools, high speeds and plenty of coolant; harder tempers give shorter chips and better finish.

Welding

Excellent by TIG and MIG with 5356 or 5554 filler (5554 for service above about 65 °C). The weld zone returns to near-O strength.

Forming

Good cold formability in O and H32; bend radii should increase with temper.

Brazing

Not recommended: the magnesium content interferes with fluxes. Use 3003 for brazed assemblies.

Anodising

Good response with a clear, protective film; suitable for marine hardware.

Corrosion

Very good in sea water, marine atmosphere and many industrial environments. Avoid direct contact with copper alloys or carbon steel in wet conditions.

Quality

Tolerances and inspection

Cold-finished 5052 rod follows the tolerances of ASTM B211 or EN 754-3; extruded bar follows ASTM B221 or EN 755-3. State a tolerance class if your design needs closer control.

  • Spectrographic chemical analysis per cast
  • Tensile testing per lot to confirm temper
  • Diameter, ovality, straightness and length checks
  • Surface inspection for scratches, die lines and pick-up
  • Heat and lot traceability on bundles, with an EN 10204 3.1 certificate

Use 5052 for welded or corrosion-exposed parts with moderate loads. Step up to 5083 for heavily loaded marine structures, or to 6061 when machinability and yield strength matter more than sea-water performance. Choose 3003 where brazing is needed.

Applications

Where 5052 round bar is used

  • Marine: deck fittings, cleats, hinges, shafts and hardware
  • Fluid systems: hydraulic and fuel line fittings, valve parts and manifolds
  • Fasteners: rivets, screws and pins that need corrosion resistance
  • Transport: tank and trailer fittings, truck and bus components
  • Food and chemical: equipment parts and fittings
  • Electronics: chassis parts, standoffs and spacers

FAQ

5052 aluminium (aluminum) round bar: frequently asked questions

Is 5052 aluminium marine grade?

Yes. With about 2.5 % magnesium and low copper, it resists sea water and salt air very well and is widely used for marine fittings and hardware.

Can 5052 be heat treated?

No. It is non-heat-treatable; strength comes from cold work. H3x tempers are stabilised with a low-temperature treatment to prevent age softening.

Which is stronger, 5052 or 6061?

6061-T6 is stronger, especially in yield (about 276 MPa against 195 MPa for 5052-H32). 5052 has better sea-water resistance and keeps more strength after welding.

What filler is used to weld 5052?

5356 is the usual choice; 5554 is preferred for parts that operate above about 65 °C. 4043 can be used but gives lower weld strength and poorer colour match after anodising.

Is 5052 good for machining?

It machines acceptably in H32 – H38 with sharp, high-rake tools and coolant. For heavy CNC machining, 6061 or 2011 machine more easily.

What does a 50 mm 5052 round bar weigh?

About 5.26 kg per metre at a density of 2.68 g/cm³.
