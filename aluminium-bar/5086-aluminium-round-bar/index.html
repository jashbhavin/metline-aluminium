5086 aluminium round bar is a weldable, seawater-resistant Al-Mg alloy with about 4 % magnesium: a little lower in strength than 5083, but easier to extrude and more forgiving in service. Overview

What is 5086 aluminium round bar?

5086 sits between 5052 and 5083 in the aluminium-magnesium family. Its 3.5 – 4.5 % magnesium gives clearly more strength than 5052 and corrosion resistance in seawater on a par with 5083, while the slightly lower magnesium makes it easier to extrude and draw into rod and less prone to sensitisation after long exposure to warm conditions.

Like every 5xxx alloy, 5086 is non-heat-treatable. Strength comes from magnesium dissolved in the aluminium and from cold work, so there is no T6 temper and, importantly, welding does not wipe out a heat-treated condition the way it does in 6061 or 6082. A welded 5086 joint keeps roughly the strength of annealed parent metal, which is why naval architects and pressure-equipment designers rely on it.

In bar form, 5086 is mostly used for parts that are machined and then welded into a 5xxx structure, or that must survive salt spray without a coating: marine shafts and pins, deck hardware, flange and fitting blanks, cryogenic supports and the brackets that join a welded aluminium hull or tank to its fittings.

Key reasons to choose 5086 bar

  • Excellent seawater and marine-atmosphere corrosion resistance, uncoated
  • Higher strength than 5052 and 5754, with good ductility in O and H111
  • Welds by MIG and TIG with little loss of strength in the heat-affected zone
  • Keeps strength and toughness at cryogenic temperatures
  • Non-magnetic, useful near compasses, sensors and minesweeping equipment

Tempers

5086 bar tempers explained

O

Fully annealed. Lowest strength, highest ductility. Order it for rod that will be bent, rolled, headed or swaged cold.

H111

Annealed and then given a small amount of strain, usually from straightening. Higher guaranteed yield than O in ASTM B221 and the common marine bar temper.

H112

As-extruded, with properties guaranteed by test. The economical choice for larger diameters and for parts that will be machined all over.

H32

Strain-hardened by drawing and stabilised. Gives about double the yield of O temper in small diameters; stabilising reduces age-softening at room temperature.

H12 / H22

Quarter-hard drawn tempers with or without partial anneal. Used for small-diameter rod where some strength and some formability are both needed.

F

As fabricated with no guaranteed properties. Acceptable only when the bar will be fully re-processed or properties do not matter.

For most buyers, extruded H112 or H111 bar is the right starting point. Drawn rod costs more per kilogram but can save machining time on small precision parts. Very large rounds are sometimes supplied as cast and machined bar; that is not the same as wrought bar, and the properties must be agreed in writing.

One thing to watch: 5xxx alloys with more than about 3 % magnesium can become sensitised if held for long periods at roughly 65 – 200 °C, making them vulnerable to intergranular corrosion. 5086 is less susceptible than 5083 or 5456, but the same rule of thumb applies: keep sustained service temperatures below about 65 °C and avoid long hot holds during fabrication.

Working with 5086

Machining, welding, forming and finishing

Machining

Soft tempers are gummy. Use sharp, polished carbide tools with high positive rake, high cutting speed and generous coolant, and use chip breakers on turning tools. Drawn H32 rod machines more cleanly than O.

Welding

MIG and TIG weld readily. Use ER5356 or ER5183 filler; ER5183 gives the higher joint strength. Do not use Al-Si fillers (4043, 4047) on 5086.

Forming

O and H111 bend, roll and head cold. Allow larger radii in H32. Hot working is possible but avoid long exposure in the sensitising range.

Cutting

Saw, waterjet and laser cutting all work; sawing is the usual way to cut bar to length.

Corrosion protection

Uncoated performance in seawater is excellent. Isolate from stainless steel and copper alloys with insulating washers or sleeves.

Anodising

Takes a protective anodic film; appearance is slightly greyer than 6xxx alloys, so it is rarely chosen for decorative anodising.

Applications

Applications of 5086 round bar

  • Marine and shipbuilding: shafts, pins, bollard and cleat bodies, rudder and hatch fittings, welded deck hardware
  • Offshore and oil and gas: platform brackets, walkway and handrail fittings, parts in salt-spray zones
  • Pressure and cryogenic equipment: flanges, nozzles and supports where low-temperature toughness is needed (confirm the governing code)
  • Defence and special vehicles: light, corrosion-resistant welded and machined parts
  • Food and general engineering: machine parts and frames that must resist washdown and cleaning chemicals

FAQ

5086 aluminium (aluminum) round bar: frequently asked questions

Is 5086 stronger than 5083?

No. 5083 has more magnesium and is roughly 10 – 15 % stronger in comparable tempers. 5086 is chosen when slightly lower strength is acceptable and easier extrusion, better formability or lower sensitisation risk is worth more.

Can 5086 round bar be welded?

Yes, by MIG or TIG with ER5356 or ER5183 filler. Because 5086 is not heat-treated, the weld zone keeps close to the strength of annealed parent metal, which makes it well suited to welded marine structures.

Can 5086 be heat treated to increase strength?

No. It is a non-heat-treatable alloy. Strength is increased by cold work (H32, H34 tempers) and reduced by annealing to O. Thermal treatments above about 65 °C for long periods should be avoided because of sensitisation.

What does a 1 inch 5086 round bar weigh?

About 0.906 lb per foot, or 1.35 kg per metre, using a density of 2.66 g/cm³. A 12 ft bar weighs roughly 10.9 lb.

Which temper of 5086 bar should I order for machined marine parts?

H112 or H111 extruded bar suits most machined parts. Choose drawn H32 for small-diameter pins and shafts needing more strength, and O when the rod will be bent or headed.

What standards apply to 5086 round bar?

ASTM B221 (and ASME SB-221) for extruded bar in North America; EN 755-2 for extruded and EN 754-2 for drawn bar in Europe; GB/T 3191 in China; JIS H4040 in Japan. Classification-society rules apply on top for ship work.
