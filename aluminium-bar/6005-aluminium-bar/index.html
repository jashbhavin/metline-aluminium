Overview

What are 6005 and 6005A aluminium bars?

6005 is an Aluminum Association alloy; 6005A is its European cousin registered under EN 573-3 as EN AW-6005A (AlSiMg(A)). Both are heat-treatable 6xxx alloys strengthened by Mg2Si precipitates. Their silicon content is a little higher than magnesium needs, which gives good strength with lower press pressure and faster extrusion than 6061 or 6082.

The practical result is a bar that reaches a T6 yield of about 225 MPa (specified minimum, EN 755-2) with a cleaner extruded surface than 6082 and better extrudability in complex shapes. That is why 6005A dominates rail-car body extrusions, bridge decks, scaffolding and many structural frames, and why bar in the same alloy is used where machined parts must match those structures.

For bar, 6005 and 6005A are less common than 6061 or 6082 and are usually ordered when a designer wants the same alloy as the surrounding extrusions, for weld compatibility and identical corrosion behaviour, or when T5 press-quenched bar is acceptable and saves a separate heat-treatment step.

Where 6005/6005A bar earns its place

  • Matches 6005A rail, bridge and building extrusions for consistent welding and finishing
  • T5 press quenching gives useful strength without separate solution treatment
  • Better extruded surface than 6082, good for anodised parts
  • Good general corrosion resistance and fatigue performance

Tempers

T5 or T6: which temper of 6005 bar?

T5

Cooled from the extrusion temperature (press quench) and then artificially aged. Lower cost, because there is no separate solution heat treatment. Suits smaller sections that cool fast enough at the press.

T6

Solution heat-treated (or effectively press-quenched for thin sections) and artificially aged to peak strength. The usual temper for structural and machined bar, and the one EN 755-2 gives bar minimums for.

T4

Solution treated and naturally aged. Softer and more formable; parts can be bent then aged to T6 afterwards.

T6511-type stress relief

Stretched after quenching to relieve residual stress, so bars stay straight during machining. Ask for it when the bar will be heavily machined.

Working with 6005

Machining, welding, forming and finishing

Machining

Good in T5 and T6, with short chips. Not as free-cutting as 2011 or 6262, but similar to 6061. Use sharp carbide and flood coolant.

Welding

MIG and TIG weld well with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. As with all 6xxx alloys, the heat-affected zone softens; Eurocode 9 gives reduced design strengths for welded 6005A.

Forming

Bend in T4 and age afterwards for tight radii. T6 accepts moderate bends.

Anodising

Gives a good, even anodised finish; better than 6082, slightly behind 6063 for decorative work.

Corrosion

Good in normal atmospheres. In marine exposure, anodise or paint, or choose a 5xxx alloy for immersed parts.

Temperature

Like other 6xxx alloys, prolonged exposure above about 150 °C over-ages and softens it.

Applications

Applications of 6005 and 6005A bar

  • Rail: brackets, connectors and machined fittings for 6005A car-body extrusions
  • Bridges and access structures: pins, connectors and parts for aluminium footbridges and gangways
  • Building: curtain-wall fixings, structural connectors and guardrail parts
  • Scaffolding and ladders: spigots, connectors and couplers
  • Electrical and solar: mounting parts and conductor supports
  • General engineering: frames, conveyors and machine parts where a medium-strength, easily extruded alloy suits

FAQ

6005 / 6005A aluminium (aluminum) bar: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 6005 and 6005A?

6005A allows more manganese, chromium and copper, with a required Mn + Cr band of 0.12 – 0.50 %. This refines the grain structure and gives slightly better toughness and strength in thicker sections. 6005 is the Aluminum Association grade used in North America; 6005A is the European grade.

Is 6005A stronger than 6061?

Close, but slightly lower in yield. EN 755-2 gives 6005A-T6 bar a minimum proof stress of 225 MPa, against about 240 MPa for 6061-T6 bar. 6005A extrudes more easily and gives a better surface.

What is the difference between T5 and T6 for 6005 bar?

T5 is cooled at the press and then aged; T6 is fully solution treated and aged. For thin sections the properties can be similar; for thicker bar T6 is more reliable and is the temper EN 755-2 specifies for bar.

Can 6005A bar be welded?

Yes, with MIG or TIG using ER4043 or ER5356 filler. The heat-affected zone loses part of its T6 strength, so welded designs use reduced allowable stresses as set out in Eurocode 9 or the governing code.

How much does a 50 mm 6005A round bar weigh?

About 5.30 kg per metre at 2.70 g/cm³, so a 3 m bar weighs about 15.9 kg.
