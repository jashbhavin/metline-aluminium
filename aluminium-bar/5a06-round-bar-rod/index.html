5A06 Aluminium Bar & Rod

5A06 (formerly LF6) is a Chinese-standard aluminium-magnesium alloy with about 6 % magnesium, the strongest of the common non-heat-treatable Al-Mg grades and a direct counterpart of the Russian AMg6. Metline supplies 5A06 round, square and hex bar in O and H112 tempers to GB/T 3191, cut and certified, for welded tanks, marine parts and defence and aerospace structures worldwide.

Overview

What is 5A06 aluminium bar?

5A06 is registered in the Chinese standard GB/T 3190, where the "A" marks it as a national variant rather than an Aluminum Association alloy. Its old Chinese name was LF6 ("rust-proof aluminium No. 6"). With 5.8 – 6.8 % magnesium, 0.5 – 0.8 % manganese and small additions of titanium and beryllium, it has essentially the same chemistry as the Soviet and Russian alloy AMg6 (1560).

All that magnesium makes 5A06 noticeably stronger than 5083 in the annealed condition while keeping the Al-Mg family's two big advantages: good resistance to seawater and the ability to be welded without losing a heat-treated temper, because there is none to lose. It has been used for decades in welded fuel and oxidiser tanks, ship structures, armoured and military vehicles, and aircraft and launch-vehicle parts in countries that work to GB or GOST standards.

The trade-off is that very high magnesium makes 5A06 more sensitive to heat exposure. Held for long periods above about 65 °C, it can become sensitised and prone to intergranular and stress corrosion, more readily than 5083. Designs that use it keep service temperatures low and control fabrication heat input.

Why engineers specify 5A06

  • Highest annealed strength of the common weldable Al-Mg alloys
  • Weld joints keep most of the parent-metal strength
  • Good corrosion resistance in marine and industrial atmospheres
  • Interchangeable with AMg6 for projects designed to GOST standards

Some supplier sheets in circulation list 5A06 with 4.5 – 5.5 % Mg. That range belongs to alloys such as 5456 or 5056, not 5A06. The GB/T 3190 limit is 5.8 – 6.8 % Mg; check that figure on any certificate you receive.

5A06 bar forms and tempers

As-extruded with guaranteed properties. The standard supply condition for round, square and hex bar that will be machined.

O annealed bar

Fully annealed for cold bending, heading and upsetting. Same minimum strength as H112 in GB/T 3191, but more consistent ductility.

Because there is no exact ASTM or EN equivalent, do not substitute 5083 or 5456 for 5A06 on a drawing without the designer's approval: strength, weld procedure and corrosion behaviour all differ. If your project is designed to ASTM or EN rules, it is usually better to stay with 5083 bar.

Working with 5A06

Machining, welding and corrosion

Machining

Machines reasonably in H112 and poorly in O, where it is soft and gummy. Use sharp polished carbide, positive rake, high speed and plenty of coolant. It polishes well.

Welding

TIG and MIG welding work well with a matching high-Mg filler (for example 5B06, AMg6-type wire, or ER5183/ER5556 where the design allows). Resistance welding is possible but more prone to hot cracking.

Sensitisation

Keep sustained service below about 65 °C. Avoid long preheats and slow cooling after welding; both can sensitise the alloy.

Corrosion

Good in seawater and marine air when not sensitised. Isolate from copper alloys and stainless steel in wet service.

Forming

Cold forms in O temper; work-hardens quickly, so allow intermediate anneals for heavy forming.

Finishing

Can be anodised for protection; the film is greyer than on 6xxx alloys.

Applications

Where 5A06 bar and rod are used

  • Welded tanks and vessels: fittings, flanges and bosses for fuel and liquid containers
  • Marine: structural fittings and machined parts for hulls and superstructures built to GB rules
  • Defence: welded and machined parts for vehicles and equipment designed to GB or GOST standards
  • Aerospace: secondary structure, brackets and tank parts where AMg6-type alloys are specified
  • Fasteners: rivets and pins cut from drawn rod and wire

FAQ

5A06 aluminium (aluminum) bar: frequently asked questions

What is the equivalent of 5A06 aluminium?

The Russian AMg6 (1560) has practically the same composition. There is no exact Aluminum Association or EN equivalent; 5456 and 5056 are the closest but contain less magnesium.

Is 5A06 the same as 5083?

No. 5A06 contains 5.8 – 6.8 % Mg against 4.0 – 4.9 % in 5083, so it is stronger when annealed (about 315 MPa minimum against 270 MPa) but more sensitive to sensitisation at warm service temperatures.

Can 5A06 be heat treated?

No. It is a non-heat-treatable Al-Mg alloy supplied O or H112. Strength can be raised only by cold work, and long exposure above about 65 °C should be avoided.

Which standard covers 5A06 round bar?

GB/T 3191 for extruded bar and rod, with composition in GB/T 3190. Projects designed to Russian standards use AMg6 bar to GOST 21488.

What filler is used to weld 5A06?

A matching high-magnesium filler such as 5B06 or AMg6-type wire is normal. ER5183 or ER5556 may be acceptable if the design permits slightly lower weld strength; never use Al-Si fillers.
