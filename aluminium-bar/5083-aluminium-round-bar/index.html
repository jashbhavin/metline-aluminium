Overview

What is 5083 aluminium round bar?

5083 is an aluminium-magnesium-manganese alloy with about 4.5 % magnesium. It cannot be hardened by heat treatment; its strength comes from magnesium in solid solution and, in some tempers, from cold work. That has one big practical consequence: welding does not destroy its strength the way it does with 6061 or 6082.

In round bar form, 5083 is usually extruded and supplied in the O, H111 or H112 temper. Annealed (O and H111) bar has a minimum tensile strength of around 270 – 275 MPa, which is higher than any other common 5xxx bar except 5456, and it keeps good elongation, so it machines into fittings and welds into frames without cracking.

Buyers choose 5083 rod for components that live in or near seawater: shafts and pins for deck machinery, bollard and cleat bodies, stanchions, flange blanks, pump parts, mooring hardware and welded brackets that tie into a 5083 hull. It is also used in cryogenic plant, because like other Al-Mg alloys it gets slightly stronger and stays tough at very low temperatures.

Why specify 5083 rod

  • Highest strength of the common weldable Al-Mg marine alloys in the annealed condition
  • Excellent resistance to seawater and industrial atmospheres without coating
  • Welded joints keep close to the strength of annealed parent metal
  • Matches 5083 plate and pipe, so a whole marine assembly can be one alloy

Tempers

Which 5083 temper to order for bar

5083-H112

As-extruded, with no deliberate cold work or anneal beyond what the process gives. The most economical bar temper and the usual choice for large diameters that will be machined.

5083-H111

Annealed, then lightly strain-hardened by straightening. Gives the highest guaranteed yield of the standard bar tempers and is common in marine specifications.

5083-O

Fully annealed for maximum ductility. Choose it for rod that will be cold headed, bent, swaged or spun.

Strain-hardened (H32 type)

Cold-drawn rod in harder tempers can be produced for small diameters where extra strength is needed; it is a special order and the properties must be agreed in advance.

5083 is non-heat-treatable, so there is no T6 temper. If a drawing calls for "5083-T6", it is an error; check whether the designer meant 6082-T6 or 5083-H111.

Sensitisation is the quality issue that matters most for 5083. If the alloy spends long periods between roughly 65 °C and 200 °C, magnesium-rich particles form along grain boundaries and the metal can become prone to intergranular corrosion and stress-corrosion cracking. Plate standards for marine use (ASTM B928) include tests for this; for bar, keep the service temperature below 65 °C and avoid long hot holds during fabrication.

Working with 5083

Machining, welding and finishing 5083 rod

Machining

Softer tempers are gummy and tend to form long, stringy chips. Use sharp, polished carbide or HSS tools with high positive rake, high speeds and plenty of coolant; chip breakers help on lathes. H111 and H112 cut more cleanly than O.

Welding

MIG and TIG weld very well. ER5183 filler matches the strength of the parent metal and is the normal choice; ER5356 is acceptable for lower-strength joints. Never use 4xxx (Al-Si) fillers on high-magnesium alloys.

Forming

O temper bends and heads well. Allow generous radii in H111 and H112 and avoid hot forming in the sensitising range.

Corrosion

Excellent in seawater and marine atmosphere, uncoated. Isolate from copper, brass and stainless steel fasteners in wet service to prevent galvanic attack.

Anodising

Takes a protective anodic film, but the high magnesium gives a slightly duller, greyer finish than 6xxx alloys.

Low temperature

Strength and toughness rise at cryogenic temperatures, which is why 5083 is used in LNG and liquid-gas equipment.

Applications

Where 5083 round bar is used

  • Shipbuilding: deck fittings, bitts, cleats, stanchion bases, hatch hinges and pins welded to 5083 hull plate
  • Offshore and coastal: gangway and walkway hardware, handrail posts, pontoon connectors and mooring components
  • Pumps and valves: bodies, flanges and shafts for seawater and brine service
  • Cryogenics: supports, flanges and fittings for LNG and industrial gas equipment
  • Transport: tanker and road-vehicle parts that are welded to 5xxx sheet
  • Defence: welded fittings for light armoured and naval structures where specified

FAQ

5083 aluminium (aluminum) round bar: frequently asked questions

Can 5083 round bar be heat treated to T6?

No. 5083 is a non-heat-treatable alloy. It is strengthened by magnesium in solid solution and by cold work, and is supplied as O, H111 or H112 bar. If you need T6-type strength in a machined part, look at 6082-T6 or 6061-T6.

Is 5083 bar good for seawater immersion?

Yes. It is one of the most seawater-resistant aluminium alloys and is used uncoated for marine fittings. Keep the service temperature below about 65 °C and isolate it from stainless steel or copper alloys to avoid galvanic corrosion.

Which filler wire should be used to weld 5083 rod?

ER5183 is the standard choice and gives joint strength close to annealed 5083. ER5356 can be used where design stresses are lower. Avoid Al-Si fillers such as ER4043 on 5083.

How much does a 50 mm 5083 round bar weigh?

About 5.22 kg per metre, so a 3 m bar weighs roughly 15.7 kg. The weight per metre is 0.002089 × d² kg with d in millimetres.

What is the difference between 5083-H111 and H112 bar?

H111 is annealed and then slightly strain-hardened by straightening, which gives a guaranteed yield of about 165 MPa under ASTM B221. H112 is as-extruded with a lower guaranteed yield of about 110 MPa, though actual values are often higher.
