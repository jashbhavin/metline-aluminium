2014 / 2014A Aluminium Bar

2014 aluminium bar is a heat-treatable aluminium-copper alloy that reaches about 480 MPa tensile strength in T6, close to the 7xxx aerospace grades and well above 6061. It machines well and is widely used for highly stressed fittings, hydraulic parts and forging stock. Metline supplies 2014 and its European variant 2014A as round, square, hex and flat bar in T4, T6 and stress-relieved tempers, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is 2014 aluminium bar?

2014 is one of the oldest high-strength aluminium alloys, developed from the original duralumin family. It contains around 4.4 % copper plus silicon, manganese and magnesium, and after solution treatment and artificial ageing it is among the strongest 2xxx alloys in common use.

The relatively high silicon content distinguishes 2014 from 2024. Silicon helps the alloy respond strongly to artificial ageing, so 2014 is normally used in the T6 family of tempers, whereas 2024 is mostly used naturally aged (T3/T4). The result is a bar with high yield strength and hardness, good machinability and good hot workability, which is why 2014 is also a favourite alloy for closed-die forgings.

The price of that strength is reduced corrosion resistance and limited weldability. 2014 bar is used where parts are machined, bolted or riveted together and protected by anodising, chromate-free conversion coatings or paint. In thick sections it can be sensitive to stress-corrosion cracking across the grain, so orientation of the bar relative to the load matters in design.

Why engineers choose 2014 bar

  • High strength and hardness in T6 / T651 at moderate cost
  • Good machinability with short, manageable chips in the aged tempers
  • Excellent forgeability: the same alloy can be bought as bar or forging
  • Long track record in aircraft, defence and heavy vehicle components

Tempers

Which 2014 temper should you order?

2014-T6

Solution heat-treated and artificially aged. Highest strength; standard for rolled and cold-finished bar under ASTM B211 and AMS 4121.

2014-T651

T6 plus stretching to relieve quench stresses. Preferred for larger rolled bar that will be heavily machined, because it moves less when material is removed.

2014-T6510 / T6511

Stress-relieved tempers for extruded bar and shapes; T6511 allows minor straightening after stretching.

2014-T4 / T451

Naturally aged. Lower yield but higher elongation and better fracture toughness; can be aged to T6 after forming.

2014-O

Annealed for maximum formability and as forging or upsetting stock before full heat treatment.

2014-F

As-fabricated, mainly used as forging stock and re-draw bar.

Working with 2014

Machining, welding, forging and corrosion protection

Machining

Machines well in T6 / T651: chips are short and finish is good. Use carbide tools with positive rake and flood coolant. T651 and T6511 keep distortion down on heavily machined parts.

Welding

Poor for arc welding: hot cracking and large strength loss in the heat-affected zone. Resistance and friction-stir welding are possible. Design for mechanical joints.

Forging

Excellent hot workability; a common forging alloy for wheels, fittings and pistons. Forge in the 420 – 460 °C range, then solution treat and age.

Heat treatment

Solution treat at around 500 °C, quench rapidly, then age at around 160 – 175 °C for T6. Follow AMS 2770 or your specification for exact cycles.

Corrosion

Lower resistance than 5xxx or 6xxx alloys. Anodise, conversion-coat or paint. Avoid sustained short-transverse tensile stress in thick parts.

Temperature

Retains strength reasonably to about 150 °C; prolonged exposure above that over-ages the alloy.

Applications

Where 2014 bar is used

  • Aerospace: machined fittings, brackets, landing-gear and actuator parts, and forging stock
  • Defence: ordnance components and missile parts that need high strength and machinability
  • Automotive and truck: heavy-duty frames, suspension parts, wheels and hydraulic pistons
  • Hydraulics and pneumatics: valve bodies, cylinder ends and high-pressure fittings
  • Machinery: high-stress gears, shafts, couplings and tooling components
  • Sport and leisure: high-load climbing hardware and bicycle components

FAQ

2014 aluminium (aluminum) bar: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 2014 and 2014A?

2014A is the European variant in EN 573-3. It has tighter limits on silicon (0.50 – 0.9 %) and iron (0.50 % max) and a nickel limit, which improves toughness slightly. Properties are very close, so the grades are often dual-certified, but aerospace drawings specify one explicitly.

Is 2014 stronger than 6061?

Yes. 2014-T6 typically reaches about 483 MPa tensile and 414 MPa yield, compared with about 310 and 276 MPa for 6061-T6. 6061 is better for welding and corrosion resistance.

Can 2014 aluminium bar be welded?

Arc welding is not recommended because of hot cracking and loss of strength. Friction-stir and resistance welding can be used in some designs, but mechanical fastening is normal.

2014 or 2024: which bar should I choose?

2014-T6 has higher yield strength and hardness and is the better forging alloy. 2024-T351 has better fatigue resistance and fracture toughness, so it is preferred for cyclically loaded parts.

Does 2014 need corrosion protection?

Yes for most environments. Its copper content lowers corrosion resistance, so parts are usually anodised, conversion-coated or painted.

What certificate comes with 2014 bar?

An EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate showing chemistry, mechanical test results and heat traceability; AMS conformance and ultrasonic reports for aerospace orders.
