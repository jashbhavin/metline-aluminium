2A11 Aluminium Bar and Rod

2A11 is an aluminium-copper-magnesium-manganese duralumin, formerly designated LY11, used as bar and rod for medium-to-high strength machined parts, fasteners and fittings. In its usual naturally aged T4 temper it behaves much like 2017 and 2017A. Metline supplies 2A11 round, square and hex bar certified to GB/T 3190 and GB/T 3191, or dual-certified where an international equivalent is acceptable, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is 2A11 aluminium?

2A11 belongs to the original family of duralumin alloys: about 4 % copper with smaller amounts of magnesium and manganese, strengthened by solution treatment and natural ageing. Under the older naming system it was called LY11, which is still printed on many drawings and certificates.

The alloy fills the middle ground of the 2xxx bar range. It is stronger than 6061 and far stronger than the 5xxx alloys, but a little below 2A12 and 2024, which carry more magnesium. In exchange it is slightly more ductile in the freshly quenched state, which is why it has long been used for rivets, bolts and parts that are upset or formed before ageing.

As bar and rod, 2A11 is mainly bought for machining: turned shafts and pins, bushings, hydraulic and pneumatic fittings, instrument parts, brackets, jigs and moulds. It cuts cleanly with short chips in the T4 temper, holds thread form well and takes anodising for protection. Like all high-copper alloys, it has only fair corrosion resistance and should be protected when it will see moisture or salt.

Why buyers choose 2A11 bar

  • Good strength in T4 without artificial ageing, with useful toughness and fatigue resistance
  • Clean machining with short chips; good thread and surface finish
  • Can be re-solution treated and aged after forming or upsetting
  • Directly interchangeable with 2017 / 2017A in many machined applications after engineering review

Tempers

2A11 tempers and heat treatment

T4

Solution treated at roughly 495 – 505 °C, water quenched and naturally aged at room temperature for at least four days. This is the standard supply condition for machining bar.

O (annealed)

Fully softened for cold forming, heading or deep machining before re-heat treatment. Re-solution treat and age after forming to recover strength.

H112 / F

As extruded, with no guaranteed properties. Used as stock for forging or when the buyer heat treats the finished part.

Freshly quenched (W)

For about the first hour or two after quenching, 2A11 is soft enough to drive as a rivet or straighten. Cold storage delays natural ageing.

Artificial ageing

2A11 is normally used naturally aged. Artificial ageing raises yield strength slightly but reduces ductility and is not usual for bar.

Service temperature

Prolonged exposure above about 100 – 150 °C over-ages T4 material. For hot service, 2618 or 2219 are better choices.

These are nearest equivalents, not identical alloys. Copper limits differ slightly, so a drawing that specifies 2A11 should be supplied as 2A11 unless the design authority accepts 2017 or 2017A in writing. Where both are acceptable, dual certification is often possible because many lots fall inside both sets of limits.

2011 is sometimes quoted as an equivalent to 2A11. It is not: 2011 is a free-machining alloy with lead and bismuth and a different strength profile. Use 2017 / 2017A as the comparison.

Quality

Tolerances, coarse grain ring and inspection

Two quality points matter most on extruded 2A11 bar. The first is the coarse grain ring: a band of recrystallised, coarse grains near the surface that forms during extrusion and solution treatment. It lowers fatigue strength and can show as orange peel after anodising. Aerospace and fastener orders normally set a maximum ring depth, and machining allowance should remove it from critical surfaces. The second is straightness, which stretch straightening after quenching controls and which also relieves residual stress.

  • Diameter tolerances to GB/T 3191, EN 755-3 or ASTM B221 for extruded bar; cold-finished bar is held tighter to EN 754-3 or ASTM B211
  • Chemical analysis by spectrometer for each cast
  • Tensile testing and hardness to confirm temper
  • Coarse grain ring depth by macro-etching a cross-section, where specified
  • Ultrasonic testing for internal discontinuities on larger diameters or when the order calls for it
  • Visual and dimensional checks, with heat and lot traceability on every bundle
Working with 2A11

Machining, joining and finishing 2A11

Machining

Machines well in T4 with sharp carbide or HSS tools, positive rake and soluble-oil coolant. The O temper is gummy; machine it after heat treatment where possible.

Welding

Fusion welding is not recommended for load-bearing parts: high copper makes the alloy prone to hot cracking, and the heat-affected zone loses strength. Use mechanical joints or riveting.

Forming

Form in the O or freshly quenched condition, then age. T4 bar accepts only mild bending.

Anodising

Sulphuric anodising gives useful protection, though the film is darker and less clear than on 6xxx alloys. Chromic or boric-sulphuric anodising is used on aerospace parts.

Corrosion protection

Copper-rich alloys are vulnerable to pitting and intergranular attack. Anodise, prime or paint parts soon after machining when they will see moisture.

Cutting allowance

When ordering cut pieces, allow for the saw kerf and any facing stock on each end.

Choose 2A11 when you need more strength than 6061 in a machined part that will not be welded and the drawing calls for the GB alloy. Move up to 2A12 or 2024 for higher fatigue-critical loads, or to 7075 for maximum strength. Where weldability and corrosion resistance matter more than strength, 6061 is the practical choice.

Applications

Where 2A11 bar is used

  • Fasteners: bolts, screws and medium-strength rivets
  • Hydraulics and pneumatics: fittings, unions, valve bodies and pistons
  • Aerospace and defence: secondary structural fittings and brackets
  • Machinery: shafts, pins, spacers, bushings and gear blanks
  • Tooling: jigs, fixtures and light mould components
  • Instruments and transport: housings, frames and machined parts

FAQ

2A11 aluminium (aluminum) bar: frequently asked questions

Is 2A11 the same as LY11?

Yes. LY11 is the older designation; the alloy was renamed 2A11 when the standard adopted four-character designations. The chemistry is the same.

What is the international equivalent of 2A11?

The nearest equivalents are 2017 (AA / ASTM, UNS A92017), EN AW-2017A and JIS A2017. They are close but not identical, so confirm acceptance with your design authority before substituting.

Is 2A11 the same as 2011?

No. 2011 contains lead and bismuth for free machining. 2A11 has no free-machining additions and corresponds to 2017.

What temper is 2A11 bar usually supplied in?

T4, solution treated and naturally aged. O is supplied for forming and H112 for forging stock or where the buyer heat treats.

Can 2A11 be welded?

Fusion welding is not advised for structural parts because of hot cracking and strength loss. Riveting, bolting or adhesive bonding are used instead.

Does 2A11 need corrosion protection?

Usually yes. Like other copper-bearing alloys it has only fair corrosion resistance; anodising or painting is recommended for parts exposed to moisture.
