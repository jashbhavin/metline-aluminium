Overview

Why use 6061 hex bar?

Starting a part from hexagon stock means the six flats already exist: the lathe only has to face, drill, tap and part off. For hydraulic and pneumatic fittings, nuts, spacers, standoffs, valve bodies and electrical terminals, that saves a milling operation on every piece and is the reason hex bar is a staple of screw-machine and CNC lathe shops.

6061 is the general-purpose alloy for this work. In T6 it gives a typical yield of about 276 MPa, enough for threaded fittings under moderate load; it machines with short chips; it anodises well for colour-coding and corrosion protection; and, unlike free-machining 2011, it can be welded and used in damp or outdoor service.

There are two production routes. Cold-finished hex (T6) is drawn through a die after extrusion: closer across-flats tolerance, sharper corners and a bright surface, ideal when the flats are a finished surface on the part. Extruded hex (T6511) is stretched for stress relief: slightly more generous tolerance and corner radii, but available in larger sizes and more stable when the part is hollowed out.

When 6061 hex is the right choice

  • Fittings and fasteners that are anodised or exposed to weather
  • Parts that may be welded to a 6xxx assembly
  • Medium-strength threaded parts where 2011 corrosion resistance is not enough
  • Batches where removing a milling operation pays for the hex stock

Rule of thumb: if the flats will be seen or gripped by a spanner as delivered, choose cold-finished T6. If the part is bored out heavily or is larger than about 50 mm, extruded T6511 is more practical and usually cheaper.

Machining

Machining 6061 hex bar on lathes and screw machines

Tooling

Sharp, polished carbide inserts with high positive rake reduce built-up edge. HSS form tools work well on cam-type screw machines.

Chip control

6061-T6 produces short chips but not as short as 2011; use chip-breaking geometries and high-pressure coolant on deep drilling.

Threading

Cuts and rolls good threads. For repeated assembly, consider thread inserts in softer service.

Bar feeding

Choose the collet or guide-tube size from the across-corners dimension, not the across-flats size.

Finishing

Anodise for colour coding and wear resistance; use hard anodising on fittings exposed to abrasion.

Welding

Weldable with ER4043 or ER5356 filler where fittings are welded to tube or plate; the weld zone softens toward T4 strength.

Applications

Where 6061 hex bar is used

  • Fluid power: hydraulic and pneumatic fittings, adaptors, plugs and manifold connectors
  • Fasteners: nuts, bolts, threaded standoffs, spacers and pillars
  • Electrical: terminal blocks, connectors and earthing fittings
  • Automotive and motorsport: AN-style fittings, adjuster nuts and suspension parts (usually anodised)
  • Furniture and architecture: connectors and decorative fixings
  • Instruments: sensor housings, tripod and camera fittings

FAQ

6061 aluminium (aluminum) hex bar: frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the across-corners size of a hex bar?

Multiply the across-flats size by 1.1547. A 24 mm hex measures about 27.7 mm across sharp corners; a 1 in hex about 1.155 in. Real bars have slightly rounded corners, so the measured value is a little smaller.

What is the difference between 6061-T6 and T6511 hex bar?

T6 hex is usually cold-finished, with tighter tolerance and sharper corners. T6511 hex is extruded and stretched for stress relief, so it stays straighter when machined and is available in larger sizes. Strength is essentially the same.

How much does 6061 hex bar weigh?

Weight per metre is 0.002338 × s² kg with s in mm. A 24 mm hex weighs about 1.35 kg/m, and a 1 in hex about 1.01 lb per foot.

Is 6061 hex bar good for threaded fittings?

Yes. It threads cleanly and has good strength in T6. For high-volume dry-service parts, 2011 machines faster; for weather or wet service, 6061 is the better choice because of its corrosion resistance.

Can you cut hex bar to bar-feeder lengths?

Yes. Bars can be cut to the length your bar feeder needs, with ends deburred, and packed to protect the flats in transit.
