6061 square bar gives machinists four flat, square faces to clamp and reference, which makes it the quickest starting stock for milled blocks, brackets, keys and fixture parts. Overview

What is 6061 square bar used for?

Square bar is the natural stock for parts that are mostly prismatic: mounting blocks, clamp jaws, spacers, keys, manifold bodies and small brackets. Because the faces are already flat and roughly square to each other, a part can go straight into a vice without first being skimmed on all sides, which saves set-ups and material compared with sawing pieces from plate.

6061 is the most common alloy for this because it combines medium strength (typical yield about 276 MPa in T6) with clean machining, good dimensional stability in stress-relieved tempers, weldability and an excellent anodised finish. It is the "do-everything" choice when no other requirement pushes you to 2011, 7075 or a 5xxx marine alloy.

Square bar comes in two forms. Extruded square (usually T6511) is economical and available in large sizes; it has small corner radii and an extruded surface. Cold-finished square (T6 or T651) is drawn after extrusion for tighter size tolerance, sharper corners and a smoother surface, and is normally limited to smaller sizes.

Square bar or plate?

  • Up to about 100 mm square, bar is usually cheaper per part than sawing from plate
  • Bar comes with straightness control along its length; plate offsets are cut-to-size
  • For wide, thin parts, plate or flat bar wastes less metal
  • For very large blocks, consider 6061-T651 plate or forged block instead

If a face of the bar stays unmachined on the finished part, cold-finished bar avoids a skim cut. If every face is machined anyway, extruded T6511 is usually the better buy. Tolerances are given in ANSI H35.2 for ASTM orders, and EN 755-4 (extruded) or EN 754-4 (drawn) for square bar under EN orders.

Machining

Milling and machining 6061 square bar

Workholding

Square bar clamps directly in a vice. For precision parts, skim one face and one edge first to create true datums, as extruded faces may have slight twist.

Milling

Use 2- or 3-flute carbide end mills with polished flutes, high spindle speed and good chip evacuation. Air blast or flood coolant prevents chip recutting.

Distortion

T6511 or T651 keep parts flat when pockets are machined. Rough, release clamps and then finish when tolerances are tight.

Drilling and tapping

Drills and taps cut cleanly in T6; use form taps for strong threads in blind holes.

Welding

Weldable with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. Expect HAZ softening to roughly T4 strength.

Anodising

Machined faces anodise evenly. Edge-break the corners before anodising so the coating does not chip.

Applications

Applications of 6061 square bar

  • Jigs and fixtures: locators, clamp jaws, risers and tooling blocks
  • Automation: sensor mounts, gripper fingers, cam blocks and end stops
  • Fluid power: small manifold and valve bodies drilled from solid
  • Frames: machine frames, guards and supports where solid section is needed
  • Power transmission: keys and keystock (cold-finished square)
  • Architecture and furniture: balusters, handles and decorative posts, often anodised

FAQ

6061 aluminium (aluminum) square bar: frequently asked questions

How much does 6061 square bar weigh?

Weight per metre is 0.0027 × a² kg with the side a in mm. A 25 mm square bar weighs about 1.69 kg/m, a 50 mm bar 6.75 kg/m and a 100 mm bar 27 kg/m. A 1 in square bar weighs about 1.17 lb/ft.

Does extruded 6061 square bar have sharp corners?

Not quite. Extruded bar has small corner radii from the die. Cold-finished square bar has sharper corners and tighter tolerance. If sharp corners matter, order cold-finished or allow for a skim cut.

Which temper of 6061 square bar is best for machining?

T6511 (extruded) or T651 (cold-finished), because they are stretched to relieve quench stresses and stay flat when pockets or slots are machined.

Is 6061 square bar strong enough for fixtures?

For most fixtures, yes. Its typical yield of about 276 MPa and good stiffness-to-weight ratio suit jigs and automation parts. For wear surfaces, hard anodise or add steel inserts.

Can you cut 6061 square bar into blocks?

Yes. Bar can be saw-cut into blocks to your sizes and packed to protect the faces in transit.
