Overview

What is 7075 aluminium rod?

7075 is a zinc-magnesium-copper alloy introduced in the 1940s for aircraft structures. Its very high strength comes from fine zinc-magnesium precipitates formed during artificial ageing. As rod, it is the standard material for highly loaded machined parts: aircraft fittings, gears and shafts, high-performance bicycle and motorsport components, moulds and precision tooling.

The price of that strength is lower corrosion resistance than 5xxx and 6xxx alloys, poor arc weldability and limited cold formability. 7075 is therefore almost always machined from bar and joined by fasteners rather than welded. For parts exposed to stress-corrosion risk, the over-aged T73 temper family trades about 10 – 15 % of strength for much better resistance to stress-corrosion cracking and exfoliation.

Rod is supplied in two main routes. Cold-finished (drawn or rolled) rod to ASTM B211 has tighter diameter tolerances and a bright surface; extruded rod to ASTM B221 covers larger diameters at lower cost. Large-diameter "rod" above roughly 200 mm is often forged or cut from thick plate.

Tempers

T6, T651, T73 or T7351?

T6

Solution treated and aged to peak strength. Normal for smaller drawn rod where residual stress is low.

T651

As T6, but stretched after quench to relieve stress. Preferred for larger rod that will be heavily machined, so parts stay straight.

T6511

Stretched and slightly straightened extruded rod. The extruded equivalent of T651.

T73 / T7351

Over-aged for high stress-corrosion resistance. Required in many aerospace fittings and anywhere sustained tensile stress meets moisture.

T76 / T7651

Intermediate over-age, mainly for exfoliation resistance. More common on extrusions and plate than on rod.

O

Annealed for forming or cold heading; the part is then heat-treated to T6 or T73.

Fabrication

Machining, joining and finishing 7075 rod

Machining

Excellent in T6 and T651: short chips, fine finish and good dimensional stability. Use carbide tools, high speeds and coolant. Rough-machine, then finish after any stress-relief if tight tolerances are needed.

Welding

Not recommended for arc welding; the weld and heat-affected zone are prone to hot cracking and lose strength. Join by fasteners, adhesive bonding or friction stir welding.

Forming

Poor cold formability in T6. Form in O or freshly quenched W condition, then age.

Anodising

Takes clear and hard (Type III) anodic coatings well; hard anodising is common on 7075 wear parts.

Corrosion

Fair. Protect by anodising, chromate or trivalent conversion coating and paint. Use T73 tempers where stress corrosion is a risk.

Temperature

Loses strength faster than 2xxx alloys above about 120 °C; not for sustained elevated-temperature service.

Applications

Where 7075 rod is used

  • Aerospace: fittings, bushes, actuator parts, landing-gear components and fasteners
  • Defence: weapon components, mounts and high-strength hardware
  • Motorsport and cycling: axles, hubs, cranks, suspension parts and steering components
  • Tooling: moulds, jigs, gauges and fixtures needing hardness and dimensional stability
  • Industrial: gears, worm shafts, valve parts, pneumatic and hydraulic components
  • Sport and outdoor: climbing hardware, archery and firearm components

FAQ

7075 aluminium (aluminum) rod: frequently asked questions

How strong is 7075-T6 rod?

Typical values are about 572 MPa tensile and 503 MPa yield with 11 % elongation. Specified minimums are lower and depend on standard and diameter; for drawn rod to ASTM B211 they are around 530 MPa tensile and 455 MPa yield in common sizes.

What is the difference between 7075-T6 and T7351 rod?

T7351 is over-aged and stress-relieved. It is about 10 – 15 % weaker than T6 but far more resistant to stress-corrosion cracking, which is why aerospace drawings often specify it.

Can 7075 rod be welded?

Not reliably by arc welding; it cracks and loses strength. Use mechanical fastening, adhesive bonding or friction stir welding.

Is 7075 harder than 6061?

Yes. 7075-T6 is about 150 HB against about 95 HB for 6061-T6, and nearly twice as strong.

How much does 7075 rod weigh?

About 0.00221 × d² kg per metre with d in mm. A 50 mm rod weighs about 5.5 kg per metre.

Do you supply 7075 rod internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies 7075 rod worldwide from Mumbai, cut to length, with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and export packing.
