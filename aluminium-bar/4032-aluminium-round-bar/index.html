4032 Aluminium Round Bar

4032 is a wrought aluminium alloy with about 12 % silicon plus magnesium, copper and nickel, giving low thermal expansion, high wear resistance and good strength at elevated temperature. It is the standard material for forged pistons in road and endurance engines, and for wear parts such as compressor scrolls, valve bodies and bushings. Metline supplies 4032 round bar as forging stock and in heat-treated T6 / T651 for machining, certified to AMS 4319, ASTM B247 or EN 573-3, worldwide.

Overview

What is 4032 aluminium?

4032 is unusual among wrought alloys: its silicon content of 11 – 13.5 % is close to that of casting alloys, yet it is cast as billet, extruded into bar and forged. Most 4xxx alloys are filler or brazing metals; 4032 is the one heat-treatable 4xxx alloy used for structural parts.

The high silicon content forms a dispersion of hard silicon particles in the matrix. These particles do three useful things. They lower the coefficient of thermal expansion to about 19.4 µm/m·°C, compared with 23 – 24 for most other aluminium alloys. They raise wear and scuff resistance, so sliding surfaces such as piston skirts can run against cast-iron or coated bores. And together with nickel, they help the alloy keep its strength and dimensional stability as it heats up.

Magnesium and copper make 4032 heat treatable: solution treatment, quench and artificial ageing to T6 give a tensile strength of about 380 MPa, comparable to 6061-T6 but with much better behaviour hot. The trade-off is lower ductility and toughness than low-silicon alloys such as 2618, which is why piston designers weigh the two alloys carefully.

Why engineers specify 4032

  • Low thermal expansion keeps clearances tight across the operating temperature range
  • Hard silicon particles give strong resistance to wear, scuffing and galling
  • Good strength retention at elevated temperature thanks to nickel and silicon
  • Lower density than 2618 or 2024, about 2.68 g/cm³
  • Machines to a fine finish with suitable tooling

The size and distribution of the primary silicon particles matter as much as the chemistry. Fine, evenly spread silicon gives better forging behaviour, fatigue strength and machined finish, so piston forgers often set microstructure requirements on the bar.

Tempers

4032 tempers explained

F (as fabricated)

Extruded bar with no guaranteed properties, used as forging stock. The forged part is then heat treated to T6.

O (annealed)

Softened for machining or forming before final heat treatment. Rarely used in service.

T6

Solution treated at about 505 – 515 °C, quenched and artificially aged at roughly 170 – 175 °C. The standard service condition for pistons and wear parts.

T651

T6 plus stress relief by stretching. Preferred for bar that will be machined, because it moves less when material is removed. Covered by AMS 4319.

Elevated-temperature tempers

Some piston makers use stabilising or over-ageing treatments so dimensions stay stable at operating temperature. Agree these with the forger.

Choosing a temper

Order F for forging stock and T651 or T6 for parts machined directly from bar.

Piston alloy choice

4032 versus 2618 for forged pistons

Almost all forged pistons are made from either 4032 or 2618. The choice depends on how hard the engine will be used and how much cold clearance and noise are acceptable.

Factor40322618 / 2618A
Silicon contentHigh (≈12 %)Very low (≈0.2 %)
Thermal expansion≈19.4 µm/m·°C≈22 – 23 µm/m·°C
Piston-to-bore clearanceTighter; quieter cold startsLarger; some cold slap
Wear and scuff resistanceVery goodGood, often coated
Ductility and toughnessLowerHigher; tolerates detonation
Ring-groove stabilityVery good over long serviceGood; grooves may need anodising
Density2.68 g/cm³2.76 g/cm³
Typical useStreet performance, endurance, OEM-style forged pistonsRacing, high boost, nitrous, heavy-duty

In practice, 4032 suits engines that run long hours with moderate power gains, where tight clearances, low noise and long ring life matter. For extreme cylinder pressures or detonation risk, 2618 is the safer choice.

Working with 4032

Forging, machining, welding and finishing

Forging

Forge at roughly 415 – 460 °C with heated dies. The alloy is less ductile than 6xxx or 2xxx grades, so control die temperature and reduction per blow to avoid cracking.

Machining

Silicon particles are abrasive. Use polished carbide or, for production runs, PCD tooling with positive rake, high speeds and moderate feeds. Keep coolant clean to protect the finish.

Heat treatment

Leave allowance for distortion during quenching, then check size and straightness and take a finishing cut. T651 bar minimises movement during machining.

Welding

Weldable with 4043 or 4047 filler, which matches its silicon level; high-magnesium fillers should be avoided. Laser welding is used on housings. Most pistons are not welded.

Anodising and coatings

High silicon gives a dark grey anodic film. Hard anodising of ring grooves and skirt coatings are common on pistons.

Corrosion

Fair to good. Pistons operate in oil; exposed parts are usually anodised or coated.

Do not machine 4032 with uncoated HSS tools for production work; tool wear is rapid and surface finish deteriorates quickly.

Applications

Where 4032 round bar is used

  • Engines: forged pistons for performance road cars, motorcycles, small aircraft and endurance engines
  • Compressors: scroll compressor parts, swash plates and pistons in automotive air-conditioning compressors
  • Automotive hydraulics: brake master-cylinder parts, transmission valve bodies and spools
  • Bearings and bushings: steering bushings, wear sleeves and sliding parts
  • Precision equipment: parts that must hold dimensions with temperature change, such as instrument and copier components
  • Housings: laser-welded enclosures that need low distortion and stable dimensions

FAQ

4032 aluminium (aluminum) round bar: frequently asked questions

Why is 4032 used for pistons?

Its high silicon content lowers thermal expansion and raises wear resistance, so pistons can run tighter bore clearances with less noise and longer ring-groove life.

Is 4032 or 2618 better for a turbocharged engine?

For moderate boost and long service, 4032 works well. For high boost, nitrous or any risk of detonation, 2618 is preferred because it is tougher and more ductile.

What temper should I order for piston forging?

Order F (as fabricated) bar or slugs. The forged piston is solution treated and aged to T6 afterwards. Order T651 if you will machine parts directly from bar.

What standard covers 4032 bar?

AMS 4319 covers 4032-T651 rolled or cold-finished bar and rod from 0.750 to 3.500 in. Forgings are covered by ASTM B247, and chemistry by EN 573-3 and the Aluminum Association registration.

Can 4032 aluminium be welded?

Yes, with 4043 or 4047 filler, and laser welding is used on housings. Avoid high-magnesium fillers. Pistons themselves are normally not welded.

Is 4032 hard to machine?

It machines to a good finish, but the silicon particles wear tools quickly. Polished carbide or PCD tooling is recommended.
