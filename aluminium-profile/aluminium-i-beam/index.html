Aluminium I-Beams

Aluminium I-beams are extruded structural sections with two flanges joined by a web, giving high bending stiffness for their weight and a corrosion-resistant member that needs no painting. Metline supplies Aluminum Association and American Standard I-beams from 3 to 12 inches deep, small and metric sections, and custom beams, mainly in 6061-T6, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is an aluminium I-beam?

An I-beam puts most of its material in the flanges, as far from the centre as possible, where it does the most work resisting bending. The web keeps the flanges apart and carries shear. The result is a member that is stiff and strong in one direction for very little weight.

Aluminium I-beams are extruded rather than rolled. That has two practical consequences. First, the shape can be optimised: the Aluminum Association (AA) standard I-beam was designed specifically for aluminium, with parallel flanges, generous fillets and a better strength-to-weight ratio than a copy of a steel shape. Second, custom beams with ribs, slots or bolt channels can be made by building a new die, which is far cheaper than new rolls.

An aluminium I-beam weighs about one third as much as a steel beam of the same size and does not rust, so it is used where weight is paid for every day (trailers, boats, vehicles, mobile structures, cranes and gantries) or where corrosion and maintenance dominate (marine, chemical plant, coastal buildings, platforms and walkways). Its modulus of elasticity is also about one third of steel, so beams are sized for deflection as much as for strength.

Why engineers specify aluminium I-beams

  • About one third the weight of steel, which cuts transport and handling costs and raises payload
  • Natural corrosion resistance with no paint or galvanising needed in most environments
  • Non-magnetic, non-sparking and fully recyclable
  • Good toughness at low temperatures, with no ductile-to-brittle transition

American Standard I-beams

American Standard aluminium I-beams

American Standard I-beams follow the proportions of the steel S-shape, with tapered inner flange faces (about 16.7 % slope) and a narrower flange than the AA section. They are specified where an aluminium beam must replace a steel S-beam with the same outside dimensions, or match existing connections.

Other beam types

Small, metric and special aluminium beams

Small I-beams

Sections under 3 in (about 12 – 60 mm deep) in 6063-T5 or 6061-T6 for display frames, models, light machinery, doors and fixtures.

Metric I-beams

Sections such as 50 × 30 mm to 200 × 100 mm made to metric drawings, usually in 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 for European and Asian projects.

Boat trailer I-beams

Heavier-web beams, typically 4 – 12 in deep, in 6061-T6 for trailer frames that are repeatedly immersed in salt water.

Wide-flange (H) beams

Flange width close to the depth, giving better resistance to lateral buckling for columns and unbraced spans.

Shoring and formwork beams

Extruded beams with a top channel for timber nailers or bolt slots, used in concrete formwork and scaffolding.

Custom beams

Integrated bolt channels, ribs or interlocking edges made from a new die for repeat applications such as bridge decks, platforms and vehicle floors.

Design

Designing with aluminium I-beams

  • Check deflection first. With one third of steel's stiffness, an aluminium beam of equal size deflects about three times as much under the same load. Deeper sections recover stiffness efficiently, because the moment of inertia grows with roughly the cube of depth.
  • Use the right code. In North America, design to the Aluminum Association Aluminum Design Manual; in Europe, EN 1999 (Eurocode 9). Steel tables and allowable stresses do not apply.
  • Watch lateral buckling. Narrow-flange beams over long unbraced spans can buckle sideways; brace the compression flange or choose an AA or wide-flange section.
  • Allow for welding. Welding 6061-T6 reduces strength near the weld to about T4 level. Place welds in low-stress regions or use bolted connections.
  • Isolate from steel. In wet environments, separate aluminium beams from carbon steel with isolating pads and use stainless fasteners to avoid galvanic corrosion.
  • Allow for expansion. A 10 m aluminium beam grows about 11 mm over a 50 °C temperature change; use slotted connections on long runs.

Applications

Where aluminium I-beams are used

  • Trailers and truck bodies: boat trailer frames, flatbed cross-members and dump body sub-frames
  • Marine: deck beams, pontoons, gangways, docks and offshore walkways
  • Lifting: portable gantry cranes, hoist beams and lifting frames
  • Buildings and bridges: pedestrian bridges, canopies, roof structures and mezzanines
  • Industrial plant: platforms, walkways and supports in chemical, water and food plants
  • Formwork and scaffolding: shoring beams and stage structures that must be carried by hand
  • Rail and transit: under-frames, equipment racks and platform structures

For a beam designed to the same deflection, the aluminium section must be deeper or heavier in proportion than steel, but because aluminium is one third the density, the finished beam is still typically around half the weight. Where depth is fixed and deflection governs, steel may be the more economical choice.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) I-beams: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an Aluminum Association and an American Standard I-beam?

AA I-beams have parallel flanges of uniform thickness and were designed for aluminium, giving more strength per kilogram and simpler bolted joints. American Standard I-beams copy the steel S-shape with tapered flanges and are used when replacing steel beams of the same size.

How much does an aluminium I-beam weigh?

A 6 × 4 in AA I-beam weighs about 4.03 lb/ft (6.0 kg/m) in the light section, and an 8 × 5 in beam about 6.18 lb/ft (9.2 kg/m). Weight scales with cross-sectional area at 2.70 g/cm³.

Is an aluminium I-beam as strong as steel?

6061-T6 has a minimum yield of 240 MPa, close to mild steel, but it is only about one third as stiff. For the same deflection the aluminium beam must be deeper, yet it usually still weighs around half as much.

Which alloy are aluminium I-beams made from?

Most are 6061-T6. 6082-T6 is common in Europe, 6063 is used for small architectural beams, and 5083 or 5086 for marine structures.

Can aluminium I-beams be welded?

Yes, by MIG or TIG. The heat-affected zone of 6061-T6 loses strength to about 165 MPa tensile, so welded joints are designed with reduced allowable stresses or bolted instead.

What lengths are available?

Standard mill lengths are about 20 – 25 ft (6 – 7.6 m), with longer lengths possible for some sections. Beams can be cut to any length and drilled or coped to drawing.
