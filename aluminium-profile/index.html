Overview

How extrusion works, and why it matters

Extrusion pushes a heated aluminium billet through a shaped steel die, producing a long profile with a constant cross-section. Almost any shape that fits inside the press circle can be made, including hollow sections, screw ports, snap-fit joints and cooling fins. That design freedom is why extrusions appear in everything from windows to solar frames to electronics.

Because the die is the main up-front cost, extrusions become very economical in volume. For short runs and prototypes, standard catalogue profiles such as T-slot framing, angles and channels avoid tooling altogether.

FAQ

Extrusion questions

What is the best alloy for aluminium extrusion?

6063 is the most widely extruded alloy because it flows easily through complex dies and gives an excellent surface for anodising. For load-bearing profiles, 6061 or 6082 are preferred.

What is T-slot aluminium extrusion?

A modular profile with T-shaped slots on its faces. Fasteners slide into the slots, so frames can be assembled and reconfigured without welding.

Can you make a custom extrusion to my drawing?

Yes. Send the drawing, alloy, finish and expected volume and we will advise on die cost, lead time and price per kilogram.

What is the difference between T5 and T6?

T5 is cooled straight from the press and artificially aged. T6 is fully solution heat treated before ageing, giving higher strength.

Need standard or custom extrusions?

Send us the profile, alloy, finish, length and quantity. We will reply with availability, lead time and a competitive quote.
