Aluminium Z Bar & Zed Section

An aluminium Z bar (zed section, or Z channel) is an extruded profile with two flanges pointing in opposite directions from a central web, so that the end view reads as the letter Z. It is used to hang and space panels, carry cladding, stiffen sheet and step one surface off another. Metline supplies Z bars in 6063, 6060, 6061, 6082 and aerospace 2024, in standard inch and metric sizes or to drawing, worldwide.

Overview

What is an aluminium Z bar?

A Z bar has a web and two flanges, one turned each way. That simple geometry lets one flange fix to a wall or frame while the other sits a fixed distance away to carry a panel, a sheet edge or a second structure. It is the standard way to create an offset or a step in a single piece of metal.

In Britain and much of the Commonwealth the section is called a zed; in North America it is usually a Z bar or Z channel. The names describe the same family, which ranges from 10 mm trim profiles that hide a joint to heavy structural sections that support rainscreen cladding.

Two everyday uses explain most demand. In cladding and roofing, rows of Z bars fixed to a wall create a ventilated cavity and a flat plane on which panels are mounted; the depth of the web sets the cavity and insulation thickness. In panel hanging, two interlocking Z profiles (one on the wall, one on the back of a panel, mirror, sign or cabinet) act as a cleat: the panel is lifted on, self-locates and can be lifted off again.

Aluminium suits these jobs because it does not rust, is light enough to handle at height, extrudes into exact shapes with integral grooves or knurled faces, and can be anodised or powder coated to match the visible finish.

Specifying a Z bar

  • Flange 1 × web height × flange 2 × wall thickness, and whether dimensions are inside or outside
  • Equal or unequal flanges, and sharp or radiused corners
  • Alloy and temper: 6063-T5/T6 for trim and cladding, 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 where it carries load
  • Finish, length, quantity and any holes or slots in the fixing flange
Alloys

Alloys and tempers for Z sections

6063-T5 / T6

The usual choice for zed trim, cladding rails and panel hangers. Sharp, thin sections, a smooth surface and the best anodised finish.

6063-T52

An architectural temper common in North American Z bars: a little softer than T5, easy to cut and bend on site.

6060

European architectural alloy, close to 6063; widely used for furniture and window zeds.

6061-T6

Structural Z bars for frames, racks, trailer and marine parts. Machines and welds well and is roughly 70 % stronger than 6063-T5 in specified yield.

6082-T6

Highest strength of the common 6xxx alloys; used for load-bearing zeds designed to Eurocode 9.

2024-T3511

Aerospace extruded Z stringers that stiffen fuselage skins. High strength and fatigue resistance, but poor corrosion resistance unless clad, anodised or primed, and not weldable.

All of these alloys share a density of about 2.70 – 2.78 g/cm³ and an elastic modulus near 69 – 73 GPa. A Z bar therefore weighs about one third of a steel zed of the same shape and is about three times more flexible, which matters for cladding rails spanning between brackets.

Engineering notes

How a Z section carries load

A Z section is point-symmetric: its shear centre and centroid coincide, so, unlike a channel, it does not twist when loaded through the centre of the web. Its principal axes, however, are inclined to the web. Load it vertically and it bends partly sideways as well as down. In cladding and purlin systems this is controlled by the panels or sheeting fixed to the flanges, which restrain the sideways movement.

For a free-standing member with no such restraint, a channel, angle pair or box section is usually more predictable. Where Z bars are used as rails, check deflection with the section properties about the principal axes, or rely on the supplier's tested span tables for proprietary cladding systems.

Surface finishes

Finishes for Z sections

Most cladding and hanging zeds are hidden and can be left mill finish. Visible trims and display profiles are usually anodised (clear, satin, bronze or black) or powder coated. For exterior anodising, specify film thickness, for example AAMA 611 Class I (≥ 18 µm) or Qualanod class 20 or 25; for military or aerospace parts, anodising to MIL-A-8625 (Type II sulphuric or Type III hard) is common. Powder coatings are typically 60 – 80 µm and can be specified to Qualicoat or AAMA 2604 / 2605.

  • Mill finish: economical; for concealed rails and parts finished after fabrication
  • Clear or satin anodised: durable, metallic look for trims and shop fittings
  • Colour anodised: bronze, black or champagne to match window and façade systems
  • Powder coated or painted: any RAL colour for signs, cabinets and panels
  • Brushed: satin texture for furniture and retail displays
Fabrication & quality

Processing, tolerances and inspection

Cutting and mitring

Saw-cut to length with deburred ends; mitres for frames and trims.

Drilling and punching

Fixing holes or slots in the wall flange at a set pitch, countersunk where screws must sit flush.

Bending

Zeds can be curved for radiused façades. Order a softer temper for tight radii.

Joining

Screws, rivets and bolts; MIG/TIG welding for 6xxx alloys (with a strength reduction near welds). 2024 is riveted, not welded.

Tolerances

Commercial sections to ANSI H35.2 or EN 755-9; precision 6060/6063 profiles to EN 12020-2. Critical hook or slot dimensions should be toleranced on the drawing.

Documents

Mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 with chemistry and tensile results; coating thickness reports and inspection reports on request.

Applications

Where aluminium Z bars are used

  • Façades and roofing: sub-frames for rainscreen panels, ACP and fibre-cement boards, and spacers over insulation
  • Interiors: hanging cleats for wall panels, mirrors, artwork, headboards and acoustic panels
  • Shop fitting and displays: slatwall, shelf supports, poster rails and signage frames
  • Cabinets and enclosures: door stops, panel stiffeners and frame members
  • Transport: stiffeners on truck and trailer side walls, caravan body framing
  • Aerospace: fuselage and wing stringers and frame members, typically in 2024 or 7075
  • Doors and windows: rebate and weather-stop profiles, thresholds and step trims

FAQ

Aluminium Z bar and zed section (aluminum Z channel): FAQs

What is a zed section used for?

Mainly to create an offset: cladding and roofing sub-frames, hanging cleats for panels and mirrors, stiffeners on sheet, step and threshold trims, and frame members in cabinets, displays and vehicles.

Which alloy is best for an aluminium Z bar?

6063-T5 or T6 for trim, cladding rails and hangers, because it extrudes sharply and anodises well. Use 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 where the zed carries significant load, and 2024 only for aerospace stringers.

How are Z bar sizes described?

As flange × web × flange × wall thickness, for example 25.4 × 19 × 25.4 × 3.2 mm (1 × 3/4 × 1 × 1/8 in). State whether dimensions are inside or outside, and whether flanges are equal.

How much does an aluminium Z bar weigh?

For a square-cornered section, weight in kg/m = wall × (flange 1 + flange 2 + web − 2 × wall) × 0.0027. A 40 × 40 × 40 × 3 mm zed weighs about 0.92 kg/m.

Do two Z bars make a panel hanging system?

Yes. One zed fixed to the wall and a mirror-image zed on the back of the panel interlock as a cleat. Size the hook depth and wall so the panel weight is carried safely, and fix the wall zed into suitable anchors.

Can you supply anodised Z bars cut to length?

Yes. Z bars can be cut, drilled, anodised or powder coated and protected with film before shipping, with a mill test certificate for each lot.
