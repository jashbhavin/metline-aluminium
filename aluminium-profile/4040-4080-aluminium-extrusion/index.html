4040 / 4080 Aluminium T-Slot Extrusion

4040 and 4080 are the 40 mm metric T-slot profiles used for machine frames, guarding, workstations and automation cells: a 40 × 40 mm square section and a 40 × 80 mm double section with open slots that take nuts, brackets and panels without welding. Overview

What are 4040 and 4080 aluminium extrusions?

The numbers describe the outside section in millimetres: a 4040 profile is 40 × 40 mm, a 4080 is 40 × 80 mm. Both belong to the "40 series", the most widely used size class in metric modular framing, sitting between the light 20 and 30 series and the heavy 45, 50 and 80 series.

Each profile is a hollow extrusion with a central core bore and an undercut T-slot in each face. A T-nut slides or drops into the slot and a bolt passes through a bracket into the nut, so frames go together with hand tools and can be taken apart, extended or rebuilt later. The core bore can be tapped (typically M8 for 8 mm slot profiles and M10 or M12 for heavier 10 mm slot profiles, depending on the design) for end fastening.

A 4040 is the default for frames of moderate size: machine guards, operator workstations, 3D printer and CNC router bases, test rigs and enclosures. A 4080 is two 40 mm modules side by side. Its height on the strong axis gives it far more bending stiffness in that direction, so it is used for long spans, gantry beams, base frames, conveyor side rails and anywhere a single member carries most of the load.

Choosing between 4040 and 4080

  • Use 4040 for uprights, cross members up to about 1 m, guarding frames and light benches
  • Use 4080 for beams over about 1 m, gantries, machine bases and members carrying concentrated loads
  • Orient a 4080 with the 80 mm side vertical in bending: the stiffness gain is several times, not double
  • Keep one slot width across the whole frame so every nut and bracket fits every profile
Slot systems

8 mm and 10 mm slot: which system do you need?

There is no single international standard for T-slot geometry. The two common 40 series families are the "slot 8" system and the "slot 10" system, and within each family the inner shape of the slot, the depth of the undercut and the core bore vary from one system brand to another. Fasteners from one family will not fit the other, and even within a family it is worth checking the slot drawing before mixing brands.

Some systems also use a drop-lock or taper feature: the inner flanges of the slot are angled a few degrees so that tightening the nut pulls the flanges in and locks the joint against vibration. If your frame relies on this, tell us, because a profile without the taper will hold a joint but will not give the same pre-load.

Profile variants

Light, standard and heavy 4040 / 4080 profiles

The same 40 × 40 mm outside size is made with very different wall thicknesses and core shapes. Weight and stiffness can differ by 50 % or more between a light and a heavy version, so compare the moment of inertia, not just the nominal size, before you substitute one profile for another.

Material

Alloys, tempers and mechanical properties

T-slot profiles are extruded from medium-strength 6xxx alloys that extrude well through complex hollow dies and anodise evenly. The choice affects strength more than stiffness: every aluminium alloy has an elastic modulus close to 69 GPa, so a heavier profile, not a stronger alloy, is the way to reduce deflection.

Design

How much load can a 4040 or 4080 carry?

For T-slot frames, deflection usually decides the profile long before strength does. A quick check uses the beam formula for a simply supported member with a load at mid-span: deflection = F × L³ / (48 × E × I).

Worked example: a 1,000 mm long standard 4040 (I ≈ 9 cm4 = 90,000 mm4, E = 69,000 MPa) carrying 1,000 N (about 100 kg) at the centre deflects 1,000 × 1,000³ / (48 × 69,000 × 90,000) ≈ 3.4 mm. The same load on a standard 4080 with the 80 mm side upright (I ≈ 58 cm4) deflects only about 0.5 mm. Double the span and deflection rises eight times, which is why long beams move up to 4080 or larger sections quickly.

Calculated for a simply supported beam with a single central load; self-weight and joint flexibility are ignored. Fixed ends roughly quarter the deflection. Use the exact I value from the profile drawing for design.

  • Aim for deflection below about L/500 on machine frames that carry precision equipment, and below L/200 for general guarding
  • Joints are rarely fully rigid: corner gussets and diagonal braces add more stiffness than a heavier profile alone
  • Check fastener pull-out and bracket strength for point loads, not just the profile
  • For dynamic loads such as gantries and robots, size for stiffness and natural frequency, not static strength
Surface

Anodising and other finishes

T-slot profiles are almost always supplied anodised. A clear anodic film (typically 10 – 15 µm for indoor framing, 20 µm or more where the frame faces weather or cleaning chemicals) protects the surface, resists fingerprints and gives the even satin look buyers expect. Black anodising is the next most common choice for machine builders and display frames; colours such as yellow are used for safety barriers.

Clear anodised

Standard satin silver. Hard, wear-resistant surface that does not chip because it is part of the metal.

Black anodised

Popular for machines, photography rigs and displays. Slight colour variation between batches is normal; order a frame from one batch where appearance matters.

Powder coating

Any RAL colour. Check slot tolerance: a thick coating inside the slot can stop nuts sliding, so slots are often masked.

Mill finish

Unfinished extrusion for frames that will be painted or hidden. Cheaper, but marks easily in handling.

Remember that saw-cut ends and any machined holes expose bare aluminium after anodising. This is normal and does not affect corrosion resistance indoors; end caps cover the cut face for a finished look and safety.

Applications

Where 4040 and 4080 profiles are used

  • Machine guarding and safety fencing: 4040 posts with mesh or polycarbonate infill around robots and presses
  • Workstations and assembly benches: adjustable shelves, tool rails, lighting and monitor arms clamp straight to the slots
  • Automation and gantries: 4080 beams carry linear guides, belts and cable chains
  • CNC routers, laser engravers and 3D printers: rigid bases and gantry beams built without welding
  • Conveyor frames: 4080 side rails with 4040 legs and cross members
  • Cleanroom and laboratory frames: closed-face profiles for wipe-down surfaces
  • Enclosures and control cabinets: panels fitted into slot gaskets, doors hinged on slot-mounted hinges
  • Exhibition stands, carts and test rigs: reusable structures that change with the project

Compared with welded steel frames, an aluminium T-slot frame weighs roughly a third as much for the same outside size, needs no painting, and can be changed on the shop floor. Steel still wins on raw stiffness per section size, which is why heavily loaded machine beds often combine a steel base with an aluminium superstructure.

FAQ

4040 / 4080 aluminium (aluminum) extrusion: frequently asked questions

What is a 4040 aluminium extrusion?

A 4040 is a metric T-slot framing profile with a 40 × 40 mm square section and a T-slot on each face, usually extruded in 6063 or 6105 aluminium and anodised. It is the standard size for machine guards, workstations, enclosures and medium-duty frames.

How much does 4040 aluminium extrusion weigh per metre?

Between about 1.35 and 2.5 kg per metre, depending on whether it is a light, standard or heavy profile. A standard 8 mm slot 4040 weighs around 1.8 kg/m and a standard 4080 around 2.8 kg/m.

Will 8 mm slot nuts fit a 10 mm slot profile?

No. The two slot systems use different nut widths and slot undercuts. Choose one system for the whole frame, and check the slot drawing even within the same slot width if you mix brands.

Is 4080 twice as strong as 4040?

In bending about its strong axis a 4080 is roughly five to six times stiffer than a 4040, because stiffness rises with the cube of section height. About its weak axis it is only modestly stiffer. Always orient a 4080 with the 80 mm side in the direction of the load.

How is a 4040 / 4080 frame joined without welding?

With T-nuts and bolts through corner brackets, gussets and joining plates, or with internal anchor fasteners that pass through a drilled access hole into a tapped core bore. Internal fasteners give a clean look; gussets give the most rigid corners.

Can you supply the profiles cut and machined as a kit?

Yes. Metline can cut profiles to your list, tap core bores, drill access holes and pack each frame as a labelled kit with brackets and fasteners, ready to assemble on arrival.
