Overview

What is 7075 aluminium extrusion?

7075 is an aluminium-zinc-magnesium-copper alloy introduced in the 1940s for aircraft structures. Zinc and magnesium form very fine precipitates during ageing that give it roughly twice the strength of 6061, while copper adds strength and chromium controls grain structure.

That strength makes 7075 hard to extrude. The billet resists deformation, so presses run slowly, dies wear faster and profiles are kept to simpler shapes with thicker walls than 6xxx alloys allow. Porthole dies, which split and re-weld the metal around a mandrel, are generally avoided for 7075 because the weld seams cannot match the parent metal in strength. Hollow 7075 sections are usually made as seamless tube from pierced billet, or the design is changed to an open section.

Most 7075 extrusions are therefore bar, rod, flat bar and relatively compact profiles such as angles, tees, Z sections and stringers. They are machined into high-load parts: aircraft fittings, landing gear components, gun and optics mounts, bicycle parts, climbing hardware, moulds and racing components.

Tempers

T6511 or T73511: choosing the temper

The two main extrusion tempers trade strength against corrosion behaviour. Peak-aged T6 gives the highest strength but is susceptible to stress-corrosion cracking (SCC) and exfoliation when stressed in the short-transverse direction in a corrosive environment. The over-aged T73 condition gives up roughly 10 – 15 % of strength in return for very high SCC resistance.

7075-T6511

Solution treated, quenched, stretched about 1 – 3 % with minor straightening, then aged to peak strength. Standard for machined bar and profiles where loads are not sustained across the grain in a corrosive setting.

7075-T73511

Same route but over-aged. Chosen for aircraft fittings, parts under sustained tensile stress and anything exposed to moisture or salt. Electrical conductivity is used as an acceptance check for the correct over-ageing.

7075-T76511

An intermediate over-aged temper with better exfoliation resistance than T6 and higher strength than T73, used on some aerospace parts.

7075-O

Annealed for forming, followed by solution treatment and ageing. Rare in extrusions outside aerospace.

If you are unsure, start from the environment. Dry, indoor, machined parts with mainly longitudinal loads can use T6511. Outdoor, marine or sustained-load parts should use T73511, or a modern alternative such as 7050-T7451 or T76511.

Working with 7075

Machining, joining and finishing 7075

Machining

Excellent in T6 and T73: short chips, fine finish and stable dimensions in stretched tempers. Use carbide or PCD tools at high speeds.

Welding

Not recommended for structural joints: fusion welding causes hot cracking and severe strength loss. Join with fasteners, adhesives or friction stir welding where qualified.

Forming

Poor at room temperature in T6. Form in O or freshly quenched W condition, then age.

Anodising

Anodises well, including hard anodising; the film has a slightly grey-yellow tone compared with 6xxx alloys.

Corrosion protection

Lower general corrosion resistance than 6xxx alloys. Anodise, apply conversion coating and primer, or use T73 tempers in exposed service.

Temperature

Strength falls with prolonged exposure above about 120 °C because the alloy over-ages.

FAQ

7075 aluminium (aluminum) extrusion: frequently asked questions

Can 7075 be extruded into complex profiles?

Only to a limited degree. 7075 extrudes slowly and is normally made as bar, rod and compact open profiles. Hollow sections from porthole dies are avoided because the internal weld seams are weak; seamless tube is used instead.

What is the difference between 7075-T6511 and T73511?

T6511 is peak-aged for maximum strength. T73511 is over-aged, giving about 10 – 15 % lower strength but far better resistance to stress-corrosion cracking. Use T73511 for parts under sustained stress in humid or salty environments.

Can 7075 aluminium extrusions be welded?

Arc welding is not recommended: the alloy is prone to hot cracking and the joint loses much of its strength. Use mechanical fastening or bonding, or a qualified friction stir welding process.

Is 7075 stronger than steel?

Typical 7075-T6 yield strength (about 505 MPa) exceeds that of mild structural steels such as S275 or A36, at about one-third of the density. It is less stiff, so parts are often made deeper to control deflection.

Do you supply 7075 extrusions with aerospace certification?

Metline supplies 7075 to ASTM, AMS and EN specifications with EN 10204 3.1 certificates. Specific testing such as ultrasonic inspection can be arranged on request.
