10 Series T-Slot Aluminium Extrusion

10 series is the inch-based T-slot framing system built on a 1-inch module: profiles are 1 × 1 in, 1 × 2 in, 2 × 2 in and so on, with a 0.256 in slot opening on 1-inch centres. Overview

What is 10 series aluminium extrusion?

"10 series" names a family of fractional (inch) T-slot profiles whose basic unit is a 1-inch square. Every larger profile is a multiple of that square, so a 1020 is 1 × 2 in, a 1030 is 1 × 3 in and a 2020 is 2 × 2 in. Slot centrelines sit 1/2 inch from each edge and 1 inch apart, which keeps hole patterns and fittings interchangeable across the whole range.

The appeal is speed. A frame is designed on a simple grid, the profiles are cut to length, and the joints are made with bolts and T-nuts that slide into the slots. No welding, no painting and no specialist tools are needed, and the frame can be taken apart, changed or extended later. That makes 10 series a natural choice for desktop machines, test rigs, guards and display structures where loads are light to moderate and the design is likely to change.

In naming, "10 series" refers to the module, while the four-digit numbers describe the section. A metric equivalent exists in the 20 mm system, but the two are not interchangeable: a 1-inch profile is 25.4 mm wide and its slot is shaped for inch fasteners (typically 1/4-20 bolts).

Typical reasons to choose 10 series

  • Compact section for desktop and benchtop equipment
  • Light enough for portable stands, carts and enclosures
  • Large catalogue of standard fittings in inch sizes
  • Easy to modify or reuse when the project changes

Stiffness, not strength, usually limits a T-slot frame. For a simply supported beam with a central load P, deflection is PL³ / 48EI. A standard 1010 spanning 1 metre with a 500 N load at mid-span deflects roughly 8 mm; a 1515 profile under the same load deflects about 1.4 mm, and a 1020 turned on edge sits between the two. If a 10 series beam deflects too much, the cheapest fix is often to turn a 1020 on edge, add a support or shorten the span before moving up a series.

Most 10 series profiles are extruded in 6105-T5, an alloy that combines good strength with a surface that anodises evenly. 6063 is used for lighter profiles and metric-market equivalents.

Minimum values from ASTM B221 for sections up to about 12.7 mm wall; EN 755-2 gives similar figures for EN AW-6063 and EN AW-6005A. Stiffness (E ≈ 69 GPa) is the same for all four alloys, so alloy choice changes strength but not deflection.

Fittings

Fittings and connection methods

Joining plates and gussets

Flat plates with 3, 5 or 6 holes, and inside corner gussets, bolted across a joint with T-nuts. Fast and adjustable; good for most static frames.

Anchor and end fasteners

Concealed connectors that fit into a counterbored hole near the profile end, giving a clean joint. They require a machined access hole.

End-tapped joints

A bolt passes through the crossing profile into a tapped centre bore in the end of the other. Strong and cheap, but needs end tapping.

T-nuts

Slide-in, drop-in and spring-loaded T-nuts for 1/4-20 or metric bolts. Drop-in nuts allow fittings to be added without disassembly.

Panels and doors

Acrylic, polycarbonate and composite panels sit in the slot with panel retainers or rubber gasket; hinges and handles fix to the slot.

Mobility and levelling

Swivel castors, levelling feet and floor mounting plates for carts and machine bases.

Surface finish

Finishes for 10 series profiles

Clear anodising

The standard finish: a hard, transparent oxide layer (typically 10 – 15 µm) that resists corrosion and scuffing and keeps a clean satin look.

Black and colour anodising

Black is popular for optical, laser and display equipment because it cuts reflections. Other dyed colours can be quoted for safety or brand colour coding.

Powder coating

A thicker, coloured coating for washdown areas or where a specific RAL colour is required. Slots may need masking so T-nuts still slide freely.

Cut ends and machined faces

Any cut, drilled or tapped surface made after anodising shows bare aluminium. It is cosmetic only, as aluminium forms its own thin oxide film, but specify machining before finishing if appearance matters.

Matching 10 series fittings (brackets, gussets, joining plates and T-nuts) can be supplied in the same finish so the assembled frame looks uniform.

Applications

Where 10 series is used

  • 3D printers and small CNC machines: frames, gantries and enclosures
  • Benchtop automation: labelling, marking and inspection stations, small conveyors
  • Laboratory and teaching rigs: test benches, sensor stands, optical breadboard supports
  • Guards and enclosures: light machine guards and acoustic boxes with panels in the slots
  • Displays and exhibitions: stands and frames that are assembled and dismantled repeatedly
  • Robotics education: frames for light robot arms and student projects

FAQ

10 series T-slot aluminium (aluminum) extrusion: FAQs

What size is 10 series T-slot?

The base profile (1010) is 1 × 1 in (25.4 mm square). Larger profiles are multiples of 1 inch, and slots are 1 inch apart on centres with a 0.256 in opening.

Is 10 series compatible with metric 2020 or 2525 profiles?

No. The dimensions, slot shape and fasteners differ. Use adapter plates if you must connect the two systems.

What bolts fit 10 series T-slot?

Typically 1/4-20 bolts with matching T-nuts. Some fittings use #10-32 or metric M6 nuts designed for the 10 series slot.

How much load can a 1010 profile take?

It depends on span and support. As an illustration, a 1 m simply supported 1010 deflects about 8 mm under a 500 N central load. Size frames on allowable deflection, then check fastener capacity.

Which alloy is used for 10 series?

Usually 6105-T5 (minimum yield 241 MPa / 35 ksi). 6063-T5 or T6 is used for some light profiles.

Can you cut and tap profiles before shipping?

Yes. We supply cut-to-length profiles with end taps, counterbores and access holes, and can pack them as labelled kits with fittings.
