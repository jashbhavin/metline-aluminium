Aluminium F Channel Extrusions

An aluminium F channel is an extruded profile with a mounting flange and a channel that grips the edge of a panel, so it can fix a sheet to a wall, frame or ceiling while finishing the edge in one piece. Metline supplies F channels for panels from about 3 mm (1/8 in) to 19 mm (3/4 in) thick, in 6063-T5 and 6061, mill finish, anodised or powder coated, cut to length and shipped worldwide.

Overview

What is an aluminium F channel?

Seen end-on, the profile looks like the letter F: a long back leg that sits flat against the substrate, and two shorter legs that form a channel to receive the panel. The back leg extends beyond the channel to give a fixing flange, which is screwed, riveted or bonded to the structure and then hidden by the panel or left visible as a neat border.

This combination is what separates an F channel from a plain U channel. A U channel caps an edge but has nothing to fix through without drilling the visible face; a J channel caps an edge with one longer leg on the back; an F channel adds a full mounting flange, so the panel is supported and the edge finished without exposed fasteners.

F channels are used wherever a sheet material needs to be held at its edge: wall and ceiling panels, signs and display boards, glass and acrylic screens, cabinet and furniture panels, multiwall polycarbonate roofing and flooring transitions. Extruded aluminium makes them straight, rigid, corrosion resistant and easy to finish to match the panel.

Sizing

How to size an F channel

F channels are specified by the thickness of the material they hold. The opening is made slightly larger than the nominal panel thickness, so the panel slides in with room for a gasket, tape or adhesive and for the thickness tolerance of the sheet.

Profile styles

F channel profile styles

Standard F channel

Square legs and an extended back flange for screw fixing. The general-purpose profile for panels and signs.

F track (wide flange)

A wider mounting flange, often 1 – 1.5 in, for fixing to studs, rafters or framing at wider spacing.

Glazing F section

Channel with room for a rubber glazing gasket, used to hold glass and acrylic screens and as an end closure for multiwall polycarbonate.

Stair nosing F profile

A rounded or ribbed nose over the channel, which receives flooring boards (commonly around 8 mm laminate) at a stair edge.

Showcase / cabinet F profile

Slim, decorative channels that frame panels in display cases and furniture with minimal sight lines.

Edge and corner F trim

Profiles that cap an outside edge and return around a corner, often with a radiused face.

Finishes

Surface finishes

Mill finish

Bare extruded aluminium. Lowest cost; good where the channel is hidden or will be painted.

Clear satin anodised

The most common finish for visible trims: even silver colour, hard and easy to clean.

Black and colour anodised

Black, bronze, gold and champagne for signs, displays and furniture.

Powder coated

White, black and RAL colours matched to panels or walls; good outdoors.

Brushed

A fine linear texture for a premium look, usually clear anodised.

Wood-grain

Sublimated wood effect for interiors and furniture.

If you mitre F channels around a panel, order them from one finishing batch so the colour matches at the corners, and ask for protective film on visible faces if the profiles will be handled during fit-out.

Installation

Cutting, fixing and installation tips

  1. CutUse a mitre saw with a fine-tooth non-ferrous blade. Cut 45° mitres for picture-frame corners and deburr all edges.
  2. Fix the channelScrew or rivet through the mounting flange into the frame or wall, typically every 300 – 600 mm, or bond with a structural adhesive tape on smooth substrates.
  3. Allow for movementLeave a small gap at the bottom of the channel for thermal expansion; aluminium and plastics expand at different rates (polycarbonate roughly three times as much as aluminium).
  4. Insert the panelSlide or press the panel into the opening, with a gasket, foam tape or silicone bead where the panel must be sealed or must not rattle.
  5. FinishFit end caps or mitre returns at open ends; clean off any adhesive before it cures.

Outdoors, use stainless steel screws with nylon washers so steel fasteners do not cause galvanic corrosion or staining on the aluminium.

Applications

Where aluminium F channel is used

  • Wall and ceiling panels: edging and fixing for PVC, ACM, fibre-cement and decorative panels
  • Signs and displays: holding sign faces, menu boards and exhibition panels against walls or frames
  • Glass and acrylic: screens, partitions, splashbacks, mirror edges and showcase panels
  • Polycarbonate roofing: closing and fixing multiwall sheets at rafters, walls and eaves
  • Flooring and stairs: stair nosings and transitions for laminate and engineered boards
  • Furniture and cabinets: panel edging, sliding-door tracks and shelf fronts
  • Lighting: holding diffuser panels and LED strip covers
  • Vehicles and marine: interior panel trims in buses, caravans and boats

Typical example: a greenhouse glazed with 10 mm multiwall polycarbonate uses an F section along the eaves and walls, with the sheet sitting in the channel on a gasket and the flange screwed to the frame; a retail display uses a slim anodised F profile to hold 6 mm acrylic panels against a back wall.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) F channel: frequently asked questions

What is an F channel used for?

An F channel holds the edge of a panel and fixes it to a wall, frame or ceiling through its extended flange. It is used for wall panels, signs, glass and acrylic screens, polycarbonate roofing, stair nosings and furniture panels.

How do I choose the right F channel size?

Measure the actual thickness of the panel and add any gasket or tape. Choose a channel whose opening is slightly larger, for example about 6.5 mm (0.255 in) for a 6 mm (1/4 in) panel.

What is the difference between F channel and J channel?

A J channel has one leg longer than the other and caps an edge; an F channel adds a separate mounting flange beyond the channel, so it can be fixed without fasteners showing through the panel face.

Which alloy is used for F channel?

6063-T5 for most decorative and architectural F channels because of its finish, and 6061-T6 where higher strength or thicker walls are needed.

Can you make a custom F channel?

Yes. Send a drawing or a sample and we will extrude a custom profile, or match the closest existing die if the volume is small.
