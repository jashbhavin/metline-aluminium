Overview

Why choose 6061 for extrusions?

Extrusion forces a heated billet through a steel die to make a long product with a constant cross-section. 6061 is one of the stronger alloys that can still be extruded economically into hollow and complex shapes, which is why it is specified for load-bearing profiles in machinery, transport and structures.

The alloy gets its strength from magnesium silicide precipitates formed during ageing, helped by a small copper addition. Chromium controls grain structure and improves resistance to stress corrosion. In return for this richer chemistry, 6061 extrudes more slowly than 6063, needs a faster quench at the press to reach full T6 strength, and produces a slightly less bright surface. For thin decorative sections 6063 is the better option; for parts that carry load, 6061 is the safer one.

In North America 6061 is the default structural extrusion grade, and the Aluminum Association standard I-beams, channels and angles are usually supplied in 6061-T6. In Europe the role is more often filled by 6082 or 6005A, which give similar strength; Metline supplies all three so a design can follow the standard used in the destination market.

Tempers

Which 6061 extrusion temper to order

T6

Solution treated (at the press or in a furnace), quenched and artificially aged. The standard structural temper for profiles and tube.

T6511

T6 plus a controlled stretch of about 1 – 3 % to relieve quench stress, with minor straightening allowed. Preferred for bar and profiles that will be machined, because parts stay straight.

T6510

Stretched like T6511 but with no straightening after stretching. Used where the specification asks for it.

T4 / T4511

Naturally aged and softer. Bend or form the profile in T4, then age it to T6.

O

Annealed for maximum ductility, for severe forming or bending, followed by full heat treatment.

T5 / T1

Cooled from the press and aged without a separate solution treatment. Lower and less consistent strength than T6; use only where the drawing permits.

Design

Designing a profile in 6061

6061 can be extruded into solid, semi-hollow and hollow shapes, including porthole-die hollows, but it is less forgiving than 6063. Following a few rules keeps die cost down and quality up.

  • Wall thickness: keep walls uniform where possible and allow for thicker minimum walls than in 6063, typically from about 1.5 – 2 mm upward depending on profile size
  • Circumscribing circle: the smallest circle enclosing the section sets press size and price; compact profiles are cheaper
  • Tongue ratio: deep, narrow channels stress the die; keep depth-to-gap ratios moderate or open the gap
  • Radii: generous internal corner radii (0.5 mm or more) improve metal flow and fatigue life
  • Quench: thick and thin walls in one section cool at different rates; very mixed sections may need a furnace solution treatment for full T6
  • Tolerances: standard tolerances follow ASTM B221 / ANSI H35.2 or EN 755-9; call out tighter limits only where function needs them

Our team reviews each drawing for extrudability and suggests changes before a die is made. For a step-by-step design guide see extrusion design and production.

Working with 6061

Machining, welding, bending and finishing

Machining

Machines well in T6 and T6511 with sharp carbide tools, high speeds and coolant. T6511 keeps long bars straight as material is removed.

Welding

MIG or TIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. The heat-affected zone drops to near T4 strength, so welded frames are designed to reduced stresses or re-heat-treated.

Bending

Limited in T6. For tight bends, bend in T4 or O and then age. Stretch forming and rotary draw bending suit long curved profiles.

Anodising

Takes clear, coloured and hard anodising, though the finish is slightly less bright than 6063. Specify visible faces on the drawing.

Powder coating

Coats well after chromate-free pretreatment; standard for architectural and outdoor equipment.

Corrosion

Good in atmospheric and fresh-water service. In seawater, anodise or coat, or use 5xxx or 6082 for immersed parts.

Applications

Where 6061 extrusions are used

  • Structures: I-beams, channels, angles and tubes for platforms, walkways, bridges and scaffolding
  • Transport: truck bodies, trailer rails, bus frames, rail interior structures and EV battery frames
  • Machinery: linear-motion bases, frames, rails and housings that will be machined
  • Marine: boat towers, rails and fittings above the waterline
  • Sports and leisure: bicycle frames and parts, climbing equipment, outdoor furniture
  • Aerospace ground equipment and defence: maintenance stands, shelters and racks

FAQ

6061 aluminium (aluminum) extrusion: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 6061-T6 and 6061-T6511 extrusions?

Both have the same minimum strength. T6511 has been stretched after quenching to relieve residual stress and may have minor straightening, so it stays straighter when machined. Order T6511 for bar and profiles that will be machined.

Is 6061 or 6063 better for extrusion?

6063 extrudes faster, into thinner walls, with a brighter surface, so it suits architectural and decorative profiles. 6061 is about 25 % stronger and is chosen for load-bearing parts. If the profile carries significant load, use 6061 (or 6082 in Europe).

Can 6061 extrusions be welded?

Yes, with ER4043 or ER5356 filler by MIG or TIG. Expect the heat-affected zone to fall to about 165 MPa tensile, and design welded joints for that reduced strength.

What is the minimum wall thickness for a 6061 extrusion?

It depends on profile size and shape, but 6061 generally needs thicker walls than 6063, often from about 1.5 – 2 mm for small and medium profiles. Send the drawing and we will confirm what can be extruded reliably.

Do you make custom 6061 profiles?

Yes. Metline supplies 6061 profiles from existing dies or from new dies made to your drawing, with samples for approval before production.
