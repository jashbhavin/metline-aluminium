Overview

What is 45 series aluminium extrusion?

The 45 series is a metric T-slot system based on a 45 mm module. The basic profile is 45 × 45 mm, and larger sections are multiples of it: 45 × 90, 45 × 180 and 90 × 90 mm. Every face carries a 10 mm slot, so the whole family shares one set of nuts, brackets and accessories.

Compared with the 40 series, the extra 5 mm of section and the 10 mm slot give a profile that is stiffer, takes larger fasteners and carries heavier brackets. A standard 45 × 45 profile has a moment of inertia of roughly 11 – 14 cm4, against about 9 cm4 for a standard 4040, so for the same span and load it deflects around a third less. That makes the 45 series a common choice for machine builders who want one system for the base frame, the guarding and the operator stations.

The 45 mm module is used by several European framing system brands, and many machine builders standardise on it. Because slot details differ slightly between brands, Metline matches the profile to the drawing or system you already use rather than assuming a single geometry.

Material

Alloy, temper and mechanical properties

45 series profiles are extruded from 6063 or 6105 aluminium. Both are heat-treatable Al-Mg-Si alloys that flow well through the multi-cavity hollow dies needed for T-slot shapes and give a clean anodised surface. 6105-T5 is stronger and is preferred for heavy sections; 6063-T5/T6 gives the finest surface.

Design

Designing a 45 series frame

Stiffness is usually the limit. For a simply supported member with a central load, deflection = F × L³ / (48 × E × I). A 1,200 mm standard 45 × 45 (I ≈ 11 cm4) with a 1,000 N central load deflects about 1,000 × 1,200³ / (48 × 69,000 × 110,000) ≈ 4.6 mm. Turning the same member into a 45 × 90 with the 90 mm side vertical cuts that to well under 1 mm.

  • Fit levelling feet or floor plates on 90 × 90 columns rather than bare profile ends

Applications

Typical 45 series applications

Machine bases

45×90 and 90×90 members carry spindles, presses and test equipment on levelling feet.

Robot cells

Rigid pedestals and perimeter guarding from a single slot system, with interlocked doors on slot hinges.

Conveyor structures

Side frames, cross members and legs that can be re-spaced when the line changes.

Workstations

Ergonomic benches with height-adjustable shelves, tool rails and lighting clamped to the slots.

Cleanroom frames

Closed-face profiles and anodised surfaces for electronics and medical assembly.

Material handling

Carts, racks and flow shelving that are bolted, not welded, and easily rebuilt.

FAQ

45 series aluminium (aluminum) extrusion: frequently asked questions

What slot width does 45 series aluminium extrusion use?

A 10 mm slot on every face. 10 mm slot nuts, brackets and panel gaskets fit across the 45×45, 45×90, 45×180 and 90×90 sections, but slot undercut details vary by brand, so check compatibility with existing hardware.

Is 45 series stronger than 40 series?

Yes. A standard 45×45 profile is roughly 20 – 50 % stiffer than a standard 4040, depending on the versions compared, and the 10 mm slot takes larger fasteners. Use 45 series for heavier machines and longer spans.

How much does a 45 × 45 profile weigh?

About 1.5 kg/m for a light version and about 2.0 kg/m for a heavy version. Larger sections scale up: a 45×90 is typically 3 – 3.5 kg/m.

Which alloy is used for 45 series profiles?

6063 in T5, T6 or T66 temper, or 6105-T5 for higher strength. Both anodise well. Stiffness is almost the same for every aluminium alloy, so choose a heavier section rather than a stronger alloy to reduce deflection.

Can you cut and machine 45 series profiles before shipping?

Yes. Profiles can be cut to length, tapped, drilled for anchor fasteners and packed as labelled kits with brackets and fasteners.
