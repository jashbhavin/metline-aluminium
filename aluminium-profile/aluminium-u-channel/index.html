Overview

What is an aluminium U channel?

A U channel is the simplest open extrusion after the angle: a base with two legs on the same side, giving a section that is stiff in one direction, easy to fix through the base and open on one face to receive a panel, a cable, a runner or another profile.

The name covers two quite different products. Architectural or trim channel has sharp inside and outside corners, a uniform wall and usually equal legs. It is extruded in 6063 or 6060, sold in small sizes, and used to cap the edges of panels, glass and boards, to make sliding tracks and to hide cables or LED strip. Structural channel is extruded in 6061 or 6082, has radiused inside corners (fillets) that reduce stress concentration, and comes in larger sizes whose section properties are tabulated for design.

Within structural channel there are two families. Aluminum Association (AA) standard channels have flanges of constant thickness and generous fillets; they are lighter for a given strength and are the usual choice for new aluminium designs. American Standard channels copy the steel C-section, with flanges that taper from thick at the root to thin at the toe; they are specified where an aluminium part must replace a steel one of the same nominal size. Both are supplied in 6061-T6 to ASTM B308.

The words "U channel" and "C channel" are used loosely by many buyers. On this site, U channel means a section with straight, parallel legs and no lips; a channel with inward-turned lips at the leg tips is a C channel, and a channel with outward flanges is a hat channel.

Choosing a U channel in one minute

  • Appearance, edging and light framing: 6063-T5 or T52 with sharp corners, anodised or powder coated
  • Load-bearing frames, trailers and platforms: 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 structural channel with radiused corners
  • Sliding doors and drawers: a channel whose inside width matches the runner plus a running clearance
  • Panel edging: inside width equal to panel thickness plus 0.2 – 0.5 mm, or a gasket allowance
Alloys

Alloys used for U channel and what each is for

6063 (architectural)

The default for trim and visible channel. Extrudes into thin, sharp-cornered sections with a smooth surface and gives the most even anodised finish. Typically supplied T5 or T52.

6060

Very close to 6063 with slightly lower strength. Widely used in Europe for window, door and furniture channel; excellent for anodising.

6463

A low-iron variant of 6063 chosen when the channel will be bright-dipped or chemically polished for a mirror-like trim finish.

6061 (structural)

About 70 % stronger than 6063-T5 in specified yield. Used for structural channel to ASTM B308, trailer and truck bodies, machine frames and marine fittings.

6082

The European structural alloy, slightly stronger than 6061 in thick sections. Common for channel designed to Eurocode 9 (EN 1999).

6005A

Medium-high strength with better extrudability than 6082, so it suits thinner-walled or more complex structural channels such as rail and bus parts.

Mechanical properties

Strength of U channel by alloy and temper

Extruded channel is bought by temper. T5 means cooled from the press and artificially aged; T6 means solution heat-treated (or press-quenched to an equivalent condition) and aged to full strength; T52 is a controlled-cooling temper used for architectural 6063; T6511 is a 6061 temper that has been stretched to relieve stress and straightened.

Because the modulus is a third of steel, an aluminium channel of the same shape deflects three times as much under the same load. For stiffness-critical members, choose a deeper section rather than a stronger alloy: doubling the leg height increases bending stiffness about the strong axis far more than any change of temper.

Surface finishes

Finishes for U channel

Mill finish

As extruded. Lowest cost and the right choice for hidden or structural channel, or where you will finish parts after fabrication.

Anodised

Clear, champagne, bronze or black anodic film. For exterior architectural work, specify film thickness: about 15 – 25 µm, for example AAMA 611 Class I (≥ 18 µm) or a Qualanod class of 20 or 25 µm.

Powder coated

Polyester powder in any RAL colour, typically 60 – 80 µm thick. Specify a Qualicoat class or AAMA 2603 / 2604 / 2605 for the exposure.

Brushed or polished

Mechanically brushed (satin) or polished before anodising, for furniture, retail and interior trim.

Wood grain

Sublimation print over a powder base coat, used for interior door and furniture channel.

Protective film

PE film on visible faces to protect the finish through fabrication and installation.

Tolerances on leg height, base width and wall thickness depend on the size of the section and the circumscribing circle, and are tighter for precision profiles to EN 12020-2. Straightness, twist and the angle between legs and base are also controlled. If a channel must hold a panel or slide on a runner, put a tolerance on the inside width on your drawing rather than relying on the general tables.

Every delivery is checked for dimensions, straightness and surface, and is supplied with a mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 showing chemistry and tensile results for the lot. Hardness checks and coating thickness reports for anodised or powder-coated channel are available on request.

Fabrication

Cutting, machining, bending and joining U channel

Cutting

Saw with a fine-tooth carbide blade and lubricant. Cut-to-length service with mitres and length tolerance to your drawing.

Machining

Drilling, slotting and punching of fixing holes in the base. 6061 and 6082 in T6 machine with short chips; 6063 is softer and can smear without sharp tools.

Bending

Channel can be rolled into curves around its weak or strong axis. Soft tempers (T4 or O) bend far more easily; T6 channel needs generous radii and may crack at the leg tips.

Welding

MIG or TIG with ER4043 or ER5356. The heat-affected zone loses much of its T6 strength, so welded structural channel is designed to reduced strength values.

Mechanical joining

Rivets, self-tapping screws and bolts through the base are common; stainless fasteners need isolating washers in wet environments to prevent galvanic corrosion.

Assembly

Channels are often slid over panel edges with an EPDM or PVC insert, which absorbs tolerances and reduces rattle.

Applications

Where aluminium U channel is used

  • Building: glass and panel edging, cladding trims, glazing beads, partition tracks, stair nosings and window sub-frames
  • Doors and furniture: sliding door and wardrobe tracks, drawer runners, shelf edges and cabinet frames
  • Transport: truck and trailer floor cross-members, side-rails, bus interiors and caravan frames, usually in 6061 or 6082
  • Marine: deck edging, rub-rails and fender channels, in 6061-T6 or 6082-T6, anodised for seawater exposure
  • Electrical and lighting: cable trunking, LED strip housings that also act as heat spreaders, and panel enclosures
  • Industrial: machine guards, conveyor side-guides, equipment frames and mounting rails
  • Signs and displays: sign-board frames, poster rails and exhibition stand edging
Comparison

U channel compared with C, hat and box sections

SectionShapeStrengthsTypical use
U channelBase and two straight legsSimple, open, easy to fix through the baseEdging, tracks, light and medium frames
C channelU with inward lipsLips retain trolleys, nuts and panels; stiffer leg tipsSlide tracks, strut systems
Hat channelU with outward flangesFlanges fix flat to a surfaceFurring, stiffeners, cladding battens
Box sectionClosed rectangleMuch higher torsional stiffnessColumns, frames under twisting loads
I-beamWeb with two flangesBest bending efficiency, symmetricBeams and spans

An open channel is weak in torsion and, when loaded through its base, tends to twist because its shear centre lies outside the section. Where a member carries a load off its centre line or spans long distances, a box section or an I-beam is usually the better choice; where the open face is needed to receive something, the U channel is hard to beat.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) U channel: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 6061 and 6063 U channel?

6061-T6 has a specified minimum yield of 240 MPa against 110 MPa for 6063-T5, so it is used for load-bearing channel and usually has radiused inside corners. 6063 extrudes sharper, thinner sections with a better surface and anodised finish, so it is used for trim, edging and architectural channel.

What is the difference between AA and American Standard channel?

Aluminum Association channels have flanges of constant thickness and larger fillets and are lighter for the same strength. American Standard channels have tapered flanges matching steel channel dimensions, and are used where aluminium replaces a steel section of the same size.

How do I calculate the weight of an aluminium U channel?

Multiply the cross-sectional area by the density. For a square-cornered channel, area = wall × (base + 2 × leg − 2 × wall) in mm², and weight in kg/m is area × 0.0027. A 50 × 25 × 3 mm channel weighs about 0.84 kg/m.

Can aluminium U channel be bent?

Yes. Channels are roll-bent into arcs about either axis. Soft tempers bend most easily; T6 channel needs larger radii and, for tight bends, may need to be supplied in T4 and aged after forming.

Which U channel is best for outdoor use?

6063 or 6061 channel anodised to at least 15 – 20 µm, or powder coated to a recognised architectural standard, performs well outdoors. In coastal or marine settings, use isolating washers with stainless fasteners and choose a thicker anodic film.

Can you make a custom U channel to my drawing?

Yes. Send a dimensioned drawing with tolerances, alloy, temper, finish and annual quantity. A new die is made for non-standard sections, and samples can be checked before full production.
