Aluminium Handle Profiles

Aluminium handle profiles are extruded grips that run along the edge of a cabinet door, drawer or sliding panel, or sit in a recess between units, so the furniture needs no separate knobs. Metline supplies gola, G, C, J, L, edge-pull and bar handle profiles in 6063 and 6060 alloys, anodised or coated in any colour, in full lengths or cut to size, to furniture makers and distributors worldwide.

Overview

What is an aluminium handle profile?

A handle profile is a length of extruded aluminium shaped so that fingers can hook behind it or into it. It is either fixed to the door or drawer front, or built into the cabinet carcass so that the doors themselves stay plain.

Handleless kitchens and wardrobes made the profile popular, but the reasons it works are practical. Extruded aluminium can be shaped into a deep, comfortable finger pull only a few millimetres thick, it is stiff enough to run the full width of a drawer bank without sagging, it does not swell in a steamy kitchen the way MDF edges can, and its anodised or coated surface is easy to wipe clean.

The same section can be cut to any door width, so one die serves a whole furniture range. That is also why handle profiles are almost always made from 6063 or 6060: these alloys flow through complex dies, give crisp edges and take an even anodised colour, which matters more on a visible handle than ultimate strength.

What to decide before you order

  • Handle style: integrated (gola), edge-mounted (G, C, J, L) or surface-mounted (bar pull)
  • Door or panel thickness the profile must fit, usually 16, 18 or 19 mm
  • Finish and colour, and whether it must match other hardware
  • Supply as full lengths for your workshop or pre-cut, drilled pieces ready to fit
Profile types

Types of aluminium handle profile

Gola (integrated channel)

A recessed channel fixed horizontally or vertically in the cabinet carcass, between the worktop and the top drawer or between drawers. The doors have no handle at all; fingers reach into the gap. L-type sections run below the worktop, C-type between drawer fronts, and vertical sections serve tall units.

G profile

Fixed to the top edge of a door or drawer front, wrapping over it and leaving a finger groove behind. Fronts are rebated or cut short to accept it. See our G profile page for sizes.

C and J profiles

Clip or screw onto the door edge to form a curved lip. A J profile has a single curved return; a C profile wraps both faces for a deeper grip.

L and edge-pull profiles

An angle-shaped lip along the top or side edge of a door, very slim when the door is closed. Popular on wardrobes and bathroom units.

Bar and T-bar pulls

Surface-mounted handles cut from extruded bar or a T section, fixed with machine screws at standard hole centres.

Sliding-door handle profiles

Vertical full-height sections that also act as the door stile, used on wardrobe sliding doors and aluminium-framed glass doors.

6061-T6Heavy bar pulls and handles that take high hand loads260 / 240 MPa

Handles are pulled thousands of times, but the loads are small; what fails in practice is usually the fixing or the finish, not the metal. A 6063-T5 profile of 1.5 mm wall is ample for a drawer pull. Where a long handle spans a tall door and is fixed only at the ends, increasing the section depth adds far more stiffness than changing alloy.

Surface finishes

Finishes for aluminium handle profiles

Anodised

Silver, matt black, champagne, bronze, gold and stainless-steel effect. For kitchens specify at least AA15 (15 µm) so the film resists cleaning chemicals and abrasion.

Brushed anodised

Mechanically brushed before anodising for a satin stainless look that hides fingerprints.

Powder coated

Any RAL colour, including soft-touch matt textures, matched to door fronts. QUALICOAT-approved systems on request.

PVDF coating

Two- or three-coat fluoropolymer for exterior doors and coastal locations where colour stability matters.

Wood-grain

Sublimation transfer to match oak, walnut or other veneers on the fronts.

Polished and electrophoretic

Bright polished or electro-coated finishes in gold, titanium and black for premium furniture.

Colour on anodised aluminium varies slightly from batch to batch. For a large project, order all profiles in one batch or approve a colour range with limit samples.

Processing

Cutting, machining and fitting

  • Cutting: profiles are cut to door widths on a non-ferrous saw; for anodised parts, cut before anodising where possible, or cap the raw ends.
  • Drilling and tapping: CNC drilling at 32 mm-system centres, countersinking for flush screws, and M4 tapping for bar pulls.
  • Notching and mitring: gola corners and G profiles at door corners are mitred or notched so the grip runs continuously.
  • Bending: bow handles and curved pulls are formed on stretch or CNC benders, preferably before finishing.
  • Fixing: gola profiles screw to the carcass sides through brackets; edge profiles are screwed, glued or clipped to the front.
  • Kitting: profiles can be packed with matching end caps, corner pieces and screws, ready for assembly.
Applications

Where handle profiles are used

  • Kitchens: gola systems and edge pulls for handleless base, wall and tall units
  • Wardrobes: vertical edge pulls on hinged doors and full-height handle stiles on sliding doors
  • Bathrooms: slim pulls on vanity units, where moisture resistance is essential
  • Offices and retail: storage cabinets, lockers and shop counters
  • Doors and windows: sliding glass doors, shower enclosures and window sashes
  • Appliances and caravans: integrated pulls on appliance doors and lightweight motorhome furniture
Tolerances and quality

Tolerances and inspection

Visible handle profiles are normally ordered to the precision tolerances of EN 12020-2 for 6060 and 6063, which control the grip dimensions, straightness and twist more closely than general profile tolerances. Every lot is inspected for cross-section, surface and coating thickness, and supplied with an EN 10204 3.1 certificate. Anodising can be specified to QUALANOD and coatings to QUALICOAT where the project requires a recognised quality label.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) handle profiles: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a gola profile and a G profile?

A gola profile is fixed into the cabinet carcass, so the doors are plain and the grip is the recess between units. A G profile is fixed to the top edge of each door or drawer front and forms the grip on the front itself.

Which alloy is best for handle profiles?

6063-T5 or 6060-T5. Both extrude fine, sharp details and give an even anodised colour. 6061-T6 is used only for heavy bar pulls where extra strength is needed.

What hole centres do aluminium bar handles use?

Furniture follows the 32 mm system, so common centres are 96, 128, 160, 192, 224 and 320 mm. Profiles can be drilled to any centres you specify.

Which finish lasts best in a kitchen?

Anodising of at least 15 µm or a quality powder coating both perform well. Anodised surfaces resist scratching better; powder coating gives a wider colour range and soft-touch textures.

Can you supply handle profiles cut and drilled to size?

Yes. Profiles can be cut to door widths, drilled, mitred and packed with matching end caps and brackets, ready to fit.

Can a new handle design be made?

Yes. A custom die can be made from your drawing. Send the section, door construction and expected annual quantity so the die and finish can be reviewed before production.
