Avoiding Porosity When Welding Aluminium

Open the original source · 2026-10-14

TWI explains that hydrogen picked up by molten aluminium forms pores if the gas cannot escape before the metal solidifies.

Hydrogen can come from the parent metal, the filler, hydrated oxide, moisture or the shielding atmosphere.

The advice is to clean parent and filler surfaces and keep shielding effective. A solid-state process that does not melt the metal avoids the gas porosity seen in fusion welding.

Moisture or hydrocarbons release hydrogen that dissolves in the weld pool and is trapped as pores on solidification.

Hydrated oxide on the surface, dirty filler, disturbed shielding and wet shielding gas are separate routes for contamination.

Control begins with clean surfaces, properly stored filler and steady shielding. Any change to parameters must be tested within a qualified welding procedure.

Limits: This explains the mechanism and general controls. It is not a qualified welding procedure, an acceptance standard or advice on handling solvents.

Aluminium Automotive Manual — Adhesive bonding

Open the original source · 2026-10-14

Design bonded joints to carry load in shear and keep peel and cleavage low, with an overlap large enough to give adequate bonded area.

A lasting bond needs substrate, surface preparation, any lubricant, adhesive and cure to work together. High initial strength does not prove the joint will survive humid service.

Once prepared, surfaces must stay clean and dry and be protected from handling and shop dirt until assembly.

Limits: This is a 2015 teaching manual, not a current adhesive specification or certified design method. Joint sizes and allowable strengths must be proven by testing for the application, and its illustrations are not ours to reuse.

Aluminium Automotive Manual — Mechanical joining

Open the original source · 2026-10-14

Clinching needs room for a punch and die on opposite sides, enough flange width and adequate spacing between joints.

A semi-tubular self-piercing rivet pierces the top layers and spreads inside the ductile bottom layer to lock the stack together.

Rivet, die, material stack and setting conditions have to suit one another, and joint quality is proven by sectioning and mechanical tests.

Fasteners of a different metal add a corrosion risk that the design must address.

Limits: The manual dates from 2015, so check current equipment capability with the supplier. Its thickness ranges and cycle times are examples, not general limits.

What is Galvanic Corrosion and How Can it be Prevented?

Open the original source · 2026-10-14

Galvanic corrosion needs three things: two different metals, an electrical path between them and a conductive electrolyte.

It can be controlled by isolating the metals electrically, choosing compatible materials or applying a suitable coating scheme.

Coating only the anodic metal can make local attack worse where the coating is damaged, so consider the whole joint and how it meets the electrolyte.

Limits: This is general guidance, not a galvanic series for a particular environment or a life prediction. It cannot be used to estimate the service life of an aluminium alloy.

Specifying Anodized Finishes on Aluminum

Open the original source · 2026-10-14

How an anodised part looks depends on the alloy and the process. A casting that can be anodised will not necessarily give an even decorative finish.

A full finish specification states the preparation, the type of anodic coating, the appearance and the coating thickness.

Hard anodic coatings stand up to abrasion, yet the aluminium beneath can still dent under a concentrated load.

Thin anodic films can act as a base for adhesives or paint, which is a different aim from a decorative architectural finish.

Limits: The source uses older MIL-A-8625 and Alcoa terms, so confirm the current governing specification. We have not taken any bath recipe, chemical safety advice or general sealing recommendation from it.

QUALICOAT Specifications — 26th Edition Introduction

Open the original source · 2026-10-14

QUALICOAT approves coating materials and pretreatment systems separately from the licences it grants to inspected coating plants.

A coating line covers pretreatment, application and curing, so using an approved powder does not by itself make the finished product certified.

Tests named in the introduction include gloss, eddy-current film thickness and cross-cut adhesion.

Postforming is forming after the coating is applied, and it needs a coating system designed for it.

Limits: The introduction is not the full specification for coaters or material makers. Metline makes no claim to a QUALICOAT licence, approval or logo.

Recycling — Materials

Open the original source · 2026-10-14

Process scrap and post-consumer scrap are separate feedstocks, and post-consumer scrap usually needs tighter control of contamination.

How scrap is collected, sorted and prepared decides how much of it can be used.

Mechanical separation followed by sorting by alloy brings the scrap closer to the target melt chemistry.

Limits: This is a descriptive overview with no general figure for yield, purity or recycling rate. The source's photographs are not licensed for our use.

Recycling — Processes

Open the original source · 2026-10-14

Some scrap carries coatings or oil that must be removed before melting to improve recovery and limit emissions.

The chemistry and contaminants of the feed decide which furnace and refining steps are used; degassing and alloy adjustment may follow.

The page puts recycling energy at roughly 5% of that needed to make primary ingot from bauxite, a broad process-level figure.

Recycling also produces emissions and residues such as dross and salt slag, which must be managed in their own right.

Limits: The energy figure is not a carbon footprint for any product and does not prove recycled content. Not every remelt plant uses salt flux or the same treatment steps.

Sustainable Aluminium Dross Management Guidance — Practical Summary

Open the original source · 2026-10-14

White dross, black dross, salt cake and treated non-metallic residues each need a different handling decision.

Keeping oxidation down during melting and transfer leaves more metal to recover.

Good recovery depends on careful handling, controlled cooling, dry covered storage, traceability and skilled operators working together.

The recovery method and any reuse of residues must suit the feed, the costs, the environmental impact and local regulations.

Limits: We reviewed only the summary, not the full report. It is not an operating procedure, a transport classification or a ruling on local compliance. The summary notes that hydrometallurgical routes are not yet used industrially, so emerging methods should not be presented as established.

Friction Stir Weld Tooling Development for Application on the 2195 Al-Cu-Li Space Transportation System External Tank

Open the original source · 2026-10-14

The report looks at friction stir welding of 2195 plate and extrusions for the former external-tank programme.

High process loads, the need for backing support and deflection of the machine and tooling made scale-up difficult.

Even when a material friction stir welds well, it may still need dedicated fixtures and tight process control.

Limits: This is 1998 research for one programme, not a current aerospace qualification or general 2195 property data. We have used none of its strength, load or cryogenic figures.
