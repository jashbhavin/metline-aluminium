2024 Aluminium Flat Bar

2024 flat bar is a rectangular bar in the aluminium-copper-magnesium alloy, chosen when a part needs high strength and very good fatigue resistance at low weight. It is supplied mainly as cold-finished T351 or extruded T3511 bar for aircraft fittings, machined structural parts and high-load components. Metline supplies 2024 flat bar in T351, T3511, T4 and T851 to ASTM, AMS and EN standards, cut to length and shipped worldwide.

Overview

What 2024 flat bar is and when to use it

2024 was developed as an aircraft alloy and it still behaves like one: high static strength, excellent fatigue performance and good fracture toughness, but limited corrosion resistance and no practical fusion weldability.

The alloy contains about 4.4 % copper and 1.5 % magnesium. After solution heat treatment, these form fine precipitates that make 2024-T351 roughly 50 % stronger than 6061-T6 in tension, and noticeably better under repeated loading. That is why it is specified for parts that see cyclic stress: wing and fuselage fittings, brackets, hinges, actuator links, gears and bolted structural plates.

In flat bar form, two production routes dominate. Cold-finished bar (T351) is rolled or drawn, solution-treated and then stretched 1 – 3 % to relieve quenching stresses, giving close tolerances and good stability during machining. Extruded bar (T3511) is pushed through a die, solution-treated and stretched, then lightly straightened; it is available in wider and thicker rectangles and is usually the more economical choice for heavy sections.

Why engineers choose 2024 flat bar

  • High tensile and yield strength in the naturally aged T3 and T4 family
  • Fatigue strength well above 6061 and 7075 in many service conditions
  • Machines to a clean finish with short chips in T351
  • Long track record in aerospace specifications and design allowables

Tempers

T351, T3511, T4 and T851 explained

2024-T351

Solution heat-treated, stress-relieved by stretching, naturally aged. The standard temper for cold-finished flat bar; stays stable during heavy machining.

2024-T3511

The extruded equivalent: solution-treated, stretched and then minor straightening allowed. Common for wider and thicker rectangles.

2024-T4

Solution-treated and naturally aged without stretching. Higher residual stress, so less suited to deep machining but fine for small parts.

2024-T851

Stretched and artificially aged. Slightly higher yield and better resistance to stress corrosion and to intergranular attack, at the cost of elongation and toughness.

For most machined flat-bar parts, T351 or T3511 is the right default. Choose T851 when the part will be exposed to elevated temperatures (above about 120 °C) for long periods, where naturally aged tempers continue to change, or when stress corrosion resistance matters more than toughness.

Tolerances & inspection

Tolerances and inspection

Dimensional tolerances follow ANSI H35.2 when bar is ordered to ASTM B211 or B221, and EN 754-5 or EN 755-5 for rectangular bar in Europe. Cold-finished T351 bar holds tighter thickness and width tolerances than extruded T3511 bar, which matters if you plan to machine only some faces.

Working with 2024

Machining, joining, forming and corrosion protection

Machining

Excellent in T351 and T851: short chips and a good surface with carbide or high-speed steel tools. Avoid O temper for machining; it is gummy.

Joining

2024 is not recommended for arc welding because it hot-cracks and loses strength. Use mechanical fastening, adhesive bonding or friction stir welding.

Forming

Form in O or freshly quenched (W) condition. T351 bar takes only gentle bends.

Corrosion protection

The copper content reduces corrosion resistance. Anodise (sulphuric or chromic), apply conversion coating and primer, or use alclad sheet where exposure is severe.

Applications

Where 2024 flat bar is used

  • Aerospace: wing and fuselage fittings, brackets, spar caps, hinges and seat-track parts
  • Defence: machined structural parts and mounts that need strength-to-weight
  • Motorsport and automotive: suspension links, brackets and high-load machined parts
  • Machinery: gears, shafts, cams and fixture components under cyclic load
  • Fasteners: rivet and bolt stock, washers and clamp plates
  • Tooling: jigs and fixtures that must be light and stiff

FAQ

2024 aluminium flat bar: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 2024-T351 and T3511 flat bar?

T351 is cold-finished (rolled or drawn) bar; T3511 is extruded bar. Both are solution-treated, stretched and naturally aged, but T3511 allows minor straightening after stretching and generally has wider dimensional tolerances.

Can 2024 aluminum flat bar be welded?

Not with conventional MIG or TIG; the welds crack and lose most of their strength. Bolt, rivet or bond 2024 parts, or use friction stir welding.

Is 2024 stronger than 6061?

Yes. Typical 2024-T351 tensile strength is about 470 MPa against 310 MPa for 6061-T6, and its fatigue strength is considerably higher.

Is 2024 or 7075 better for aircraft fittings?

7075 is stronger in static load. 2024 is preferred where fatigue and crack growth control the design, such as tension-loaded lower wing structure.

Does 2024 flat bar need corrosion protection?

Usually, yes. Its copper content makes it vulnerable in moist or salty air, so parts are normally anodised or primed and painted.

How much does 2024 flat bar weigh?

Multiply thickness by width in millimetres and by 0.00278 to get kg per metre. A 20 × 100 mm bar weighs about 5.56 kg/m.
