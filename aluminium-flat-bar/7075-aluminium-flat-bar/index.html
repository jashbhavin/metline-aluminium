7075 Aluminium Flat Bar

7075 aluminium flat bar is a rectangular bar in the zinc-magnesium-copper alloy that gives the highest strength of any common aluminium grade, close to many structural steels at about one third of the weight. It is used for highly stressed machined parts, tooling and aerospace fittings, where weldability and corrosion resistance matter less than strength. Metline supplies 7075 flat bar in T6, T651, T73 and T7351, extruded, rolled or saw-cut from plate, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is 7075 aluminium flat bar?

7075 is a 7xxx-series alloy in which about 5.6 % zinc and 2.5 % magnesium form fine MgZn2 precipitates during ageing. The result, in T6 temper, is a typical tensile strength of about 570 MPa: nearly twice that of 6061-T6, and higher than mild steel.

Developed in the early 1940s for aircraft structures, 7075 is still the reference high-strength alloy for wing and fuselage fittings, but most flat bar today goes into machined parts outside aerospace: mould and die components, jigs and fixtures, robotic arms, bicycle and motorsport parts, firearm components and climbing equipment. A flat bar is a practical starting shape for these, because it reduces the amount of material to be machined away compared with plate.

Flat bar in 7075 reaches the customer by three routes. Smaller sections are extruded (ASTM B221, AMS 4154) or rolled and cold finished (ASTM B211, AMS 4122/4124). Wide or thick flats are often saw-cut from 7075-T651 plate, which gives the same stretched, stress-relieved condition and is certified to the plate standard. Tell us which route your specification requires, because the certificate and tolerances differ.

Before you choose 7075 flat bar

  • It is not suitable for arc welding: fusion welds crack and lose most of their strength
  • T6 is susceptible to stress-corrosion cracking in the short-transverse direction; T73 / T7351 is the fix
  • It costs more than 6061, so use it where the strength actually reduces weight or size
  • It machines beautifully and holds tight tolerances, which often justifies the price on its own

Tempers

T6, T651 or T7351: which 7075 temper?

7075-T6

Solution heat-treated and aged to peak strength. Normal for small cold-finished and extruded bars. Highest strength, but residual quench stress and the highest SCC sensitivity.

7075-T651

T6 plus stretching to relieve quench stress. Stays straight and flat when machined. The usual choice for plate-cut flat bar and larger rolled bar.

7075-T6511

The extruded-bar version of T651: stretched, with minor straightening allowed afterwards.

7075-T73 / T7351

Over-aged to trade about 10 – 15 % of strength for much better resistance to stress-corrosion cracking and exfoliation. Specified for aerospace fittings loaded in the short-transverse direction. Conductivity is used to verify the temper.

7075-T76 / T7651

Intermediate over-age that improves exfoliation resistance with a smaller strength loss than T73. More common in plate and extrusions than bar.

7075-O

Annealed for forming. Rarely bought as flat bar except where it will be formed and then heat-treated.

Working with 7075

Machining, joining, finishing and heat

Machining

Excellent. Hard tempers give short chips and fine finishes, and parts hold tolerance well. Use sharp carbide, high spindle speeds and good chip evacuation. T651 limits distortion when large volumes are removed.

Welding

Not recommended for fusion welding: hot cracking and severe strength loss. Join with fasteners, adhesives or friction stir welding. If a weldable 7xxx alloy is required, 7020 or 7005 are the usual alternatives.

Forming

Very limited in T6. Form in O or freshly quenched W condition, then heat-treat.

Anodising

Takes hard and decorative anodising, with a slightly yellow-grey tint compared with 6063. Hard anodise is common on wear surfaces.

Corrosion protection

Lower corrosion resistance than 5xxx and 6xxx. Anodise, chromate or paint parts in outdoor or marine service, and use T73 where SCC is a risk.

Temperature

Loses strength progressively above about 120 °C as the alloy over-ages. Not for sustained elevated-temperature service; consider 2xxx grades there.

Applications

Where 7075 flat bar is used

  • Aerospace: wing and fuselage fittings, brackets, seat tracks, ground support equipment
  • Tooling: mould plates, die components, jigs and fixtures where low weight eases handling
  • Automation and robotics: arms, end-effectors and high-speed moving parts
  • Defence: weapon components and equipment mounts
  • Sport and leisure: climbing hardware, bicycle components, archery and motorsport parts
  • Consumer electronics: machined housings and frames that need high stiffness-to-weight

FAQ

7075 aluminium (aluminum) flat bar: frequently asked questions

How strong is 7075-T6 flat bar?

Typical tensile strength is about 572 MPa (83 ksi) and yield about 503 MPa (73 ksi). Guaranteed minimums are lower and depend on size and standard; for extruded bar up to 25 mm EN 755-2 requires 540 MPa tensile and 480 MPa yield.

What is the difference between 7075-T651 and 7075-T7351?

Both are stretched to relieve stress. T651 is aged to peak strength; T7351 is over-aged, giving roughly 10 – 15 % lower strength but far better resistance to stress-corrosion cracking. Aerospace fittings with short-transverse loads normally use T7351.

Can 7075 flat bar be welded?

Not by conventional MIG or TIG; the welds crack and lose most of their strength. Use mechanical fastening, bonding or friction stir welding, or switch to a weldable alloy such as 6061 or 7020.

Is plate-cut 7075 flat bar as good as extruded bar?

Mechanically, yes: strips sawn from 7075-T651 plate keep the plate properties and are stress-relieved. The differences are the certificate standard (plate rather than bar) and saw-cut edges that may need machining.

How much does 7075 flat bar weigh?

Width × thickness (mm) × 0.00281 gives kg per metre. A 50 × 10 mm bar weighs about 1.41 kg/m and a 100 × 20 mm bar about 5.62 kg/m.

Do you supply 7075 flat bar to AMS specifications?

Yes. We supply to AMS, ASTM and EN specifications with mill certificates, and ship worldwide on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
