4B91 T4 / T6 Aluminium Profile for Automotive Structural Parts

4B91 is a grade name that appears on some automotive drawings and supplier lists for heat-treatable structural extrusions supplied in T4 and T6. It is not registered with the Aluminum Association or in EN 573-3, so the governing chemistry must come from the specification named on your drawing. Metline supplies structural automotive profiles to that specification, or in registered alloys such as 6061, 6082 and 6005A, to buyers worldwide.

Overview

What is 4B91, and how should buyers treat it?

In the Chinese GB numbering system a designation beginning with "4" indicates an alloy in which silicon is the main alloying element, and a letter in the second position marks a national variant rather than an international registration. 4B91 follows that pattern, but we have not been able to confirm it in any public edition of GB/T 3190 or in the published grade lists of the automotive extrusion standard GB/T 33910.

That matters for purchasing. A designation that is not in a public standard can mean an internal company grade, a draft or regional registration, or a transcription error for a nearby grade such as 4B01. Product sheets circulating for 4B91 show a chemistry (silicon up to 0.5 %, magnesium up to 1.2 %) that reads like a 6xxx alloy, not a silicon-led 4xxx alloy, and quote T4 strengths that are higher than any common Al-Mg-Si alloy reaches in T4. We therefore recommend confirming the source document before ordering.

The part itself is usually familiar: a T4 or T6 structural profile for frames, brackets, reinforcements and chassis members. Those requirements are fully covered by registered alloys with decades of automotive use, so a buyer who cannot obtain a reliable 4B91 specification is rarely stuck. The rest of this page explains how to verify the grade, what properties to expect from this class of material, and which registered alloys fit.

Before you order 4B91

  • Ask the drawing owner for the standard and edition that defines 4B91 chemistry and properties
  • Check whether 4B01 (listed in GB/T 33910) was intended
  • If no specification exists, agree a registered alloy such as 6005A, 6061 or 6082 instead
  • Have the substitution approved by the part designer, especially for crash-relevant parts
Specification

How to verify 4B91 chemistry and properties

  1. Find the defining documentThe drawing or material specification should name the standard that registers the grade. A grade without a document cannot be certified meaningfully.
  2. Compare chemistry against the alloy familyA "4" designation should show silicon as the largest addition. If the limits show magnesium greater than or comparable to silicon, the material is in effect a 6xxx alloy and should be compared with 6061 or 6082.
  3. Check the property tableT4 properties must be physically reasonable: for Al-Mg-Si alloys, T4 yield strength is typically well below T6, often around half of it.
  4. Agree the certificate wordingDecide whether the certificate will state 4B91 against the customer specification, or a registered alloy against ASTM B221, EN 755-2 or GB/T 33910.
  5. ValidateFor structural parts, run first-article tensile, bend and, where relevant, crush tests before series supply.

Tolerances & quality

Tolerances and inspection

Unless the drawing states tighter values, profile dimensions follow the general tolerance tables of the order standard. Automotive programmes often add their own limits on critical features such as mounting faces, wall thickness at crush zones and twist over the part length.

Tolerance and inspection references

Working with structural profiles

Forming, joining and finishing T4 / T6 profiles

Forming in T4

Bend or stretch-form while ductile, then age to T6. The forming window shortens as natural ageing proceeds over the first days after quenching.

Welding

MIG and TIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. Allow for a softened heat-affected zone in the strength calculation.

Riveting and bonding

Self-piercing rivets, flow-drill screws and structural adhesives are common in mixed-material bodies.

Machining

T6 machines cleanly; T4 is gummier. Use sharp carbide tools with coolant.

Surface treatment

Anodising, e-coat, powder coat and wet paint are all suitable; anodised colour depends on silicon and iron content.

Corrosion

Good general corrosion resistance; isolate from steel fasteners in wet areas to avoid galvanic attack.

Applications

Typical automotive uses of T4 / T6 structural profiles

  • Body structure: door frames, roof bows, pillar reinforcements and front, middle and rear frame members
  • Chassis and suspension: subframe members and brackets; forged parts are usually chosen for arms and knuckles
  • Powertrain mounts: radiator supports, brackets and lifting lugs
  • EV battery packs: tray frames, cross members and module rails
  • Interior structure: seat frames, pedal brackets and instrument-panel carriers

FAQ

4B91 automotive aluminium (aluminum) profile: frequently asked questions

Is 4B91 a standard aluminium alloy?

We could not confirm it in public editions of GB/T 3190 or GB/T 33910, and it is not an Aluminum Association or EN grade. Ask the drawing owner for the document that defines it.

What can I use instead of 4B91?

For T4 / T6 structural profiles, 6005A, 6061 and 6082 are the usual registered alloys. The choice depends on the strength, extrudability and joining method required, and the designer should approve it.

Could 4B91 be a typo for 4B01?

It is possible. 4B01 is listed in GB/T 33910 for automotive extrusions. Check with the drawing owner before ordering either grade.

What do T4 and T6 mean?

T4 is solution heat-treated and naturally aged, giving a ductile condition for forming. T6 is solution heat-treated and artificially aged, giving near-peak strength.

Can you certify profiles to a customer specification?

Yes. If you provide the specification, we certify chemistry and properties against it on an EN 10204 3.1 certificate.

Do you supply worldwide?

Yes. Metline ships automotive profiles worldwide from Mumbai on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms with export packing.
