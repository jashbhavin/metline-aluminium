5083 O / H111 / H32 Aluminium Sheet for Vehicle Bodies

5083 is the strongest of the common non-heat-treatable aluminium alloys, with 4.0 – 4.9 % magnesium. It keeps most of its strength after welding and resists salt and moisture, which makes it the standard choice for welded truck and trailer bodies, tankers, tipper bodies, chassis members and armoured vehicles. Metline supplies 5083 sheet and plate in O, H111, H32 and related tempers to ASTM B209, EN 485 and GB/T 33227, worldwide.

Overview

Where 5083 fits in vehicle design

5083 combines high magnesium with manganese and a little chromium. In the annealed state it is about 60 % stronger than 5052, and because its strength does not depend on heat treatment, welded joints keep close to the strength of annealed parent metal. Few other aluminium alloys combine that strength, weldability and corrosion resistance.

Those properties point 5083 towards heavy, welded vehicle structures rather than pressed car-body panels. Typical uses are tipper and dump bodies, tanker shells for fuel and bulk liquids, flatbed and trailer floors and side walls, chassis cross members for lightweight trucks, rail and bus structures, and armour plate for military vehicles. In passenger cars it appears mainly in specialised parts such as fuel tanks, reinforcement plates and some structural brackets.

For visible, deep-drawn car panels 5083 is less suitable. High-magnesium alloys show stretcher-strain markings (Lüders bands) after forming, and alloys above about 3 % magnesium can become sensitised to intergranular corrosion after long exposure above roughly 65 °C. That is why car inner panels use the specially processed 5182 or the leaner 5754, and outer panels use 6xxx alloys.

Why buyers specify 5083 for vehicles

  • Highest strength of the standard non-heat-treatable alloys
  • Welded joints retain most of the parent strength
  • Very good resistance to salt spray, moisture and many chemicals
  • Good toughness and impact behaviour, including at low temperature

Working with 5083

Welding, forming and corrosion design

Welding

MIG and TIG with ER5183 filler, which matches the strength of 5083. Welded O-temper joints retain close to full parent strength; H32 joints soften to about O-temper level in the heat-affected zone.

Forming

O and H111 bend to about 1 – 1.5 × t in thin gauges; H32 needs larger radii. Expect Lüders bands on stretched surfaces.

Temperature limit

Avoid long service above about 65 °C, for example next to exhaust systems or in hot-asphalt bodies; 5454 is the usual alternative for warm service.

Machining

Moderate. Use sharp carbide tools; chips are long and stringy in O temper.

Surface treatment

Paint and powder coat after conversion coating; anodising gives a slightly grey film.

Galvanic contact

Isolate from steel fasteners and frames with insulating washers, sealants or coatings.

Applications

Where 5083 is used in vehicles

  • Tipper and dump bodies: floors and side walls that resist abrasion and impact
  • Tankers: shells, heads and baffles for fuel and liquid transport
  • Trailers and flatbeds: floors, side walls and cross members
  • Fuel tanks: welded tanks for trucks and off-road vehicles
  • Chassis parts: cross members and reinforcement plates on lightweight trucks and buses
  • Protected vehicles: armour hulls and panels to defence specifications

Wheel hubs and engine blocks are sometimes listed for 5083 in sales literature. Wheels are cast (A356) or forged (6061/6082), and engine blocks are cast; 5083 sheet and plate are not used for them.

FAQ

5083 automotive aluminium (aluminum) sheet: frequently asked questions

Is 5083 used for car body panels?

Rarely for passenger-car panels, because of stretcher-strain marks and warm-service sensitisation. It is the standard alloy for welded truck, trailer, tipper and tanker bodies.

What is the difference between 5083-O, H111 and H32?

O and H111 are annealed (275 – 350 MPa tensile). H32 is strain-hardened and stabilised to 305 – 380 MPa tensile with a much higher yield strength of at least 215 MPa.

Can 5083 be used near exhausts or hot cargo?

Long exposure above about 65 °C can sensitise 5083 to intergranular corrosion. Use 5454 or another lower-magnesium alloy for warm service.

What filler is used for welding 5083?

ER5183 is standard because it matches 5083 strength; ER5356 is also used where slightly lower weld strength is acceptable.

Is 5083 cast plate used for engine parts?

No. Cast 5083 tooling plate is used for fixtures, jigs and machine tables because it is flat and stable. Engine blocks are cast in foundry alloys.

Do you ship 5083 sheet worldwide?

Yes. Metline supplies 5083 sheet and plate worldwide from Mumbai with EN 10204 3.1 certificates, on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
