7021 T6 Automotive Aluminium Profile

7021 is one of the strongest extrusion alloys in the weldable Al-Zn-Mg group, with typical T6 yield strength near 390 MPa. Vehicle makers use it where a beam must be lighter than a 7003 or 6082 design can achieve, such as bumper beams, sill reinforcements and battery-protection members. Overview

What is 7021 aluminium and why use it in vehicles?

7021 carries about 5.5 % zinc and 1.5 % magnesium, roughly 7 % in total. That is near the practical upper limit for an alloy that is still extruded into automotive hollows, and it is why 7021 sits above 7003 and 7005 on strength while remaining far easier to process than copper-rich aerospace 7075.

Zirconium (0.08 – 0.18 %) is the only grain-control addition; chromium and manganese are kept very low. Zirconium holds a fibrous, unrecrystallised structure in the extruded section, which improves toughness and reduces sensitivity to stress corrosion and to quench rate. Copper is restricted to 0.25 % maximum.

The price of the higher alloy content is lower extrusion speed, a narrower process window and a higher need for SCC control. In practice, 7021 is chosen for parts where the weight saving justifies that care: a 7021 bumper beam can be made thinner than a 7003 beam with the same bending capacity, saving material across a high-volume vehicle programme.

When 7021 makes sense

  • Beams and reinforcements that are sized by yield strength and bending
  • Programmes where every kilogram matters, such as EV body and battery structures
  • Designs that can tolerate a slightly simpler section than 6xxx would allow
  • Suppliers able to control quench, ageing and residual stress for SCC

Tempers

7021 tempers

T6

Solution heat-treated (or press-quenched with water), then artificially aged to peak strength. The standard temper for beams sized by strength.

T5

Press-quenched and aged; possible on thinner sections when the quench at the press is fast enough.

Over-aged (T7x)

A second ageing step beyond peak lowers strength somewhat but improves stress-corrosion and exfoliation resistance and crash ductility. Many OEM specifications for 7xxx crash parts call for this type of practice.

Working with 7021

Welding, forming and corrosion control

Welding

Weldable by MIG and TIG with 5xxx filler (ER5356, ER5183), but the higher zinc and magnesium make it more prone to hot cracking than 7003 or 7005. Many crash structures in 7021 are therefore joined mechanically, by self-piercing rivets, flow-drill screws or bolts, rather than welded.

Stress corrosion

Like all Al-Zn-Mg alloys, 7021 can crack under sustained tensile stress in damp or salty service if the temper, residual stress and grain structure are wrong. Specify a controlled ageing practice, avoid cold forming after ageing, keep assembly stresses low, and ask for SCC test data (for example ASTM G47 alternate immersion) on critical parts.

Forming and bending

Stretch bending and rotary-draw bending are best done in the freshly quenched (W) or T4 condition, then aged to T5 or T6. Bending in the fully aged condition needs generous radii and raises residual stress.

Machining

Machines more cleanly than 6xxx alloys in the aged condition, with short chips and a good finish using sharp carbide tools and flood coolant.

Surface finishing

Accepts anodising, e-coating and powder coating. Zinc-rich alloys anodise with a slight yellow-grey tint, so check colour needs for visible parts. Coatings also add a barrier against galvanic and stress corrosion.

Joining to steel

Isolate 7021 from steel, carbon fibre and copper with sealants, coatings or insulating washers, and use coated fasteners, to avoid galvanic corrosion in wet road environments.

Surface damage

Scratches and dents act as stress raisers and corrosion sites on a highly stressed beam. Protect profiles in handling and transport and coat cut edges where specified.

FAQ

7021 automotive aluminium (aluminum) profiles: FAQ

How strong is 7021-T6?

Typical values are about 460 MPa tensile and 390 MPa yield, with about 9 % elongation. Minimum tensile strength commonly specified for sections up to 20 mm is about 410 MPa.

Can 7021 be welded?

Yes, with 5xxx filler wire, but it is more prone to hot cracking than 7003 or 7005. Many crash parts in 7021 are riveted, screwed or bolted instead.

What is the difference between 7021 and 7003?

7021 contains more magnesium (1.2 – 1.8 % against 0.5 – 1.0 %), which raises strength by roughly a quarter but makes extrusion slower and stress-corrosion control more demanding.

Is 7021 used for car wheels or brake callipers?

Not normally. Wheels are cast (A356) or forged (6061, 6082), and callipers are cast or forged. 7021 is used for extruded beams and reinforcements.

What surface treatments suit 7021 profiles?

E-coating, powder coating and anodising. Coatings also act as a barrier against galvanic and stress corrosion.
