6053 T6 Aluminium Automotive Profile, Rod and Wire

6053 is a magnesium-rich 6xxx alloy with chromium, best known as the material for solid and tubular rivets and other cold-headed parts. In vehicles it is used as drawn rod, wire and small extruded sections that are headed, riveted or machined into fasteners and fittings, then aged to T6 or T61. Overview

What is 6053 aluminium, and where does it fit in a vehicle?

6053 belongs to the same Al-Mg-Si family as 6061, but its chemistry is tuned for cold working rather than for maximum strength. Magnesium is higher (1.1 – 1.4 %), silicon is balanced against the magnesium, and iron is held lower than in 6061. The cleaner, more ductile structure lets wire and rod be upset into rivet heads and fastener shapes without cracking, then heat-treated to useful strength.

That makes 6053 a component alloy rather than a body-structure alloy. In the automotive and wider transport sector it appears where aluminium parts are riveted or headed: solid rivets in bus, coach and truck bodies, tubular rivets in trim and interior assemblies, cold-headed pins and studs, and small turned fittings. Using an aluminium rivet in an aluminium structure also avoids the galvanic couple created by steel or copper fasteners.

For large load-bearing extrusions such as crash rails, sills and battery-tray frames, engineers normally specify 6061, 6082 or 6005A, which are designed for heavy extrusion and offer higher guaranteed strength in thick sections. This page explains where 6053 is the right choice and where another alloy will serve better.

Why manufacturers choose 6053

  • Excellent cold-heading and upsetting behaviour for rivets and fasteners
  • Heat-treatable to T6 or T61 after forming for good shear strength
  • Good general corrosion resistance and a close galvanic match to aluminium structures
  • Lower iron than 6061 gives more formability with only slightly lower strength
Chemical composition

6053 chemical composition (wt %)

6053 is unusual in that its silicon limit is defined as a proportion of the magnesium actually present. This keeps the two elements close to the balanced ratio for Mg2Si, which favours ductility in cold heading. Tempers

Choosing the right 6053 temper

O (annealed)

Softest condition, used for heading complex shapes. Parts are solution treated and aged after forming to reach T6.

T4

Solution treated and naturally aged. Rivets are sometimes driven in this condition and age further in service, or are aged after installation.

T61

The usual condition for rivet wire to ASTM B316: solution treated and artificially aged to a strength that still allows the rivet to be driven.

T6

Full artificial ageing for maximum strength in machined or headed fittings that are not upset after heat treatment.

If you are replacing a steel rivet or fastener, check the required shear load carefully. Aluminium rivets have a fraction of the shear strength of steel, so the joint usually needs more or larger rivets.

Working with 6053

Heading, riveting, machining and finishing

  • Cold heading: head in O or T4 condition with a light lubricant; upset ratios are good, but very deep upsets should be trialled before production
  • Riveting: drive rivets promptly after solution treatment, or use T61 stock within the driving range given by the rivet standard
  • Machining: machines well in T6; use sharp, polished tools to avoid built-up edge on the softer tempers
  • Welding: weldable by TIG and MIG with 4043 or 5356 filler, though 6053 is rarely chosen for welded parts
  • Anodising: responds well to clear and coloured anodising, useful for visible trim rivets
  • Corrosion: low copper gives good resistance in road-salt environments, especially when anodised

FAQ

6053 aluminium (aluminum): frequently asked questions

What is 6053 aluminium used for?

Mainly solid and tubular rivets, cold-headed fasteners and small fittings. Its lower iron and balanced Mg-Si chemistry give better cold-heading behaviour than 6061, at slightly lower strength.

Is 6053 suitable for structural automotive extrusions?

It is not the usual choice. Large structural profiles such as crash rails and battery frames are made in 6061, 6082 or 6005A, which are designed for heavy extrusion and have higher guaranteed strength.

Why is the silicon limit for 6053 given as a percentage of magnesium?

The registration ties silicon to 45 – 65 % of the actual magnesium content so the alloy stays close to the balanced Mg2Si ratio. That limits free silicon and helps ductility during cold heading.

What is the difference between 6053-T6 and T61?

Both are solution treated and artificially aged. T61 is aged to a condition suited to rivet wire under ASTM B316, while T6 is aged for maximum strength in parts that will not be upset after heat treatment.

Which standard covers 6053 rivet wire?

ASTM B316 / B316M, the specification for aluminium and aluminium-alloy rivet and cold-heading wire and rods.

Can Metline supply 6053 outside India?

Yes. We ship rod and wire worldwide with mill certificates and export packing on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
