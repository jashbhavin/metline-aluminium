6082 T5 and T6 Aluminium Automotive Extrusions

6082 is the strongest of the mainstream 6xxx extrusion alloys and the European standard for load-bearing aluminium sections. In vehicles it is used for bumper beams and crash boxes, sills, sub-frame members, battery-tray frames, seat structures and commercial-vehicle bodies and chassis. Metline supplies 6082 profiles, tubes and bars to drawing in T4, T5, T6 and crash-optimised tempers, cut, machined, bent and finished, to customers worldwide.

Overview

Why 6082 is used for structural vehicle extrusions

6082 has the highest magnesium and silicon content of the common 6xxx extrusion alloys, plus 0.4 – 1.0 % manganese. The extra alloying gives a T6 yield strength of at least 250 – 260 MPa, and the manganese keeps a fine, fibrous grain structure that improves toughness and crash behaviour. The price is extrudability: 6082 needs higher pressure and lower speeds than 6060 or 6063, and very thin walls are harder to achieve.

For vehicle structures those trade-offs are usually worth it. Bumper beams, crash boxes and sill sections must absorb energy by folding progressively without cracking; sub-frame and battery-frame members must carry high static and fatigue loads; commercial-vehicle bodies must stay stiff over long service lives. 6082 meets all of these and is readily welded, machined and bent.

In Europe 6082 largely replaced 6061 for structural extrusions, and the two are close equivalents in strength. Specialist crash alloys such as 6008 or 7003 are used on some programmes, but 6082 remains the reference against which they are compared.

What 6082 extrusions bring to a vehicle structure

  • Highest T6 strength of the common 6xxx extrusion alloys
  • Manganese-controlled grain structure for toughness and crash folding
  • Good weldability by MIG, TIG, friction-stir and laser
  • Good corrosion resistance thanks to low copper

Tempers

T4, T5, T6 and crash tempers

T4

Solution treated and naturally aged. Used for stretch- or press-bent members that are aged to T6 after forming.

T5

Press-quenched and aged. Economical for thin-walled sections where press quenching reaches enough strength.

T6

Fully solution treated (or water press-quenched) and aged. The standard for highly loaded profiles.

T7-type (over-aged)

Aged beyond peak strength to improve ductility and crash folding, and to reduce sensitivity to stress corrosion. Specified for some crash boxes and bumper beams.

For crash parts, the drawing usually adds axial crush or three-point bend tests on sample lengths, because tensile results alone do not show whether a section folds cleanly or cracks.

Mechanical properties are governed by EN 755-2 and tolerances by EN 755-9 for profiles (EN 755-8 for porthole tubes). 6082 is less common in North American standards, so US programmes often specify 6061-T6 instead; Metline can certify to either.

Working with 6082

Welding, bending, machining and joining

  • MIG and TIG welding: use ER5356 or ER5183 for strength, or ER4043 to reduce hot-cracking risk; the heat-affected zone falls to roughly half of T6 strength unless re-aged
  • Friction-stir welding: common for battery-tray floors and wide panels made from joined extrusions
  • Bending: tight bends in T4 or freshly quenched condition; T6 needs generous radii and stretch bending
  • Machining: T6 gives short chips and good finish; slightly more abrasive than 6061 due to higher silicon
  • Mechanical joining: flow-drill screws and self-pierce rivets join profiles to castings and sheet; check rivetability in T6 by trial
  • Corrosion: good resistance; isolate steel fasteners and use sealants at mixed-material joints

FAQ

6082 aluminium (aluminum) automotive extrusions: frequently asked questions

What are the minimum properties of 6082-T6 extrusions?

EN 755-2 requires 290 MPa tensile and 250 MPa yield for profile walls up to 5 mm, and 310 MPa and 260 MPa for walls over 5 up to 25 mm, with 8 – 10 % elongation.

Is 6082 better than 6061 for automotive profiles?

They are near-equivalents. 6082 is slightly stronger in thicker walls and is the European standard; 6061 is common in North America and Asia. Use the alloy your design standard specifies.

Can 6082 be used for crash boxes?

Yes. 6082 is widely used for crash boxes and bumper beams, often in an over-aged T7-type temper to improve folding behaviour. Axial crush tests confirm the performance.

What filler wire should be used to weld 6082?

ER5356 or ER5183 give higher weld strength; ER4043 reduces hot-cracking risk. Expect strength loss in the heat-affected zone unless the assembly is re-aged.

What is the difference between 6082-T5 and T6?

T5 is cooled at the press and aged, giving 270 MPa tensile and 230 MPa yield minimum in thin walls. T6 is fully solution treated and aged, reaching 290 – 310 MPa tensile and 250 – 260 MPa yield.

Do you supply 6082 extrusions worldwide?

Yes. Metline ships 6082 profiles, tube and bar worldwide with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and export packing on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
