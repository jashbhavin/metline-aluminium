High-strength body sheet

6013 T4 / T6 Aluminium Sheet for Auto Body Parts

6013 is one of the strongest 6xxx sheet alloys: in T6 its yield strength approaches 360 MPa, close to that of 2024-T3, yet it remains weldable and far more corrosion resistant. It is used for high-strength body reinforcements, structural brackets and performance-vehicle panels. Metline supplies 6013 sheet and blanks in T4 and T6, certified to AMS 4347 and AMS 4216 or the OEM specification, worldwide.

Overview

Why 6013 is used for demanding body parts

6013 was developed as a 6xxx alternative to 2024 for aircraft fuselage skin: strong, but weldable and with better corrosion resistance. Its high magnesium, silicon and copper content (around 1 % each) and a manganese addition give a large ageing response. In automotive use, that strength lets engineers make thinner, lighter reinforcements and structural parts than with 6016 or 6061.

The trade-off is formability and cost. 6013-T4 is formable, but less so than 6016 or 6014, and its copper content means it is not chosen for visible outer panels on mass-market cars, where filiform corrosion and hemming performance matter. It is a specialist alloy for parts where strength per kilogram justifies the price: door intrusion reinforcements, pillar and roof reinforcements, seat structures, motorsport and premium-vehicle parts.

Parts are usually formed in T4 and then aged. The automotive paint bake gives only part of the strength gain, because the full T6 cycle is longer, so structural parts are often given a separate ageing step after forming.

Where 6013 earns its place

  • Highest strength among common 6xxx sheet alloys
  • Weldable by laser, MIG and friction-stir welding, unlike most 2xxx sheet
  • Better corrosion resistance than 2024
  • Formable in T4, then aged to T6

Fabrication

Forming, ageing and joining 6013

Forming in T4

Allow generous radii (typically 1.5 – 2 × t or more for bends) and avoid sharp draws. Form within the agreed shelf life, as natural ageing raises strength over time.

Ageing

Full T6 strength needs a dedicated ageing cycle (several hours at about 190 °C is typical for this alloy class). A paint bake alone gives a partial increase.

Welding

Laser, MIG, TIG and friction-stir welding are all used. 4xxx fillers reduce hot-cracking risk.

Mechanical joining

Self-piercing rivets work but need die settings suited to the higher strength; check joint quality on trials.

Corrosion

Good when painted or e-coated. As a copper-bearing alloy it is more sensitive to filiform corrosion than 6016, so pre-treatment matters.

Machining

Machines well in T6 and produces short chips; suitable for machined brackets cut from sheet.

FAQ

6013 automotive aluminium sheet: frequently asked questions

How strong is 6013-T6 compared with 6061-T6?

Typical yield is about 340 – 360 MPa for 6013-T6 versus about 276 MPa for 6061-T6, roughly 25 – 30 % higher, with similar weldability.

Is 6013 used for outer body panels?

Rarely. Its copper content and lower formability make copper-free 6016 or 6014 a better choice for visible skins. 6013 is used for reinforcements and structural parts.

Does paint baking give 6013 full T6 strength?

No. A paint bake gives a partial increase. Full T6 needs a dedicated, longer ageing cycle after forming.

Can 6013 aluminum sheet be welded?

Yes. Laser, MIG, TIG and friction-stir welding work well, which is a key advantage over 2024.

What specifications cover 6013 sheet?

AMS 4347 covers 6013-T4 sheet and AMS 4216 covers 6013-T6 sheet and plate; ASTM B209 and OEM specifications are also used.

Do you ship 6013 sheet worldwide?

Yes, with EN 10204 3.1 certificates, interleaving and sealed export packing.
