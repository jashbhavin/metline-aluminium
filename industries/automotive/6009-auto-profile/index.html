6009 Aluminium for Automotive Profiles and Body Parts

6009 is a heat-treatable Al-Mg-Si-Cu alloy introduced for car bodies: formable in the T4 temper, then strengthened by artificial ageing or the paint-bake cycle. It is best known as body sheet, but the same chemistry is extruded into profiles where a part must match 6009 panels or be formed before ageing. Metline supplies 6009 profiles, sheet and blanks in T4 and T6 to drawing, worldwide.

Overview

What 6009 aluminium is

6009 and its sister alloy 6010 were introduced in the mid-1970s as the first purpose-designed 6xxx automotive body alloys. The idea was simple: deliver the metal soft (T4) so it can be stamped or bent like a 5xxx alloy, and let the heat of the paint oven raise its strength so that the finished part resists denting.

6009 balances its silicon and magnesium with a small copper and manganese addition. Copper speeds up the ageing response, so the alloy gains useful strength in a short bake. Manganese controls grain size and helps formability. Its formability in T4 was compared with 5182 at the time, but without the stretcher-strain marks that make 5xxx sheet unsuitable for visible surfaces.

Today most new outer panels use copper-free 6016 or 6014, or 6022 and 6111 in North America. 6009 remains in use on existing designs, for replacement parts and in profiles and formed brackets that must match the alloy of surrounding parts so that they can be welded together and recycled as one scrap stream. Because 6009 and 6010 are so close in chemistry, scrap from the two does not need to be segregated.

Where 6009 still makes sense

  • Existing vehicle programs and service parts already validated in 6009
  • Profiles and brackets formed in T4 and aged in the paint oven with 6009 panels
  • Parts needing better formability than 6061 with a faster ageing response than 6063
  • Projects that want one alloy family across sheet and extrusion for recycling

Fabrication

Forming, ageing and joining 6009

Form in T4

Do the bending, flaring or stamping in T4. The alloy naturally ages slowly at room temperature, so form within the agreed shelf life.

Age after forming

A paint-bake cycle gives part of the strength gain. A dedicated ageing cycle gives full T6 strength for structural parts.

Welding

Spot, MIG and laser welding all work. The low iron and controlled copper give good spot weldability compared with 2xxx body alloys.

Hemming

T4 hems well for closure panels; hem before ageing to avoid cracking.

Corrosion

Copper reduces corrosion resistance compared with copper-free 6016, so painted or e-coated finishes are normal.

Machining

Machines well in T6; T4 is softer and can be gummy, so use sharp tools.

Applications

Typical 6009 automotive parts

  • Body panels and inner panels on programs validated in 6009
  • Formed brackets and reinforcements bent in T4 and paint-baked
  • Bumper face bars and reinforcements on older designs
  • Seat frames and trim structures made from bent tube
  • Service and replacement parts matching the original alloy
  • Truck cab and trailer parts where formability and moderate strength are needed

FAQ

6009 automotive aluminium: frequently asked questions

Is 6009 a sheet alloy or an extrusion alloy?

It was developed mainly as auto body sheet, but the chemistry can be extruded. Profiles in 6009 are used where parts must match 6009 panels or be formed in T4 before ageing.

How strong is 6009-T4?

Typical T4 values are about 230 MPa tensile strength, 125 MPa yield and 25 % elongation. Strength rises after paint baking or full T6 ageing.

What is the difference between 6009 and 6010?

6010 has more silicon and magnesium, so it is stronger but slightly less formable. Their chemistries are close enough that scrap need not be separated.

Why do some data sheets show different 6009 chemistry?

Several published tables are wrong, for example showing manganese up to 1.2 %. The registered limits are Si 0.6 – 1.0, Mg 0.40 – 0.8, Cu 0.15 – 0.6 and Mn 0.20 – 0.8 %.

Can 6009 aluminum parts be welded?

Yes. Resistance spot, MIG and laser welding are all used. Heat input locally reduces the aged strength, as with other 6xxx alloys.
