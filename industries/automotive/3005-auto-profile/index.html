3005 H112 Aluminium Profiles for Automotive Use

3005 is an aluminium-manganese-magnesium alloy that is about 20 % stronger than 3003 while keeping its corrosion resistance, brazeability and good thermal conductivity. As H112 extrusions it is used for coolant tube, header and manifold sections and small brackets in radiators, heater cores and air-conditioning systems. Overview

What is 3005 aluminium, and why is it used in vehicles?

3005 belongs to the 3xxx family, where manganese is the main alloying element. It adds 0.2 – 0.6 % magnesium to the 1.0 – 1.5 % manganese base of 3003, and that magnesium gives a useful step up in strength without making the alloy heat-treatable. Strength therefore comes from the alloy content and, in rolled tempers, from cold work.

In automotive engineering 3005 is best known as heat-exchanger material: radiator and heater tube, header plates, side supports and fin stock are made from it, either bare or as the core of clad brazing sheet. The extruded form covers the parts that are easier to make as a profile than from strip, such as round and oval coolant tube, manifold and header sections, end tanks for small coolers and simple brackets that must survive the brazing furnace alongside the core.

The H112 temper means the profile is used as extruded, with no deliberate cold work or annealing but with tested mechanical properties. That suits brazed assemblies well: any cold-worked strength would be lost in a furnace cycle near 600 °C anyway, so designers work with annealed-level properties for the finished part. Where a part is not brazed, 3005 H112 still offers a little more strength than 3003 at almost the same cost.

Why engineers choose 3005 extrusions

  • Higher strength than 3003 and 1xxx alloys, with similar brazing and welding behaviour
  • Melting range well above controlled-atmosphere brazing temperatures
  • Good resistance to coolant and atmospheric corrosion, especially with zinc-protected designs
  • Thermal conductivity of roughly 160 W/m·K, suitable for heat-transfer parts

Magnesium above roughly 0.3 – 0.5 % can interfere with fluxes used in controlled-atmosphere (Nocolok-type) brazing, because it reacts with the flux. For brazed parts, agree the magnesium level and the flux system with the brazer before ordering.

Tolerances & quality

Tolerances, cleanliness and testing

Unless the drawing states tighter values, profile dimensions follow the general tolerance tables of the order standard. Automotive programmes often add their own limits on critical features such as mounting faces, wall thickness at crush zones and twist over the part length. Heat-exchanger parts also need clean internal surfaces: extrusion debris left inside a manifold or tube can block channels or contaminate the brazing flux.

Coolant-side and external corrosion life is typically proven with tests such as SWAAT (ASTM G85 Annex A3) or neutral salt spray (ASTM B117), with hours set by the vehicle maker.

Working with 3005

Brazing, welding, bending and finishing

Brazing

3005 brazes well with 4343 or 4045 filler from clad sheet or paste. Control magnesium (see note above) when using non-corrosive flux. Post-braze strength is roughly that of the O temper.

Welding

TIG and MIG welding are straightforward with ER4043 or ER1100-type fillers. The heat-affected zone softens to annealed strength, which is close to H112 anyway.

Bending and forming

H112 and O tube bend readily with mandrel support; flaring, beading and end-forming of coolant tube are routine.

Machining

Soft and gummy like other 3xxx alloys. Use sharp, high-rake tools and good lubrication for clean cuts and threads.

Surface finishing

Accepts powder coat, wet paint and conversion coatings. Anodising gives a slightly greyer film than 6xxx alloys because of the manganese.

Corrosion protection

Zinc-rich surfaces, sacrificial fins or coatings are commonly used in heat-exchanger designs to delay pitting perforation.

Applications

Where 3005 profiles are used in vehicles

  • Radiators and heater cores: coolant tube, header and side-plate sections, and small end tanks for brazed cores
  • Air-conditioning systems: manifold and receiver-drier sections and connection tube in brazed condenser assemblies
  • Coolant and oil lines: bent and end-formed tube for engine, transmission and EV thermal circuits
  • EV battery thermal management: manifolds and distribution tube feeding cold plates
  • Brackets for brazed assemblies: mounting parts that must go through the brazing furnace with the core
  • Trim and covers: light-duty trim strips and covers where a painted 3xxx alloy is sufficient

Exhaust systems are sometimes listed for 3005, but in practice exhaust pipes run far too hot for aluminium alloys; aluminised or stainless steel is used there. 3005 belongs on the coolant and air-conditioning side of the vehicle.

FAQ

3005 automotive aluminium (aluminum) profiles: frequently asked questions

What does H112 mean for 3005 profiles?

H112 is the as-fabricated condition with tested mechanical properties: the profile is used as it comes off the extrusion press, without deliberate strain hardening or annealing. Its properties are close to those of the annealed O temper.

Is 3005 stronger than 3003?

Yes. The 0.2 – 0.6 % magnesium addition makes 3005 roughly 15 – 25 % stronger than 3003 in the same temper, while keeping similar corrosion resistance and formability.

Can 3005 extrusions be brazed?

Yes, with standard Al-Si fillers. Because magnesium reacts with non-corrosive fluxes, check the magnesium level against your flux system; many brazers specify the lower part of the range for controlled-atmosphere brazing.

Can 3005 be heat treated to increase strength?

No. It is non-heat-treatable. Strength comes from manganese and magnesium in the alloy and from cold work in rolled tempers; heat treatment only softens it.

Is 3005 used for exhaust pipes?

No. Exhaust gas temperatures are far beyond what aluminium alloys tolerate. 3005 tube is used on the coolant, oil and air-conditioning side of the vehicle.

Do you ship 3005 profiles internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies 3005 extrusions worldwide from Mumbai with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and export packing, on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
