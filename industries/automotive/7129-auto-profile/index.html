7129 T4 / T6 / O Automotive Aluminium Profile

7129 is an aluminium-zinc-magnesium-copper extrusion alloy developed for automotive bumpers. It offers strength close to 7021, with a small copper addition that gives a surface suited to plating, painting and clear-coating. Overview

What is 7129 aluminium?

7129 is a registered Aluminum Association alloy with about 4.7 % zinc, 1.6 % magnesium and 0.7 % copper. It was developed alongside 7029 as a bumper alloy: strong enough for impact beams, and with a surface that takes chrome plating, paint or clear coat well. Of the pair, 7129 is the one normally chosen where a decorative or bright finish is needed.

The copper addition sets 7129 apart from copper-free crash alloys such as 7003 and 7021. Copper raises strength and improves resistance to stress corrosion in the peak-aged condition, but it also makes the alloy harder to weld and slightly less resistant to general corrosion. 7129 is therefore usually joined mechanically and protected by its finish.

Some older product listings describe 7129 with 1.2 – 1.5 % manganese, 2 – 3 % magnesium and no zinc. Those figures describe a different family of alloy entirely. A genuine 7129 certificate will show zinc as the main alloying element, as in the table below.

Why buyers choose 7129

  • High strength for bumper beams and reinforcements
  • Surface suited to plating, painting and clear coating
  • Can be formed in O or T4 and aged to T6 afterwards
  • Long track record as a dedicated automotive bumper alloy

Tempers

Choosing between O, T4 and T6

7129-O

Fully annealed. Used when a bumper face bar or trim must be formed heavily, for example roll-formed sweep or deep end returns. The part is then solution heat-treated and aged to reach full strength.

7129-T4

Solution heat-treated and naturally aged. Bends and stretch-forms more easily than T6 and is aged to T6 after forming. Room-temperature hardening continues for some weeks, so form within the agreed window.

7129-T6

Peak-aged for maximum strength. Supplied straight for beams that need only cutting, punching and machining, and as the final condition of formed parts.

Working with 7129

Forming, joining and finishing

Welding

Fusion welding of 7129 is possible but not recommended for structural joints: the copper content increases hot-cracking sensitivity. Use bolts, self-piercing rivets, flow-drill screws or adhesive bonding.

Stress corrosion

Like all Al-Zn-Mg alloys, 7129 can crack under sustained tensile stress in damp or salty service if the temper, residual stress and grain structure are wrong. Specify a controlled ageing practice, avoid cold forming after ageing, keep assembly stresses low, and ask for SCC test data (for example ASTM G47 alternate immersion) on critical parts.

Forming and bending

Stretch bending and rotary-draw bending are best done in the freshly quenched (W) or T4 condition, then aged to T5 or T6. Bending in the fully aged condition needs generous radii and raises residual stress.

Machining

Machines more cleanly than 6xxx alloys in the aged condition, with short chips and a good finish using sharp carbide tools and flood coolant.

Surface finishing

Accepts anodising, e-coating and powder coating. Zinc-rich alloys anodise with a slight yellow-grey tint, so check colour needs for visible parts. Coatings also add a barrier against galvanic and stress corrosion.

Joining to steel

Isolate 7129 from steel, carbon fibre and copper with sealants, coatings or insulating washers, and use coated fasteners, to avoid galvanic corrosion in wet road environments.

Bright finishing

For plated or polished parts, specify surface class and protect profiles from handling marks. Polish, then apply nickel-chrome plating, paint or clear coat to the finisher's process.

Applications

Automotive applications of 7129

  • Bumper beams and face bars: the alloy's original purpose, in painted, plated or clear-coated form
  • Bumper reinforcements and brackets: high strength in compact sections
  • Pickup and truck bumpers: where a bright, decorative surface is part of the design
  • Side-impact and door beams: as an alternative to 7003 and 7021
  • Roof rails and exterior trim: structural trim that must also look good

7129 is a wrought extrusion alloy. It is not suited to engine-bay parts with sustained high temperatures, where 7xxx alloys over-age and lose strength, and it is not used for wiring or battery-box sheet.

FAQ

7129 automotive aluminium (aluminum) profiles: FAQ

What is the composition of 7129?

About 4.2 – 5.2 % zinc, 1.3 – 2.0 % magnesium and 0.5 – 0.9 % copper, with low iron and silicon. It is an Al-Zn-Mg-Cu alloy, not an Al-Mn-Mg alloy as some listings suggest.

What is the difference between 7029 and 7129?

They have similar mechanical properties. 7129 is the variant normally specified when the part will be plated, painted or clear-coated.

Can 7129 be welded?

It can be, but structural welding is not recommended because of hot-cracking risk. Mechanical fastening or bonding is preferred.

Why buy 7129 in T4 or O?

To form the part (bending, sweeping, stretch-forming) while it is soft, then heat-treat it to T6 for full strength.

Is there a European equivalent of 7129?

There is no direct EN designation. 7021 and 7003 are the usual European alternatives for crash beams.
