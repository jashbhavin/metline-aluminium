6014 Aluminium Profiles and Extrusions for Automotive

6014 is a lean, vanadium-bearing Al-Mg-Si alloy best known as body sheet for doors, bonnets and tailgates. Extruded in the same chemistry, it gives profiles that match 6014 panels for welding, hemming and closed-loop recycling, with very good formability in T4 and a useful paint-bake response. Metline supplies 6014 profiles to drawing in T4 and T6, worldwide.

Overview

Why extrude profiles in 6014?

Modern aluminium body structures aim to keep as few alloys as possible, so that press-shop and end-of-life scrap can be remelted back into the same product. A closure made from 6014 sheet with a 6014 or other 6xxx reinforcement profile is easier to recycle than one that mixes 5xxx, 6xxx and copper-rich alloys.

6014 contains less silicon and magnesium than 6016 or 6082, plus 0.05 – 0.20 % vanadium for grain control. The lean chemistry gives excellent bendability and hemming in T4 and good crash behaviour, while the paint-bake cycle still lifts yield strength noticeably. In extruded form it suits door frames, window channels, sill and roof profiles, and inner reinforcements that must be bent or hemmed together with sheet.

Strength is lower than 6005A or 6082, so 6014 profiles are used where formability, surface quality and alloy matching matter more than maximum strength. For heavily loaded members, a stronger extrusion alloy is the better choice.

Benefits of 6014 extrusions

  • Same alloy as 6014 body sheet for simpler welding and recycling
  • Very good bendability and hemming in T4
  • Paint-bake hardening gives extra strength in the finished vehicle
  • Good corrosion resistance thanks to low copper

Fabrication

Bending, joining and finishing

Bending and stretch-forming

T4 bends to tight radii without cracking, suitable for door frames and roof profiles.

Hemming with sheet

Profiles can be hemmed or roller-hemmed together with 6014 or 6016 sheet in closures.

Welding

MIG, laser and spot welding. Matching the sheet alloy simplifies weld procedures.

Paint bake

The e-coat and paint ovens raise yield strength; pre-aged material responds faster.

Finishing

Anodises, e-coats and powder coats well; good choice for visible trim-like structural parts.

Corrosion

Low copper gives good resistance to filiform and intergranular corrosion.

Applications

Where 6014 profiles are used

  • Door frames and window channels matched to 6014 door panels
  • Tailgate and bonnet reinforcements hemmed or bonded into closures
  • Roof and sill profiles that are stretch-bent to body contours
  • Inner reinforcements for body structures with recycling targets
  • Exterior trim-structural parts with an e-coated or painted finish
  • Seat and interior structures needing good bendability

FAQ

6014 automotive profiles: frequently asked questions

Is 6014 an extrusion alloy or a sheet alloy?

It is mainly used as body sheet, but it extrudes well. Profiles in 6014 are chosen to match 6014 panels for welding, hemming and recycling.

Why does 6014 contain vanadium?

Vanadium (0.05 – 0.20 %) helps control grain structure, improving toughness and bendability in a lean, low-copper alloy.

How strong are 6014 aluminum profiles?

Moderate. Typical T6 yield is roughly 190 – 220 MPa. For heavily loaded members, use 6005A, 6082 or 6061.

Do 6014 profiles harden in the paint oven?

Yes. Like 6014 sheet, T4 profiles gain yield strength during the paint-bake cycle.

Can you match 6014 profiles to our sheet supplier's chemistry?

We supply to EN 573-3 limits or to your tighter internal ranges if they are stated on the order.
