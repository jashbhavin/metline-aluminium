6060 Aluminium Automotive Profiles (T4, T5, T6, T64, T66)

6060 is a lean Al-Mg-Si extrusion alloy that flows through complex, thin-walled dies at high speed and anodises to a very even finish. In vehicles it is used for trim, roof rails, window and sunroof frames, interior structures and cooling profiles, where appearance, tight tolerances and moderate strength matter more than crash performance. Metline supplies 6060 profiles to drawing in T4, T5, T6, T64 and T66 tempers, cut, machined and finished as required, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is 6060, and why is it used for automotive profiles?

6060 is the leanest of the common 6xxx extrusion alloys. With only about 0.35 – 0.6 % each of magnesium and silicon, it extrudes faster and through more intricate dies than 6063, 6061 or 6082, and it can be press-quenched with air in thin sections. The trade-off is strength: 6060-T6 reaches roughly two thirds of the strength of 6082-T6.

That balance makes 6060 a natural choice for vehicle parts that are seen or touched, or that need complex hollow shapes, but do not absorb crash energy. Bright-anodised roof rails, window surrounds, door sill trims and interior decorative strips are typical. 6060 is also popular for thin-walled cooling and heat-sink profiles in electric vehicles because of its high thermal conductivity.

In Europe 6060 and 6063 are close relatives and often interchangeable on drawings; 6063 has a slightly higher magnesium ceiling and gives a little more strength. Both are covered by the precision-profile tolerance standard EN 12020, which is useful for trim parts that must fit tightly against glass, seals and painted panels.

Why designers choose 6060 for vehicle profiles

  • Very good extrudability for thin walls, fine details and multi-cavity hollows
  • Even, bright anodised finish with low risk of streaking
  • High thermal conductivity for cooling plates and electronics housings
  • Precision tolerances to EN 12020 for parts that mate with seals and glass

Tempers

Which 6060 temper for which part?

T4

Solution treated and naturally aged. Softest heat-treated temper, chosen when the profile will be stretch-bent or roll-formed, then optionally aged to T6.

T5

Press-quenched and artificially aged. The economical everyday temper for trim and frames with walls up to a few millimetres.

T6

Fully solution treated and aged for higher strength. Used for interior structures and rails that carry light loads.

T64

Under-aged for better bendability while keeping more strength than T4. Useful for curved roof rails and window frames.

T66

A T6 with tightly controlled chemistry and process to reach higher minimums. Chosen when 6060 must carry a little more load without changing alloy.

Stretched tempers

Profiles are stretched after quenching to straighten them and reduce residual stress; a T5 or T6 profile is normally stretch-straightened as standard.

FAQ

6060 aluminium (aluminum) automotive profiles: frequently asked questions

Is 6060 strong enough for automotive structures?

For lightly loaded frames, trim and cooling parts, yes. 6060-T6 has a minimum yield of 140 – 150 MPa, which is too low for crash rails, bumper beams or suspension parts; those use 6082, 6005A or 7xxx alloys.

What is the difference between 6060-T6 and T66?

Both are solution treated and artificially aged. T66 uses controlled chemistry and processing to reach higher guaranteed values, for example 215 MPa tensile and 160 MPa yield in walls up to 3 mm instead of 190 and 150 MPa for T6.

Is 6060 the same as 6063?

They are close but not identical. 6063 allows more magnesium and silicon, so it reaches slightly higher strength; 6060 extrudes a little faster. Both anodise well and share the EN 12020 precision tolerance standard.

Which temper should I use for stretch-bent roof rails?

T4 or T64 is normally used for bending, because both retain more elongation. The bent part can then be artificially aged to reach T6-level strength.

Can 6060 profiles be anodised in colour?

Yes. 6060 is one of the best alloys for even clear, black and coloured anodising, which is why it is widely used for visible trim.

Do you export 6060 profiles?

Yes. Metline supplies 6060 extrusions worldwide with mill certificates, protective packing and EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
