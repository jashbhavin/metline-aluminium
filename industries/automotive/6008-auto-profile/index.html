6008 Aluminium Profiles for Automotive Structures

6008 is a 6xxx extrusion alloy with a small vanadium addition, developed for automotive structural and crash-relevant profiles. It extrudes like 6005A, reaches similar or slightly higher strength, and folds progressively under axial crush instead of cracking. Metline supplies 6008 profiles in T4, T6 and agreed overaged tempers to drawing, for bumper systems, crash boxes and body structures worldwide.

Overview

What 6008 is designed to do

A crash box or bumper beam has to absorb a defined amount of energy by folding in a controlled concertina pattern. If the alloy cracks at the fold lines, energy absorption drops sharply and the load on the rest of the structure rises. Standard high-strength 6xxx alloys can crack in this test, especially in thick walls or after full T6 ageing.

6008 addresses this with a vanadium addition of 0.05 – 0.20 %. Vanadium forms fine dispersoid particles that help keep a fibrous, unrecrystallised grain structure through the extrusion and heat treatment. That structure resists the grain-boundary cracking that limits crush performance. The rest of the chemistry is close to 6005A, so 6008 extrudes well in thin-walled, multi-cavity hollows.

Vehicle makers typically specify 6008 for crash-relevant profiles, often in an overaged T7 condition that trades a little yield strength for more ductile folding. Non-crash structural parts may use the same alloy in T6 to keep one alloy across the body structure, which simplifies scrap recycling.

What 6008 offers

  • Progressive folding under axial crush with fewer cracks
  • 6005A-level extrudability for complex hollow sections
  • T6 strength comparable to or above 6005A
  • Good weldability and corrosion resistance for body-in-white use

Fabrication

Forming, joining and finishing 6008

Bending

Bumper beams are often sweep-bent in T4 or the as-quenched state and then aged. Stretch-bending gives consistent radii with minimal springback.

Heat treatment

Ageing time and temperature decide the balance between strength and crush ductility. Keep the agreed cycle constant from lot to lot.

Welding

MIG and laser welding with 4xxx or 5xxx filler. Crash boxes are commonly welded or bolted to the bumper beam and base plate.

Machining

Cut-outs, slots and holes for towing eyes and sensors are machined or punched after ageing.

Corrosion protection

Good corrosion resistance; parts inside the body are e-coated with the body-in-white.

Mixed-material joints

Use self-piercing rivets, flow-drill screws or bonded joints with insulation where 6008 meets steel.

Applications

Where 6008 profiles are used

  • Front and rear bumper beams: multi-cavity sections that bend and absorb low-speed impacts
  • Crash boxes: axially loaded tubes that fold progressively between beam and longitudinal
  • Longitudinal members and sills: energy-absorbing body-structure profiles
  • Battery-tray side impact members: profiles protecting EV battery packs
  • A- and B-pillar reinforcements: where profiles replace stamped parts
  • Subframe members: welded hollow profiles combining stiffness and ductility

A typical front crash-management system uses a stretch-bent 6008 bumper beam joined to two square 6008 crash boxes. In a low-speed impact the crash boxes fold first and can be replaced, protecting the longitudinal members behind them.

FAQ

6008 automotive aluminium profiles: frequently asked questions

Why does 6008 contain vanadium?

Vanadium forms fine dispersoids that help keep a fibrous grain structure after extrusion and heat treatment. That improves the alloy's ability to fold under crush loads without cracking.

Is 6008 stronger than 6005A?

They are similar. Typical T6 yield strengths are close, and both extrude thin-walled hollows well. 6008 is chosen mainly for its crash behaviour rather than for higher strength.

What temper is used for crash boxes?

Often an overaged T7 condition agreed with the vehicle maker. It gives slightly lower yield than T6 but more ductile, crack-free folding.

Can 6008 aluminum profiles be welded?

Yes. MIG and laser welding with 4xxx or 5xxx filler are common. As with other 6xxx alloys, the heat-affected zone loses some strength.

How is crash performance tested?

By axial crush tests on short profile lengths or by three-point bending, rated on fold pattern and cracking. These tests are added to the order when the part is crash-relevant.

Do you ship 6008 profiles internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies 6008 profiles worldwide with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and export packing.

Developing a crash-management system?

Send the profile drawing, ageing practice and test requirement. Our team will reply with feasibility and a delivered price.
