5005 Automotive Aluminium Profiles and Extrusions

5005 is a low-magnesium aluminium alloy (0.5 – 1.1 % Mg) valued for its clean, even response to anodising, good formability and corrosion resistance. As F, O, H111 and H112 extrusions it is used for vehicle trim, window and door surrounds, rails, covers and other lightly loaded parts that must look good for years. Metline supplies 5005 profiles to EN 755, GB/T 33910 and customer drawings, worldwide.

Overview

What is 5005 aluminium?

5005 is the leanest of the common 5xxx alloys. Its magnesium content of 0.5 – 1.1 % is enough to raise strength above 1xxx and 3xxx grades, but low enough that the alloy behaves almost like pure aluminium in forming and finishing. It is non-heat-treatable: strength comes from magnesium in solid solution and, in rolled tempers, from cold work.

The property that sets 5005 apart is its anodising response. Because it contains very little iron-manganese or silicon-rich phases that cloud the oxide film, it gives a clear, uniform anodic coating that matches the colour of 6063 extrusions. That is why architects pair 5005 sheet with 6063 frames, and why vehicle designers use 5005 for trim parts that are anodised and placed next to other bright aluminium.

Extruded 5005 is less common than 5005 sheet, but it has a place in automotive work: thin trim mouldings, window channels, door sill covers, interior strips and rails where a profile shape is needed and appearance after anodising matters more than structural strength. It also suits parts that are bent or flared after extrusion, because O and H111 profiles are very ductile.

Why designers choose 5005 profiles

  • Clear, consistent anodised finish that colour-matches 6063 and 5005 sheet
  • Very good formability for bending, flaring and roll-forming after extrusion
  • Good resistance to road salt splash and atmospheric corrosion
  • Welds easily and needs no heat treatment

Some product literature states that 5005 contains "5 % magnesium". That is incorrect: the limit is 0.5 – 1.1 %. Alloys with around 5 % magnesium are 5083, 5182 and 5056, which behave very differently.

Tempers

Choosing between F, H112, H111 and O

5005-F

As extruded with no property guarantee. Economical for trim where only dimensions and finish matter and the part is not load-bearing.

5005-H112

As extruded with tested minimum properties. The default for automotive profiles that need a certificate showing strength.

5005-H111

Annealed and then lightly straightened. Good when the part will be bent or flared and must also be straight on delivery.

5005-O

Fully annealed for maximum ductility: tight bends, end-forming and roll-forming into curved mouldings.

Tolerances & quality

Tolerances and finish quality

Unless the drawing states tighter values, profile dimensions follow the general tolerance tables of the order standard. Automotive programmes often add their own limits on critical features such as mounting faces, wall thickness at crush zones and twist over the part length. For anodised trim, surface quality is often the critical requirement: agree a limit sample for die lines, streaks and colour variation before series production.

Working with 5005

Anodising, forming, welding and machining

Anodising

Gives a clear, even film suitable for natural, black and coloured finishes. Specify anodising quality material and film thickness (for example 10 – 25 µm for exterior trim).

Bending and flaring

O and H111 profiles bend tightly and flare easily. H112 bends well with appropriate radii.

Welding

Weldable by TIG and MIG with ER5356 filler; weld colour after anodising will differ slightly from the base metal.

Machining

Soft and gummy; use sharp, polished tools with high rake and lubricant.

Painting and coating

Accepts powder coat and wet paint after conversion coating.

Heat treatment

Not heat-treatable. Annealing only restores ductility; it does not add strength.

Applications

Where 5005 profiles are used in vehicles

  • Exterior trim: window surrounds, belt-line mouldings and decorative strips, anodised to match other bright parts
  • Roof rails and rack trims: light-duty rails and end caps where appearance dominates
  • Door sills and scuff plates: anodised or brushed covers
  • Interior trim: dashboard and door inserts, seat trims and console strips
  • Bus, coach and caravan bodies: trim, edge and joint profiles
  • Covers and guards: lightly loaded covers and ducts that are formed after extrusion

5005 is not a structural or crash alloy. For load-bearing parts, use a heat-treatable 6xxx grade such as 6063 or 6082.

FAQ

5005 automotive aluminium (aluminum) profiles: frequently asked questions

How much magnesium does 5005 contain?

0.50 – 1.1 %. It is the leanest common 5xxx alloy, not a 5 % magnesium alloy.

Can 5005 extrusions be heat treated?

No. 5005 is non-heat-treatable. Strength comes from magnesium in solid solution; annealing only softens it.

Why use 5005 instead of 6063 for trim?

When the part is heavily formed after extrusion, or must match 5005 sheet exactly after anodising. Otherwise 6063 is stronger and easier to extrude.

What is the difference between H111 and O?

Both are annealed. H111 has a small amount of strain from straightening or stretching, which keeps long profiles straight; properties are practically the same.

What strength does 5005-H112 extrusion have?

EN 755-2 specifies at least 100 MPa tensile and 40 MPa yield strength for walls up to 25 mm, with elongation of 16 – 18 % or more.

Do you ship 5005 profiles internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies 5005 profiles worldwide from Mumbai with 3.1 certificates and export packing, on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
