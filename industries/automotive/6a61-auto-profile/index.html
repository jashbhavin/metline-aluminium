6A61 T6 Automotive Aluminium Extrusions

6A61 is a Chinese GB designation for a copper-bearing Al-Mg-Si extrusion alloy, broadly in the 6061 family, listed for automotive profiles in GB/T 33910. In T6 it guarantees about 260 MPa tensile and 240 MPa proof strength. Metline supplies 6A61-T6 profiles to drawing, or an agreed 6061 / 6082 alternative, for vehicle programmes worldwide.

Overview

What is 6A61 aluminium?

GB/T 3190 registers a number of national 6xxx variants with a letter in the second position (6A01, 6A02, 6A16, 6A61 and others). 6A61 is one of them: silicon, magnesium and a controlled 0.25 – 0.45 % copper addition give a strong age-hardening response, and chromium controls grain structure during extrusion and solution treatment. Its chemistry and strength put it close to international 6061.

Within the automotive profile standard GB/T 33910, 6A61 is used where designers want more strength than 6063 or 6005A without moving to harder-to-extrude 7xxx alloys. Typical parts are structural brackets, frame members, sub-frame components and battery-tray rails, mostly in relatively compact solid or hollow sections.

Outside China the alloy name is not widely recognised, so export drawings often list 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 as an accepted alternative. We can supply either route and certify accordingly.

Why 6A61 is specified

  • Higher T6 strength than 6063 and 6005A for load-carrying profiles
  • Good machinability and weldability, similar to 6061
  • Good response to anodising and e-coat for corrosion protection
  • Listed in the national automotive profile standard GB/T 33910

Tempers & processing

Tempers and heat treatment

T6

Solution heat-treated (press quench or separate furnace) and artificially aged. The normal delivery condition.

T4 for forming

Profiles can be supplied naturally aged for bending, then artificially aged to T6 after forming.

Quench control

Copper-bearing 6xxx alloys are more quench-sensitive than 6063; thicker sections need water quench to reach T6 minimums.

Ageing

Typical artificial ageing is in the range of about 170 – 180 °C for several hours, set to achieve the drawing properties.

Tolerances & inspection

Tolerances and inspection

Automotive profiles are almost always made to a part drawing, and the drawing tolerances override the general tables. Where the drawing is silent, the general tolerance standard of the order applies. Critical dimensions, such as mating faces, screw bosses and crash-relevant wall thicknesses, should be marked so they can be measured on every lot.

Tolerance and inspection framework

General dimensional tolerances
EN 755-9 for EN orders; ASTM B221 with ANSI H35.2 tables for ASTM orders; GB/T 14846 where a GB drawing applies
Straightness and twist
Per the same standards, or tighter values agreed for long members and parts that go straight into robotic welding
Wall thickness
Measured at drawing-defined points; closed hollow sections are checked for eccentricity and die-line quality
Mechanical testing
Tensile test per lot to EN ISO 6892-1 or ASTM B557, with hardness (Webster or Brinell) for in-process checks
Microstructure
Grain size and recrystallised surface layer checked on request, which matters for crash and bending performance
Documentation
EN 10204 3.1 certificate; PPAP-style first-article dimensional report on request for series parts
Fabrication

Joining, machining and finishing

Like other copper-bearing 6xxx alloys, 6A61 machines cleanly in T6 and can be MIG, TIG, laser or friction-stir welded, with ER4043 or ER5356 filler for arc processes. The heat-affected zone loses a large share of its T6 strength, so crash and load paths are usually designed around mechanical joints, adhesive bonding or welds in low-stress areas. The copper content means painted or anodised finishes are recommended where parts see road salt.

Mill finish

Supplied as extruded and aged, for parts that are welded, bonded or painted later in the body shop or e-coat line.

Anodising

Clear or coloured anodic films for trim, roof rails and visible parts. Film class is specified in micrometres, for example AA15 or AA20 for exterior exposure.

E-coat (electrophoretic paint)

Thin, uniform paint that penetrates hollow sections; often used after anodising for a smooth, durable finish on trim.

Powder coating

Thicker polyester or epoxy layers for chassis brackets, racks and accessories that see stone chipping.

Conversion coating

Chromium-free titanium or zirconium pre-treatment before adhesive bonding or painting, improving bond durability.

Machining and fabrication

Cut-to-length, CNC machining, punching, bending and drilling on request, so profiles arrive ready for assembly.

Applications

Where 6A61 profiles are used

  • Body-in-white structures: reinforcements and cross-members
  • Chassis and sub-frame parts: brackets, links and mounts machined from profiles
  • Battery systems: tray rails, cross-beams and module frames
  • Commercial vehicles: frames, racks and loading equipment
  • Two-wheelers: frames and swing-arm sections

Older literature claims 6A61 for pistons, connecting rods or brake discs. Those parts use cast, forged or specialist materials; 6A61 extrusions are structural and bracket parts.

FAQ

6A61 automotive aluminium (aluminum) extrusions: frequently asked questions

Is 6A61 equivalent to 6061?

It is close: both are copper-bearing Al-Mg-Si alloys with chromium, and their T6 minimums (about 260 / 240 MPa) match. Formal equivalence must be accepted by the part owner.

What standard covers 6A61 automotive profiles?

GB/T 33910 for automotive extruded profiles, with composition in GB/T 3190.

Can 6A61 be welded?

Yes, by MIG, TIG, laser or friction stir welding, with the usual strength loss in the heat-affected zone.

What temper is supplied?

T6 normally; T4 can be supplied for parts that are bent before final ageing.

Can you supply 6061 instead?

Yes. Where the drawing allows, we supply 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 profiles to ASTM B221 or EN 755.
