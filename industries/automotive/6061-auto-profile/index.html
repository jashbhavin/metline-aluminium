6061 T4, T5 and T6 Aluminium Automotive Profiles

6061 extrusions give vehicle engineers a strong, weldable and corrosion-resistant structural section that machines cleanly and accepts anodising or paint. They are used for sub-frame members, cross-car beams, battery-tray frames, drive-shaft tubes, roof and cargo systems and commercial-vehicle bodies. Metline supplies 6061 profiles and tubes to drawing in T4, T5 and T6, cut, machined, bent and finished as needed, to customers worldwide.

Overview

Why 6061 is used for automotive extrusions

Extrusion lets designers place material exactly where it carries load: multi-cell hollows for stiffness, thick flanges for bolted joints, integrated screw bosses and channels for wiring or coolant. 6061 is the most widely specified heat-treatable alloy for such sections outside Europe, because it combines a guaranteed T6 yield of 240 MPa with good weldability, machinability and corrosion resistance.

Compared with the lean alloys 6060 and 6063, 6061 is roughly 40 – 60 % stronger, but it extrudes more slowly and is less suited to very thin walls or bright decorative finishes. Compared with 6082, its European counterpart, 6061 has very similar strength; the choice often depends on the region and the standards in use.

In passenger cars, 6061 profiles are common in sub-frames, seat structures, steering-column and cross-car beams, and battery-tray frames. In commercial vehicles they form truck-body frames, tail-lift structures and trailer rails. 6061-T6 tube is also a long-established material for lightweight drive shafts.

What 6061 profiles offer

  • High guaranteed strength in T6 with good elongation
  • Good weldability by MIG, TIG, friction-stir and laser processes
  • Clean machining for bolt bosses, bores and mounting faces
  • Good corrosion resistance and a reliable anodising response

Tempers

T4, T5 and T6: choosing a temper

6061-T4

Solution treated and naturally aged. Used where profiles will be stretch-bent, hydroformed or crimped before final ageing to T6.

6061-T5

Cooled on the press and artificially aged. Lower cost, suitable for thin sections where press quenching achieves enough strength.

6061-T6

Separately solution treated (or fully press-quenched with water) and aged. The standard structural temper for frames and sub-frames.

6061-T6511

T6 stretched to straighten and relieve stress, with minor straightening allowed. Preferred for bar and profiles that will be heavily machined.

Working with 6061

Welding, bending, machining and finishing

  • Welding: MIG and TIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler; the heat-affected zone drops to roughly T4-level strength, so place welds away from peak stress or re-age the assembly
  • Friction-stir welding: widely used for battery-tray floors and cooling plates, with lower strength loss than fusion welding
  • Bending: bend in T4 or the freshly quenched condition, then age; T6 tolerates only generous radii
  • Machining: T6 and T6511 machine with short chips and good finish using sharp carbide tools
  • Joining: self-pierce rivets, flow-drill screws and structural adhesives join 6061 profiles to sheet and castings
  • Corrosion: isolate from steel fasteners with coatings or washers to avoid galvanic attack in road salt

FAQ

6061 aluminium (aluminum) automotive extrusions: frequently asked questions

What is the minimum strength of 6061-T6 extrusions?

ASTM B221 and EN 755-2 both require 260 MPa tensile and 240 MPa yield strength for 6061-T6 profiles, with minimum elongation of 8 – 10 % depending on wall thickness.

Is 6061-T5 the same strength as T6?

Not necessarily. T5 relies on cooling at the press, which may not fully quench thicker walls, so its properties are agreed per order. Specify T6 when you need the tabulated minimums.

6061 or 6082 for automotive profiles?

They are near-equivalents in strength. 6082 is more common in Europe and slightly stronger in thick sections; 6061 is standard in the Americas and much of Asia. Choose the alloy that matches your design standard and supply chain.

Can 6061 profiles be welded into battery trays?

Yes. 6061 is welded by MIG, TIG and friction-stir welding. Friction-stir welding is common for tray floors because it limits strength loss and distortion.

Can you bend 6061 profiles?

Yes. For tight radii, bend in T4 and age to T6 afterwards. T6 profiles can be stretch- or rotary-bent to more generous radii.

Do you supply 6061 profiles worldwide?

Yes. Metline ships 6061 extrusions worldwide with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and export packing on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
