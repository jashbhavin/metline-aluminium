Brazing Composite Aluminium Sheet and Deep-Drawing Brazing Sheet for Vehicles

Brazing composite sheet is clad aluminium sheet, cut to length rather than coiled, used for the heavier and more deeply formed parts of brazed heat exchangers: stacked plates, headers, tanks, side plates and cold plates. Deep-drawing grades are supplied in the O temper with guaranteed cupping values so they can be pressed into embossed plates without splitting. Metline supplies both, 0.21 – 5 mm thick, to vehicle and heat-exchanger makers worldwide.

Overview

What is brazing composite sheet?

Brazing composite sheet uses the same roll-bonded construction as clad coil: an Al-Si filler layer on one or both faces of a stronger core. The difference is form and purpose. Sheet is supplied flat in cut lengths for presses and transfer lines that make plates, headers and housings, and it is often thicker, up to about 5 mm, for parts that carry pressure or bolts.

Deep-drawing brazing sheet is the specialised version for stacked-plate heat exchangers. Each plate of an engine-oil cooler, transmission cooler or battery chiller is pressed with dimples, ribs and a raised rim, then stacked with others and brazed into a single block. The sheet must stretch and draw without cracking the clad or core, so it is supplied fully annealed and tested for cupping height.

Many sheet structures add a third layer. A 7072 liner gives sacrificial protection on the coolant side; a second filler layer allows brazing on both faces; and a 1100 interlayer can slow silicon diffusion into a sensitive core. The structure is written from outside to inside, for example 4343 / 3003 / 7072.

Typical parts made from brazing sheet

  • Oil, transmission and fuel cooler plates
  • Headers, tanks and side plates for radiators and condensers
  • Evaporator and battery-chiller plates
  • EV battery cold plates and inverter cooling plates

Processing

Pressing, cleaning and brazing

Pressing

Use the O temper for deep embossing; lubricate with an evaporative or easily removed oil. Heavy lubricants left on the clad cause brazing defects.

Clad orientation

Mark which face carries the filler. Plates assembled with the clad on the wrong side will not braze.

Cleaning

Degrease after pressing; a clean, oxide-light surface gives even flux coverage and wetting.

Brazing cycle

CAB at roughly 595 – 610 °C with fluoride flux, or vacuum with 4004 / 4104 clad. Keep time above the filler liquidus short to limit erosion of thin plates.

Post-braze ageing

6xxx-core plates gain strength by natural ageing or a short artificial age after brazing.

Leak testing

Brazed assemblies are helium or pressure-decay tested; burst and pressure-cycle tests follow the system maker's specification.

FAQ

Brazing composite aluminium (aluminum) sheet: FAQ

What is deep-drawing brazing sheet?

Clad brazing sheet supplied fully annealed (O temper) with guaranteed elongation and cupping values, so it can be pressed into embossed plates for stacked-plate coolers without cracking.

What is the cupping value?

The depth a sheet can be stretched by a spherical punch before it cracks, measured in millimetres. Brazing sheet on 3xxx cores typically requires 7 – 10 mm depending on thickness.

When should a 6951 core be used?

When the brazed part needs more strength than a 3003 core can give. 6951 gains strength by ageing after brazing and is common in vacuum-brazed plates.

Is brazing sheet used for car body panels?

No. It is designed for brazed heat exchangers. Body panels use unclad 5xxx and 6xxx sheet.

How is the clad side identified?

By marking or by agreed orientation in the pack. Always confirm clad orientation for one-side clad sheet before pressing.

What thickness range is available?

From about 0.21 mm to 5 mm, in widths up to 1600 mm and lengths up to 10 m.
