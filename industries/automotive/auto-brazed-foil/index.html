Fin stock for brazed heat exchangers

3003 / 4343 H14 and H16 Automotive Brazing Clad Foil

Brazing clad foil is thin fin stock, typically 0.05 – 0.20 mm, made from a 3xxx core with an aluminium-silicon filler layer on one or both faces. Formed into louvred fins, it supplies the braze metal that joins fins to tubes in radiators, condensers, heater cores and battery coolers. Metline supplies 4343 / 3003 / 4343 and 4045-clad foil in H14 and H16 to automotive heat-exchanger makers worldwide.

Overview

What is brazing clad fin foil?

Fins account for most of the air-side heat-transfer area of a vehicle radiator or condenser, and in a brazed core they also supply most of the filler metal. Each fin is clad on both faces, so wherever a fin crest touches a tube the molten clad forms a small fillet in the furnace. A good fin joint is continuous along every crest; a poor one leaves gaps that cut heat transfer.

The core is usually 3003 or a modified 3xxx alloy. Many fin cores add zinc (often in the region of 1 – 2.5 %) to make the fin electrochemically more active than the tube, so the fins corrode sacrificially and protect the tubes against pitting. Others add zirconium or adjust manganese and silicon to raise strength and sag resistance at brazing temperature.

Fin foil is rolled to very tight gauge control, slit to narrow widths and wound onto coils for high-speed fin mills, which stamp louvres and fold the strip in one pass. Any variation in thickness or temper changes fin height, louvre angle and finally the cooling capacity.

Properties that matter most in fin foil

  • Sag resistance: fins must not collapse while soft at 600 °C
  • Resistance to erosion by molten filler, which can thin or perforate the fin
  • Post-braze strength for pressure cycling and vibration
  • Corrosion potential lower than the tube, so the fin protects it

Design notes

Fin design and brazing considerations

Gauge reduction

Fin gauges have fallen steadily to save weight. Thinner fins need cores with higher sag resistance and tighter clad control, or they collapse or erode in the furnace.

Erosion

Too much clad, too high a peak temperature or too long a time at temperature lets silicon penetrate the core and thin the fin. Match cladding ratio and braze cycle.

Galvanic design

Design the system so that corrosion potentials step down from tube to fin: the fin should be the most anodic part. Zinc in the fin core is the usual tool.

Bare fin alternative

In some designs, bare fins are used with clad tubes or with flux pre-loaded with silicon. This changes which part carries the filler; agree the concept before ordering.

Applications

Where brazing clad foil is used

  • Engine radiators: louvred fins between flat tubes
  • Air-conditioning condensers and evaporators: fins on microchannel tube
  • Heater cores: compact cores in the cabin HVAC unit
  • Charge-air and oil coolers: turbulators and fins in plate-fin cores
  • EV thermal management: battery chillers, inverter and motor coolers, heat-pump exchangers

Brazing fin foil is not used in exhaust or catalytic-converter parts, which run far above the melting range of aluminium filler alloys.

FAQ

Brazing clad aluminium (aluminum) foil: FAQ

What does 3003 / 4343 mean?

A 3003 core with a 4343 aluminium-silicon filler layer. Fin foil is normally clad on both faces, written 4343 / 3003 / 4343.

Should I choose H14 or H16 foil?

H14 forms more easily and suits taller fins; H16 is stiffer and suits very thin gauges and fine louvres. Both anneal during brazing.

Why add zinc to the fin core?

Zinc lowers the corrosion potential of the fin, so it corrodes in preference to the tube and protects the tube from perforation.

What is sag resistance?

The ability of a fin to keep its shape while soft at brazing temperature. Poor sag resistance lets fins collapse, which reduces air flow and heat transfer.

What cladding ratio is typical for fin foil?

Commonly about 5 – 15 % per side. Too little gives weak joints; too much increases erosion of the thin core.

Can you supply narrow slit coils for fin mills?

Yes. Foil can be slit from about 7 mm wide with tight width tolerances and shipped worldwide.
