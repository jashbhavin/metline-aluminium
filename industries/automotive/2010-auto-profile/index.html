2010 Aluminium for Automotive Profiles and Sheet

2010 is a low-copper, heat-treatable aluminium alloy (about 1 % copper and 0.7 % magnesium) registered for automotive body sheet and also produced as extruded profiles. It forms well in T4, hardens in the paint oven and reaches moderate strength in T6. Metline supplies 2010 sheet and extrusions to drawing, with full material certification, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is 2010 aluminium?

2010 was introduced as one of the first-generation aluminium autobody sheet alloys, in the same family as 2008 and 2036. Compared with 2008, it carries more copper and roughly double the magnesium, which gives a higher strength after ageing while keeping good formability in T4.

Although 2010-T4 was tested extensively for body panels, the industry eventually standardised on 6xxx alloys, and 2010 never became a mainstream production sheet. It remains a registered alloy and is still requested for extruded sections, tube and formed parts where its copper-magnesium chemistry gives a useful mix of strength, formability and machinability.

For buyers the practical questions are whether the drawing truly requires 2010, which properties the part must meet, and whether a 6xxx alloy would be easier to source and protect from corrosion. This page answers those questions.

Key characteristics

  • Heat-treatable: soft and formable in T4, stronger after paint bake or T6 ageing
  • More copper and magnesium than 2008 for higher aged strength
  • Machines with short chips, better than most 6xxx alloys in T4
  • Needs paint or coating in corrosive environments, like all copper-bearing alloys

Tempers

Which 2010 temper to order

T4

Solution heat-treated and naturally aged. Choose it for parts that will be bent, stretched or pressed and then painted.

T6

Artificially aged to peak strength. Choose it for extruded or machined components that are not formed afterwards.

T4 + paint bake

The usual in-service condition of formed body parts, between T4 and T6 in strength.

Natural ageing continues for several weeks after quenching, so T4 material formed long after production will be slightly stronger and less formable than fresh material. For critical forming, agree a maximum age from heat treatment.

Working with 2010

Forming, joining and finishing 2010

Forming

Form in the T4 (naturally aged) condition, when yield strength is low and elongation high. Low yield also reduces springback, which is why these alloys were first used for inner panels.

Paint-bake hardening

A paint-bake cycle (around 170 – 180 °C for 20 – 30 minutes) partially ages the metal and raises yield strength in the finished part. Full T6 needs a longer artificial ageing treatment.

Joining

Resistance spot welding, self-piercing riveting, clinching and adhesive bonding are the normal body-shop methods. Fusion welding with 4043 / 4047 filler is possible but more crack-sensitive than 6xxx alloys, so qualify the procedure.

Corrosion protection

Copper lowers corrosion resistance compared with 5xxx and 6xxx grades. Parts need conversion coating, e-coat and paint; avoid unprotected exposure to road salt and contact with bare steel.

Machining

Machines well in T4 and better in T6, with short chips. Use sharp carbide tools and coolant.

Anodising and finishing

Anodises with a slightly yellowish tint because of the copper; powder coating and e-coat are the more common finishes on vehicle parts.

Applications

Typical automotive uses of 2010

  • Formed body components: inner panels and reinforcements pressed in T4 and paint-baked
  • Extruded brackets and rails: parts machined or drilled after T6 ageing
  • Trim carriers and frames: door and seat frames where moderate strength and good machinability help
  • Tube parts: bent tubes for frames and racks that are painted after forming

Some sources list 2010 for brake discs, brake calipers or wheels. Brake discs are cast iron or carbon-ceramic, and calipers and wheels are cast or forged in proven alloys such as A356 and 6061 / 6082. We do not recommend 2010 for those safety parts.

FAQ

2010 automotive aluminium (aluminum): frequently asked questions

What type of alloy is 2010?

A heat-treatable aluminium-copper-magnesium alloy with about 0.7 – 1.3 % copper and 0.4 – 1.0 % magnesium, originally registered for automotive body sheet.

Is 2010 covered by ASTM B221?

It is not one of the alloys tabulated with standard minimum properties in ASTM B221 or EN 755-2. Profiles are made to the registered composition with properties agreed on the drawing, using those standards for tolerances where useful.

What is the difference between 2010 and 2008?

2008 contains 0.5 – 0.8 % silicon and less magnesium, giving the lowest T4 yield and best formability. 2010 has more copper and magnesium and little silicon, giving a higher aged strength.

Does 2010 need corrosion protection?

Yes. Like all copper-bearing aluminium alloys, it should be conversion coated and painted, e-coated or anodised for exposed automotive service.

Can I replace 2010 with 6061?

Often yes, for extruded and machined parts, after checking the strength, forming and joining requirements. 6061 has standardised minimums and better corrosion resistance.

Do you ship 2010 profiles worldwide?

Yes. Metline supplies 2010 to order worldwide with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and export packing.
