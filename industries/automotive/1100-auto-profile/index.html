1100 and 1200 H112 Automotive Flat Aluminium Tube

1100 and 1200 flat tube is a thin, multi-port extruded aluminium tube (often called microchannel or MPE tube) made from commercially pure aluminium in the as-extruded H112 temper. It is the heat-transfer element of brazed automotive condensers, radiators, oil coolers and battery cooling plates. Metline supplies coiled or cut-length flat tube, bare, zinc-sprayed or pre-fluxed, to heat-exchanger makers worldwide.

Overview

What is automotive flat aluminium tube?

A flat (microchannel) tube is a wide, thin extrusion divided internally by webs into a row of small parallel channels, typically between 2 and 35 ports. Refrigerant or coolant flows through the ports while air passes over brazed fins on the flat faces. The many small channels give a very large internal wetted surface in a small envelope, which is why flat tube has replaced round copper tube in almost every modern vehicle condenser.

Commercially pure alloys 1100 and 1200 are the classic choice for flat tube because they extrude through the very complex port dies at high speed, have thermal conductivity above 210 W/m·K and braze reliably in controlled-atmosphere (CAB) furnaces at about 600 °C. Their strength is low, but the small port sizes mean the walls carry refrigerant pressure through geometry rather than material strength.

Tube is extruded continuously, sized, optionally coated with zinc or brazing flux in line, and wound into coils. The heat-exchanger maker straightens and cuts the coil to length, assembles the tubes with louvred fins and headers, and brazes the whole core in one furnace pass.

Why heat-exchanger makers use 1100 / 1200 flat tube

  • Excellent extrudability through multi-port dies with very thin webs
  • High thermal conductivity for compact, efficient cores
  • Clean brazing response with standard 4343 / 4045 clad fins and Nocolok-type flux
  • Lighter and lower in cost than copper-brass designs, and fully recyclable
Chemical composition

1100 and 1200 chemical composition (wt %)

Both alloys are registered with the Aluminum Association and appear in EN 573-3 as EN AW-1100 (Al99,0Cu) and EN AW-1200 (Al99,0). The difference is copper: 1100 contains a deliberate copper addition, while 1200 limits copper to a trace. The certificate for each coil reports the actual analysis.

Tolerances & testing

Tolerances, cleanliness and test requirements

Heat-exchanger tube is judged on dimensional consistency, internal cleanliness and leak tightness rather than on strength alone. A tube that is 0.02 mm too thick will not fit the header slot; one with a blocked port will cut cooling capacity and may fail leak test.

Dimensions

Width and height tolerances are usually tighter than general ASTM B221 / EN 755 tables and are agreed on the drawing, often within a few hundredths of a millimetre on height. Flatness and twist are checked on every coil.

Burst and leak

Tube is burst-tested to the pressure set by the customer for the refrigerant. CO2 (R744) systems run far higher pressures than R134a or R1234yf and need heavier walls or stronger 3xxx alloys.

Corrosion

Corrosion life is commonly proven by acidified salt spray tests such as SWAAT (ASTM G85 Annex A3) or neutral salt spray (ASTM B117), with the hours required set by the vehicle maker.

Coating checks

Zinc and flux loads are measured by weight per unit area; flux adhesion is checked by tape test.

Cleanliness

Ports must be free of extrusion debris and the surface free of oil that would stop flux wetting.

Traceability

Each coil is identified by alloy, lot and date and covered by a 3.1 certificate.

Applications

Where flat aluminium tube is used in vehicles

  • Air-conditioning condensers: the largest use; parallel-flow condensers in front of the radiator
  • Evaporators and gas coolers: including CO2 heat-pump systems in electric vehicles, with heavier wall designs
  • Engine radiators and charge-air coolers: where flat tube or folded tube is brazed to fins and plastic or aluminium tanks
  • Oil and transmission coolers: compact cores for engine oil, gearbox and power-steering fluid
  • EV battery and power-electronics cooling: flat tubes or harp-style plates carrying coolant beneath cells and inverters
  • Off-highway and HVAC: the same tube is used in truck, bus and residential heat exchangers

Older literature sometimes lists flat tube for brake lines. In practice brake pipes are round steel or copper-nickel tube; flat multi-port tube is a heat-transfer product.

FAQ

Automotive flat aluminium (aluminum) tube: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 1100 and 1200 flat tube?

Both are 99.0 % aluminium. 1100 carries 0.05 – 0.20 % copper, which raises strength slightly and makes the tube a little more noble electrochemically; 1200 is essentially copper-free. Many designers pair 1100 tube with zinc spraying or zinc-bearing fins so that the outer layer corrodes sacrificially.

Why is flat tube zinc-sprayed?

During brazing the zinc diffuses into the tube surface and forms a zinc-rich layer that is anodic to the core. Corrosion then spreads sideways through that layer instead of pitting straight through the wall, which greatly delays leaks.

What temper is microchannel tube supplied in?

H112, the as-extruded condition. Brazing at about 600 °C anneals the tube anyway, so finished-core calculations use O-temper properties.

Can 1100 flat tube be used in CO2 (R744) systems?

Only with walls designed for the much higher pressures involved. Most CO2 gas coolers use 3xxx-series tube with heavier walls; the burst pressure on your drawing decides the choice.

How is flat tube supplied?

Usually in coils for in-line straightening and cutting, or as straight cut lengths. Port ends are sealed and coils are packed in moisture-protected cases.

Do you ship flat tube internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies flat tube worldwide from Mumbai with mill certificates, export packing and EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
