5454 H112 Automotive Aluminium Extrusion Profile

5454 is an aluminium-magnesium-manganese alloy with about 2.7 % magnesium, formulated to stay resistant to stress corrosion in service up to roughly 65 – 120 °C, where higher-magnesium alloys become sensitised. As H112 extrusions it is used for chassis and frame members, tanker structures, wheel and brake-area brackets, rails and trim on vehicles. Metline supplies 5454 profiles to ASTM B221, EN 755 and GB/T 33910, worldwide.

Overview

Why 5454 is chosen for vehicle parts

5454 contains 2.4 – 3.0 % magnesium, 0.5 – 1.0 % manganese and a small chromium addition. That magnesium level is deliberately kept below about 3 %, the point above which aluminium-magnesium alloys can precipitate a continuous film of β-phase on grain boundaries during long exposure to warm temperatures. The result is a strong, weldable alloy that tolerates heat far better than 5083 or 5182.

This is why 5454 is the traditional alloy for hot-product tankers (asphalt, heated chemicals), for parts near engines and brakes, and for wheels made from sheet. As an extrusion, it offers the same benefit in profile form: frame members, rails, brackets and tank structures that must be welded, resist corrosion and stay safe in warm service.

H112 is the as-extruded condition with tested properties. Because 5454 is non-heat-treatable, the extruded strength comes from the alloy itself, and welding causes little loss of strength in the heat-affected zone.

Why engineers specify 5454 extrusions

  • Resists sensitisation in warm service where 5083 and 5182 are not recommended
  • Good strength for a non-heat-treatable alloy, with minimal loss after welding
  • Very good corrosion resistance against road salt and moisture
  • Good fatigue behaviour for frame and chassis parts

Working with 5454

Welding, forming and finishing

Welding

ER5554 filler is designed for 5454 in warm service; ER5356 is common where service is at ambient temperature. Avoid fillers above 3 % Mg if the weld will run hot.

Bending

H112 and O profiles bend well with suitable radii; mandrel bending is used for tube.

Machining

Fair; sharp, high-rake tools and good chip clearing are needed.

Corrosion

Very good in salt spray and humid conditions; isolate from steel fasteners.

Finishing

Accepts paint, powder coat and anodising; anodised colour is slightly grey.

Temperature

Recommended for sustained service up to about 120 °C, depending on stress and design code.

Applications

Where 5454 profiles are used

  • Tanker structures: frames, rails and stiffeners for hot-product tankers
  • Chassis and frame members: welded frames on trucks, trailers and buses
  • Wheel- and brake-area brackets: parts exposed to heat from brakes or exhausts
  • Rails and guides: seat rails, sliding-door guides and luggage racks
  • Trim and frames: windscreen and window frames, door trim profiles on commercial vehicles

FAQ

5454 automotive aluminium (aluminum) extrusions: frequently asked questions

Why use 5454 instead of 5083?

Because 5454 keeps its magnesium below 3 %, it does not become sensitised in warm service. 5083 is stronger but is not recommended above about 65 °C.

What strength does 5454-H112 extrusion have?

ASTM B221 specifies at least 215 MPa (31 ksi) tensile and 85 MPa (12 ksi) yield; typical results are higher.

What filler should I use for welding 5454?

ER5554 for parts that will run warm; ER5356 is acceptable at ambient temperature.

Is 5454 heat-treatable?

No. Strength comes from magnesium and manganese in the alloy; annealing only softens it.

Is 5454 suitable for tankers?

Yes. It is the traditional alloy for hot-product tankers, in plate for shells and as extrusions for frames and stiffeners.

Do you supply 5454 profiles internationally?

Yes. Metline ships 5454 extrusions worldwide from Mumbai with EN 10204 3.1 certificates, on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
