1050 Embossed Aluminium Heat Shield Sheet

Embossed aluminium heat shield sheet is thin 1050, 1100, 3003 or 5052 sheet rolled with a raised pattern, then stamped into shields that sit between hot exhaust parts and the vehicle floor, fuel tank, wiring or engine-bay components. Bright aluminium reflects most radiant heat, and the embossing adds stiffness and stops the shield rattling. Metline supplies embossed heat shield sheet and coil in standard and custom patterns to automotive and component makers worldwide.

Overview

How embossed aluminium heat shields work

Heat travels from an exhaust pipe, catalytic converter or turbocharger to nearby parts mainly by radiation. A bright aluminium surface reflects most of that infrared energy instead of absorbing it, and its own low emissivity means it re-radiates little of what it does absorb. A shield placed a few millimetres away therefore keeps the part behind it much cooler.

Embossing does three useful jobs. It stiffens thin sheet so a 0.5 mm shield keeps its shape without heavy ribs; it breaks up the flat surface so the shield resonates less and does not drum or rattle; and it reduces the contact area where a shield touches a mounting or a neighbouring layer, cutting heat conduction. Many shields combine two embossed aluminium skins with an insulating layer between them for higher-temperature locations.

1050 is the most common alloy because it is soft, deep-drawable, highly reflective and inexpensive. 1100 and 3003 add a little strength, and 5052 is used where the shield must resist vibration fatigue or road debris.

A correction to earlier claims

  • Aluminium itself softens well below its melting range of roughly 645 – 660 °C, so a shield must be positioned where its own temperature stays far lower
  • Aluminium shields can face sources hotter than that, such as exhaust manifolds, because they reflect heat and are spaced away from the source
  • For shields that touch or sit very close to manifolds and turbochargers, aluminised steel or stainless steel is used instead

Soft O and H111 tempers are used for deep-drawn shields with complex contours. Half-hard H14 and H24 suit flatter shapes that need to hold their form. H24 is partially annealed after rolling, so it offers similar strength to H14 with better elongation.

Embossing patterns

Embossing patterns and what they do

Stucco

Irregular, orange-peel texture. Diffuses reflection, hides handling marks and gives moderate stiffening. The most common pattern for underbody shields.

Hemispherical (dimple)

Regular domes in a staggered grid. Gives the highest stiffness and greatest reduction of contact area; popular for exhaust and catalytic-converter shields.

Diamond / rhombus

Pyramidal pattern with good stiffness in two directions and a clear technical look.

Cobblestone

Larger, rounded irregular bosses; used for decorative or acoustic layers.

Tread / checker

Heavier raised pattern for footplates and step panels rather than thermal shields.

Custom

Pattern depth and pitch matched to the stamping and acoustic requirements of a specific shield.

Embossing slightly reduces apparent thickness tolerance and adds to overall height, so tool designers need the embossed thickness (overall height) as well as the base gauge.

Design

Heat shield design and fabrication tips

  • Keep an air gap: a gap of several millimetres between source and shield does much of the work; a shield in contact with the pipe conducts heat directly
  • Use layered construction for hot zones: two embossed skins with a fibre or mica insulating layer handle higher temperatures than a single sheet
  • Choose temper for the draw depth: O temper for deep draws, H14 or H24 for shallow shields that must resist denting
  • Isolate from steel: use aluminised or stainless fasteners and washers, and avoid trapping moisture against steel brackets to prevent galvanic corrosion
  • Allow for expansion: aluminium expands about twice as much as steel; slotted mounting holes stop distortion and noise
  • Protect against road salt: 1xxx and 3xxx alloys resist corrosion well, but drainage holes prevent water pooling in the shield

FAQ

Embossed aluminium (aluminum) heat shield sheet: FAQs

Why is 1050 used for heat shields?

It is soft enough for deep drawing, highly reflective, corrosion resistant and inexpensive. Where more stiffness is needed, 3003 or 5052 is used.

What temperature can an aluminium heat shield withstand?

The shield itself should stay well below about 300 °C to keep its shape. It can protect against much hotter sources, because it reflects radiant heat and sits at a distance from the source.

Which embossing pattern is best?

Stucco is the general-purpose choice. Hemispherical patterns give more stiffness and lower contact area for exhaust shields.

What thickness is typical?

Most stamped shields use 0.4 – 1.0 mm base thickness; layered shields often combine two thin skins with an insulating core.

Can embossed sheet be supplied in slit coil for progressive dies?

Yes. Coil can be slit to width and supplied with or without stamping oil.

Do you ship worldwide?

Yes, with mill certificates and export packing from Mumbai.
