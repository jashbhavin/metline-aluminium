6016 Aluminium Automotive Body Sheet

6016 is the most widely used aluminium alloy for European car outer panels: bonnets, doors, wings, roofs and tailgates. It forms well in T4, gives a smooth class-A painted surface, and gains dent resistance in the paint oven. Metline supplies 6016 sheet, blanks and coil in T4 and pre-aged T4P, with MF or EDT surfaces, certified to EN 485 or the OEM specification, worldwide.

Overview

Why 6016 dominates outer panels

An outer body panel must do four things: form to a sharp, accurate shape; hem around its inner panel without cracking; show no surface marks after painting; and resist denting from hail, door knocks and hand pressure in service. 6016 meets all four. Its silicon-rich chemistry gives a strong paint-bake response, while low copper keeps corrosion resistance high and avoids filiform corrosion at hems and cut edges.

Lean in magnesium and almost free of copper, 6016 has slightly lower strength than the North American alloys 6111 or 6022 in T4, which helps formability and hemming. After the paint bake, its yield strength roughly doubles, giving dent resistance comparable to the thicker steel panel it replaces. Unlike 5xxx alloys, it does not show stretcher-strain (Lüders) marks, so it suits class-A visible surfaces.

Outer panels are typically 0.9 – 1.2 mm thick in 6016, compared with about 0.7 mm for steel. Even at the greater gauge, a 6016 bonnet weighs roughly half as much as its steel equivalent.

What 6016 delivers

  • Class-A painted surface with no Lüders marks
  • Very good flat hemming
  • Strong paint-bake hardening for dent resistance
  • Excellent corrosion resistance and recyclability

How it works

The paint-bake cycle explained

1. Deliver in T4 or T4P

Sheet arrives solution treated and quenched. T4P (pre-aged) sheet has had a short warm treatment at the mill that stabilises it and speeds the later bake response.

2. Press and hem

Soft T4 material is stamped and hemmed. Forming adds a few per cent of strain, which also helps the later hardening.

3. Paint bake

The e-coat and paint ovens hold the body at roughly 170 – 185 °C for about 20 minutes. Fine Mg-Si precipitates form and the yield strength rises.

4. In service

The finished panel has much higher dent resistance than the sheet had in the press, without a separate heat-treatment step.

Without pre-ageing, 6016 that has sat at room temperature for weeks responds sluggishly in a short bake, because natural-ageing clusters must first dissolve. The T4P treatment prevents this, which is why most current outer-panel 6016 is delivered pre-aged.

Fabrication

Pressing, hemming and joining 6016

Stamping

Use larger radii and adjusted draw beads compared with steel; compensate for greater springback in die design.

Hemming

Good flat and roller hemming in T4. Prolonged natural ageing before hemming reduces hem quality.

Joining

Hemming with adhesive, self-piercing rivets and clinching are standard; spot welding is used on some inners.

Surface quality

Specify EDT surface for consistent press lubrication and uniform paint appearance on class-A panels.

Paint line

Compatible with zirconium or phosphate pre-treatments and e-coat used on mixed-material bodies.

Corrosion

Very good resistance to filiform corrosion at hems and cut edges thanks to low copper.

FAQ

6016 aluminium (aluminum) body sheet: frequently asked questions

Why is 6016 used for outer body panels?

It forms and hems well in T4, shows no Lüders marks, and roughly doubles its yield strength in the paint bake for dent resistance. Low copper gives excellent corrosion resistance.

What is the difference between T4 and T4P?

T4P has an extra pre-ageing treatment at the mill. It slows natural ageing in storage and gives a faster, larger strength increase in the paint oven.

How strong is 6016 after paint baking?

Typical yield after a 2 % pre-strain and 20-minute bake at about 185 °C is roughly 190 – 230 MPa, up from about 100 – 125 MPa as delivered.

What thickness is used for 6016 bonnets and doors?

Outer panels are typically 0.9 – 1.2 mm. Inner panels and reinforcements may be thicker or made from 5754 or 6014.

How does 6016 compare with 6111?

6111 contains copper and is stronger in T4, common in North America. 6016 is nearly copper-free, with better hemming and filiform corrosion resistance.

Do you supply 6016 sheet worldwide?

Yes. Metline ships 6016 sheet, blanks and coil worldwide with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and protective export packing.
