Automotive Brazing Clad Aluminium Coil

Brazing clad coil is a multi-layer aluminium strip: a strong core alloy roll-bonded to a thin aluminium-silicon filler layer that melts in the brazing furnace and joins the heat exchanger together. It is the base material for tubes, headers, plates and side supports in vehicle radiators, condensers, heater cores, oil coolers and EV battery cold plates. Metline supplies clad coil in 0.05 – 6 mm thickness and slit widths to drawing, worldwide.

Overview

What is brazing clad aluminium coil?

A modern automotive heat exchanger is assembled from dozens or hundreds of parts and then joined in a single furnace pass. The joining metal comes from the parts themselves: tube stock, headers and plates are clad on one or both sides with an Al-Si alloy whose melting point is 30 – 60 °C below that of the core. In the furnace the clad melts, flows by capillary action into every joint and solidifies as a fillet.

Clad coil is produced by hot-rolling a core slab together with thinner filler-alloy liner plates until they bond metallurgically, then cold-rolling to final gauge and annealing or temper-rolling. The result is a strip whose cladding ratio (filler thickness as a percentage of total thickness) is controlled to a tolerance of about ±1 – 3 percentage points.

Different heat-exchanger parts need different combinations. A radiator tube might be 4343 / long-life 3xxx / 7072, with braze clad on the air side and a sacrificial zinc liner on the coolant side. A header might be 4343 / 3003 with clad on one face only. A stacked-plate oil cooler may use 4343 on both sides of a 3003 core.

What to define when buying clad coil

  • Layer structure and cladding ratio on each side
  • Core alloy, including any long-life chemistry required by the OEM
  • Temper, which controls forming before brazing and sag resistance during it
  • Slit width, coil ID/OD, edge condition and burr limits for your forming line

How it brazes

How clad coil behaves in the brazing furnace

  1. Form and assembleCoil is stamped, roll-formed or folded into tubes, headers and plates, then assembled with fins.
  2. Flux (CAB only)For controlled-atmosphere brazing, a potassium fluoroaluminate flux is applied; it melts just below the filler and strips the oxide film.
  3. HeatThe assembly is heated in nitrogen (CAB) or vacuum to roughly 595 – 610 °C, above the filler liquidus but well below the core solidus.
  4. Flow and filletMolten Al-Si filler flows by capillary action into joints and forms fillets between tube, fin and header.
  5. CoolThe joint solidifies; the core is now in an annealed condition with a silicon-diffused surface layer.

Vacuum brazing uses magnesium-bearing fillers (4004, 4104) instead of flux: magnesium evaporates and breaks up the oxide film. Bismuth in 4104 improves wetting and makes the process more tolerant of furnace conditions.

How to order brazing clad coil

  1. SpecifyLayer structure (e.g. 4343 / 3003 / 7072), cladding ratio per side, thickness, width, temper, coil ID/OD and quantity.
  2. Confirm the standardYour OEM or heat-exchanger material specification, plus any post-braze strength or SWAAT requirement.
  3. QuoteWe confirm availability, tolerances, certification and delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP) for your plant or port.
  4. Process and inspectCoil is slit, checked for width, burr and telescoping, sampled for cladding ratio and tensile properties, and packed eye-to-wall or eye-to-sky as required.
  5. Document and shipEach lot ships with an EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate and packing list, in seaworthy export packing by sea or air; third-party inspection on request.

FAQ

Brazing clad aluminium (aluminum) coil: FAQ

What is the cladding ratio?

The thickness of the filler layer as a percentage of total strip thickness, per side. Tube and plate stock is commonly 5 – 15 %; the tolerance is typically ±1 – 3 percentage points depending on the ratio.

Which clad alloy should I choose, 4343 or 4045?

4343 has a wider melting range and less fluidity, suiting thicker clad on tubes and plates. 4045 melts over a narrower range and flows more, which suits fin stock and joints that need more filler. Both are used with flux in CAB furnaces.

Why is 7072 used on the coolant side?

It contains about 1 % zinc, which makes it electrochemically more active than the core. It corrodes slowly and sacrificially, protecting the core from pitting perforation.

Can magnesium-bearing cores be CAB brazed?

Only with care. Magnesium reacts with fluoride flux and reduces wetting, so CAB cores are normally kept low in magnesium. Higher-Mg and 6xxx cores are usually vacuum-brazed with 4004 or 4104 filler.

Which temper should I order?

O or H2x for parts that are deep-drawn or heavily formed; H14 or H16 for tube stock that needs stiffness for welding or folding. Your forming process and sag resistance needs decide the choice.

Do you slit to narrow widths?

Yes. Coil can be slit from about 7 mm wide, with burr and telescoping limits agreed for tube-making lines.
