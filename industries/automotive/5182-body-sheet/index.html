5182-O Aluminium Sheet for Automotive Body Panels

5182-O is a 4.5 % magnesium aluminium alloy in the annealed condition, and the most widely used aluminium sheet for deep-drawn car-body inner panels. It combines high strength for a non-heat-treatable alloy with excellent drawability, which suits door, bonnet and tailgate inners and complex structural pressings. Metline supplies 5182-O sheet and coil to GB/T 33227, EN 485 and OEM specifications worldwide.

Overview

Why 5182-O is the standard inner-panel alloy

An aluminium car body usually combines two sheet families. Outer panels (bonnet, doors, roof, wings) use 6xxx alloys such as 6016 or 6014, which harden in the paint oven and give dent resistance. Inner panels and structural pressings use 5xxx alloys, which do not harden in the oven but can be drawn into far deeper and more complex shapes. 5182-O is the strongest and most formable of these inner-panel alloys.

Its 4.0 – 5.0 % magnesium gives a high work-hardening rate, so the sheet strengthens as it is stretched and spreads strain evenly across the part instead of necking early. That is exactly what deep inner panels need: a door inner may have a draw depth of well over 100 mm with tight corners and many embossed features. 5182 is also the alloy used for beverage-can ends, which shows how well it tolerates severe forming.

The trade-offs are well understood. 5182 forms Lüders bands (stretcher-strain marks) during forming, so it is used where surfaces are hidden. It also must not be used for parts that sit for long periods above about 65 °C, because high-magnesium alloys then become sensitised to intergranular corrosion. Within those limits it is the most efficient sheet for inner structures.

Why body engineers choose 5182-O

  • Highest strength among common 5xxx inner-panel alloys (about 275 MPa typical tensile)
  • Excellent deep-drawing and stretch-forming, with total elongation around 25 %
  • Good corrosion resistance and compatibility with aluminium pre-treatment and e-coat
  • Joins by self-piercing rivets, adhesive bonding, clinching, spot and laser welding

Working with 5182-O

Forming, joining and design limits

Deep drawing

High n-value and good uniform elongation allow deep, complex inners. Use EDT texture or prelube for consistent friction; aluminium needs larger draw radii than steel.

Lüders bands

Type A and B stretcher-strain marks appear on formed 5182. Use it for hidden parts, or specify a pre-strained or special-processed variant if marks must be minimised.

Joining

Self-piercing riveting, clinching and structural adhesives are standard; spot welding needs controlled surface oxide. Hemming to 6xxx outers is routine.

Paint shop

5182 does not bake-harden; it may soften very slightly in the oven. Its properties are stable for design.

Warm service limit

Do not use for parts held above about 65 °C for long periods; choose 5754 or 6xxx for heat-exposed parts.

Springback

Lower modulus than steel means more springback; die compensation and controlled yield strength keep parts in tolerance.

Applications

Where 5182-O is used

  • Door inners: deep-drawn inner panels hemmed to 6xxx outers
  • Bonnet and boot-lid inners: complex stiffening patterns
  • Tailgate and liftgate inners: large, deep pressings
  • Structural pressings: floor reinforcements, wheelhouses and brackets in cool locations
  • Commercial vehicles: cab inner panels and reinforcements

FAQ

5182-O automotive aluminium (aluminum) sheet: frequently asked questions

Why is 5182 used for inner panels and not outer panels?

It forms Lüders bands during pressing, which would show through paint on outer panels, and it does not harden in the paint oven. Outer panels use bake-hardenable 6xxx sheet.

What strength does 5182-O have?

Typically about 275 MPa tensile and 130 MPa yield, with around 25 % elongation. Automotive specifications usually require at least 250 – 255 MPa tensile and a yield window of about 110 – 150 MPa.

Can 5182 be used near the engine or exhaust?

Not for parts held above about 65 °C for long periods, because it can become sensitised to intergranular corrosion. Use 5754 or a 6xxx alloy there.

How is 5182 joined in a car body?

Mainly by self-piercing rivets, clinching and structural adhesive, with hemming to outer panels. Spot and laser welding are also used.

What surface options are available?

Mill finish or EDT texture, untreated or titanium/zirconium pre-treated, and dry, oiled or prelubed surfaces.

Do you supply 5182-O sheet internationally?

Yes. Metline ships 5182-O sheet and coil worldwide from Mumbai with EN 10204 3.1 certificates, on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
