6022 Aluminium Automotive Body Sheet (T4P / Bake-Hardened)

6022 is a 6xxx-series body sheet alloy designed for car closures: hoods, doors, deck lids, fenders and roofs. It is delivered in a soft, pre-aged T4P temper so it stamps and hems cleanly, then gains strength in the paint-bake oven to give the finished panel its dent resistance. Metline supplies 6022 sheet and coil in automotive thicknesses, with mill or EDT surfaces and the pretreatment and lubricant your press shop specifies, to vehicle and component makers worldwide.

Overview

What is 6022 auto body sheet?

6022 is a silicon-rich Al-Mg-Si alloy. Its silicon content (0.8 – 1.5 %) is well above the amount needed to combine with its magnesium (0.45 – 0.70 %), and that excess silicon speeds up the precipitation of strengthening Mg-Si clusters during the short bake that cures automotive paint. The result is a sheet that is soft enough to draw a deep door outer, yet ends up markedly stronger on the finished car.

The alloy was developed for North American vehicle programmes as an outer-panel material and is now used alongside 6016 (the usual European choice) and 6014 on closures around the world. Copper is held to a maximum of 0.11 %, which keeps resistance to filiform corrosion under paint high, an important difference from older copper-bearing body alloys such as 6111.

Body sheet is a far more demanding product than general-purpose 6061 or 6082 sheet. Beyond strength, the buyer specifies anisotropy (r-value), work hardening (n-value), hemming performance, surface texture, freedom from paint-brush lines and roping, and the pretreatment and lubricant on the coil. Every one of these affects whether a panel can be stamped at production rate without splits or visible defects.

Why closure engineers choose 6022

  • Strong paint-bake response, so thin-gauge outer panels still resist dents
  • Good stretch-forming and drawing behaviour in the pre-aged T4P temper
  • Low copper gives good filiform and general corrosion resistance under paint
  • Compatible with hemming, adhesive bonding and self-pierce riveting used in aluminium closure lines

Mechanical properties

Properties as delivered and after paint bake

Body sheet is specified in two states: the T4P condition in which it is formed, and the condition after a simulated paint bake, which represents the panel on the finished car. The standard laboratory simulation is a 2 % tensile pre-strain (representing stamping strain) followed by about 20 minutes at 170 – 185 °C. A practical point that buyers often overlook: T4-type 6xxx sheet continues to age slowly at room temperature. Yield strength creeps upward over the first months after solution treatment and formability declines with it. Pre-ageing (the "P" in T4P) stabilises the sheet and improves bake response, but coils should still be stamped within the shelf life stated by the specification, and stored away from heat.

Temper designations

T4, T4P and bake-hardened conditions explained

T4

Solution heat-treated on a continuous annealing line, quenched and naturally aged. Good formability, but bake response is weaker because room-temperature clusters must first dissolve in the oven.

T4P (pre-aged)

A short low-temperature treatment straight after quenching creates stable clusters that grow quickly in the paint oven. This is the normal delivery condition for 6022 outer panels. Some suppliers and national standards add suffixes such as T4Pa to identify a specific pre-ageing practice.

Simulated bake (T8x / T6B-type)

Laboratory condition after 2 % stretch and an oven cycle. It is a test state used to prove the panel will reach its target strength, not a delivery temper.

T6 / T61

Full artificial ageing before forming. Rarely used for outer panels because formability drops sharply, but occasionally specified for flat reinforcements or brackets.

When you request a quote, state both the delivery temper and the bake-test condition required (pre-strain, temperature and time), because suppliers can tune pre-ageing to suit a particular paint shop.

Flat-hem and rope-hem tests grade the outer bend surface for cracking and orange peel. Outer-panel sheet normally has to reach the top one or two grades.

Paint-brush lines and roping

Lines parallel to rolling that appear after stretching and painting. Exposed panels are graded and must meet the agreed level.

Microstructure

Solution-treated sheet must show no eutectic melting (overheating) and a fine, uniform grain.

Surface quality

Exposed outer surfaces must be free of scratches, dents and roll marks; inner panels accept minor cosmetic marks that do not affect forming.

Traceability

Each coil carries a unique number linked to cast, rolling and heat treatment records and the 3.1 certificate.

Working with 6022

Stamping, hemming and joining 6022 panels

Aluminium body sheet behaves differently from deep-drawing steel. Its modulus is about one third of steel, so springback is larger, and its r-value is below 1, so it thins faster in deep draws. Die design, binder pressure and lubrication should be set up for aluminium rather than carried over from a steel tool.

  • Stamping: generous die radii (typically 5 – 10 × thickness on draw radii), even lubricant film and compensation for springback in the die surface
  • Hemming: 6022 outer panels are roller- or table-hemmed over the inner; a hem adhesive is normally used to seal and stiffen the joint
  • Joining: self-pierce riveting, clinching, flow-drill screws and structural adhesive are the main methods; resistance spot welding is possible with tip dressing and high current
  • Laser processes: laser welding and laser brazing are used on some roof and tailgate joints
  • Paint shop: conversion coating and electrocoat adhere well to properly pretreated 6xxx sheet; the bake cycle completes the hardening
  • Scrap handling: keep 6xxx closure scrap separate from 5xxx inner scrap to preserve its value in closed-loop recycling

FAQ

6022 aluminium (aluminum) body sheet: frequently asked questions

What does T4P mean for 6022 sheet?

T4P is a pre-aged T4 temper. After solution treatment and quenching the sheet gets a short, low-temperature treatment that forms stable clusters. These make the sheet age less at room temperature and harden faster in the paint oven, so the panel reaches its target strength in a normal bake cycle.

How much does 6022 gain in the paint bake?

Typically the yield strength rises from roughly 110 – 150 MPa as delivered to around 185 – 235 MPa after a 2 % pre-strain and a bake of about 20 minutes at 170 – 185 °C. The exact gain depends on the pre-ageing practice, the strain in the part and the oven profile.

What is the difference between 6022 and 6016?

Both are low-copper Al-Mg-Si body sheet alloys. 6022 has higher silicon and a little copper, giving a stronger bake response; 6016 is the long-standing European outer-panel alloy and is often chosen for very tight flat hems. Many OEMs approve both for closures.

Does 6022 sheet have a shelf life?

Yes. T4-type sheet keeps ageing slowly at room temperature, which raises yield strength and reduces formability. Specifications usually define a period from solution treatment within which coils should be formed, and coils should be stored cool and dry.

Can 6022 be spot welded?

It can, but aluminium needs much higher current than steel and frequent electrode dressing. Most aluminium closures are joined by hemming with adhesive, self-pierce riveting or clinching instead.

Do you supply 6022 sheet outside India?

Yes. Metline supplies automotive sheet worldwide from Mumbai, with mill certificates, protective export packing and EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
