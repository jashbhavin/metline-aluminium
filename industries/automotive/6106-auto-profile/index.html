6106 T6 Automotive Aluminium Profiles and Extrusions

6106 is a medium-strength Al-Mg-Si-Mn extrusion alloy (EN AW-6106, AlMgSiMn) that sits between 6063 and 6005A: it extrudes into thin, complex hollow sections almost as easily as 6063 but reaches a guaranteed 250 MPa tensile strength in T6. Vehicle makers use it for frames, rails, seat structures and housings where shape complexity and weight matter more than maximum strength. Metline supplies 6106-T6 profiles to drawing, with finishing and machining, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is 6106 aluminium and why is it used in vehicles?

6106 is a 6xxx-series alloy designed for extrusion. Its magnesium and silicon form Mg2Si precipitates during ageing, which gives the strength, while a small manganese addition refines the grain structure and lifts strength above that of 6063 without destroying the alloy's flow through the die. The result is a material that can be pushed through porthole dies into multi-cavity hollows with walls around 1 – 2 mm, yet still meets structural property minimums.

In automotive work that combination is valuable. A single 6106 extrusion can integrate screw ports, clip channels, ribs and closed cells that would otherwise need several stamped steel parts and spot welds. Because the profile cross-section is fixed, part designers move complexity into the die and keep downstream operations to cutting, bending, machining and joining.

6106 is most common in Europe, where it is listed in EN 573-3 and EN 755-2 alongside 6060, 6063 and 6005A. It is less familiar in North America, where engineers usually reach for 6063 or 6061 instead; for export projects we can advise on the closest ASTM-registered substitute.

Why designers choose 6106

  • Higher guaranteed strength than 6063 with similar extrudability and surface quality
  • Suits thin-walled, multi-hollow sections for frames, rails and housings
  • Very good arc weldability with 4xxx or 5xxx fillers, and good response to anodising
  • Good general corrosion resistance and full recyclability within the 6xxx scrap stream

Tempers

6106 tempers for automotive parts

T6

Solution heat-treated (on the press by controlled quenching, or separately) and artificially aged. The standard delivery condition for structural members and housings.

T5

Cooled from the press and artificially aged. Suits thin sections where press quenching achieves near-T6 properties at lower cost.

T4

Naturally aged. Softer and more ductile for stretch-bending roof rails and window frames, then aged after forming, often in the paint-bake oven or a separate furnace.

T64 / T66 variants

Under-aged or enhanced tempers can be agreed where a design needs extra bendability or slightly higher strength; specify target values on the drawing.

Tolerances & inspection

Tolerances, testing and documentation

Automotive profiles are almost always made to a part drawing, and the drawing tolerances override the general tables. Where the drawing is silent, the general tolerance standard of the order applies. Critical dimensions, such as mating faces, screw bosses and crash-relevant wall thicknesses, should be marked so they can be measured on every lot.

Working with 6106

Joining, forming and finishing 6106

6106 behaves much like 6063 in the workshop. MIG and TIG welding work well with ER4043 (lower crack sensitivity) or ER5356 (higher fillet strength); expect the heat-affected zone to lose a large part of its T6 strength, so place welds in low-stress areas or allow for the reduction in design. Self-piercing rivets, flow-drill screws, adhesive bonding and friction stir welding are all used in mixed-material bodies.

For bending, T4 material or local heating gives tighter radii than T6; stretch-bending is common for roof rails and window frames. Machining is straightforward in T6, producing short chips with sharp carbide tools.

Mill finish

Supplied as extruded and aged, for parts that are welded, bonded or painted later in the body shop or e-coat line.

Anodising

Clear or coloured anodic films for trim, roof rails and visible parts. Film class is specified in micrometres, for example AA15 or AA20 for exterior exposure.

E-coat (electrophoretic paint)

Thin, uniform paint that penetrates hollow sections; often used after anodising for a smooth, durable finish on trim.

Powder coating

Thicker polyester or epoxy layers for chassis brackets, racks and accessories that see stone chipping.

Conversion coating

Chromium-free titanium or zirconium pre-treatment before adhesive bonding or painting, improving bond durability.

Machining and fabrication

Cut-to-length, CNC machining, punching, bending and drilling on request, so profiles arrive ready for assembly.

Applications

Where 6106 profiles are used in vehicles

  • Seat structures and rails: thin-wall hollow tracks and cross-members
  • Roof rails, window and door frames: stretch-bent profiles with a good anodised or painted finish
  • Sub-frames for ancillaries: brackets and carriers for batteries, cooling modules and electronics
  • Electric drive and electronics housings: finned extrusions for inverters and on-board chargers
  • Commercial vehicles and buses: body frames, side-wall profiles, floor and luggage-rack sections
  • Rail and trailers: interior structures and side-wall profiles where similar section complexity is needed

6106 is not a primary crash-management alloy. Bumper beams and crash boxes usually use higher-strength or specially processed 6xxx and 7xxx grades; 6106 is chosen where moderate strength and a complex section are the priorities.

FAQ

6106 automotive aluminium (aluminum) profiles: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 6106 and 6063?

6106 adds 0.05 – 0.20 % manganese and allows a little more copper, which raises the T6 minimums from about 215 / 170 MPa (6063) to 250 / 200 MPa while keeping similar extrudability and finish quality.

Is there an ASTM equivalent for 6106?

6106 is registered with the Aluminum Association, but it is rarely called up in ASTM-based North American drawings. 6063 or 6061 are the usual substitutes there, depending on whether finish or strength matters more.

Can 6106-T6 be welded?

Yes. Arc weldability is very good with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. The heat-affected zone loses a significant part of its T6 strength, so welded parts are designed to reduced allowable stresses or re-aged.

Can 6106 be anodised for visible trim?

Yes. Its low iron and moderate alloy content give a clean, even anodised film, close to 6063. For mirror-bright trim, a bright-trim alloy such as 6463 is better.

What wall thickness can 6106 hollows achieve?

Small multi-cavity hollows can be extruded with walls of around 1 – 1.5 mm, depending on section size and shape. We review every drawing for extrudability before quoting.

Do you supply 6106 profiles outside Europe?

Yes. Metline supplies 6106 profiles worldwide from Mumbai with EN 10204 3.1 certificates, export packing and EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
