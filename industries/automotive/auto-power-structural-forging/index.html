Automotive forgings

Aluminium Forgings for Vehicle Powertrain, Chassis & Structure

Forged aluminium replaces steel and cast iron in suspension arms, knuckles, pistons, brake calipers, drive flanges and EV motor parts, typically cutting part weight by 35 – 50 % while keeping fatigue strength high. The workhorse alloys are 6082-T6 and 6061-T6 for chassis parts, 2618 and 4032 for hot engine parts, and 7075-T73 or 2014-T6 where strength comes first. Metline supplies forging stock, die forgings, rings, discs and shafts to automotive manufacturers and their suppliers worldwide.

Overview

Why vehicles use forged aluminium

Forging squeezes a heated aluminium billet between shaped dies until it fills the cavity. Unlike casting, the metal never melts, so there is no shrinkage porosity, and the grain flow follows the contour of the part. That continuous grain flow is what gives forged knuckles, control arms and connecting rods their high fatigue life and impact toughness.

A forged aluminium lower control arm typically weighs around half as much as a stamped or forged steel arm with the same stiffness target. Because suspension parts are unsprung mass, every kilogram saved improves ride, grip and steering response more than the same saving in the body. For electric vehicles the gain is doubled: lighter chassis parts offset battery mass and add range.

Forging also suits the safety logic of the automotive industry. A forged part has predictable, near-isotropic properties along its load paths, it can be ultrasonically inspected, and it fails by yielding rather than by sudden fracture from an internal void. That is why steering, braking and wheel-carrying parts are forged far more often than cast when they are made from aluminium.

Where forged aluminium pays back

  • Unsprung chassis parts: control arms, knuckles, hub carriers, tie-rod ends
  • Reciprocating engine parts: pistons and, in performance engines, connecting rods
  • Brake parts: caliper bodies and mounting brackets
  • Driveline and EV drive parts: flanges, yokes, motor end shields and hollow shafts
  • Crash and body nodes where an extrusion or casting cannot carry the load
Alloy selection

Forging alloys and their properties

Most automotive forgings are made from heat-treatable alloys, because only solution treatment and ageing give the strength that steel-replacement parts need. Minimums for 6061, 2014, 2618 and 7075 follow ASTM B247 die-forging values for sections up to about 100 mm; 6082 follows EN 586-2. Typical values are for information only and are not design guarantees.

For aluminium the physical constants barely change between alloys: density is 2.68 – 2.80 g/cm³ (about one third of steel), elastic modulus about 69 – 72 GPa, and thermal conductivity 120 – 170 W/m·K, which helps brake and engine parts shed heat. The lower modulus means aluminium parts are designed with deeper sections than the steel parts they replace; good forging design puts that extra depth exactly where stiffness is needed.

Engine system

Forgings in the engine: heat and cyclic load

Engine parts see high temperature and millions of load reversals. Only a few aluminium alloys keep useful strength above 150 °C, which narrows the choice quickly.

PartTypical alloyEngineering demand
Pistons (performance, diesel, turbo)2618-T61, 4032-T6Strength at 200 – 300 °C crown temperatures; low expansion for tight bore clearance
Connecting rods (motorsport)7075-T6, 2618-T61Very high fatigue and compressive load at minimum reciprocating mass
Turbocharger compressor wheels2618-T61Creep resistance at high rotational speed and outlet temperature
Rocker arms, valve spring retainers2014-T6, 7075-T6Wear and fatigue under fast cyclic load
Main bearing caps, front-cover flanges, pump bodies6061-T6, 6082-T6Rigidity and accurate machining datums

Crankshafts, camshafts and gear teeth stay in steel in volume engines because no aluminium alloy matches steel on contact fatigue and wear at those loads. Where an older specification lists aluminium for such parts, it usually means a housing, carrier or support ring around the steel part rather than the shaft itself.

Chassis, suspension & brakes

Chassis, suspension and brake forgings

This is the largest market for automotive aluminium forgings. Suspension and steering parts need high yield strength, fatigue resistance under road load spectra, and corrosion resistance against road salt, often with no paint on internal surfaces.

PartTypical alloy & temperNotes
Upper and lower control arms6082-T6, 6061-T6, 6151-T6I-beam or H-section forgings with thin webs; bushings pressed into forged eyes
Steering knuckles and hub carriers6082-T6, 6061-T6Complex 3D forgings; bearing bore and brake mounts machined after heat treatment
Tie-rod ends, link arms, stabiliser links6082-T6, 6061-T6Long thin parts; grain flow along the axis is critical for fatigue
Subframe and shock-tower nodes6082-T6, 6061-T6Joined to extrusions by welding, riveting or bolting
Brake caliper bodies (monobloc)6061-T6, 2014-T6Stiffness against fluid pressure; forging avoids porosity that leaks under pressure
Brake pedal and master-cylinder brackets6082-T6, 6061-T6Safety parts; often 100 % crack-tested

A typical premium-car front axle uses forged 6082 or 6061 for both control arms and the knuckle. The parts are forged from extruded or continuously cast bar, solution treated, quenched, aged to T6, shot-peened in high-stress areas and machined. Alloys with added manganese and chromium are chosen because they keep a fine, unrecrystallised grain structure after forging, which raises fatigue strength and resistance to intergranular corrosion.

Driveline and electric drive

Driveline, body-structure and EV drive forgings

Drive flanges and yokes

Propeller-shaft yokes, companion flanges and coupling hubs in 6061-T6, 6082-T6 or 2014-T6. Forging aligns the grain around bolt holes and splines.

Hollow shafts

Aluminium drive shafts for light vehicles are usually welded tube with forged end fittings. Forged hollow shafts in 6082 or 7075 are used where torque density allows.

Differential and gearbox carriers

Forged carriers and covers in 6xxx alloys replace cast iron where weight and stiffness both matter; gears themselves stay steel.

Body and crash nodes

Forged nodes join extruded rails in space-frame bodies and battery-tray frames. 6082-T6 and 6061-T6 are chosen for weldability.

EV motor parts

End shields, rotor support hubs and mounting rings in 6061-T6 or 6082-T6. Low density reduces rotating inertia; good conductivity helps cooling.

Thermal management

Forged cooling plates, manifolds and heat-sink bodies in 6061 or 6063, where a casting would risk leakage through porosity.

Electric drive units run their motors at 15,000 – 20,000 rpm or more, so balance, dimensional stability after machining and freedom from internal defects matter more than peak strength. Forgings in T6 that have been stress-relieved or rough-machined before final ageing hold their shape best.

Design and process

Design and processing considerations

  1. Forging stockExtruded bar is the usual feed; continuously cast bar can give a finer, more uniform grain in 6082 and lower cost. Billet must be free of coarse grain rings that would end up at the surface.
  2. Forging temperature6xxx alloys are typically forged at about 430 – 500 °C, 2xxx at 420 – 460 °C and 7xxx at 380 – 440 °C, with dies preheated to limit chilling.
  3. Heat treatmentSolution treatment, quench (often into polymer or warm water to reduce distortion) and artificial ageing to T6, T61 or T73, per AMS 2770 or the customer process.
  4. FinishingTrimming, shot peening of high-stress zones, CNC machining of bores and faces, and anodising, e-coating or conversion coating.
  5. InspectionGrain-flow macro-etch on first articles, ultrasonic testing, penetrant or eddy-current crack testing, hardness and conductivity checks, and tensile tests from prolongations.
  • Draft and radii: allow 3 – 7° draft and generous fillet radii; sharp corners cause laps and cold shuts.
  • Parting line: place it where flash can be trimmed without cutting across the main grain flow.
  • Galvanic contact: isolate steel bushings, studs and ball joints from aluminium with coatings or sleeves.
  • Stress corrosion: specify T73 or T7451 rather than T6 for 7xxx parts that carry sustained tensile stress.

FAQ

Automotive aluminium (aluminum) forgings: FAQ

Which car parts are most often made as aluminium forgings?

Control arms, steering knuckles and hub carriers are the largest group, followed by brake calipers and brackets, pistons, drive flanges and yokes, wheels and EV motor parts such as end shields and rotor hubs.

Is 6082 or 6061 better for forged suspension parts?

They are close substitutes. 6082-T6 has a slightly higher minimum yield strength and is the usual choice in Europe; 6061-T6 is common in North America and Asia. Both weld and resist road-salt corrosion well. The choice usually follows the customer specification and local bar availability.

Why are pistons forged in 2618 or 4032 rather than 6061?

6xxx alloys lose strength quickly above about 150 °C. 2618 keeps strength at piston crown temperatures thanks to iron and nickel additions, and 4032 has high silicon for low thermal expansion and wear resistance, allowing tighter piston-to-bore clearances.

How much weight does forged aluminium save compared with steel?

For the same stiffness, a well-designed aluminium forging usually weighs 35 – 50 % less than the steel or cast-iron part it replaces. The saving depends on how far the design can deepen sections to make up for aluminium's lower elastic modulus.

Do forgings need ultrasonic testing?

Safety-critical parts such as knuckles, arms and calipers are usually ultrasonically tested or crack-tested to a defined acceptance class, and grain flow is checked by macro-etch on first articles. Specify the inspection standard and class on the purchase order.

Can Metline supply forging stock as well as finished forgings?

Yes. We supply extruded and cast forging bar in 6xxx, 2xxx and 7xxx alloys, as well as die forgings, rings, discs, shafts and blocks, with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and export packing to any destination.
