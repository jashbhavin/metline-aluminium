Automotive Micro-Channel Aluminium Tube

Micro-channel tube is a flat aluminium extrusion divided into many small parallel ports, brazed to louvred fins to make compact, light heat exchangers. It is the standard tube for vehicle air-conditioning condensers and is now widely used in radiators, heat pumps and EV battery cooling. Metline supplies micro-channel tube in 1050, 1100, 3003 and 3102, bare or coated, in coils or cut lengths, to heat-exchanger makers worldwide.

Overview

What is micro-channel aluminium tube?

A micro-channel tube, also called a multi-port extrusion (MPE) or parallel-flow tube, looks like a thin aluminium ribbon. Inside, thin webs divide it into a row of small ports, each with a hydraulic diameter typically around 0.5 – 1 mm. Refrigerant or coolant flows through the ports while air flows across fins brazed to the flat outer faces.

Small ports give a very high ratio of internal surface area to volume, and they let thin walls carry high pressure. Compared with a round-tube-and-fin design of the same duty, a micro-channel exchanger is typically smaller, lighter and needs less refrigerant charge. Air-side pressure drop is also lower, because the flat tube presents a slim profile to the airflow.

That is why micro-channel tube replaced round copper tube in vehicle condensers decades ago and has since spread to radiators, evaporators, heat-pump outdoor coils and stationary HVAC equipment. In electric vehicles, flat multi-port tubes and wider MPE sections carry coolant along battery modules, where a slim profile and a flat contact face are exactly what pack designers need.

Advantages of micro-channel tube

  • High heat transfer in a compact, lightweight core
  • Lower refrigerant charge than round-tube designs
  • All-aluminium construction: brazed in one pass and simple to recycle
  • Thin walls rated for high pressure, including CO2 (R744) systems with suitable design
Design

Port shapes and tube types

Rectangular / square ports

The most common design. Easy to extrude and inspect, with a good balance of flow area and wall strength.

Round or oval ports

Better pressure resistance for a given wall, used in higher-pressure and CO2 systems.

Triangular and star ports

Increase internal surface area for better heat transfer, at the cost of higher pressure drop and harder extrusion.

Ports with internal fins (micro-fins)

Small ribs inside each port enlarge the wetted surface; common in high-efficiency condensers.

H-port and reinforced designs

Thicker webs or end walls where tubes must resist stone impact, crushing during assembly or very high pressure.

Wide MPE and serpentine tube

Sections 60 mm wide or more for battery cooling, often bent into a serpentine that runs between cell rows.

Related tube types made from clad strip rather than extrusion include folded B-type tube and HF welded tube, used mainly in radiators and heater cores. See our B-type tube guide and welded tube page.

Alloys

Alloys for micro-channel tube

Micro-channel tube must extrude through very fine dies at high speed, so softer alloys with low alloy content dominate. Stronger 3xxx alloys are chosen where pressure, corrosion life or post-braze strength demands it.

AlloyMain compositionCharacterTypical use
1050Al ≥ 99.50 %Excellent extrudability and thermal conductivityCondensers, battery cooling tube
1100Al ≥ 99.00 %, Cu 0.05 – 0.20 %Slightly stronger; copper makes it marginally more nobleCondensers and radiators, often zinc-coated
1197High-purity aluminium (about 99.97 %)Very low impurities for uniform corrosion behaviourLong-life tube with zinc diffusion layer
3102Mn 0.05 – 0.40 %Small Mn addition: more strength while keeping extrudability; good corrosion behaviourCondensers and evaporators
3003Mn 1.0 – 1.5 %, Cu 0.05 – 0.20 %Highest strength of the group; harder to extrudeHigh-pressure tube, CO2 systems, radiators

Long-life tube alloys are often modified with small, controlled additions of manganese, titanium or zinc diffusion from a surface coating. Agree the chemistry and corrosion test with the heat-exchanger maker.

Surface coatings

Why micro-channel tube is coated, and which coating to choose

Coatings applied in line after extrusion serve two purposes: corrosion protection of the thin tube walls, and brazing preparation so that less flux or filler is needed later.

CoatingWhat it isPurposeTypical use
Zinc arc sprayThin zinc layer sprayed on the flat faces at the pressDiffuses into the surface during brazing, forming a zinc-rich sacrificial layer that stops pitting from perforating the tubeCondensers and radiators exposed to road salt
Flux coatingPotassium fluoroaluminate (NOCOLOK-type) flux bonded with a binderRemoves oxide in the furnace without spraying flux on the whole coreCAB-brazed cores with bare fins or clad fins
Si-flux coatingFlux mixed with silicon powder, sometimes with zincSilicon reacts with the tube surface to form filler metal in situ, so bare (unclad) fins can be usedCost-reduced cores with bare fins
BareNo coating; clean, oil-free surfaceLowest cost; flux sprayed on assembled coreIndoor HVAC, protected locations
E-coat (after brazing)Electrophoretic paint on the finished exchangerExtra barrier against aggressive environmentsCoastal, industrial and heavy-duty duty

Zinc load and flux load are measured in grams per square metre and agreed with the brazer. Too little zinc gives poor protection; too much can drive excessive corrosion of fins and fillets. Flux coatings are checked for adhesion so that they survive cutting and core assembly.

Quality

Leak, burst and corrosion testing

Air tightness

Ports must be continuous and leak-free over the whole coil. Eddy-current inspection at the press detects wall defects; finished cores are helium- or pressure-decay tested.

Burst pressure

Each tube design is burst-tested to a pressure set by the system maker, normally several times the maximum operating pressure. CO2 systems run several times higher pressures than R134a or R1234yf and need heavier walls, round ports or 3003-type alloys.

Corrosion

Acidified salt spray (SWAAT, ASTM G85 Annex A3) and neutral salt spray (ASTM B117) are the usual tests, run on brazed samples for the hours the OEM specifies.

Cleanliness

Ports must be free of extrusion debris and the outer surface free of oil, which would prevent flux wetting and leave unbrazed fins.

Dimensions

Width, height and wall thickness are measured continuously or by frequent sampling; profile projectors check port geometry.

Traceability

Each coil carries a label linking it to the cast, extrusion run and test results on the EN 10204 3.1 certificate.

The whole core, including headers, tubes, fins and side plates, is brazed in one pass in a CAB furnace at about 600 °C. Our condenser header pipe guide covers the header side.

Selection

How to specify micro-channel tube

  1. Define the dutyRefrigerant or coolant, maximum operating and burst pressure, heat load and allowable pressure drop.
  2. Choose the sectionWidth, height, number and shape of ports, wall thickness; the cross-section drawing is the core of the specification.
  3. Choose the alloy1050 or 1100 for best extrusion and conductivity, 3102 for a balance, 3003 for pressure and strength.
  4. Choose the surfaceBare, zinc spray, flux or Si-flux, matched to your fin type, brazing process and corrosion target.
  5. Define supply formCoils for in-line cutting, or straight lengths with or without necked ends.
  6. Agree testsBurst, leak, SWAAT hours, coating load and dimensional checks, with the certificate content.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Specifying only width and height, without port geometry and wall thicknesses
  • Using a zinc load that suits the tube but over-corrodes fins and fillets
  • Choosing a high-strength alloy that cannot extrude the required thin webs
  • Forgetting that post-braze properties, not as-extruded values, govern the finished core

FAQ

Automotive micro-channel aluminium (aluminum) tube: FAQ

What is a micro-channel tube?

A flat aluminium extrusion divided into many small parallel ports. Brazed to fins, it forms compact heat exchangers such as vehicle condensers and radiators.

Which alloys are used for micro-channel tube?

Mainly 1050, 1100 and 3102 for their extrudability and conductivity, and 3003 where higher strength or pressure rating is needed. High-purity 1197 is used for some long-life designs.

Why is micro-channel tube zinc-coated?

Zinc diffuses into the surface during brazing and forms a sacrificial layer. Corrosion then spreads sideways in that layer instead of pitting through the thin wall.

Can micro-channel tube be bent?

Yes. It can be bent across its width (hard-way bends in serpentine coils) or along its flat face with suitable tooling and radii. Annealed or soft alloys bend more easily; the port design decides the minimum radius.

Is micro-channel tube suitable for CO2 (R744) systems?

Yes, with designs rated for the much higher pressures of CO2: thicker walls, round or reinforced ports and often 3003-type alloys.

How is micro-channel tube used in EV battery cooling?

Wide multi-port tubes or serpentine tubes run alongside cell rows, carrying coolant. The flat face gives good thermal contact with the cells or a thermal interface pad.

In what form is the tube supplied?

In coils for in-line straightening and cutting, or as straight cut lengths with plain or necked ends.
