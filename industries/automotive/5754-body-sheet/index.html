Automotive body sheet

5754-O Aluminium Sheet for Auto Body Structures

5754 in the soft O or H111 temper is the standard aluminium sheet for car body inner panels and structural pressings: it draws deeply, welds well and resists road-salt corrosion without paint-bake hardening. Metline supplies 5754-O sheet, blanks and coil for body-in-white, chassis and heat-shield parts, certified to EN 485 and ASTM B209, to vehicle and component makers worldwide.

Overview

Why 5754 is used inside the car body

Aluminium body structures use two families of sheet. Heat-treatable 6xxx alloys (6016, 6014, 6111) form the visible outer panels because they harden in the paint oven and resist denting. Non-heat-treatable 5xxx alloys, chiefly 5754 and 5182, form the hidden inner panels and structural parts, where deep drawing, crash energy absorption and corrosion resistance matter more than surface appearance.

5754 contains about 3 % magnesium. That gives it a good balance: strong enough for door inners, floor pans and structural reinforcements, ductile enough for deep draws, and with magnesium below the level where long-term sensitisation (beta-phase grain-boundary precipitation) becomes a serious risk at warm service temperatures. 5182, with about 4.5 % magnesium, is stronger but more susceptible to sensitisation and is therefore kept away from hot zones.

A common drawback of Al-Mg alloys is stretcher-strain marking (Lüders lines) during pressing. In 5754-O this is acceptable on hidden parts, which is exactly where it is used. Outer skins stay in 6xxx alloys that do not show these marks.

What 5754-O brings to body engineering

  • Deep drawability for complex inner panels and reinforcements
  • Excellent corrosion resistance, including in salt-spray and humid underbody zones
  • Good weldability by MIG, laser and resistance spot welding, plus adhesive bonding
  • Stable properties: no natural ageing, so blanks can be stored before pressing

Fabrication

Pressing, joining and corrosion behaviour

Deep drawing

Aluminium has a lower r-value and modulus than steel, so draw dies need larger radii, slightly different blank-holder forces and allowance for more springback. 5754-O draws well when lubrication is controlled.

Lüders lines

Al-Mg sheet can show stretcher-strain marks at low strain. Use 5754 on hidden parts, or specify a temper or levelling route that reduces the effect if appearance matters.

Spot and laser welding

Resistance spot welding needs higher currents and frequent electrode dressing compared with steel. MIG and laser welding with 5xxx filler (ER5356, ER5183) work well.

Bonding and riveting

Many aluminium body structures combine adhesive with self-piercing rivets or flow-drill screws. A conversion pre-treatment improves long-term bond durability.

Sensitisation limit

Keep sustained service temperatures below about 65 °C where possible. 5754 is far less prone to intergranular attack than 5182, but the rule still applies near exhausts.

Mixed-metal joints

Isolate aluminium from steel and carbon-fibre parts with sealants, coatings or insulating fasteners to avoid galvanic corrosion.

FAQ

5754 automotive aluminium sheet: frequently asked questions

Why is 5754-O used for inner panels rather than outer panels?

5754 draws deeply and resists corrosion, but it can show stretcher-strain (Lüders) marks and does not harden in the paint oven. Those traits are acceptable on hidden inner and structural parts, while 6xxx sheet is used for visible skins.

What is the difference between 5754-O and 5754-H111?

Both are annealed. H111 receives a small amount of strain from levelling or stretching after annealing. EN 485-2 gives them the same tensile limits, so they are often interchangeable for pressing.

Does 5754 harden during paint baking?

No. It is a non-heat-treatable Al-Mg alloy. Strength in the finished part comes from work hardening during pressing, not from the paint cycle.

Can 5754 aluminum sheet be spot welded?

Yes. Resistance spot welding works with higher current than steel and regular electrode dressing. Many body structures also use self-piercing rivets and structural adhesive.

Is 5754 safe to use near the exhaust?

For short excursions, yes. For sustained temperatures above about 65 °C, Al-Mg alloys can sensitise over time; 5754 is less prone than 5182, but heat shields or other alloys are preferred in hot zones.

What certificate comes with the sheet?

Each lot ships with an EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate showing chemistry and tensile results, traceable to the coil.
