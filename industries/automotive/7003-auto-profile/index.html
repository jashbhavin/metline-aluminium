7003 T5 Automotive Aluminium Profile

7003 is a weldable aluminium-zinc-magnesium extrusion alloy that reaches yield strengths around 300 MPa in the T5 temper while still extruding into thin, multi-cell hollow sections. That combination makes it a standard choice for bumper beams, side-impact door beams and other crash-relevant profiles. Metline supplies 7003 profiles in T5 and T6 to drawing, with certification, to vehicle and component makers worldwide.

Overview

What makes 7003 suited to automotive profiles?

7003 belongs to the medium-strength, weldable branch of the 7xxx family: alloys that rely on zinc and magnesium for strength but carry little or no copper. Compared with aerospace 7075, they are far easier to extrude and weld; compared with 6082, they offer roughly 15 – 30 % more yield strength in the same section.

The alloy keeps magnesium deliberately low (0.5 – 1.0 %). That choice is what lets 7003 flow through complex hollow dies at reasonable speeds, a weakness of richer 7xxx grades. Zirconium (0.05 – 0.25 %) is added to hold an unrecrystallised, fibrous grain structure after extrusion, which improves toughness and resistance to stress corrosion.

Al-Zn-Mg alloys of this type are also relatively quench-insensitive. A 7003 profile can often be cooled with air or water mist at the press exit and then artificially aged, giving the T5 temper without a separate solution heat treatment. For the buyer, this means good strength with fewer process steps and less distortion than a fully water-quenched part.

Why engineers specify 7003

  • Higher strength than 6xxx extrusions, so walls can be thinner and beams lighter
  • Extrudes into multi-chamber bumper and door-beam sections
  • Weldable, with heat-affected zones that recover much of their strength by natural ageing
  • Good energy absorption in crash folding when the temper is controlled

Tempers

T5 or T6: which 7003 temper to order

7003-T5

Cooled at the press and artificially aged. The usual automotive temper: good strength, low distortion and a cost advantage because no separate solution treatment is needed.

7003-T6

Separately solution heat-treated, quenched and aged. Slightly higher strength and more uniform properties through thick walls, at the cost of more distortion risk in long thin profiles.

W / T4 for forming

Profiles that must be stretch-bent into a curved bumper beam are often formed shortly after quenching, then aged. Agree the forming window with your supplier, because Al-Zn-Mg alloys harden steadily at room temperature.

Over-ageing practices (two-step ageing) are sometimes specified to trade a little strength for better SCC resistance and crash ductility. If your OEM specification calls for one, state it on the order.

Working with 7003

Welding, forming and corrosion protection

Welding

MIG and TIG weld well with 5xxx fillers such as ER5356, ER5183 or ER5556; 4xxx fillers give brittle welds on Al-Zn-Mg and should be avoided. The heat-affected zone softens during welding but regains a large share of its strength by natural ageing over several weeks at room temperature.

Stress corrosion

Like all Al-Zn-Mg alloys, 7003 can crack under sustained tensile stress in damp or salty service if the temper, residual stress and grain structure are wrong. Specify a controlled ageing practice, avoid cold forming after ageing, keep assembly stresses low, and ask for SCC test data (for example ASTM G47 alternate immersion) on critical parts.

Forming and bending

Stretch bending and rotary-draw bending are best done in the freshly quenched (W) or T4 condition, then aged to T5 or T6. Bending in the fully aged condition needs generous radii and raises residual stress.

Machining

Machines more cleanly than 6xxx alloys in the aged condition, with short chips and a good finish using sharp carbide tools and flood coolant.

Surface finishing

Accepts anodising, e-coating and powder coating. Zinc-rich alloys anodise with a slight yellow-grey tint, so check colour needs for visible parts. Coatings also add a barrier against galvanic and stress corrosion.

Joining to steel

Isolate 7003 from steel, carbon fibre and copper with sealants, coatings or insulating washers, and use coated fasteners, to avoid galvanic corrosion in wet road environments.

FAQ

7003 automotive aluminium (aluminum) profiles: FAQ

What does T5 mean for 7003?

The profile is cooled directly after extrusion (by air or water mist) and then artificially aged, without a separate solution heat treatment. Al-Zn-Mg alloys respond well to this route, so 7003-T5 reaches a guaranteed minimum yield of 260 MPa under EN 755-2.

Is 7003 weldable?

Yes. It is one of the weldable 7xxx alloys. Use 5xxx filler wire (ER5356, ER5183 or ER5556). The heat-affected zone softens but recovers much of its strength through natural ageing over several weeks.

Why is 7003 used for bumper beams instead of 6082?

Its higher yield strength allows thinner walls for the same bending resistance, which saves weight across the vehicle front, while it still extrudes into multi-cell hollows.

Is 7003 at risk of stress corrosion cracking?

Like all Al-Zn-Mg alloys it can be, if the temper, residual stress or grain structure is wrong. Zirconium, controlled ageing, low assembly stress and protective coatings keep the risk low. Critical parts are SCC-tested.

What is the JIS equivalent of 7003?

JIS A7003 under JIS H4100. The European designation is EN AW-7003 (AlZn6Mg0.8Zr).

Can Metline supply bent and machined 7003 beams?

Yes. Profiles can be cut, bent, punched and machined to drawing on request and shipped worldwide with mill certificates.
