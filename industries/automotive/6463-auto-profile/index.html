6463 T4 / T5 / T6 Automotive Aluminium Profiles

6463 is the bright-trim version of 6063: the same magnesium-silicon strengthening, but with iron held to 0.15 % or less so that chemically or electrolytically brightened and anodised surfaces look almost mirror-like. Vehicle makers use it for window surrounds, roof rails, grille bars, badges and interior trim. Metline supplies 6463 profiles in T4, T5 and T6, mill finish or bright-anodised, to customers worldwide.

Overview

Why 6463 is the trim alloy

The brightness of an anodised aluminium surface depends on how many intermetallic particles sit in the metal. Iron-rich particles scatter light and leave a grey, cloudy film after anodising. 6463 limits iron to 0.15 % (6063 allows 0.35 %) and keeps copper and manganese low, so after mechanical polishing, chemical or electrolytic brightening and a thin clear anodic film the surface reflects light with high specular gloss.

Mechanically, 6463 behaves almost exactly like 6063. It extrudes easily into thin, intricate open and hollow shapes, can be stretch-bent around roof and window curves, and reaches about 240 MPa tensile strength in T6. That is ample for trim that carries little load but must stay straight and dent-free.

Because the bright finish depends on chemistry, surface handling and anodising practice together, 6463 is usually ordered with a finishing specification: gloss level, film thickness, colour and the faces that are visible on the vehicle.

What makes 6463 different

  • Low iron (≤ 0.15 %) for a clear, high-gloss anodised surface
  • Extrudability and bendability equal to 6063 for slim, curved trim
  • Responds to chemical brightening, electropolishing and thin clear anodising
  • Good outdoor corrosion resistance once anodised and sealed

Tempers

Choosing T4, T5 or T6

T4

Naturally aged and soft. Use it for profiles that will be stretch-bent or roll-formed into tight curves, then artificially aged to T5/T6 strength after forming.

T5

Press-quenched and aged. The most economical choice for straight or gently curved trim; strength is ample for decorative parts.

T6

Fully solution-treated and aged for maximum strength and dent resistance, for roof rails, bars and parts carrying some load.

Finishing

How bright automotive trim is finished

  1. Extrude with surface careClean dies, controlled exit temperature and soft handling avoid die lines and pick-up that would show through the bright finish.
  2. PolishVisible faces are mechanically polished or buffed where the design calls for a mirror look.
  3. BrightenChemical brightening (phosphoric-nitric baths) or electropolishing levels the surface on a microscopic scale.
  4. AnodiseA thin clear anodic film, often 5 – 10 µm for maximum brightness, or thicker films where exterior durability matters more than gloss.
  5. Seal and protectHot-water or cold seal, then protective film or interleaving for transport.

Satin (etched), brushed and black or bronze anodised finishes are also common on 6463, and many vehicles now use painted or e-coated profiles for a dark trim look.

Applications

Where 6463 is used in vehicles

  • Window and belt-line trim: bright or black-anodised mouldings around side glass
  • Roof rails and cross bars: T6 profiles with anodised or painted finishes
  • Grilles and badges: bright bars and frames
  • Interior trim: door, dashboard and console inlays, sill plates
  • Lighting: reflector and lamp bezel sections where brightness matters

6463 is not used for wheels, suspension arms or engine parts; those need cast or forged alloys with far higher strength.

FAQ

6463 automotive aluminium (aluminum) profiles: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 6463 and 6063?

The main difference is iron: 6463 allows 0.15 % maximum against 0.35 % for 6063. Lower iron means fewer particles to cloud the anodic film, so 6463 can be brightened to a near-mirror finish. Strength is essentially the same.

Which temper should I order for curved trim?

T4 for tight stretch-bends, followed by artificial ageing; T5 for straight or gently curved parts.

How thick should the anodic film be on exterior trim?

Thin films (about 5 – 10 µm) give the brightest look; thicker films (15 – 20 µm or more) last longer outdoors. Many programmes balance the two with a medium film and a high-quality seal.

Is 6463 strong enough for roof rails?

In T6 (about 240 MPa typical tensile) it is used for roof rails and bars with appropriate section design. Highly loaded carrier systems may need 6061 or 6082.

Do you supply finished 6463 trim worldwide?

Yes. Metline supplies mill-finish or finished 6463 profiles worldwide with certificates and protective export packing.
