6201 Aluminium Alloy Wire for AAAC Conductors

6201 is the heat-treatable aluminium-magnesium-silicon alloy used to make all-aluminium alloy conductor (AAAC) and the strength wires of ACAR. In its T81 temper it is roughly twice as strong as hard-drawn 1350 while keeping a minimum conductivity of 52.5 % IACS, so an overhead line can span further without a steel core. Overview

What 6201 wire is and why AAAC uses it

Pure electrical-grade aluminium (1350) conducts well but is too weak to hold long spans on its own, which is why conventional overhead lines wrap it around a galvanised steel core (ACSR). 6201 offers another route. A small addition of magnesium and silicon makes the alloy heat-treatable, so the drawn wire can be aged to high strength while giving up only part of the conductivity of pure aluminium.

A conductor stranded entirely from 6201 wires is called AAAC (all-aluminium alloy conductor); in Europe and many IEC markets the alloy is also known by the trade name Almelec or by the IEC wire type AL3. Because every wire is the same material, AAAC has no steel core to corrode, no galvanic couple between core and outer layers, and no magnetic losses. It is lighter than ACSR of similar diameter and has a harder surface than AAC, so it resists handling damage during stringing.

The trade-off is conductivity. 6201-T81 must reach at least 52.5 % IACS against about 61 % for 1350, so an AAAC conductor has roughly 15 % higher resistance than an AAC of the same cross-section. For many distribution and sub-transmission lines, especially in coastal or polluted areas, the corrosion and weight advantages outweigh that difference.

Reasons to choose 6201 AAAC

  • High strength-to-weight ratio, allowing longer spans than AAC with fewer structures
  • All-aluminium construction: no core corrosion and no galvanic attack in coastal air
  • No magnetic core, so no hysteresis or eddy losses from steel
  • Harder, more damage-resistant surface than 1350 wire
  • Simple fittings and joints, with one material throughout
Product forms

6201 wire rod, round wire and AAAC conductor

6201 wire rod

Continuously cast and rolled rod, usually 9.5 mm, delivered in a condition suitable for drawing and subsequent ageing. This is the feedstock for wire mills.

6201-T81 / T83 round wire

Drawn wire, aged to temper, typically from about 1.5 to 4.8 mm diameter, on reels for stranding into conductors.

AAAC conductor

Concentric-lay stranded conductor of 7, 19, 37, 61 or 91 wires, supplied on drums to ASTM B399, IEC 61089 / EN 50182 or national standards.

ACAR strength wires

6201-T81 wires combined with 1350-H19 wires in ACAR conductor to tune strength against conductivity.

ABC messenger and covered conductors

6201 strands used as the load-bearing neutral messenger in aerial bundled cables and as the conductor of covered (spacer) overhead lines.

Earth and bonding conductors

Alloy strands used where an earthing conductor must also carry mechanical load.

Compared with the structural alloy 6061, 6201 has no deliberate copper or chromium and very tight manganese and chromium limits. Those elements stay in solid solution and scatter electrons, so leaving them out is what keeps conductivity above 52.5 % IACS.

Tempers

6201-T81 and T83: how they differ

Both tempers follow the same route: the rod is solution heat-treated (or quenched directly from the rolling mill), cold drawn to final size and then artificially aged. Ageing time and temperature, and the amount of cold work, decide where the wire sits on the strength-conductivity curve.

Conductor construction

AAAC stranding and conductor design

AAAC is built from wires of equal diameter laid in concentric layers around a single centre wire, each layer laid in the opposite direction to the one beneath it, with the outer layer usually right-hand lay.

Number of wiresLayer build-upTypical use
71 + 6Small distribution conductors and messengers
191 + 6 + 12Medium distribution and sub-transmission
371 + 6 + 12 + 18Larger distribution and transmission
611 + 6 + 12 + 18 + 24Large transmission conductors
911 + 6 + 12 + 18 + 24 + 30Very large cross-sections

Because 6201 is weaker than steel, an AAAC conductor of equal diameter has lower rated strength than a high-steel-content ACSR, but its strength-to-weight ratio is similar or better, so sag under everyday conditions is comparable. Its higher coefficient of expansion means more sag at high operating temperatures than ACSR, which matters on lines designed for heavy emergency loading. Engineers run a sag-tension calculation for the actual spans, ice and wind loads before choosing between the two.

FAQ

6201 aluminium (aluminum) wire and AAAC: frequently asked questions

What is 6201 aluminum wire used for?

Mainly for AAAC (all-aluminium alloy conductor) overhead lines, as the strength wires in ACAR conductors, and as the load-bearing messenger in aerial bundled cables and covered conductors.

What is the difference between 6201-T81 and 6201-T83?

Both are solution heat-treated, cold drawn and artificially aged. T81 is the standard hard temper with the higher strength and a minimum conductivity of 52.5 % IACS; T83 is processed for higher conductivity with somewhat lower minimum strength. ASTM B398 gives the limits for each.

Is AAAC better than ACSR?

It depends on the line. AAAC is lighter, does not corrode at the core and has no magnetic losses, which suits coastal and polluted areas and moderate spans. ACSR has higher rated strength and less sag at high temperature, which suits long spans and heavy ice loads.

What is the conductivity of 6201 wire?

At least 52.5 % IACS for T81, equal to a resistivity of 0.032841 Ω·mm²/m at 20 °C. 1350 aluminium is about 61 % IACS.

What is the IEC equivalent of 6201 wire?

IEC 62641 designates Al-Mg-Si alloy wire of this class as type AL3, and IEC 61089 / EN 50182 call conductors stranded from it AL3 conductors. The French trade name Almelec is also widely used.

Can you supply 6201 rod as well as finished AAAC?

Yes. Metline supplies 6201 wire rod for wire mills, T81/T83 drawn wire for conductor makers and finished AAAC on drums, with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and export packing.
