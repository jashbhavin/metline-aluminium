Class 120 Acetal Enamelled Aluminium Round Wire

Acetal enamelled aluminium round wire is soft electrical-grade aluminium wire coated with a polyvinyl acetal (PVA) enamel rated for thermal class 120 °C. The enamel is tough, resists abrasion during winding and is highly compatible with transformer oil, which is why it is a long-standing choice for oil-immersed transformer coils and low-temperature-rise motors. Metline supplies acetal enamelled aluminium wire in grade 1 and grade 2 insulation to coil makers worldwide.

Overview

What acetal enamelled aluminium wire is

Winding wire is the conductor of every coil, motor and transformer. It needs a thin, continuous insulation film that survives winding, varnish impregnation and years at temperature. Polyvinyl acetal enamel, often known by the older trade name Formvar, was one of the first synthetic wire enamels and is still valued for its toughness, flexibility and resistance to hydrolysis in sealed oil systems.

The thermal class of 120 °C (class E under IEC 60085) is modest compared with modern polyester-imide and amide-imide enamels, which reach 180 – 220 °C. But oil-immersed distribution transformers run well below that limit, and in that service acetal's compatibility with mineral oil and paper insulation, its mechanical toughness and its cost make it a sound choice.

Using aluminium instead of copper as the conductor cuts coil weight and material cost. For the same resistance, an aluminium winding needs about 1.6 times the cross-section of copper (roughly 1.27 times the diameter), yet weighs only about half as much. Designers accept a slightly larger coil in return.

Key strengths of acetal enamel

  • Excellent compatibility with transformer oil and paper insulation
  • High resistance to hydrolysis in sealed, oil-filled equipment
  • Tough, abrasion-resistant film that survives high-speed winding
  • Good flexibility and adhesion on soft aluminium conductors

Processing

Winding, impregnation and jointing

  • Pre-bake before varnish dipping: acetal film under winding stress can craze (fine cracking) on contact with solvents. Heating the wound coil at about 120 °C for an hour or more relieves the stress before impregnation.
  • Control winding tension: annealed aluminium is soft; excessive tension thins the conductor and raises resistance.
  • Stripping: acetal is not solderable through the film. Remove it mechanically or with a suitable stripping agent before jointing.
  • Jointing aluminium: use crimp connectors rated for aluminium, cold-pressure welding, TIG or ultrasonic welding. Avoid plain soldered joints to copper without suitable flux and process control.
  • Storage: keep spools dry and in their packaging until use; aluminium oxide forms quickly on bare ends.
Applications

Where acetal enamelled aluminium wire is used

Oil-immersed transformers

Distribution and small power transformer windings, where the enamel works alongside paper insulation in mineral oil.

Motors with low temperature rise

Small and medium motors and micro-motors whose hot-spot temperature stays within class 120.

Oil-filled motors

Coils in medium and large oil-filled machines that benefit from oil compatibility.

Instrument coils and reactors

Coils for meters, chokes and reactors.

Relays and solenoids

Electromagnetic coils where cost and weight savings from aluminium are valued.

FAQ

Acetal enamelled aluminium (aluminum) wire: frequently asked questions

What does "class 120" mean for enamelled wire?

It is the thermal class of the insulation: the enamel is rated for long-term operation at a hot-spot temperature up to 120 °C (class E under IEC 60085). Short excursions above this are tolerated, but sustained higher temperatures shorten insulation life.

Why is acetal enamel used in oil-immersed transformers?

Polyvinyl acetal is highly compatible with mineral transformer oil and paper, resists hydrolysis in sealed oil systems and forms a tough film that survives winding. Transformer windings in oil normally run within its class 120 rating.

Can acetal enamelled aluminum wire be soldered directly?

No. Acetal is not a solderable enamel. Strip the film mechanically or chemically first, and use jointing methods suited to aluminium such as crimping, cold welding or TIG welding.

Why does acetal wire sometimes craze during varnishing?

Stressed acetal film can develop fine cracks when exposed to solvents. Pre-baking the wound coil at about 120 °C for an hour or more before impregnation relieves the stress and prevents crazing.

Which insulation grade should I choose?

Grade 2 (thicker film) is normal for transformer windings because it gives higher breakdown voltage and fewer pinholes. Grade 1 saves space where the voltage between turns is low.

Do you supply enamelled aluminium wire internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies enamelled aluminium winding wire worldwide on standard spools, with test certificates to the order standard.
