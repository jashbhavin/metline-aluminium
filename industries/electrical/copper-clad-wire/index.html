Copper Clad Aluminium (CCA) Wire

Copper clad aluminium wire is an aluminium core with a metallurgically bonded copper skin, typically 10 % or 15 % copper by volume. It weighs about 40 % as much as copper wire of the same diameter, keeps a solderable copper surface, and at radio frequencies carries current almost entirely in the copper layer. Metline supplies CCA wire in hard and annealed classes for coaxial cable, braiding and winding wire to cable makers worldwide.

Overview

What CCA wire is and why it is used

CCA wire is made by bonding a copper sheath to an aluminium rod and drawing the composite down to wire size. The common route wraps copper tape around a clean aluminium rod, seam-welds it and then draws the bimetal through successive dies, so the copper and aluminium deform together and stay bonded. The copper fraction is fixed by volume and stays the same at every diameter.

The result combines properties of both metals. The aluminium core keeps weight and metal cost low. The copper surface gives good contact resistance, can be soldered and crimped like copper, resists oxidation better than bare aluminium, and dominates conduction at high frequency because of the skin effect.

CCA is not a replacement for copper everywhere. Its DC resistance is about 1.5 times that of copper at equal diameter, it is weaker and less fatigue-resistant than copper, and the aluminium core can corrode if exposed at a cut end in damp conditions. It is chosen where its weight, cost or high-frequency behaviour gives a clear benefit.

Construction

Copper thickness and volume ratio

Specifications control both the average copper fraction and the thinnest point of the copper layer, expressed as a percentage of wire diameter (d). The thinnest-point rule matters because a thin spot exposes aluminium during stripping or crimping.

TypeClassCopper at thinnest pointCopper volume ratio
CCA10A, 10H≥ 1.75 % of d8 – 12 %
CCA15A, 15H≥ 2.50 % of d13 – 17 %
CCAM / CCAM tinned10A≥ 2.00 % of d10 – 12 %

A worked example: a 1.0 mm CCA 10 % wire has a copper layer about 26 µm thick. The skin depth in copper is about 21 µm at 10 MHz and under 7 µm at 100 MHz, so at cable-TV and broadband frequencies practically all the current flows in copper. That is why CCA performs almost like solid copper as a coaxial centre conductor.

For DC and power-frequency use, CCA needs a larger diameter than copper for the same resistance, which offsets part of its weight advantage. For RF and high-frequency winding wire the trade-off is far better, because the copper skin carries the current.

Applications

Where CCA wire is used, and where it is not

  • Coaxial cable: centre conductors of CATV, satellite and antenna cables
  • Braided shields: CCA braid gives a solderable, light shield
  • Magnet and winding wire: enamelled CCA for loudspeaker voice coils, small motors, transformers and induction coils where low mass matters
  • Audio and video cables: signal conductors where weight and cost are priorities
  • Automotive and aerospace harnesses: selected signal and light-duty circuits
  • RF and high-frequency conductors: where skin effect concentrates current in the copper layer

Where CCA should not replace copper

  • Structured data cabling: ANSI/TIA-568 calls for copper conductors, and CCA conductors run hotter under Power over Ethernet
  • Fixed building power wiring, unless the local wiring code and the product listing explicitly permit it
  • Applications with heavy flexing or vibration, where copper fatigue life is needed
  • Terminations where the cut aluminium core would be exposed to moisture without sealing
Working with CCA

Drawing, enamelling and termination

CCA rod and wire can be drawn further on standard copper drawing lines with die sequences adjusted for the softer core, and annealed in-line. Enamelling and tinning lines handle CCA much like copper wire, which is why it substitutes easily in winding wire production.

For termination, crimp terminals rated for CCA or for aluminium conductors are preferred; soldering works on the copper surface but heavy heat can soften the core. Seal cut ends in damp locations so the aluminium core is not exposed to moisture. Store spools dry, because condensation on the copper surface causes tarnish that complicates enamelling and soldering.

FAQ

Copper clad aluminium (aluminum) wire: FAQ

What does 10 % or 15 % mean for CCA wire?

It is the copper share of the cross-section by volume. CCA 10 % has a thinner copper layer and is lighter; CCA 15 % has a thicker layer, slightly lower resistivity and better tolerance to stripping and crimping.

What is the difference between class A and class H?

Class A is annealed: softer, with higher elongation, for winding and stranding. Class H is hard drawn: stronger, for coaxial centre conductors and applications needing stiffness.

How much lighter is CCA than copper?

CCA 10 % has a density of about 3.32 g/cm³ against 8.89 g/cm³ for copper, so it is about 37 % of the weight at the same diameter, giving roughly 2.7 times the length per kilogram.

Can CCA wire be soldered?

Yes. The copper surface solders with normal fluxes and alloys. Avoid prolonged heat, which can soften the aluminium core, and seal cut ends in humid locations.

Is CCA suitable for Ethernet or building wiring?

Generally no. Data-cabling standards specify copper conductors, and CCA has higher resistance that causes heating under Power over Ethernet. Building wiring codes usually require copper or approved aluminium conductors.

What is CCAM wire?

CCAM (also written CCAA) uses an aluminium-magnesium alloy core under the copper. The alloy core is stronger, which suits very fine wire for braids and high-speed drawing.
