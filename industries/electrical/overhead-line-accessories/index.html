Overhead Line Accessories for Transmission & Distribution

Overhead line accessories are the clamps, connectors and link fittings that hold a conductor to its insulators and towers and carry current across joints. They must grip without damaging the strands, survive decades of vibration and weather, and stay cool and corona-free under load. Metline supplies aluminium alloy clamps, compression sleeves, joints and jumper terminals matched to AAC, AAAC, ACSR and ACAR conductors, to utilities and contractors worldwide; the steel and copper-alloy parts of a string are described here so the aluminium items fit them.

Overview

What overhead line accessories do

A conductor is only as reliable as the hardware that holds it. Most overhead line failures that are not caused by storms start at a fitting: a clamp that abraded the strands, a joint that overheated, or a bolt that worked loose under aeolian vibration.

Line hardware falls into three functional groups. Suspension fittings carry the conductor's weight on tangent towers and allow it to swing. Tension fittings anchor the conductor at angle, terminal and section towers and must hold almost its full breaking load. Connecting fittings (shackles, clevises, links, eyes and hooks) join clamps to insulators and insulators to the tower, and set the geometry of the insulator string.

Around these sit the protective and current-carrying accessories: armour rods and preformed grips that shield strands at clamps, vibration dampers and spacers, mid-span compression joints, jumper terminals, and parallel-groove or bimetallic connectors at taps and transitions.

Suspension

Suspension clamps and associated hardware

Aluminium suspension clamp

A cast or forged aluminium alloy body and keeper that cradle the conductor on tangent towers. The aluminium body is non-magnetic, so it adds no hysteresis or eddy-current loss and runs cooler than an iron clamp on high-current lines.

Trunnion and double-support clamps

Trunnion designs pivot freely so the conductor can swing without bending at the clamp mouth. Double-support clamps share load across two insulator strings for long spans, crossings and high-security lines.

Armour-grip suspension (AGS)

An elastomer insert and preformed rods spread the clamping force over a long length of conductor, reducing stress concentration and improving fatigue life where vibration is severe.

Armour rods

Helical preformed aluminium alloy rods wrapped round the conductor at the clamp to protect strands from abrasion and flashover burns.

A well-designed suspension clamp has a smooth, flared mouth with a radius sized to the conductor diameter, so the conductor leaves the clamp without a sharp bend. It also limits slip: high enough to hold the conductor in normal service, but low enough to let it slide under a broken-conductor event, protecting the tower from unbalanced load.

Full-tension fittings are commonly required to withstand at least 95 % of the conductor's rated tensile strength without slip or failure, a criterion found in IEC 61284 and in ANSI C119.4 for connectors. When a line is uprated with a high-temperature conductor, dead-ends and jumper terminals must be qualified for the new continuous and emergency temperatures as well.

Connecting hardware

Shackles, clevises, links, hooks and eye bolts

Connecting fittings form the mechanical chain between tower, insulator and clamp. They are usually hot-dip galvanised forged steel, sized by their specified minimum failing load, and they must couple correctly with the insulator ends.

  • U-bolts and eye bolts: attach the string to the crossarm or tower steel
  • Shackles and clevises: provide articulation and connect pin-type ends; clevis-tongue dimensions follow IEC 60471
  • Ball and socket fittings: couple cap-and-pin insulators; dimensions follow IEC 60120 so units from different makers fit together
  • Link plates and extension links: set string length, split loads in double strings and mount grading rings
  • Hooks and yoke plates: carry multiple strings or bundled conductors on high-voltage lines

Always check the failing load of every link against the string design load with the required safety factor, and confirm that coupling standards match between insulator and hardware.

Where aluminium conductors meet copper equipment, use bimetallic connectors or lugs with a friction-welded or explosion-bonded joint rather than direct contact, and apply contact grease to exclude moisture. On EHV lines, choose aluminium clamp bodies so magnetic losses do not heat the conductor at the clamp.

Engineering

Design and installation points that prevent failures

  1. Match the conductorFittings are sized to conductor diameter and construction. A clamp groove too large lets strands move; too small and it crushes them. Compression dies must match the sleeve and the conductor exactly.
  2. Control vibrationInstall Stockbridge-type dampers or spacer-dampers in line with the damping study for the span, tension and terrain. Aeolian vibration fatigue at clamp mouths is a leading cause of strand breaks.
  3. Clean and grease jointsWire-brush the conductor, apply oxide-inhibiting compound, and press or tighten to the specified sequence and torque. Poor joint preparation is the usual cause of hot spots.
  4. Check corona and RIVOn lines above about 132 kV, use fittings with rounded profiles and, where needed, grading or corona rings to limit audible noise and radio interference.
  5. Inspect periodicallyThermographic surveys find overheating joints early; visual inspection finds loose bolts, missing pins and damaged armour rods.

FAQ

Overhead line accessories: frequently asked questions

Are these accessories suitable for both transmission and distribution lines?

Yes. The same families of fittings are used at every voltage; what changes is size, failing load, the number of strings and, above roughly 132 kV, the need for corona-controlled profiles and grading rings.

Why are suspension clamps on high-voltage lines made of aluminium rather than iron?

Aluminium is non-magnetic, so it does not create hysteresis and eddy-current losses around a high-current conductor. It is also lighter and galvanically compatible with aluminium conductors.

Can standard ACSR fittings be used on AAAC conductors?

Not automatically. AAAC has no steel core, so compression dead-ends and joints use an all-aluminium design, and clamp grooves must match the AAAC diameter. Always select fittings for the exact conductor.

Are the accessories standard items or made to drawing?

Most aluminium clamps and sleeves are standard catalogue items sized by conductor and failing load. Special aluminium fittings, such as custom jumper terminals or clamp bodies for unusual conductors, can be made to the customer's drawing.

Can fittings be supplied together with the conductor?

Yes. Ordering conductor and aluminium hardware together lets us check that clamp grooves, compression dies and jumper terminals match the conductor exactly, and simplifies shipping and documentation.

How do I choose between bolted and compression dead-ends?

Compression dead-ends hold nearly the full conductor strength and are standard for transmission lines and high tensions. Bolted clamps are quicker to install without hydraulic tools and are common on distribution lines with lower tensions.
