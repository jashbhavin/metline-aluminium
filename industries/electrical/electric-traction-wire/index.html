Aluminium Trolley and Contact Wire for Electric Traction

Trolley and contact wires carry current to the pantograph or trolley pole of an electric vehicle. Aluminium alloy grooved contact wire, typically in Al-Mg-Si alloys such as 6101 or 6201, weighs about a third as much as copper wire of the same section and suits mine haulage, cranes, industrial trolleys and other lower-speed systems, while aluminium also forms feeders, messenger wires and conductor rail on mainline and metro networks. Metline supplies aluminium traction conductors and alloy wire to rail and mining projects worldwide.

Overview

Where aluminium fits in electric traction

Electric traction systems deliver power through an overhead contact line or a conductor rail. The contact wire itself rubs against the collector thousands of times a day, so it needs strength, wear resistance and conductivity together. On high-speed mainlines that job is done by copper or copper-alloy wire, because copper resists arc erosion and wear best. Aluminium earns its place in other parts of the system and in lower-speed applications.

ComponentAluminium roleTypical material
Grooved trolley / contact wireContact wire for mine locomotives, cranes, industrial trolleys and low-speed linesAl-Mg-Si alloy (6101, 6201 types)
Feeder and return conductorsParallel feeders along the track that cut voltage dropAAC, AAAC or ACSR conductors

In each case aluminium is chosen for the same reasons: it carries a given current at about half the weight of copper, it is cheaper per ampere carried, and it resists atmospheric corrosion well.

Dimensional tolerances are typically ±1 – 2 % on profile dimensions and ±2° on groove angles. The breaking forces correspond to a tensile strength of about 255 MPa, and the resistivity limit to roughly 52.5 % IACS, both typical of heat-treated Al-Mg-Si conductor alloy.

The equivalent-copper figures show the trade-off clearly: a 130 mm² aluminium alloy wire matches the DC resistance of a 70 mm² copper wire while weighing about 350 kg/km, against roughly 620 kg/km for that copper wire. Lower weight means lower tension forces on supports and lighter structures.

Alloys

Which aluminium alloys are used, and why

AlloyTypical conductivityStrengthSuitability
6101 (T6, T61, T63)about 55 – 59 % IACSMedium–highContact wire, conductor rail and busbar extrusions
6201 (T81)about 52.5 % IACS minHigh (≈ 300 MPa+ in wire)High-strength conductor wire, AAAC feeders and contact wire
1350 (H19)about 61 % IACSLow–mediumFeeder conductors (AAC, ACSR), not wearing contact surfaces
5052 / 3003about 35 % / 40 – 50 % IACSMediumNot normally used as conductors; conductivity is too low for traction current

Al-Mg-Si alloys dominate because heat treatment raises strength without destroying conductivity: magnesium silicide precipitates harden the metal while leaving the aluminium matrix fairly pure. Non-heat-treatable alloys such as 5052 gain strength from magnesium in solid solution, which lowers conductivity much more, so they are a poor choice for current-carrying traction wire despite appearing in some older product literature.

For lines with frequent heavy current collection at speed, copper alloy wire to EN 50149 remains the standard choice. Aluminium alloy wire is economical where speeds and wear rates are moderate, where structures are light, or where theft of copper is a serious operational problem.

Applications

Applications in rail, mining and industry

  • Mine haulage: trolley wire for underground and open-pit electric locomotives and haul trucks
  • Cranes and hoists: overhead trolley conductors for gantry and port cranes
  • Industrial transport: electric trolleys, transfer cars and monorails in plants
  • Metros: aluminium-stainless composite third rail with much lower resistance than steel rail
  • Tunnels: rigid overhead conductor rail in aluminium extrusion that clamps a copper contact wire
  • Mainline electrification: aluminium feeders, return conductors and auxiliary wires alongside copper contact wire
  • Trolleybus and tram depots: lower-speed overhead lines where weight matters

A typical metro upgrade, for example, replaces steel third rail with composite aluminium rail. Because the aluminium body has a fraction of the resistance of steel rail, substations can be spaced further apart and traction voltage at the train stays higher.

Practical advice

What to check when buying traction wire

  • Electrical requirements: current, allowable voltage drop and temperature limit determine the section
  • Mechanical requirements: tension, span, wind and ice loads; check breaking force and creep
  • Profile compatibility: groove geometry must match existing clamps, hangers and splices
  • Standards: state the governing specification and the tests to be witnessed
  • Drum lengths: long continuous lengths reduce splices on the running surface
  • Documentation: mill test certificate with chemistry, breaking force, elongation, resistivity and twist test results

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) traction wire: FAQ

Is aluminium used for railway contact wire?

On high-speed mainlines the contact wire is copper or copper alloy because of its wear and arc resistance. Aluminium alloy contact wire is used on lower-speed systems such as mine haulage, cranes and industrial trolleys, and aluminium is widely used for feeders, return conductors and conductor rail.

Which aluminium alloy is used for trolley wire?

Heat-treatable Al-Mg-Si alloys such as 6101 and 6201, which combine a tensile strength around 250 – 300 MPa with conductivity above about 52 % IACS.

What does "equivalent copper section" mean?

It is the copper cross-section with the same DC resistance. A 130 mm² aluminium alloy contact wire, for example, has about the same resistance as a 70 mm² copper wire.

How much does aluminium contact wire weigh?

About 350 kg/km for 130 mm², 460 kg/km for 170 mm² and 540 kg/km for 200 mm².

Why do metros use aluminium composite third rail?

An aluminium body with a stainless steel wearing surface has far lower resistance than a steel rail and weighs much less, so losses fall and substations can be spaced further apart.

Can you supply in long drum lengths?

Yes. Contact and feeder wire is supplied on drums sized to the project, with lengths agreed to minimise splices.
