5052 Marine Grade Aluminium Hexagon Bar

5052 hexagon bar is a medium-strength aluminium-magnesium bar that resists seawater and salt spray without coating, which makes it a practical stock for nuts, spacers, fittings and small machined parts on boats and coastal equipment. Overview

What is 5052 hexagon bar used for at sea?

5052 contains about 2.5 % magnesium and a small chromium addition. It gains its strength from magnesium in solid solution and from cold work, not from heat treatment, so it keeps a stable structure in service and is not prone to the sensitisation that can affect alloys with more than 3 % magnesium.

The hexagonal section gives six flats for a spanner or a lathe collet, which is why hex bar is the starting stock for nuts, couplings, standoffs, through-hull spacers and threaded fittings. In 5052 those parts sit happily in salt spray, bilge water and fuel, provided they are isolated from copper alloys and stainless steel.

It is worth being clear about what 5052 is not. It is a moderate-strength alloy, roughly half the strength of 6061-T6, and it is not one of the alloys that classification societies approve for hull structure. For highly loaded or class-inspected parts, 5083 hex bar or a 6xxx alloy with a protective finish is the better choice.

Why buyers choose 5052 hex bar

  • Very good resistance to seawater and marine atmospheres, uncoated
  • Weldable with ER5356 filler, so hex fittings can be welded into fabrications
  • Compatible with 5052 sheet used for fuel tanks and small-craft hulls
  • Easy to cold form in O temper for swaged or flared parts

Tempers

O or H112: which temper to order

5052-O

Fully annealed. The softest condition, with the highest elongation. Choose O when the hex will be flared, swaged, crimped or cold headed after machining.

5052-H112

As extruded, with properties that come from hot working. The normal choice for machined nuts, couplings and spacers where moderate strength is enough.

5052-H32 / H34 (drawn)

Cold-drawn hex with higher proof strength and tighter tolerances across flats. Useful for automatic-lathe work and where the flats must fit tooling closely.

Quality

Tolerances and inspection

Across-flats tolerance, corner radius, straightness and twist all follow the ordering standard: EN 755-6 for extruded hex, EN 754-6 for drawn hex, or ANSI H35.2 for ASTM orders. Tolerances widen as section size grows, and drawn hex holds noticeably tighter limits than extruded hex of the same size, which matters if parts are fed through a collet.

  • Dimensional check of across flats, across corners and length on each lot
  • Visual inspection for die lines, scratches and corrosion staining
  • Straightness and twist measured on full-length bars where the standard requires it
  • Mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 with chemistry and tensile results
  • Third-party inspection on request before shipment
Working with 5052

Machining, welding and finishing hex bar

Machining

Fair to good. Softer tempers produce long, gummy chips; H112 and drawn tempers machine more cleanly. Use sharp, polished high-rake carbide or HSS tools, generous coolant and chip breakers on automatic lathes.

Threading

Roll or cut threads readily. For threads that are assembled and disassembled often, consider a thread insert or a harder alloy, because aluminium threads gall against stainless fasteners.

Welding

TIG and MIG weld well with ER5356 filler. The weld zone returns to roughly annealed strength, which for 5052 is close to the H112 condition anyway.

Anodising

Takes a clear protective anodic film. It is not a bright-anodising alloy, and the film is slightly yellow-grey on higher-magnesium stock.

Galvanic isolation

Separate 5052 parts from bronze, copper and stainless steel with nylon or PTFE washers and sleeves, especially below the waterline.

Temperature

Suitable for continuous service up to about 65 – 100 °C. Strength rises slightly at cryogenic temperature.

Applications

Typical uses of 5052 hexagon bar

  • Fasteners and nuts for aluminium hatches, covers and fittings where a matching alloy avoids galvanic pairs
  • Spacers and standoffs for consoles, electronics mounts and deck equipment
  • Fuel and water fittings welded into 5052 tanks on small craft
  • Rail and stanchion bases, cleat bodies and small deck hardware
  • Couplings and adaptors for low-pressure hose and drainage lines
  • Coastal equipment: fish-farm cages, pontoon hardware and dock fittings

FAQ

5052 aluminium (aluminum) hex bar: FAQ

Is 5052 hex bar suitable for saltwater?

Yes. 5052 resists seawater and salt spray very well without coating. Isolate it from stainless steel and copper alloys to avoid galvanic corrosion.

What is the difference between 5052-O and 5052-H112 hex bar?

O is fully annealed and most ductile, for parts that are formed after machining. H112 is the as-extruded condition, with the same specified minimums in most standards but usually higher actual strength, and is the normal machining temper.

How much does 5052 hex bar weigh?

Weight per metre is 0.00232 × (across flats in mm)². A 20 mm hex weighs about 0.93 kg/m and a 50 mm hex about 5.8 kg/m.

Can 5052 hex bar be anodised?

Yes. It takes a clear protective anodic film well. It is not used for bright decorative anodising.

Is 5052 approved by classification societies for ship structure?

No. IACS UR W25 lists 5083, 5383, 5059, 5086 and others, but not 5052. Use 5083 bar for class-inspected structural parts.

Do you supply cut lengths?

Yes. Hex bar can be supplied in standard lengths or cut to your lengths, with an EN 10204 3.1 certificate.
