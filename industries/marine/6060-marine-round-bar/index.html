6060, 6061, 6005A and 6082 Marine Aluminium Round Bar

Round bar in the four main 6xxx alloys covers almost every machined aluminium part on a vessel, from light trim pins in 6060 to load-carrying shafts and bolts in 6082-T6. All four are heat-treatable, machine well in T6, take a protective anodised finish and resist marine atmospheres. Metline supplies them from about 6 mm to 250 mm diameter, cut to length and certified, to shipyards and machine shops worldwide.

Overview

How the four 6xxx round bar grades differ

Every 6xxx alloy gets its strength the same way: silicon and magnesium dissolve during solution heat treatment and then precipitate as fine Mg2Si particles during ageing. What separates the grades is how much of each element they contain and which grain-controlling additions they carry. More silicon and magnesium mean more strength; manganese or chromium keeps the grain fine in large diameters.

For a marine buyer, that translates into a simple ladder. 6060 has the least alloy content, extrudes fastest and gives the best anodised surface, but its T6 proof strength is only about 150 MPa. 6005A raises that to about 215 – 225 MPa. 6061, with a copper and chromium addition, reaches about 240 MPa minimum and is the grade most drawings from the Americas and Asia call for. 6082, with the most silicon and a manganese addition, is the strongest at about 260 MPa minimum in mid-range diameters and is the European structural default.

None of them should be treated as fully equivalent to 5xxx alloys in constant seawater immersion. On deck, in superstructures, in engine rooms and inside the hull, though, anodised or painted 6xxx round bar gives long service with very little maintenance.

Quick selection

  • Pins, handles and trim where looks matter more than load: 6060-T6
  • General machined parts, moderate load: 6005A-T6
  • Drawings to ASTM B221 / B211 or JIS: 6061-T6 or T6511
  • Shafts, bolts and fittings that carry real load: 6082-T6

Fabrication

Machining, joining and protecting 6xxx round bar

Turning

Use T6 or T6511 for production turning. 6061 and 6082 break chips best; 6060 is softer and can smear without sharp tools.

Welding

ER5356 is the usual marine filler; ER4043 for crack-sensitive joints. Allow for about 50 % strength loss next to welds in T6 material.

Anodising

Sulphuric anodising (15 – 25 µm, sealed) for marine exposure. Hard anodising for wear parts such as pins and bushes.

Galvanic contact

Isolate from stainless and bronze; use aluminium or coated fasteners where possible.

Applications

Marine applications

  • Shafts and pins: hinge pins, davit pins, pulley axles and roller spindles (6082, 6061)
  • Stanchions and posts: machined bases and solid posts for railings and canopies
  • Fasteners: aluminium bolts and studs for aluminium structures, avoiding galvanic couples
  • Bushes and sleeves: turned from larger bar, often hard anodised
  • Handles and trim: door handles, grab rails and decorative parts (6060)
  • Mast and rigging fittings: tangs, end plugs and sheave housings

FAQ

Marine 6xxx aluminium (aluminum) round bar: FAQs

Which 6xxx round bar is best for marine shafts?

6082-T6 for European drawings or 6061-T6 for ASTM drawings. Both give about 240 – 260 MPa minimum proof strength; hard-anodise wear surfaces.

Is 6061 or 6082 better in seawater?

6082 has less copper and is usually considered slightly better in chloride environments, but both need anodising or coating for long exposure.

What diameters are available?

Extruded bar typically from about 6 mm to 250 mm; larger diameters and cold-finished small diameters on request.

How heavy is a 50 mm aluminium round bar?

About 5.30 kg per metre in any 6xxx alloy.

What is the difference between T6 and T6511?

Both have the same strength. T6511 is stretched to relieve stress and lightly straightened, so it distorts less during machining.
