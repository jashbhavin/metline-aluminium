5A30 and 5456 Marine Grade Aluminium Plate

5A30 (GB/T 3190) and 5456 (Aluminum Association) are near-identical aluminium alloys with 4.7 – 5.5 % magnesium, manganese and chromium, giving more strength than 5083 in welded hull and deck plate. 5A30 is supplied mainly as H112 plate to GB/T 22641; 5456 is supplied in H116 and H321 to ASTM B928 with corrosion testing for naval and commercial ships. Overview

Two names for one high-strength marine alloy

5456 was developed in the United States as a stronger alternative to 5083 for welded naval structures, and it remains a standard hull, deck and armour plate for patrol boats, landing craft and military vehicles. China's GB/T 3190 registers 5A30 with practically the same composition: 4.7 – 5.5 % magnesium, 0.5 – 1.0 % manganese and a small chromium addition. Buyers often see the two names together, and for most purposes they are interchangeable.

The extra magnesium compared with 5083 lifts strength by roughly 5 – 10 % in matching tempers, and welds made with ER5556 filler keep that advantage. The alloy also has good fatigue and impact behaviour, which is why it has been used for armour plate and blast-resistant panels as well as hulls.

The difference between the names shows up in how the plate is delivered. 5A30 is usually supplied as-rolled H112 plate to GB/T 22641, the Chinese standard for aluminium ship plate. 5456 for international marine service is normally ordered in the special H116 or H321 tempers to ASTM B928, which require each lot to pass exfoliation (ASTM G66) and intergranular corrosion (ASTM G67) tests. If your vessel is classed by DNV, ABS, Lloyd's Register or Bureau Veritas, those tested tempers are what the surveyor will expect.

What to specify

  • Alloy name as it appears in your rules or drawing: 5A30 or 5456
  • Temper: H112 (GB/T 22641), H116 or H321 (ASTM B928), or O for forming
  • Corrosion testing requirement (ASTM G66 / G67) and class certification
  • Thickness, plate size, quantity and surface protection

Tempers

H112, H116 and H321 explained

H112

As-rolled with minimal controlled strain hardening; mechanical properties are guaranteed but no corrosion test is required. The usual delivery condition for 5A30 to GB/T 22641.

H116

Strain-hardened with controlled processing to produce a microstructure resistant to exfoliation and intergranular corrosion. Tested to ASTM G66 and G67 under ASTM B928.

H321

Strain-hardened and thermally stabilised, again with B928 corrosion testing. Strength is similar to H116; the choice usually follows the shipyard's practice or the rules.

O

Fully annealed for maximum formability, used where plate must be pressed or rolled to tight curvature.

Fabrication

Welding and fabrication

Filler choice

ER5556 is the matching filler for 5456 and gives the highest weld strength; ER5183 is also widely used. Avoid ER5356 for primary structure.

Weld design

Welds in H116 plate fall back towards O-temper strength in the heat-affected zone; class rules give reduced welded design values.

Temperature limit

As with all alloys above 3 % Mg, keep long-term service below about 65 °C to avoid sensitisation.

Forming

Bend radii of about 1.5 – 3 × thickness for H116 plate; form tighter curves in O temper.

Cutting

Waterjet, plasma or sawing; remove plasma-affected edges on critical joints.

Protection

Unpainted plate performs well above and below the waterline; antifouling must be copper-free or applied over a barrier coat.

Applications

Applications

  • Hull shell and decks of patrol boats, crew boats, ferries and landing craft
  • Armour and protective panels on vessels and military vehicles
  • Superstructures on steel ships, often joined through structural transition joints
  • Fuel and water tanks and pressure vessels
  • Rail and road tankers needing high welded strength

Joining aluminium superstructures to steel hulls is usually done with explosion-bonded transition strip; see our aluminium-steel transition joint page.

FAQ

5A30 and 5456 marine aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQs

Is 5A30 the same as 5456?

Practically yes. Both have 4.7 – 5.5 % Mg, 0.5 – 1.0 % Mn and 0.05 – 0.20 % Cr. 5A30 limits Si + Fe to 0.40 % combined and sets a minimum titanium content.

What is the difference between 5456-H116 and H321?

Both are strain-hardened tempers tested for exfoliation and intergranular corrosion to ASTM B928. H321 includes a thermal stabilisation step; strengths are similar.

Which filler wire is used for 5456?

ER5556 is the matching filler; ER5183 is also common. Both give higher weld strength than ER5356.

Is 5456 stronger than 5083?

Slightly: ASTM B928 minimums for H116 plate are about 317 / 228 MPa for 5456 against 305 / 215 MPa for 5083.

How much does a 20 mm 5456 plate weigh?

About 53.2 kg per m², or roughly 239 kg for a 1500 × 3000 mm plate.
