5454, 5456 & 5754 Marine Aluminium Hexagonal Bar

Hexagonal bar in 5454, 5456 or 5754 is used to make nuts, unions, couplings and spanner-flat fittings for boats and marine equipment, in an aluminium-magnesium alloy that matches the surrounding structure and resists seawater. 5454 is the choice for warm service, 5456 for the highest strength and 5754 for general fittings. Overview

Three Al-Mg alloys for marine hex fittings

A hexagon is the natural starting shape for anything that will be turned with a spanner: nuts, plugs, unions, hose tails, adaptors and threaded bosses. Machining these from hex bar removes far less metal than starting from round, and the flats come ready-made.

In marine work the alloy matters as much as the shape. Brass and stainless fittings on an aluminium hull set up galvanic cells in seawater, so builders prefer fittings in the same aluminium-magnesium family as the hull. The three alloys on this page cover most needs:

5454 (about 2.7 % Mg) is the 5xxx alloy designed for elevated temperature. Because its magnesium stays below 3 %, it does not become sensitised in long service above 65 °C, which makes it the right hex bar for fittings on engine cooling, exhaust-adjacent and hot-fluid systems. 5456 (about 5.1 % Mg) is the strongest of the three, similar to 5083 but stronger, and suits highly loaded fasteners and fittings that stay cool. 5754 (about 3.1 % Mg) is the common European general-purpose grade, with very good corrosion resistance and formability.

Quick selection

  • Warm or hot service, fuel and cooling systems: 5454
  • Highest strength, cool locations, structural fittings: 5456
  • General deck and cabin fittings, good formability: 5754
  • All three: seawater resistant, weldable, non-magnetic

Tempers

Tempers for hex bar

H112

As extruded, with guaranteed minimums. The default for nuts, unions and fittings that are machined and not formed.

O

Fully annealed and most ductile. Used where hex stock is upset or cold-headed into bolts and special fasteners.

H111

Annealed then lightly strain-hardened in straightening. Higher proof stress than O for the same ductility class.

Cold-finished

Drawn hex bar (ASTM B211 or EN 754) gives tighter flats and a brighter surface for screw-machine work.

Standards & class rules

Standards and class acceptance

Hex bar is usually ordered to ASTM B221 (extruded) or ASTM B211 (cold-finished), EN 755 or EN 754, or GB/T 3191. Fastener-grade material may also call for ASTM B316, which covers rivet and cold-heading wire and rod. Note that the IACS UR W25 extrusion table names 5083, 5383, 5059 and 5086 (plus 6005A, 6061 and 6082); it does not list 5454, 5456 or 5754 for extruded products. These alloys are therefore used for fittings and machinery parts rather than primary hull structure, or accepted by a society case by case. Check with your surveyor if a hex fitting forms part of a classed structure.

Working with Al-Mg hex bar

Machining, threading and joining

Machining

All three alloys are soft and can smear. Sharp, polished carbide inserts with high positive rake, chip breakers and flood coolant give clean threads. H112 and H111 machine better than O.

Threads

Roll threads where possible: rolled threads in Al-Mg alloys are stronger and more fatigue-resistant than cut ones. Use anti-seize on aluminium-to-aluminium threads to prevent galling.

Welding

Weld 5454 with ER5554 for service above 65 °C, or ER5356; weld 5456 with ER5556 to keep strength; weld 5754 with ER5356 or ER5183.

Galvanic isolation

When an aluminium fitting meets a stainless or bronze part, add an insulating washer or sleeve and keep the joint sealed from seawater.

Anodising

All three anodise well; hard anodising improves thread wear resistance on fittings that are assembled often.

Cold heading

O-temper 5454 and 5754 head and upset well for special bolts and rivets; 5456 needs more force and larger radii.

Applications

Marine uses of Al-Mg hex bar

  • Fuel and cooling systems: unions, adaptors and hose tails in 5454 for warm lines and tanks
  • Fasteners: nuts, special bolts and studs for aluminium structures where stainless would cause galvanic attack
  • Deck hardware: stanchion bases, threaded sockets and awning fittings in 5754
  • Structural fittings: highly loaded threaded bosses and tie-rod ends in 5456
  • Pipe supports: threaded spacers and standoffs for pipe and cable trays
  • Shore and offshore: pontoon connectors, ladder fittings and aquaculture hardware

A typical example is a fuel-system fitting kit on an aluminium fast ferry: tank sender bosses and fill-line unions are turned from 5454-H112 hex bar because the fuel return can run warm, while the matching tank plate is 5454 or 5083 depending on location.

How to order marine hex bar

  1. SpecifyAlloy and temper, across-flats size and length or cut pieces, quantity and the governing standard with its edition (ASTM B221 / B211, EN 755 / 754 or GB/T 3191). If the vessel is classed, name the society and whether a class certificate is required.
  2. ConfirmWe confirm the standard and tolerance class can be met, which tests are included (tensile, chemistry, corrosion tests where the standard calls for them) and the delivery terms: EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP.
  3. InspectMaterial is checked for dimensions, straightness, twist and surface condition before packing; third-party or class surveyor inspection can be arranged on request.
  4. DocumentEach lot ships with a mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 showing the cast analysis and mechanical results, traceable by cast or lot number.
  5. Pack and shipBars or plates are bundled or cased with moisture protection, marked with alloy, temper, size and lot, and shipped by sea or air to your port or yard.

FAQ

Marine aluminium (aluminum) hex bar: FAQs

Which alloy should I choose for hex fittings near an engine?

5454. Its magnesium content stays below 3 %, so it does not become sensitised to intergranular corrosion in long service above 65 °C, unlike 5456 or 5083.

Is 5456 hex bar stronger than 5083?

Yes. In H112, ASTM B221 minimums are 285 MPa tensile and 130 MPa proof for 5456 against 270 and 110 MPa for 5083. Like 5083, it should be kept away from long exposure above 65 °C.

Are 5454, 5456 and 5754 hex bars class-approved for hull structure?

IACS UR W25 does not list these three alloys for extruded products, so they are normally used for fittings and equipment. A society can accept them case by case; for certified structural hex parts, 5083 or 5383 is the usual choice.

How much does an aluminium hex bar weigh?

Weight per metre is 0.866 × s² × density ÷ 1000, with s the across-flats size in millimetres. A 30 mm 5454 hex bar weighs about 2.10 kg per metre.

Can aluminium hex nuts be used on a boat instead of stainless?

Yes, on aluminium structures where galvanic corrosion is the main risk. They have lower strength than stainless, so size them accordingly and use anti-seize to prevent galling.

Can Metline cut hex bar to length?

Yes. We supply full bars or cut pieces with chamfered or deburred ends, packed to protect the flats, with an EN 10204 3.1 certificate, worldwide.
