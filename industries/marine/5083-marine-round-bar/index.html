5083 & 5383 Marine Aluminium Round Bar

5083 round bar is the standard aluminium bar for shipbuilding fittings that sit in or near seawater: it resists marine corrosion, welds without losing a heat-treated temper and is one of the few extruded grades covered by IACS class rules for hull structure. 5383 is its higher-strength, more corrosion-resistant development. Metline supplies both in O, H111 and H112, cut and certified, to yards and fabricators worldwide.

Overview

Why 5083 is the default marine round bar

5083 contains about 4.5 % magnesium with manganese and chromium. It gets its strength from magnesium in solid solution and from cold work, not from heat treatment, so welding does not wipe out a carefully aged temper the way it does with 6061 or 6082.

That single fact explains its place in shipbuilding. A bar welded into a hull, deck or tank keeps most of its strength around the weld, and the alloy's very low copper content gives it some of the best resistance to seawater of any structural aluminium. Round bar in 5083 is machined into stern-tube and rudder-stock bushes, bollard and cleat bodies, hinge pins, pad eyes, flange blanks and the many small bosses that are welded into 5083 plate structures.

5383 was developed in the 1990s as an optimised 5083. Tighter limits on iron and silicon, a higher manganese range and a zirconium addition give roughly 15 % more proof strength after welding, better fatigue performance and improved resistance to intergranular and exfoliation corrosion. Both alloys appear side by side in the extrusion table of IACS UR W25, the unified class requirement that the major classification societies apply to aluminium for hulls.

What a marine buyer gets with 5083 / 5383 bar

  • Excellent resistance to seawater, spray and marine atmosphere without coating
  • Weld zones keep close to parent-metal strength in O and H112 tempers
  • Listed in IACS UR W25 for extruded products, so class approval is straightforward
  • Same alloy family as 5083 hull plate, giving uniform corrosion behaviour across a welded assembly

Tempers

O, H111 or H112: which temper to order

O (annealed)

Fully soft with a defined maximum strength. Order O when the bar will be bent, upset or cold-headed, for example into U-bolts, shackle pins or formed handrail returns.

H111

Annealed, then lightly strain-hardened by straightening. Proof strength is higher than O (165 MPa minimum for 5083) while ductility stays good.

H112

As extruded, with guaranteed minimum properties. The usual choice for bar that will be machined into fittings and bushes.

No heat-treated tempers

5083 and 5383 cannot be solution treated and aged. Any T-temper on a 5083 order is an error to query before production.

Sensitisation limit

With more than 3 % magnesium, both alloys should not be used where metal temperature stays above about 65 °C for long periods, such as next to engine exhausts.

Plate tempers

H116 and H321, the corrosion-tested tempers, apply to rolled plate rather than extruded bar; see the 5083 marine plate page.

Standards & equivalents

Standards, class approval and equivalent designations

For classed vessels, extruded 5083 and 5383 must come from works approved by the classification society for those grades, with tests witnessed or reviewed as the society requires. IACS UR W25 lists 5083, 5383, 5059 and 5086 for extruded bars and sections in O, H111 and H112, together with 6005A, 6061 and 6082 in T5 or T6. DNV, ABS, Lloyd's Register, Bureau Veritas, ClassNK, KR and CCS apply these requirements through their own material rules. Working with 5083

Machining, welding and finishing 5083 bar

Machining

Soft and somewhat gummy. Use sharp, polished carbide with high positive rake, large chip gullets and plenty of coolant to prevent built-up edge. H112 and H111 give a cleaner finish than O.

Welding

TIG and MIG weld readily. ER5183 is the usual filler for matching strength; ER5556 gives a little more; ER5356 suits lower-stressed joints. Avoid 4xxx fillers, which form brittle Mg2Si in the weld.

Forming

O and H111 bar bends and upsets well at room temperature. Avoid hot forming in the 150 – 300 °C range, which can sensitise the alloy.

Galvanic contact

Isolate from stainless steel, bronze and copper-nickel with insulating bushes, sleeves and sealant. Never use copper-based antifouling on bare aluminium.

Anodising and painting

Takes sulphuric anodising for a protective, uniform finish, though slightly darker than 6xxx alloys. Most underwater fittings are left bare or painted as part of the hull system.

Low temperature

Strength and toughness increase as temperature falls, so 5083 is used for cryogenic and LNG equipment as well as cold-water vessels.

Applications

Where marine yards use 5083 / 5383 round bar

  • Hull and deck fittings: bollards, cleats, fairleads, pad eyes and towing points machined from bar and welded to 5083 plate
  • Steering and propulsion: rudder bearing housings, bushes, stern-tube flanges and shaft-seal carriers
  • Hinges and closures: hinge pins, hatch dogs, door handles and wedges on watertight closures
  • Piping: flange blanks, weld bosses and pipe supports in seawater, fuel and bilge systems
  • Offshore and aquaculture: fish-farm pen fittings, gangway pins, buoy and mooring hardware
  • Cryogenic and tank equipment: nozzles and machined parts for LNG and pressure equipment

A typical example is a welded aluminium workboat: the hull is 5083-H116 plate, longitudinal stiffeners are 5083 or 6082 extrusions, and every boss, lifting eye and bollard base is turned from 5083-H112 round bar so that the whole welded structure shares one corrosion behaviour and one filler wire.

FAQ

5083 marine aluminium (aluminum) round bar: FAQs

Is 5083 round bar approved for classed vessels?

The alloy is. IACS UR W25 lists extruded 5083 and 5383 in O, H111 and H112 for hull construction. Each lot must come from a works approved by your classification society and carry its certificate if the order requires it.

What is the difference between 5083 and 5383 bar?

5383 is an optimised 5083 with lower iron and silicon, more manganese and a zirconium addition. Its minimum proof strength in H112 is 190 MPa against 110 MPa for 5083, and it keeps more strength after welding with better corrosion and fatigue resistance.

Can 5083 bar be heat treated?

No. It is a non-heat-treatable alloy. It can be annealed to O or stabilised, but strength comes only from magnesium and cold work, which is also why welds do not destroy its properties.

Which filler wire is used to weld 5083 round bar?

ER5183 is standard for strength matching. ER5556 gives slightly higher weld strength; ER5356 is acceptable for lightly stressed joints. Do not use ER4043 on 5083.

Is 5083 bar suitable near engines or exhausts?

Not for long-term service above about 65 °C. At those temperatures high-magnesium alloys can become sensitised and prone to intergranular corrosion. Use 5454 or a 6xxx alloy for warm locations.

Can you supply 5083 bar cut to length with certificates?

Yes. Metline supplies 5083 and 5383 round bar in full lengths or cut pieces with an EN 10204 3.1 certificate, and arranges class or third-party inspection on request, for delivery worldwide.

Need 5083 or 5383 round bar for a vessel?

Send diameters, temper, quantities, the governing standard and the class society. We will reply with availability, certification and a delivered price.
