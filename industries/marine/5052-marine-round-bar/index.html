5052 Marine Grade Aluminium Round Bar

5052 round bar combines very good seawater resistance with easy welding and cold forming, making it a sensible choice for pins, fittings, rivets and machined parts that live in salt spray or bilge water. It is a moderate-strength alloy rather than a structural one. Metline supplies 5052 rod and bar extruded in O and H112 or cold drawn in H32 and H34, cut to length and certified, worldwide.

Overview

What 5052 round bar offers marine users

5052 is a non-heat-treatable aluminium-magnesium alloy. Magnesium near 2.5 % gives moderate strength, chromium refines the grain, and the alloy's low copper content is the main reason it resists seawater so well.

Round bar in 5052 is chosen less for raw strength than for durability and versatility. It machines into fittings and bushes, cold heads into rivets and pins, and welds into tank and frame assemblies without the dramatic strength loss of heat-treated 6xxx and 7xxx bar. The fatigue strength is high for an alloy of its static strength, which suits vibrating parts on powered craft.

Bar is supplied either straight from the extrusion press (O or H112) or extruded and then cold drawn through a die to H32 or H34. Drawing adds strength, improves surface finish and tightens diameter tolerance, which matters for automatic-lathe work and close-fitting pins.

Key points for buyers

  • Good for immersed and splash-zone parts that are not highly loaded
  • Matches 5052 sheet used for small-craft hulls and fuel tanks, avoiding mixed-alloy galvanic pairs
  • Not listed for hull structure in IACS UR W25; use 5083 or 5086 bar for class-inspected parts
  • Drawn H32 / H34 bar gives higher strength and tighter tolerance than extruded bar

Tempers

Selecting a temper for 5052 bar

O

Annealed. Highest ductility for cold heading, swaging, flaring or bending rod into handles and brackets.

H112

As extruded. The usual condition for larger diameters and general machining stock.

H32

Drawn and stabilised. Moderate strength gain with good residual ductility; a common choice for pins and turned parts.

H34

Drawn and stabilised to a higher strength. For parts that are machined rather than formed.

H38

Full-hard drawn rod, mainly in small diameters. Highest strength, lowest formability.

Cut blanks

Bar sawn into discs or slugs for machining, forging or impact extrusion.

Quality

Tolerances and inspection

Diameter, ovality, straightness and length tolerances follow EN 755-3 for extruded bar, EN 754-3 for drawn bar, or the ANSI H35.2 tables used with ASTM orders. Drawn bar is held to a much tighter diameter band than extruded bar, so specify drawn stock when the bar feeds a collet or bar feeder without turning the outside diameter.

  • Diameter and length measured on every lot
  • Straightness checked on full lengths
  • Surface inspection for die lines, pick-up and handling damage
  • Mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 with chemistry and tensile results
  • Ultrasonic inspection of large-diameter bar on request
Working with 5052

Machining, forming, welding and finishing

Machining

Machines better in drawn H32 / H34 than in O, where chips are long and stringy. Use sharp, polished tools with high positive rake, plenty of cutting fluid and chip breakers.

Cold forming

In O and H32 tempers 5052 rod can be bent, swaged, flattened and cold headed. Formability falls as cold work increases.

Hot working

Can be forged; forging is normally carried out roughly between 260 °C and 510 °C.

Welding

TIG and MIG with ER5356 filler. The heat-affected zone softens towards the annealed condition; no post-weld heat treatment is used.

Anodising

Takes clear protective anodising. Not suited to bright decorative finishes.

Galvanic contact

Use insulating washers and sleeves where 5052 meets stainless steel, bronze or carbon fibre in wet service.

Applications

Uses of 5052 round bar in marine work

  • Pins, axles and bushes for hinges, hatches and davits under light load
  • Rivets and cold-headed fasteners for riveted aluminium boats and fittings
  • Handrail and stanchion fittings machined and welded into rails
  • Tank fittings and bosses welded into 5052 fuel and water tanks
  • Turned parts such as spacers, knobs, cleats and fairleads
  • Coastal and offshore hardware: pontoon pins, fish-farm fittings, buoy hardware

FAQ

5052 marine aluminium (aluminum) round bar: FAQ

Is 5052 round bar good for marine use?

Yes. It resists seawater, salt spray and marine atmospheres very well and welds easily. It is a moderate-strength alloy, so use it for fittings and lightly loaded parts rather than primary structure.

Can 5052 bar be heat-treated to T6?

No. 5052 is a non-heat-treatable alloy. It is strengthened only by cold work, giving H tempers such as H32 and H34. T6 applies to heat-treatable alloys like 6061.

What is the weight of 5052 round bar?

About 0.002105 × d² kg per metre, with d in mm. A 25 mm bar weighs about 1.32 kg/m and a 100 mm bar about 21.0 kg/m.

Is 5052 bar easy to machine?

It is fair to machine. Drawn H32 or H34 bar gives shorter chips and a better finish than annealed bar. For high-volume turned parts, 6061-T6 or a free-machining alloy machines faster.

Which is better for a boat fitting, 5052 or 316 stainless?

Stainless is stronger, but bolting it to an aluminium hull creates a galvanic couple. A 5052 or 5083 fitting avoids that and saves weight; use stainless only with proper isolation.

Do you supply 5052 bar cut to length?

Yes. Metline supplies standard lengths or cut pieces, with mill certificates and export packing.
