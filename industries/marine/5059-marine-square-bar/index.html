High-magnesium marine square bar

5059 and 5086 Marine Grade Aluminium Square Bar

Square bar in 5086 or 5059 gives marine fabricators a weldable, seawater-resistant section for brackets, chocks, stiffeners and machined blocks. 5086 is the economical, widely produced option; 5059 offers about twice the extruded yield strength for weight-critical, class-built craft. Metline supplies both in the extruded tempers listed by IACS, cut to length and certified, to yards worldwide.

Overview

Why square bar in high-magnesium alloys

Square bar is the shipyard's raw material for chocks, foundation pads, doubler blocks, hatch-coaming stiffeners and machined brackets. Making it from the same alloy family as the hull keeps welded joints simple and corrosion behaviour uniform.

5086 contains about 4 % magnesium with manganese and chromium. It is listed in ASTM B221 and IACS UR W25, welds easily with ER5356 or ER5183 and resists seawater uncoated. Its extruded strength is modest, so it suits pads, chocks and parts sized by stiffness or bearing area rather than by high stress.

5059 contains 5 – 6 % magnesium with zinc and zirconium and is the strongest established marine 5xxx alloy. Extruded 5059-H112 has a minimum yield of 200 MPa, against 95 MPa for 5086-H112, and keeps more strength next to welds. Designers specify it for fittings on 5059-plated hulls or wherever a smaller, lighter section must carry the load. It is normally extruded to order.

What buyers should know

  • Both alloys are class-listed for extruded marine products
  • 5086 is the economical, easily formed option; 5059 is the high-strength option
  • Both resist seawater without coating and weld without heat treatment
  • Neither should see sustained temperatures above about 65 °C

Fabrication

Working with 5086 and 5059 square bar

Welding

ER5183 for structural joints in both alloys, ER5356 acceptable for 5086. Clean joints immediately before welding and qualify procedures on the actual alloy.

Machining

Both machine adequately with sharp, polished, high-rake tools and good coolant. Expect long chips in O temper 5086.

Cutting

Saw or waterjet for best edges; plasma-cut edges to be welded should be dressed back.

Bending

5086-O / H111 bends readily. 5059 needs larger radii and more allowance for springback.

Isolation

Use non-conductive washers and sleeves where bars meet steel, stainless or bronze.

Temperature

Do not use either alloy for structure held above about 65 °C for long periods.

Applications

Typical applications

  • Engine and machinery foundations: chocks, pads and doubler blocks
  • Hatch coamings and door frames: stiffeners and edge bars
  • Deck equipment bases for cranes, winches and davits on aluminium hulls
  • Machined brackets and lugs welded to hull or superstructure
  • Fender and rubbing-strake backing on workboats
  • Offshore platform and pontoon fittings exposed to splash and immersion

FAQ

5059 and 5086 aluminium (aluminum) square bar: FAQ

Is 5086 square bar suitable for shipbuilding?

Yes. 5086 is listed in IACS UR W25 for extruded products in O, H111 and H112 and is widely used for chocks, pads and fittings on aluminium vessels.

How much stronger is 5059 than 5086 square bar?

In the extruded H112 condition, 5059 has a minimum yield of 200 MPa against 95 MPa for 5086, and a minimum tensile strength of 330 MPa against 240 MPa.

How much does 5086 square bar weigh?

About 0.00266 × (side in mm)² kg per metre. A 40 mm bar weighs about 4.26 kg/m.

Can these bars be anodised?

They can take a clear protective anodic film, though it is slightly grey on high-magnesium alloys. Most marine parts are left bare or painted.

Do you supply class-certified square bar?

Where the producing mill holds approval for the product, yes. Mention the classification society when you enquire.
