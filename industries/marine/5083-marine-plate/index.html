5083 Marine Grade Aluminium Plate (H116 / H321)

5083 is the standard aluminium alloy for welded ship and boat hulls. In the H116 and H321 tempers it combines a minimum yield of 215 MPa with proven resistance to exfoliation and intergranular corrosion, and it keeps most of its strength after welding. Metline supplies 5083 plate in H116, H321, H111 and O to ASTM B928, EN 485 and classification-society rules, worldwide.

Overview

Why 5083 is the default hull alloy

5083 contains 4.0 – 4.9 % magnesium, 0.4 – 1.0 % manganese and a little chromium. It is the strongest of the widely available non-heat-treatable aluminium alloys, and because its strength comes from solid solution and cold work rather than precipitation hardening, welding reduces it only to the annealed level rather than destroying it.

That welded strength, combined with outstanding resistance to seawater, is why 5083 has been the backbone of aluminium shipbuilding for decades: ferries, workboats, patrol craft, fishing vessels, yachts, superstructures on steel ships, and offshore modules. It also keeps its toughness at very low temperature, which makes 5083-O a standard material for LNG tanks and cryogenic vessels.

For hull use the temper matters as much as the alloy. Ordinary strain-hardened tempers such as H32 can become susceptible to exfoliation when the magnesium-rich beta phase forms continuous films at grain boundaries. The H116 and H321 tempers are produced and tested specifically to avoid this, and are the only rolled tempers that classification rules accept for most hull plating.

What to specify for hull plate

  • Alloy and temper: 5083-H116 or 5083-H321 (do not substitute H32)
  • Standard: ASTM B928, EN 485 / EN 13195, or the class society's rules
  • Classification society and certificate type: class certificate or EN 10204 3.2
  • Corrosion testing: ASTM G66 and G67, or approved metallographic method

Tempers

5083 tempers explained

H116

Strain-hardened with controlled processing so that the plate meets both strength and corrosion-test requirements. Widely used for hull shell, decks and bulkheads.

H321

Strain-hardened and thermally stabilised, with a controlled maximum strength. Same minimums as H116 under UR W25, certified separately.

H111

Annealed and slightly strain-hardened. Used where plates are formed heavily, and for many offshore and pressure applications.

O

Fully annealed. For deep forming and cryogenic tanks; LNG containment commonly uses 5083-O.

H112

As hot-rolled, mainly for thicker plate. Listed for marine use with lower minimum strength than H116.

H32 (not for hulls)

A general-engineering temper without the marine corrosion controls. Fine for many industrial uses, but not a substitute for H116 / H321 in hull plating.

Corrosion acceptance

Exfoliation and intergranular corrosion testing

Under IACS UR W25 and ASTM B928, H116 and H321 plate for hulls is accepted only after showing resistance to exfoliation and intergranular corrosion.

TestAcceptance criterion
ASTM G66 (ASSET), exfoliationNo evidence of exfoliation; pitting rating PB or better
ASTM G67 (NAMLT), intergranularMass loss not more than 15 mg/cm²
Metallographic examinationGrain-boundary precipitation matches the approved reference micrograph at 500×

These tests prove the plate leaves the mill in good condition. In service, avoid prolonged temperatures above about 65 °C, which can still sensitise 5083 over time. That is why exhaust trunks and hot tanks are designed in 5454 or insulated.

Fabrication

Welding, forming and cutting 5083 plate

Welding

MIG and TIG with ER5183 filler, the standard for 5083 hulls. In the heat-affected zone strength falls to about the O-temper level (minimum yield 125 MPa), which class rules use for welded design.

Porosity control

Clean and brush joints just before welding, keep filler wire dry and sealed, and shelter welding from wind. Hydrogen from moisture and oil is the main cause of porosity.

Forming

H116 and H321 bend to moderate radii; allow for springback. For severe shapes, form in O or H111. Hot forming above about 200 °C should be avoided unless approved, because it can affect corrosion properties.

Cutting

Plasma, waterjet, saw and router cutting are all used. Dress back plasma-cut edges that will be welded.

Machining

Fair; the alloy is gummy. Sharp carbide, high rake and good coolant are needed. For machined tooling, cast and stress-relieved 5083 plate is a separate product.

Corrosion design

Isolate from steel and copper alloys, use aluminium-compatible antifouling, fit anodes and avoid crevices that trap water.

Applications

Where 5083 plate is used

  • Hulls and decks of ferries, workboats, patrol craft, fishing vessels and yachts
  • Superstructures on steel ships, joined with explosion-bonded transition joints
  • Offshore accommodation modules, walkways and access structures
  • LNG and cryogenic tanks in O temper, where toughness at −162 °C matters
  • Pressure vessels to ASME SB-209 within the alloy's temperature limits
  • Road and rail tankers, tipper bodies and transport structures
  • Armour in specially processed tempers to military specifications

FAQ

5083 marine aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQ

What is the difference between 5083-H116 and 5083-H321?

Both meet the same minimum strengths (yield 215 MPa, tensile 305 MPa) and must pass exfoliation and intergranular corrosion tests. H321 includes a final thermal stabilisation and has a controlled maximum strength; H116 is produced by controlled rolling. They are certified separately; order the one on the drawing.

Can I use 5083-H32 for a boat hull?

It is not recommended for class-built hulls. H32 lacks the corrosion-test controls of H116 and H321, and IACS UR W25 does not list it. It is fine for many industrial uses.

What is the density of 5083 aluminium?

2.66 g/cm³. A 2000 × 6000 × 10 mm plate weighs about 319 kg.

Which filler wire is used to weld 5083?

ER5183 is the standard filler for hulls. ER5356 can be used for lightly stressed joints. Avoid 4043 and 4047.

Is 5083 suitable for cryogenic tanks?

5083-O keeps its toughness at LNG temperatures and is widely used for cryogenic storage and transport tanks.

What is the maximum service temperature for 5083?

About 65 °C for sustained service. Longer exposure at higher temperatures can sensitise the alloy to intergranular corrosion.

Do you supply class-certified 5083 plate?

Yes, where the producing mill holds approval from the society you name. We confirm certificate type and corrosion testing before you order.
