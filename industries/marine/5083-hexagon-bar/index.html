Marine hexagon bar

5083 and 5383 Marine Grade Aluminium Hexagon Bar

5083 hexagon bar is the standard stock for aluminium nuts, bosses, couplings and fittings on welded aluminium vessels: it is the same alloy as most hull plate, resists seawater uncoated and keeps its strength when welded. 5383 is the higher-strength development of 5083 for weight-critical craft. Metline supplies both in O, H111 and H112, cut to length and certified, worldwide.

Overview

5083 and 5383 hex bar for marine fittings

5083 is the most widely used aluminium alloy in shipbuilding. Hexagon bar in the same alloy lets yards machine nuts, bosses and threaded fittings that weld straight into 5083 plating with the same filler and procedures, and that age in seawater at the same rate as the hull around them.

The alloy contains 4.0 – 4.9 % magnesium with manganese and chromium. It is not heat-treatable; extruded hex bar is supplied as-extruded (H112), annealed (O) or annealed and lightly strain-hardened (H111). Because its strength comes from solid solution rather than precipitation, welding softens it only modestly, unlike 6061 or 6082.

5383 is a tighter-controlled, slightly richer version of 5083 with more manganese and a zirconium allowance. In the extruded H112 condition its minimum yield is 190 MPa against 110 MPa for 5083, and it keeps more strength after welding. It is used where designers of fast craft want smaller, lighter fittings and is usually extruded to order.

Why yards order 5083 hex

  • Same alloy and filler as the hull, so welded fittings need no special procedure
  • Excellent seawater resistance without coating
  • Listed by IACS UR W25 for extruded marine products in O, H111 and H112
  • Readily available in common across-flats sizes

Tempers

Which temper to order

H112

As extruded. The usual condition for machined nuts, bosses and fittings. Minimum yield 110 MPa (5083) or 190 MPa (5383).

H111

Annealed then lightly strain-hardened by straightening. 5083-H111 has a higher specified yield (165 MPa) than H112 because of that controlled cold work.

O

Fully annealed, for parts that will be swaged, flared or cold formed after machining.

Fabrication

Machining and welding 5083 hex bar

Machining

5083 is gummy in O and fair in H112. Use sharp, polished carbide inserts with high positive rake, high speeds and flood coolant; chip breakers help on automatic lathes.

Threads

Rolled threads outperform cut threads in fatigue. Use anti-seize or thread inserts where stainless fasteners are used.

Welding

ER5183 is the standard filler for 5083 and 5383. ER5356 is acceptable for less-stressed joints. Do not use 4xxx fillers.

Temperature limit

Avoid sustained service above about 65 °C; long exposure between about 65 °C and 200 °C can sensitise the alloy to intergranular corrosion.

Galvanic isolation

Separate from bronze, copper and stainless steel with insulating sleeves and washers.

Finishing

Normally left bare in seawater service; can be anodised or painted.

Applications

Typical applications

  • Nuts, couplings and unions for seawater cooling, bilge and drain lines
  • Through-hull and deck bosses welded into 5083 plating
  • Standoffs and mounting studs for equipment and consoles
  • Cleats, fairleads and bollard parts on workboats and ferries
  • High-strength fittings in 5383 on fast ferries and patrol craft
  • Offshore, aquaculture and port hardware in permanent immersion

FAQ

5083 and 5383 aluminium (aluminum) hex bar: FAQ

What is the yield strength of 5083-H112 hex bar?

The specified minimum is 110 MPa, with minimum tensile strength 270 MPa, under ASTM B221, EN 755-2 and IACS UR W25. Actual values are usually higher.

What is the difference between 5083 and 5383?

5383 is a more tightly controlled variant with more manganese and a zirconium allowance. Extruded 5383-H112 has a minimum yield of 190 MPa against 110 MPa for 5083 and keeps more strength after welding.

Can 5083 hex bar be welded to 5083 plate?

Yes, with ER5183 filler and the same procedure used for the hull. That is one of the main reasons for using 5083 hex for marine fittings.

How much does 5083 hex bar weigh?

About 0.002304 × (across flats in mm)² kg per metre. A 30 mm hex weighs about 2.07 kg/m.

Is 5083 hex bar suitable for hot areas?

Not for sustained service above about 65 °C, where sensitisation can occur. Use 5454 for warm areas.
