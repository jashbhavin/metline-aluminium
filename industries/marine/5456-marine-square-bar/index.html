5456, 5454 & 5754 Marine Aluminium Square Bar

Square bar in 5456, 5454 or 5754 gives boatbuilders a solid, weldable aluminium-magnesium section for seatings, chocks, frames and machined blocks, matched to the alloy of the surrounding plate. 5456 gives the most strength, 5454 tolerates warm service and 5754 is the economical general-purpose grade. Overview

Matching square bar to plate and service

The simplest rule for welded aluminium boats is to keep the whole structure in one alloy family, so welds, plate and fittings corrode alike and one filler wire serves the job. Square bar in 5456, 5454 or 5754 lets a builder do that for the solid parts of a structure.

Each alloy suits a different part of a vessel. 5456 pairs with 5456 or 5083 hull plating where a seating or chock carries heavy load and the space stays cool. 5454 pairs with 5454 tank and casing plate in warm spaces, because it does not become sensitised above 65 °C. 5754 is used with 5754 superstructure and interior plating, and for general deck hardware where strength demands are moderate and cost matters.

Unlike 6xxx square bar, none of these alloys relies on heat treatment, so welding a square bar to plate costs little strength beyond the local annealing of any cold work. That makes them well suited to welded seatings and frames that are fabricated, faired and painted as part of the hull.

Which alloy for which job

  • 5456: heavy machinery seatings, crane and davit bases in cool spaces
  • 5454: tank stiffeners, engine-room frames and exhaust-area supports
  • 5754: superstructure framing, furniture frames, ladders and general hardware
  • All: seawater-resistant, non-magnetic, weldable with 5xxx fillers

Standards

Standards and class acceptance

Square bar in these alloys is ordered to ASTM B221 (extruded), EN 755-2 with EN 755-4 tolerances, or GB/T 3191. IACS UR W25 does not list 5456, 5454 or 5754 for extruded products (it names 5083, 5383, 5059 and 5086), although 5456 and 5754 are listed for rolled plate. For classed structures, agree the use of these bars with the society in advance or choose 5083 or 5383 square bar. For fittings, frames and equipment outside class scope, a 3.1 mill certificate to the product standard is usually sufficient.

Design & fabrication

Design and fabrication notes

Filler choice

5456: ER5556 or ER5183. 5454: ER5554 for warm service, ER5356 for cool. 5754: ER5356 or ER5183.

Fillet welds

Size fillet welds on the as-welded strength of the weaker of bar and plate; the bar's H112 strength does not survive in the weld zone.

Fit-up

Square bar sits flush on plate, so root gaps are small. Clean oxide just before welding and keep joints dry.

Machining

Use sharp tools with high rake and coolant; helical-coil inserts for tapped holes that see repeated assembly.

Galvanic isolation

Use insulating pads and sleeves where steel engine feet, stainless bolts or bronze parts bear on the bar.

Heat

Keep 5456 and 5754 seatings away from long-term heat above 65 °C; use 5454 where that cannot be avoided.

Applications

Applications on vessels and offshore

  • Seatings: engine, generator, pump and winch foundations
  • Tank stiffening: 5454 bars on warm fuel and water tanks
  • Frames and coamings: hatch and door frames, window surrounds
  • Fendering: replaceable rubbing strips on workboats
  • Superstructure and interiors: 5754 framing for furniture, partitions and ladders
  • Offshore and coastal: pontoon frames, boat landings, aquaculture structures

On a typical aluminium crew-transfer vessel, the deck crane pedestal sits on a 5456 square-bar ring welded to 5083 deck plate, the engine-room fuel day tank is stiffened with 5454 bar, and the wheelhouse furniture frames use 5754, each chosen for its location.

How to order marine square bar

  1. SpecifyAlloy and temper, side length and length or cut pieces, quantity and the governing standard with its edition (ASTM B221, EN 755 or GB/T 3191). If the vessel is classed, name the society and whether a class certificate is required.
  2. ConfirmWe confirm the standard and tolerance class can be met, which tests are included (tensile, chemistry, corrosion tests where the standard calls for them) and the delivery terms: EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP.
  3. InspectMaterial is checked for dimensions, straightness, twist and surface condition before packing; third-party or class surveyor inspection can be arranged on request.
  4. DocumentEach lot ships with a mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 showing the cast analysis and mechanical results, traceable by cast or lot number.
  5. Pack and shipBars or plates are bundled or cased with moisture protection, marked with alloy, temper, size and lot, and shipped by sea or air to your port or yard.

FAQ

Marine aluminium (aluminum) square bar: FAQs

Which is strongest: 5456, 5454 or 5754 square bar?

5456. Its ASTM B221 minimum in H112 is 285 MPa tensile, against 215 MPa for 5454 and 180 MPa for 5754 (EN 755-2).

Which square bar should I use in a hot engine room?

5454. It does not become sensitised in long service above 65 °C, which 5456 and, to a lesser degree, 5754 can.

Can these square bars be used for classed hull structure?

IACS UR W25 does not list them for extruded products, so use needs the society's agreement. For certified structural square bar, 5083 or 5383 is the usual route.

Do I lose strength when welding square bar to plate?

Only modestly. These alloys are not heat-treated, so the weld zone falls to roughly annealed strength, which for O and H112 is close to the purchased value.

What does a 50 mm 5456 square bar weigh?

About 6.65 kg per metre, or 39.9 kg for a 6 m bar.

Can Metline cut square bar to a list?

Yes. We supply cut pieces to your cutting list with an EN 10204 3.1 certificate and export packing, worldwide.
