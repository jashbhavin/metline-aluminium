5A02 Marine Grade Aluminium Square Bar

5A02 square bar is a solid, four-sided extrusion in a 2.5 % magnesium aluminium alloy, chosen for brackets, keys, stiffeners, frames and machined blocks that must survive salt air and seawater. It is supplied in O and H112 tempers to GB/T 3191 and corresponds closely to AMg2, 5251 and 5052. Metline supplies 5A02 square bar in standard and custom sizes, cut to length and certified, to customers worldwide.

Overview

Where square bar fits in marine fabrication

Square bar offers flat, square reference faces on all four sides. Fabricators use it as stock for machined blocks, as a weld-on stiffener or doubler, as the frame of a hatch or grating, and as keys and locating pieces in deck machinery. In marine work the bar often ends up welded to plate or tube, so the alloy needs to be weldable and should match the corrosion behaviour of the surrounding structure.

5A02 fits that brief for lightly to moderately loaded parts. It is an aluminium-magnesium alloy with 2.0 – 2.8 % magnesium and a small manganese or chromium addition, specified in GB/T 3190. It has no heat-treated temper to lose during welding, accepts ordinary ER5356 filler, and pairs naturally with 5xxx hull and superstructure plate so that there is no galvanic difference between bar and plate.

Strength is modest: typical tensile strength is roughly 170 – 220 MPa in the as-supplied tempers. For a heavily loaded stiffener, a higher-magnesium alloy such as 5083 or a heat-treated 6082-T6 bar is a better match. For trim, brackets, fittings, grating frames and other secondary items, 5A02 square bar is economical and durable.

Magnesium in solid solution provides most of the strength and the corrosion resistance. Iron and silicon are held at 0.40 % each so that the bar stays ductile and takes a clean anodised finish. The copper limit of 0.10 % is what separates genuine marine 5xxx alloys from general-purpose grades that pit more readily in seawater.

Fabrication

Fabrication guidance

Milling

Climb-mill with sharp, high-rake cutters and flood coolant. Light finishing passes reduce burrs, which soft Al-Mg alloys tend to raise.

Sawing

Use carbide-tipped saw blades with a negative or neutral hook and lubrication to avoid loading the teeth.

Welding

MIG with ER5356 is fastest for fillet welds to plate; TIG for small fittings. Keep joints clean and dry to avoid porosity.

Bending

O temper bends cold to tight radii. H112 bends to moderate radii; anneal locally if cracking appears on the outside face.

Finishing

Brushed, anodised or painted. For painting, use a chromate-free etch primer suited to aluminium before the marine topcoat.

Fastening

Use aluminium, stainless with isolating washers, or coated fasteners. Seal faying surfaces to keep seawater out of crevices.

Applications

Applications on ships and boats

  • Hatch and door frames: welded square frames for hatches, access panels and lockers
  • Gratings and walkways: edge frames and cross-bars for deck gratings and engine-room walkways
  • Brackets and cleats: machined blocks for mounting equipment to bulkheads
  • Keys and guides: keys, rubbing strips and slide guides in deck machinery
  • Railing and ladder parts: balusters, steps and connection blocks
  • Fender and rubbing strips: sacrificial rubbing bars on small craft and pontoons

FAQ

5A02 marine aluminium (aluminum) square bar: FAQs

What is 5A02 square bar used for on boats?

Mainly secondary structure and fittings: hatch and door frames, grating frames, brackets, keys, rubbing strips and ladder or railing parts, especially where they are welded to 5xxx plate.

Is 5A02 strong enough for structural stiffeners?

For lightly loaded members, yes. For primary stiffeners or frames, class rules normally call for higher-strength approved grades such as 5083 plate and 6082 or 6005A extrusions.

Can 5A02 square bar be anodised?

Yes. It takes clear sulphuric anodising well, which adds salt-spray resistance. Colour anodising works but is less vivid than on 6xxx alloys.

What is the weight of a 50 mm 5A02 square bar?

About 6.70 kg per metre, so a 6 m bar weighs about 40.2 kg.

Which temper should I choose?

H112 for machined parts, because it cuts more cleanly; O for parts that will be bent or formed cold.
