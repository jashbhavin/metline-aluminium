5A06 Marine Grade Aluminium Plate

5A06 is the strongest common non-heat-treatable aluminium-magnesium alloy, with 5.8 – 6.8 % magnesium, and the Chinese counterpart of Russian AMg6. In O and H112 tempers it gives welded hull, deck and tank structure higher strength than 5083 while keeping good seawater resistance. Metline supplies 5A06 sheet and plate cut to size, with certificates, to shipyards and fabricators worldwide.

Overview

Why 5A06 is used for welded marine structures

Designers of welded aluminium structures face a basic trade-off. Heat-treated alloys such as 6082 are strong as supplied but lose roughly half their strength next to every weld. Non-heat-treatable 5xxx alloys start weaker but keep their strength through welding. 5A06 pushes the 5xxx side of that trade-off as far as it practically goes: its high magnesium content gives annealed plate a minimum tensile strength of about 315 MPa, which welded joints largely retain.

The alloy is defined in GB/T 3190 and descends from the Soviet AMg6, which was developed for welded hulls, rocket tanks and pressure vessels. A small titanium addition refines the grain, and a trace of beryllium (a few thousandths of a per cent) limits oxidation of the magnesium-rich melt during casting and welding. In Chinese and CIS shipbuilding, 5A06 is used for hull plating on fast craft, high-strength decks, tanks and armour-style protective panels.

The price of all that magnesium is a higher sensitivity to temperature. Above about 65 °C, sustained exposure lets the beta phase precipitate at grain boundaries, which can lead to intergranular corrosion and stress-corrosion cracking in seawater. 5A06 should therefore be kept away from hot machinery spaces unless the design has been checked, and it should not be cold-worked heavily before service.

Where 5A06 makes sense

  • Welded structure where strength after welding governs plate thickness
  • Weight-critical craft where a thinner plate than 5083 gives a real saving
  • Vessels built to GB, CCS or GOST-based designs that already specify 5A06 or AMg6
  • Service temperatures that stay below about 65 °C

Fabrication

Welding and fabrication of 5A06

Filler wire

Use a high-magnesium filler such as ER5183 or ER5556 (or the matching Al-Mg6 wire). Lower-magnesium fillers produce welds weaker than the parent plate.

Weld quality

High-Mg alloys are prone to porosity if oxide and moisture are present. Brush, degrease and weld promptly; control shielding gas flow.

Post-weld strength

Joints in O-temper plate retain close to the annealed parent strength; design codes still apply a weld-zone allowance.

Forming

Cold forming is possible in O temper. Avoid heavy cold work before service, which increases susceptibility to stress corrosion.

Machining

Machines more freely than lower-Mg 5xxx alloys because it is harder; use sharp carbide tools and coolant.

Heat exposure

Do not hold at 65 – 200 °C during fabrication (for example during paint curing) for longer than necessary.

Applications

Applications of 5A06 marine plate

  • Hull shell plating: bottom and side shell on high-speed craft where weight saving is critical
  • Decks and frames: welded decks, web frames and girders
  • Tanks: fuel, water and cargo tanks with welded seams
  • Protective panels: ballistic and impact panels on patrol vessels
  • Offshore equipment: gangways, walkways and module structures
  • Non-marine welded structures: pressure vessels, tank trucks and rail vehicle parts

FAQ

5A06 marine aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQs

Is 5A06 stronger than 5083?

Yes. Annealed 5A06 plate has a minimum tensile strength of about 315 MPa against about 275 MPa for 5083-O, thanks to its higher magnesium content.

What is 5A06 equivalent to?

Russian AMg6 is practically the same alloy. There is no direct Aluminum Association equivalent; 5083 and 5456 contain less magnesium.

Why does 5A06 contain beryllium?

A trace of beryllium, at most 0.005 %, reduces oxidation of the magnesium-rich melt and weld pool. It is far below levels of concern in the finished plate.

Can 5A06 be used for hull plating?

Yes, on vessels built to rules that approve it. Keep service temperatures below about 65 °C and use high-magnesium filler wire.

What does a 6 mm 5A06 sheet weigh?

About 15.8 kg per m², so a 1500 × 3000 mm sheet weighs about 71 kg.
