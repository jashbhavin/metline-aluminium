Marine Grade 6060, 6005A, 6082 and 6061 Aluminium Hexagonal Bar

Heat-treatable 6xxx hex bar is the standard stock for turned marine fittings, couplings, nuts and spacers that need more strength and better machinability than 5xxx alloys. 6060 is the softest and easiest to anodise, 6005A and 6061 sit in the middle, and 6082-T6 is the strongest. Overview

Choosing a 6xxx hex bar for marine fittings

A hexagon gives a turned part its spanner flats for free. Marine engineers use hex bar for hose tails, union nuts, through-hull adaptors in non-immersed positions, cable glands, stand-offs, threaded spacers and the bodies of deck hardware. The 6xxx (Al-Mg-Si) alloys dominate this work because they machine cleanly in T6 temper, anodise well and offer two to three times the proof strength of soft 5xxx alloys.

The four grades on this page cover the full range of 6xxx strength. They share the same basic metallurgy (magnesium silicide precipitates formed by solution heat treatment and ageing), but they differ in how much silicon, magnesium and manganese they contain, and that changes strength, extrudability and surface quality.

For parts on deck, in the superstructure or inside the hull, any of them performs well, particularly when anodised. For parts permanently wetted by seawater, the 5xxx alloys (5083, 5086, 5A02) are the more conservative choice, and 6xxx parts should be anodised or painted and electrically isolated from stainless steel and bronze.

6060

Lowest strength, finest surface and best decorative anodising. Use for trim fittings, light-duty spacers and visible hardware.

6005A

Medium strength with good extrudability. A sensible upgrade from 6060 where loads are moderate.

6061

The ASTM-world general-engineering grade: good strength, excellent machinability and anodising. Standard in North America and Asia.

6082

Highest strength of the group and the European structural standard. Choose for loaded fittings and fasteners.

Fabrication

Machining, welding and corrosion protection

Machining

T6 bar of all four grades machines well; 6061 and 6082 give the cleanest chips. Use positive-rake carbide and coolant. Avoid O and T4 for heavy turning.

Threads

Cut or rolled threads both work in T6. Use anti-seize on stainless fasteners and avoid aluminium threads in high-cycle loading.

Welding

Weldable with ER5356 or ER4043, but the heat-affected zone loses roughly half its T6 strength. Hex fittings are usually machined, not welded.

Anodising

Clear anodising to 15 – 25 µm is the most effective protection in marine air; 6060 gives the most even appearance.

Galvanic isolation

Separate from stainless steel, bronze and carbon steel with insulating washers and sleeves; seal crevices.

Immersion

For constant seawater immersion, switch to 5083 or 5086, or protect with coatings and anodes.

Applications

Typical marine uses

  • Hose and pipe fittings for fuel, fresh-water, hydraulic return and ventilation systems
  • Union nuts and adaptors on deck equipment and davits
  • Stand-offs and spacers for electrical panels, antennas and instruments
  • Cable gland bodies and bulkhead connectors
  • Deck hardware such as cleat bases, rail fittings and canopy hardware

FAQ

Marine 6xxx aluminium (aluminum) hex bar: FAQs

Which is stronger, 6082 or 6061 hex bar?

In T6, 6082 is slightly stronger: EN 755-2 minimums are 310 / 260 MPa for 6082 (20 – 150 mm) against 260 / 240 MPa for 6061.

Is 6060 hex bar suitable for marine use?

Yes for lightly loaded fittings above the waterline, especially when anodised. It is the weakest of the four grades, so check load-bearing parts.

What does T8511 mean?

Solution heat-treated, cold-worked, stress-relieved by stretching and artificially aged. It gives T6-class strength with reduced residual stress.

How much does a 24 mm aluminium hex bar weigh?

About 1.35 kg per metre in any 6xxx alloy.

Can 6xxx hex bar be used underwater?

It can with anodising or coating and galvanic isolation, but 5083 or 5086 is the safer choice for permanent seawater immersion.

Do you supply hex bar to ASTM standards?

Yes. 6061 hex bar can be certified to ASTM B221 (extruded) or B211 (cold finished); 6060, 6005A and 6082 are usually certified to EN 755.
