5754 Marine Aluminium Sheet & Plate

5754 is Europe's workhorse aluminium-magnesium plate: very good seawater resistance, excellent formability and easy welding at a lower cost than 5083. It is one of the rolled alloys named in IACS UR W25 for hull construction and is widely used for superstructures, interiors, tanks and small-craft hulls. Overview

Why boatbuilders use 5754

5754 (EN AW-5754, AlMg3) contains 2.6 – 3.6 % magnesium, with manganese plus chromium held between 0.10 and 0.60 %. It sits between 5052 and 5083 in strength and is the most widely stocked Al-Mg plate in European markets.

For marine work its attraction is balance. It is markedly stronger than 5052 and 5005, resists seawater and marine atmosphere almost as well as 5083, bends and presses more easily than 5083, and welds readily with 5xxx fillers. Its lower magnesium also makes it less prone to sensitisation than 5083 or 5456, though long exposure to high temperature should still be avoided.

IACS UR W25 lists 5754 among the rolled alloys for hull construction, in the O and H111 tempers, so classification societies accept it for plating where its strength is enough. In practice 5754 is chosen for superstructures, wheelhouses, decks of smaller craft, bulkheads, interior linings, tanks and the hulls of small boats, while 5083 or 5383 is reserved for highly stressed bottom and side shell plating of larger vessels.

Strengths of 5754 plate

  • Listed in IACS UR W25 for rolled products (O and H111)
  • Very good seawater and marine-atmosphere corrosion resistance
  • Excellent formability for shaped superstructure panels and pressings
  • Lower cost and wider availability than 5083 in many markets

Tempers

Which 5754 temper for which job

H111

Annealed with slight strain hardening from levelling. The standard marine plate temper and the one most often certified to class.

O

Fully annealed, for deep pressings, spun parts and tight bends.

H22 / H32

Quarter-hard sheet for flat panels, linings and tank walls that need more stiffness and dent resistance.

H24 / H34

Half-hard sheet for flat, unformed panels and chequer-style deck applications.

H112

As-rolled thicker plate for doublers, brackets and machined parts.

Class scope

UR W25 lists 5754 in O and H111. Other tempers can be used for non-structural parts or with the society's agreement.

Standards & class rules

Standards and classification

5754 is ordered to EN 485-1 to -4 (sheet and plate), with composition to EN 573-3; Chinese projects use GB/T 3880 or the marine plate standard GB/T 22641. For classed vessels, plate must come from a works approved by the society for 5754, with one tensile test per batch (and per additional 2000 kg) as UR W25 requires. Corrosion testing to ASTM G66 and G67 is required by UR W25 only for the higher-magnesium alloys in H116 and H321, not for 5754.

Fabrication

Welding, forming and finishing 5754

Welding

MIG and TIG with ER5356 or ER5183 filler. Weld zones fall to O-temper strength (190 MPa minimum), which is already the design basis for H111.

Forming

H111 bends to tight radii (about 1 × t for thin plate) and presses well, making it ideal for curved wheelhouse fronts and coamings.

Cutting

Plasma, waterjet, laser (thin sheet) and saw. Edge-prepare welded joints mechanically.

Painting

Degrease, abrade or etch-prime, then apply a marine epoxy system. Avoid copper antifouling on aluminium.

Anodising

Gives a uniform protective finish for windows, trim and interior panels.

Distortion

Thin 5754 superstructure panels distort under welding heat; use intermittent welds, jigs and correct sequence.

Applications

Where 5754 plate is used at sea

  • Superstructures and wheelhouses on workboats, ferries and yachts
  • Small-craft hulls such as RIB hulls, tenders, fishing boats and landing craft
  • Decks and bulkheads where class strength requirements are moderate
  • Tanks: fresh-water, grey-water and fuel tanks
  • Interiors: linings, ceilings, partitions and furniture
  • Deck equipment: hatches, doors, gangways and ladders

A typical aluminium fishing vessel might use 5083-H116 for the bottom and side shell, 5754-H111 for the wheelhouse, casing and internal bulkheads, and 6082 extrusions for frames, trimming cost and weight while keeping the structure in compatible alloys.

FAQ

5754 marine aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQs

Is 5754 a marine-grade aluminium?

Yes. It resists seawater very well and is one of the rolled alloys listed in IACS UR W25 for hull construction, in O and H111 tempers.

What is the difference between 5754 and 5083?

5083 has more magnesium (4.0 – 4.9 %) and is about 40 % stronger in the annealed state, so it is used for main hull plating. 5754 is easier to form, cheaper and less prone to sensitisation, and suits superstructures, interiors and small craft.

What is 5754-H111?

Annealed plate given a small amount of strain hardening during levelling. Its UR W25 minimums are 190 – 240 MPa tensile and 80 MPa proof, the same as O temper.

Can 5754 be used for fuel tanks?

Yes. 5754 is widely used for fuel and water tanks. For tanks that run above about 65 °C, 5454 is the safer choice.

How much does a 5754 plate weigh?

2.67 kg per m² per mm. A 1500 × 3000 × 5 mm plate weighs about 60 kg.

Do you supply 5754 marine plate internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies 5754 sheet and plate cut to size with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and export packing, and arranges class certification from approved works on request.

Need 5754 plate for a hull or superstructure?

Send temper, thicknesses, sizes, quantity and the class society. We will reply with availability, certification and a delivered price.
