5052 Marine Grade Aluminium Square Bar

5052 square bar is a corrosion-resistant aluminium-magnesium bar for brackets, cleats, backing blocks and frames that sit in seawater or salt spray. It welds easily to 5052 sheet and needs no coating to survive marine exposure, though it is a moderate-strength alloy rather than a structural hull grade. Metline supplies 5052 square bar in O and H112, and drawn tempers on request, cut to length worldwide.

Overview

Where square 5052 bar is used on boats

A square section gives flat faces for drilling, tapping and welding, and a strong, compact shape for brackets and frames. In 5052 those parts gain the alloy's very good resistance to seawater and its ability to be welded without heat treatment.

Boat builders use 5052 square bar for engine-bed packing, cleat and fairlead bases, backing blocks behind deck fittings, small frames, ladder treads and the edge stiffeners of hatches. Because the bar matches the 5052 sheet used for small-craft hulls and tanks, welded assemblies stay single-alloy, which simplifies welding procedures and avoids galvanic differences.

Square bar is normally extruded and supplied in O or H112. Smaller sizes can be cold drawn to H32 or H34 for higher strength, sharper corners and tighter tolerances. For heavily loaded or class-inspected structure, 5083 square bar is the stronger, class-listed option.

Practical advantages

  • Flat faces make it simple to drill, tap and weld into fabrications
  • Very good seawater resistance with no coating required
  • Same alloy as common small-craft sheet, so welded assemblies are uniform
  • Readily bent or twisted in O temper for brackets and handles

Working with 5052

Fabrication tips for 5052 square bar

Welding

Weld with ER5356 by MIG or TIG. Clean and brush the joint immediately before welding to avoid porosity. 5052 does not need or respond to post-weld heat treatment.

Machining

Drill and tap with sharp tools and cutting fluid. H112 and drawn tempers give cleaner holes and threads than annealed bar.

Bending

O temper bends easily; H112 bends to moderate radii. Heat is not normally needed and can soften strain-hardened tempers.

Fastening

Use aluminium or isolated stainless fasteners. Insulating washers stop galvanic attack where stainless bolts pass through 5052.

Finishing

Can be left bare, painted after etch priming, or anodised for a protective clear film.

Service temperature

Suitable for normal marine temperatures and cryogenic service; not for sustained use above about 100 °C.

Applications

Applications of 5052 square bar

  • Backing blocks and pads behind cleats, winches and deck hardware
  • Engine and equipment mounts on small craft, welded to hull stringers
  • Hatch and door frames, edge stiffeners and lid hinges
  • Ladders, steps and gangway parts
  • Tank baffles and supports welded inside 5052 tanks
  • Marina and pontoon hardware exposed to splash and spray

FAQ

5052 aluminium (aluminum) square bar: FAQ

Is 5052 square bar marine grade?

It is widely used in marine service because it resists seawater very well. It is not a class-approved structural hull alloy, so use 5083 or 5086 for class-inspected parts.

What temper is 5052 square bar supplied in?

Usually H112 (as extruded) or O (annealed). Small sizes can be drawn to H32 or H34 for higher strength and tighter tolerances.

How much does 5052 square bar weigh?

0.00268 × (side in mm)² kg per metre. A 25 mm square bar weighs about 1.68 kg/m; a 50 mm bar about 6.70 kg/m.

Can 5052 square bar be welded to 5083 plate?

Yes. Use ER5183 or ER5356 filler. The joint strength is governed by the weaker 5052 side.

Do you cut square bar to size?

Yes. Metline supplies standard lengths or cut pieces with mill certificates and export packing.
