5052 Marine Aluminium Plate & Sheet

5052-H32 is the most widely used aluminium sheet for small boats, fuel tanks and marine fittings: it resists seawater, bends without cracking and welds easily. It is a moderate-strength alloy and not a class-approved hull grade for ships, so it suits recreational craft, tanks and outfitting rather than primary ship structure. Metline supplies 5052 sheet and plate in O, H32 and H34, plain or tread, worldwide.

Overview

Why 5052 is the default sheet for small craft

5052 is an aluminium alloy with about 2.5 % magnesium and 0.25 % chromium. It cannot be heat-treated; its tempers come from controlled cold rolling, and the H3x tempers are given a low-temperature stabilising treatment so that properties do not drift over time.

Three properties make it the workhorse of aluminium boat building. It resists seawater and marine atmospheres far better than 3xxx and 6xxx sheet. In H32 it can be folded into chines, tank corners and gunwales without cracking. And it welds by MIG or TIG with almost no loss of strength, because the as-welded condition is not far from the delivered condition.

Its limits matter just as much. 5052-H32 has a minimum yield of about 160 MPa, while 5083-H116 offers 215 MPa and keeps more of it after welding, so ship hulls, workboats and anything built under classification-society rules use 5083, 5086 or 5383 instead. 5052 is not in IACS UR W25, the requirement that governs class-approved hull aluminium.

Where 5052 plate fits best

  • Hulls of dinghies, jon boats, skiffs and small fishing boats built to recreational-craft rules
  • Fuel, water and holding tanks on boats of all sizes
  • Consoles, hatches, lockers, bulkhead linings and interior joinery
  • Tread plate for decks, steps and engine-room floors

The fatigue strength of 5052-H32 is about 117 MPa at 5 × 108 cycles, unusually high for its static strength. That is one reason it performs well in vibrating applications such as outboard-powered hulls and tanks.

Tempers

Choosing between O, H32, H34 and H112

5052-H32

Quarter-hard, stabilised. The standard marine temper: enough strength for panels and tanks, and bends to tight radii. If a drawing just says "5052 marine sheet", it almost always means H32.

5052-H34

Half-hard, stabilised. Higher strength for flat panels and covers that see little forming; bend radii must be larger than for H32.

5052-O

Annealed for deep drawing, spinning and compound curves. Strength rises a little with the cold work of forming.

5052-H112

As hot-rolled plate, usually thicker gauges. Used for machined parts, base plates and heavier tank ends.

5052-H22 / H24

Strain-hardened and partially annealed. Similar strength to H32 / H34 with slightly better formability; common in some markets.

Tread plate H32 / H114

Diamond or five-bar pattern rolled into the surface for slip resistance on decks and steps.

Quality

Tolerances, surface and inspection

Thickness, width, length, flatness and squareness follow ASTM B209 (ANSI H35.2 tables) or EN 485-4 for cold-rolled sheet and EN 485-3 for hot-rolled plate. For boat building, flatness and surface condition often matter more than thickness tolerance, because distortion and scratches show through paint or bare finishes.

  • Thickness measured across the width; flatness checked on a table for plate
  • Surface inspected for roll marks, scratches, oil stains and corrosion spots
  • PVC film specified where sheet will be handled and formed before finishing
  • Mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 with chemistry and tensile results for each lot
  • Ultrasonic testing of thick plate and third-party inspection on request
Working with 5052

Forming, welding, machining and finishing

Bending

Recommended 90° inside radii for H32 run from almost zero on very thin sheet to about 1 – 2 × thickness at 6 mm. H34 needs larger radii; O can be bent flat on itself. Bend across the rolling direction where possible and allow for springback.

Welding

MIG and TIG weld readily with ER5356 filler, which gives the best strength and colour match. ER4043 can be used on 5052 but gives a weaker, darker weld. 5052 is not heat-treatable, so no post-weld heat treatment is used or needed.

Weld preparation

Remove oil and the oxide film just before welding with solvent and a stainless brush. Most porosity comes from moisture or contamination on the joint or filler.

Machining

Fair. H32 and harder tempers machine better than O. Use sharp tools with high rake, oil-based coolant, and light finishing passes.

Finishing

Paints well after etch priming, and takes a protective anodic coating. For bright decorative anodising, 5005 is a better choice.

Corrosion practice

Avoid copper-based antifouling and contact with bronze or stainless fittings without isolation. Keep bilges dry and drained.

Applications

Marine applications of 5052 plate

  • Small-craft hulls: jon boats, skiffs, dinghies and fishing boats where scantlings allow a moderate-strength alloy
  • Tanks: fuel, fresh-water and holding tanks; 5052 is widely accepted for marine fuel tanks
  • Superstructure and outfitting: consoles, T-tops panels, hatches, lockers and lining panels
  • Decks and walkways: 5052 tread plate for steps, engine-room floors and gangways
  • Pontoons and docks: floats, ramps and cladding for marinas
  • Coastal enclosures: electrical boxes, signage and equipment housings exposed to salt air

FAQ

5052 marine aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQ

Is 5052 aluminium good for boat hulls?

Yes, for small craft built to recreational rules: jon boats, skiffs and many fishing boats use 5052-H32. Larger, faster or class-built hulls use 5083 or 5086, which are stronger after welding and approved by classification societies.

What is the difference between 5052 and 5083 for marine use?

5083 has more magnesium and manganese, giving about a third more yield strength and class approval for hulls. 5052 is easier to bend, cheaper and ideal for tanks, small hulls and outfitting.

Can 5052-H32 be bent without cracking?

Yes. It is chosen for exactly that. Use an inside radius of about 1 – 2 × thickness for plate around 6 mm and less for thin sheet, and bend across the rolling direction.

Which filler wire should be used to weld 5052?

ER5356 is preferred for strength and appearance. ER4043 can be used but produces a weaker, darker weld. No post-weld heat treatment is required.

How much does a 5052 aluminium sheet weigh?

2.68 kg per m² per millimetre of thickness. A 1220 × 2440 × 3 mm sheet weighs about 23.9 kg; a 1500 × 3000 × 5 mm sheet about 60.3 kg.

Is 5052 suitable for marine fuel tanks?

Yes. 5052 is widely used for fuel and water tanks because it resists corrosion, welds well and forms easily. Follow the tank construction rules that apply to your boat.

Do you export 5052 sheet?

Yes. Metline ships 5052 sheet and plate worldwide with export packing and EN 10204 3.1 certificates.
