5A02 Marine Grade Aluminium Hexagonal Bar

5A02 is a non-heat-treatable aluminium-magnesium alloy with about 2.5 % magnesium, specified in the Chinese GB/T 3190 standard and close in character to Russian AMg2 and to 5052 / 5251. As an extruded hexagonal bar in O or H112 temper it gives seawater-resistant, weldable stock for nuts, couplings, spacers and fittings on boats and offshore equipment. Overview

What is 5A02 hexagonal bar and why is it used at sea?

A hexagonal bar is the natural starting stock for any part that will end up with six flats: nuts, union bodies, hose fittings, threaded spacers, bushes and turned connectors. Starting from hex rather than round saves a milling operation and a lot of swarf. When those parts go on a vessel, the alloy has to cope with salt spray, condensation and contact with seawater, and that is where the 5xxx family earns its place.

5A02 is the Chinese national designation for a medium-magnesium aluminium alloy. Its magnesium content of 2.0 – 2.8 % sits between the 5052 and 5154 grades familiar to Western buyers, and a small manganese (or chromium) addition refines the grain and supports corrosion resistance. Like every 5xxx alloy it gains strength from magnesium in solid solution and from cold work, not from heat treatment, so welding does not destroy a heat-treated condition the way it does with 6061 or 6082.

For marine hex bar the practical result is a material that machines into fittings without special tooling, can be welded with ordinary 5xxx filler wire, keeps a stable oxide film in salt air and is unlikely to suffer the intergranular or exfoliation attack that troubles high-magnesium grades kept warm for long periods. It is not a high-strength alloy; if your fitting carries heavy structural load, 6082-T6 or 5083 bar is the better starting point.

Points to settle before ordering 5A02 hex bar

  • Across-flats size and whether corners should be sharp or radiused
  • Temper: O (soft, for forming and heading) or H112 (as-extruded, normal for machining)
  • Which standard governs: GB/T 3191, an EN 755 equivalent grade, or your own drawing
  • Cut length and quantity, and whether ends should be sawn square or chamfered
  • Any class-society or third-party inspection the end customer requires

Because copper is capped at 0.10 % and magnesium stays below 3 %, 5A02 is not susceptible to the sensitisation (grain-boundary precipitation of the Al3Mg2 beta phase) that limits 5083 and 5A06 in service above about 65 °C. That makes it a comfortable choice for parts near engines, exhausts or sun-heated decks.

Working with 5A02

Machining, welding and finishing 5A02 hex bar

Machining

Soft Al-Mg alloys can form long, stringy chips and built-up edge. Use sharp, polished carbide or HSS tools with high positive rake, generous chip clearance and plenty of coolant. H112 machines more cleanly than O.

Threading

Roll threads where possible: the alloy cold-works well and rolled threads resist galling better than cut threads. Use an anti-seize compound on stainless fasteners.

Welding

MIG or TIG with ER5356 or ER5183 filler. The weld zone keeps close to annealed strength, which is already the condition of O-temper bar, so there is little loss to design around.

Forming and heading

O temper takes cold heading, flaring and bending readily, which is why the same grade is also drawn into rivet wire.

Anodising

Takes clear sulphuric anodising for extra salt-spray resistance; colour match is acceptable but slightly duller than 6xxx alloys.

Galvanic contact

Isolate from copper alloys, carbon steel and stainless steel with washers, sleeves or sealant; in submerged service add sacrificial anodes.

Applications

Where 5A02 hex bar is used on vessels and offshore

  • Pipe and hose fittings: unions, adaptors and nipples for fuel, fresh-water and bilge lines on small craft
  • Fasteners and spacers: nuts, stand-offs and threaded bushes where aluminium-to-aluminium contact avoids galvanic cells
  • Deck hardware: stanchion bases, cleat and fairlead bodies, awning and canopy fittings
  • Cable and electrical: gland bodies and earthing studs on aluminium hulls
  • Instrumentation: sensor housings and connector bodies on buoys and survey equipment
  • Repair work: replacement fittings on older vessels built to GB or GOST specifications

For large structural members such as frames, girders or masts, rolled 5083 plate and 6082 or 6005A extrusions are the usual class-approved choices; hex bar is mainly for turned components.

FAQ

5A02 marine aluminium hex bar: frequently asked questions

What is 5A02 aluminium equivalent to?

There is no exact match, but the closest grades are Russian AMg2, EN AW-5251 and 5052 (A5052 in JIS). All are aluminium-magnesium alloys with roughly 2 – 2.8 % magnesium and similar corrosion behaviour.

Is 5A02 suitable for seawater?

Yes. Its magnesium content and low copper give very good resistance to seawater and salt spray, and it does not suffer the sensitisation that affects high-magnesium alloys kept above about 65 °C. Isolate it from stainless steel and copper alloys to avoid galvanic corrosion.

Can 5A02 be heat treated to increase strength?

No. It is a non-heat-treatable alloy; strength comes from magnesium in solid solution and from cold work. Heating only anneals it to the soft O condition.

What is the difference between O and H112 temper hex bar?

O is fully annealed for maximum ductility, suited to heading and forming. H112 is the as-extruded condition with slightly higher strength and is the usual choice for machined fittings.

How much does a 5A02 hex bar weigh?

Multiply 0.866 × across-flats² (mm) × 2.68 and divide by 1,000 for kg per metre. A 30 mm hex weighs about 2.09 kg/m, and a 50 mm hex about 5.80 kg/m.

Which filler wire should I use to weld 5A02?

ER5356 is the common choice; ER5183 gives a little more weld strength. Both match the corrosion behaviour of the parent metal.
