2018 T61 Aluminium Forgings for Aerospace

2018 is an aluminium-copper-nickel-magnesium forging alloy designed to keep its strength at elevated temperature. Forged and heat-treated to T61, it has been used for aircraft piston-engine cylinder heads, pistons and other parts that run warm. Metline supplies 2018 die forgings and forging stock to AMS and ASTM specifications, with full mill certification, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is 2018 aluminium?

2018 belongs to the small family of nickel-bearing 2xxx alloys, alongside 2218 and 2618. About 2 % nickel forms stable aluminium-copper-nickel and aluminium-iron-nickel particles that resist coarsening when the metal is hot, so 2018 holds its strength better than 2014 or 2024 at temperatures around 150 – 250 °C.

The alloy is used almost entirely as forgings. Its classic application is the air-cooled aircraft piston engine, where cylinder heads and pistons must combine strength, thermal conductivity and resistance to softening. ASM describes 2018 as an age-hardenable forging alloy with relatively high strength, fair ductility and good elevated-temperature properties.

For new designs, 2618A is the more widely used nickel-bearing alloy, especially for jet-engine compressor parts and high-performance pistons, and 4032 is common for pistons where low expansion matters. 2018 remains relevant where existing drawings and engine type certificates call it up, and for warm-running fittings in which its properties are already qualified.

Temper

The T61 temper

T61 means the forging is solution heat-treated, quenched (often in boiling or hot water to limit distortion and residual stress in complex shapes) and artificially aged. The hot-water quench is why 2018 and 2618 forgings are listed in T61 rather than T6: strength is slightly lower than a cold-water quench would give, but the parts are more stable dimensionally, which matters for pistons and heads that are machined to fine clearances.

Working with 2018

Machining and finishing

  • Machines well in T61 with carbide tools; finish-machine after heat treatment.
  • Not fusion-welded for structural parts.
  • Protect with anodising or conversion coatings; pistons are often given wear coatings on skirts and ring grooves.
  • Inspect forgings ultrasonically and with penetrant where the drawing requires.

FAQ

2018 aluminium (aluminum) forgings: FAQ

What is 2018 aluminium used for?

Mainly forged cylinder heads and pistons for aircraft piston engines, and other parts that must keep strength at elevated temperature.

What does T61 mean for 2018?

Solution treated, quenched in hot water to limit distortion, then artificially aged. It trades a little strength for dimensional stability.

Is 2018 the same as 2618?

No. Both contain nickel, but 2618 has more magnesium and added iron and is the more common choice for new high-temperature forgings.

Can 2018 forgings be welded?

Fusion welding is not recommended for structural parts; repairs need a qualified procedure and engineering approval.

Is AMS 4140 still current?

SAE lists AMS 4140 as a stabilised document. It is still cited on drawings; confirm the revision your programme requires.
