Aerospace rivet wire

2A10 Aerospace Aluminium Rivet Wire

2A10 is the high-shear rivet alloy of the Chinese GB system, used for aircraft rivets that need more strength than 2A01 (2117-type) rivets but must be driven without the refrigerated storage that 2A12 (2024-type) rivets require. Metline supplies 2A10 wire and wire rod for cold heading in O, H14 and T4, to GB/T 3190 and GB/T 3196, worldwide.

Overview

What 2A10 wire is used for

Solid rivets are still the main fastener in aircraft skins and frames. The rivet alloy has to be soft enough to upset without cracking when the tail is formed, yet strong in shear once installed. 2A10 (old designation LY10) is designed for exactly that balance at the higher end of the strength range.

Its chemistry is an aluminium-copper alloy with about 4.2 % copper and only 0.15 – 0.30 % magnesium. The low magnesium slows natural ageing, so heat-treated 2A10 rivets stay workable long enough to be driven at room temperature. That avoids the "ice-box" handling, where 2024-type rivets must be kept frozen after solution treatment and driven within a short window.

Buyers are rivet and fastener manufacturers who cold-head the wire into rivet blanks, and maintenance organisations supporting aircraft designed to GB and HB standards. 2A10 rivets are generally limited to service temperatures of about 100 °C.

Why fastener makers choose 2A10

  • Higher shear strength than 2A01 / 2117-type rivets
  • Driven after heat treatment without refrigerated storage
  • Good cold-heading behaviour in the annealed condition
  • Matches the rivet alloy called up on GB/HB-standard airframes

Properties

Properties of 2A10 rivet wire

The property that defines rivet wire is shear strength after heat treatment. GB/T 3196 sets minimum shear strength values for heat-treated 2A10 rivet wire by diameter; the figures commonly quoted are about 245 MPa for wire up to 8 mm and about 235 MPa above 8 mm. Tensile strength in the heat-treated condition is typically around 390 MPa with good elongation, which is what allows the rivet tail to upset without cracking.

Tempers

2A10 wire tempers

O (annealed)

Softest condition for cold heading of rivet blanks. Rivets are then solution heat-treated by the fastener maker.

H14 / drawn

Drawn wire with controlled hardness for feeding and heading on high-speed machines, depending on the heading process.

T4

Solution heat-treated and naturally aged. The condition in which test samples demonstrate the specified shear strength.

A T6 condition is sometimes listed for 2A10 wire, but rivet wire is normally specified in O, H14 or T4. Confirm the condition with your heading and heat-treatment route.

FAQ

2A10 aluminium (aluminum) rivet wire: FAQ

What is 2A10 aluminium used for?

Mainly for high-strength aircraft rivets. The wire is cold headed into rivet blanks, heat-treated and driven, giving more shear strength than 2A01 (2117-type) rivets.

Do 2A10 rivets need ice-box storage?

No. The low magnesium content slows natural ageing, so heat-treated 2A10 rivets can be driven at room temperature without the refrigeration needed for 2024-type rivets.

What is the copper content of 2A10?

3.9 – 4.5 % copper, with 0.30 – 0.50 % manganese and 0.15 – 0.30 % magnesium, per GB/T 3190.

Is there a US equivalent to 2A10?

There is no exact Aluminum Association equivalent. It sits between 2117 and 2024 rivet alloys in strength; the Russian V65 alloy is the closest comparison.

What diameters can be supplied?

Round wire from about 1 mm to 10 mm, plus shaped wire to drawing, in coils or spools.
