5086 Aerospace Aluminium Sheet & Plate

5086 is a non-heat-treatable aluminium-magnesium alloy with about 4 % magnesium, valued for weldability, toughness at low temperature and resistance to salt-laden air. In aerospace it is used for welded tanks, ground support equipment, shelters and launch-site structures rather than flight-critical airframe parts. Metline supplies 5086 sheet and plate in O, H111, H112, H32, H34, H116 and H321 tempers, certified to ASTM B209, ASTM B928 or legacy QQ-A-250/7, to buyers worldwide.

Overview

What 5086 does in aerospace programmes

5086 sits between 5052 and 5083 in the 5xxx family. With 3.5 – 4.5 % magnesium it is noticeably stronger than 5052, yet a little more formable and slightly less prone to sensitisation than the higher-magnesium 5083 and 5456.

Airframe skins, spars and frames are made from heat-treatable 2xxx and 7xxx alloys, and 5086 is not a substitute for them. Its value is in structures that must be welded, that live outdoors or near the sea, or that must stay tough at cryogenic temperatures. Typical aerospace uses include welded tanks and pressure vessels on the ground, ground support equipment (GSE) such as stairs, platforms and towable carts, transportable shelters and equipment containers, radar and antenna enclosures, and structures at launch sites and naval air stations where salt spray is constant.

Because strength comes from cold work rather than precipitation hardening, 5086 keeps most of its strength through the heat of welding. A welded 5086-H32 assembly loses some of its strain-hardened strength in the heat-affected zone, but it does not fall to the very low values seen in welded 2024 or 7075. That property, together with good fatigue behaviour and no need for cladding, is the reason the alloy appears on so many support-equipment drawings.

Why engineers specify 5086 plate

  • Strength close to 5083 with slightly better formability
  • Excellent resistance to marine atmospheres and seawater without cladding
  • Welds readily by MIG and TIG with 5356 or 5183 filler
  • Gains strength and keeps toughness at cryogenic temperatures
  • H116 and H321 tempers are tested for resistance to exfoliation and intergranular corrosion

Tempers

Choosing a 5086 temper

5086-O / H111

Annealed. Lowest strength and best formability, used for deep-formed tank heads, spun parts and pieces that will be bent tightly. H111 has a small amount of strain from levelling.

5086-H112

As hot rolled with guaranteed minimum properties. Common for thick plate that will be machined into flanges, bases and fittings.

5086-H32

Strain-hardened and stabilised to quarter-hard. The general-purpose temper for welded structures, panels and GSE frames.

5086-H34

Half-hard and stabilised. Higher strength for panels and covers that are not formed severely; allow larger bend radii than H32.

5086-H116

Special marine temper tested to ASTM G66 and G67 for resistance to exfoliation and intergranular attack. Specify it for equipment exposed to sea spray.

5086-H321

Stabilised temper with controlled cold work, also tested for corrosion resistance under ASTM B928. Often treated as interchangeable with H116 for plate.

Working with 5086

Forming, welding, machining and service limits

Welding

Excellent by MIG and TIG. ER5356 is the usual filler; ER5183 gives slightly higher weld strength in thick sections. Do not use ER4043, which gives brittle, low-ductility welds on high-magnesium alloys.

Forming

O and H111 bend tightly. H32 accepts inside radii of roughly 1 – 2 × thickness in thin sheet; H34 needs more. Bend across the rolling direction where you can.

Machining

Fair. Soft tempers tend to be gummy; H32, H34 and H112 machine more cleanly. Use sharp, polished carbide tools with high positive rake and plenty of coolant.

Temperature limit

Avoid long service above about 65 °C. Prolonged heat can precipitate a magnesium-rich phase along grain boundaries (sensitisation) and make the alloy prone to intergranular and stress corrosion. For warm parts choose 5454.

Cryogenic service

Strength and toughness rise as temperature falls, so 5086 is a good choice for welded tanks and lines handling cryogenic liquids on the ground.

Finishing

Can be anodised, though the film looks slightly greyer than on 6xxx alloys. Painted systems need a suitable pretreatment and primer.

Applications & comparison

Typical uses of 5086 plate, and how it compares

  • Ground support equipment: maintenance stands, passenger and cargo stairs, tow carts, de-icing and refuelling equipment bodies
  • Tanks and vessels: welded fuel, water and hydraulic-fluid storage tanks; cryogenic storage and transfer equipment at test and launch sites
  • Shelters and containers: transportable equipment shelters, air-cargo containers and protective cases for avionics and spares
  • Naval aviation: deck equipment, hangar fittings and structures on carriers and air stations exposed to sea spray
  • Radar and communications: antenna mounts, radomes support frames and outdoor electronic enclosures
  • Test infrastructure: welded frames, platforms and fixtures for test cells and assembly halls

A typical example is a towable aircraft maintenance platform: the deck is 5086-H32 tread plate, the welded side frames are 5086-H112 plate and extrusions, and all joints are welded with ER5356. The whole unit can stand outdoors on a coastal flight line for years without paint.

FAQ

5086 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQs

Is 5086 used for aircraft skins or wing structure?

No. Primary airframe structure uses heat-treatable 2xxx and 7xxx alloys. 5086 is chosen for welded tanks, ground support equipment, shelters, containers and outdoor structures where corrosion resistance and weldability matter more than peak strength.

What is the difference between 5086-H32 and 5086-H116?

Both have similar strength. H116 is a special temper that must pass ASTM G66 exfoliation and G67 intergranular corrosion tests under ASTM B928, so it is the right choice for equipment exposed to seawater or sea spray. H32 has no corrosion test requirement.

Can 5086 be heat treated?

No. It is strengthened only by cold work. Heat can anneal it to the O temper or stabilise a strain-hardened temper, but it cannot be solution treated and aged like 6061.

Which filler wire should be used for welding 5086?

ER5356 is standard and ER5183 is used where higher weld strength is needed in thick plate. Avoid ER4043 on 5086.

Is QQ-A-250/7 still valid?

It was cancelled for new design and replaced by ASTM B209, but many existing drawings still cite it. We can supply material that meets the requirements on your drawing and certify it accordingly.

Why is 5086 not recommended above 65 °C?

With about 4 % magnesium, long exposure to moderate heat can form a continuous magnesium-rich phase on grain boundaries, which makes the alloy susceptible to intergranular and stress corrosion. 5454 is the usual alternative for warm service.
