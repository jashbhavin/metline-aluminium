6082 Aerospace Aluminium Sheet & Plate (T6, T651)

6082 is the strongest of the widely stocked European 6xxx plate alloys and, as BS L113 sheet and BS L115 plate, an established material for aerospace fittings, tooling, ground support equipment and machined parts. T651 plate is stretched to stay flat during heavy machining, and the alloy welds, anodises and resists corrosion well. Metline supplies 6082 sheet and plate in O, T4, T6 and T651, cut to size and certified to aerospace, EN or ASTM standards, worldwide.

Overview

What 6082 aerospace plate is used for

6082 is an aluminium-silicon-magnesium-manganese alloy. Its silicon content is higher than the Mg2Si ratio requires, and the excess raises strength after ageing; manganese controls the grain structure and adds a little strength of its own. With copper held at 0.10 % maximum, corrosion resistance is better than 6061.

The alloy was developed in Europe and replaced the older British grade HE30/H30, which is why it is sometimes still called "H30" in workshops. For aerospace use, the British L-series covers 6082 in all main forms: BS L113 for T6 sheet and strip, BS L115 for T651 plate, L111 for bar and sections, L112 for forgings and L114 for tube. Many European aerospace suppliers and MRO shops use these specifications for parts where 6061-class strength is enough.

Typical aerospace plate applications are machined brackets, mounting plates and fittings in secondary structure, avionics and equipment trays, cargo-system and seat components, assembly jigs and tooling plates, and a very wide range of ground support equipment such as stairs, platforms, towbars and engine stands. Primary structure such as wing skins, spars and fuselage frames uses 2024, 7075, 7050 or aluminium-lithium plates, which offer far higher strength and damage tolerance, so treat claims that 6082 is used for wings or fuselage skins with caution.

In general engineering 6082 is sometimes described as having replaced 6061 because of its higher strength. In reality the two are very close: 6082-T651 plate is slightly stronger in thick sizes and more corrosion resistant, while 6061 is more widely stocked outside Europe and has more US aerospace specifications. The choice usually follows the standards system of the drawing.

Why buyers choose 6082 plate

  • High strength for a weldable 6xxx alloy: about 300 MPa minimum tensile in T6 / T651
  • T651 plate is stress-relieved by stretching, so it stays flat when machined heavily
  • Very good corrosion resistance with low copper, plus good anodising response
  • Recognised in British aerospace (L113, L115), EN 485 and ASTM B209 documents

Tempers

Which 6082 temper to order

6082-T651 (BS L115)

Solution treated, stretched 1.5 – 3 % and aged. The standard for plate above about 6 mm that will be machined: residual stress is low, so parts stay flat.

6082-T6 (BS L113)

Solution treated and aged without stretching. Normal for sheet up to about 6 mm, used flat or lightly formed.

6082-T4

Solution treated and naturally aged. Bend or form, then age to T6 if needed.

6082-O

Annealed for the deepest forming. Strength is restored by full heat treatment after forming.

T5 and T6511

These are extrusion tempers. If a plate enquiry mentions them, it usually means T6 or T651; we confirm before quoting.

Cast tooling plate

If flatness matters more than strength, consider cast and milled tooling plate alongside 6082-T651.

Machining

Machining 6082 plate for aerospace parts

6082-T651 machines well with carbide tooling at high spindle speeds and flood coolant. Because it contains almost no copper, chips are a little longer and stringier than with 6061, so chip-breaker geometries, high-pressure coolant and good evacuation help on deep pockets.

The main risk in machining plate is distortion. Quenching leaves the surface in compression and the core in tension; removing material unbalances those stresses. T651 stretching reduces them to a low level, so specify T651 for any plate that will lose a large share of its weight to machining. For thin-walled parts, rough-machine both sides, let the part relax, then finish-machine.

  • Use sharp, polished carbide tools with high positive rake and large flute volume
  • Machine symmetrically from both faces to keep residual stress balanced
  • Leave about 1 – 2 mm finishing stock after roughing on large or thin parts
  • Avoid clamping distortion: use vacuum or distributed clamping on thin plate
  • Deburr and clean before anodising to avoid marks in the finished coating
Working with 6082

Welding, forming, finishing and corrosion

Welding

Weldable by TIG, MIG and friction stir welding. Use ER4043 for crack resistance or ER5356 / ER5183 for higher strength and better colour match after anodising. The heat-affected zone falls to roughly 50 – 60 % of T6 strength unless re-heat-treated.

Forming

Bend O or T4 sheet tightly; T6 needs inside radii of about 1.5 – 3 × thickness. Bend across the rolling direction where possible.

Anodising

Takes sulphuric, hard and, for aerospace process specifications, chromic or boric-sulphuric anodising. The film is slightly less bright than on 6063.

Corrosion

Very good resistance to atmospheric and marine exposure. Isolate from carbon fibre and stainless or titanium fasteners with sealant and primer.

Temperature

Keeps useful strength up to about 100 – 150 °C; prolonged exposure above that over-ages the alloy.

Cryogenic service

Like other 6xxx alloys, strength and toughness hold up well at low temperature.

Applications

Typical aerospace uses of 6082 plate

  • Machined fittings and brackets in secondary structure and systems installations
  • Avionics trays, equipment shelves and mounting plates
  • Cargo and seat systems: base plates, rails and machined components
  • Assembly tooling: jigs, fixtures, drilling templates and base plates for aircraft assembly
  • Ground support equipment: maintenance platforms, stairs, towbars, engine stands and trolleys
  • Space and UAV: structural panels, brackets and mounting plates in small satellites and drones
  • Test rigs: frames and fixtures for structural and systems testing

A typical example is an assembly fixture for a fuselage panel: the base and uprights are cut from 6082-T651 plate, machined flat and anodised, giving a stiff, light fixture that resists corrosion in a humid hangar.

FAQ

6082 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQs

What are BS L113 and BS L115?

They are British aerospace specifications for 6082: L113 covers T6 sheet and strip and L115 covers T651 plate. Both use the standard 6082 chemistry with minimum properties set by thickness.

Is 6082 plate stronger than 6061?

Slightly, in most thicknesses: EN 485-2 gives 6082-T651 a minimum of 295 – 300 MPa tensile up to 60 mm, against about 290 MPa for 6061-T651 under ASTM B209. 6082 also resists corrosion better because of its low copper.

Should I order T6 or T651?

Order T651 for plate over about 6 mm that will be machined, because stretching relieves quench stresses and prevents warping. T6 is normal for thinner sheet.

Is 6082 used for aircraft wing skins or fuselage?

No. Primary structure uses 2024, 7075, 7050 or aluminium-lithium alloys. 6082 plate is used for fittings, equipment mounts, tooling and ground support equipment.

Can 6082 plate be welded?

Yes, by TIG, MIG or friction stir welding with ER4043, ER5356 or ER5183 filler. The weld zone loses part of its T6 strength, so welded designs use reduced allowables or post-weld heat treatment.

How much does 6082 plate weigh?

It weighs 2.70 kg per m² per millimetre of thickness. A 1250 × 2500 × 10 mm plate weighs about 84.4 kg.

Do you cut 6082 plate to size and ship internationally?

Yes. Metline cuts 6082 plate to size by saw or waterjet, protects it with film on request and ships worldwide with an EN 10204 3.1 certificate.
