4032 Aerospace Aluminium Forgings & Forged Pistons

4032-T6 forgings combine the low thermal expansion and wear resistance of a 12 % silicon alloy with the soundness and grain flow of a forging. They are the standard material for forged pistons in aircraft piston engines, compressors and hydraulic pumps. Metline supplies 4032 forgings and forged piston blanks to ASTM B247, legacy AMS 4145 or your drawing, worldwide.

Overview

Why pistons are forged from 4032

A piston sees combustion pressure, inertia loads and temperatures well above 200 °C at the crown, while sliding in a steel or cast-iron bore. It has to be light, strong when hot, resistant to wear and dimensionally steady as it heats up.

Cast pistons in Al-Si alloys handle most of those demands, but a forging adds strength, ductility and fatigue resistance because closed-die forging closes porosity and aligns grain flow with the piston's shape. 4032 brings the alloy advantages to the forging: about 12 % silicon for low expansion and wear resistance, nickel and copper for strength at temperature, and magnesium for a strong T6 temper.

The result is a piston that can run with tight skirt clearance, stays quiet when cold, seals well and resists scuffing. Aircraft piston engines, high-performance automotive engines, compressors and hydraulic pumps all use forged 4032 pistons for these reasons. Outside pistons, 4032 forgings are used for wear-resistant bushings, pump and valve parts and swashplates.

What a 4032 forging gives you

  • Lower thermal expansion than 2618 or 2xxx forgings: tighter clearances
  • Wear resistance from hard silicon particles in the microstructure
  • Forged soundness: no casting porosity, controlled grain flow
  • T6 strength of about 380 MPa tensile, retaining useful strength when hot

AMS 4145 describes the nominal composition as 12.2Si-1.1Mg-0.9Cu-0.9Ni. Some older listings give silicon as 6 – 12 % and omit nickel; those figures are wrong for 4032 and should not be accepted on a certificate.

Properties

Mechanical and physical properties of 4032-T6 forgings

At piston operating temperatures, 4032 keeps a useful fraction of its room-temperature strength, more than 6xxx alloys but less than 2618, which was developed specifically for long exposure at high temperature. That trade-off drives the choice between the two forging alloys.

Heat treatment

Heat treatment and tempers

Forgings are solution heat-treated, quenched and artificially aged to T6. A typical cycle, consistent with AMS 2770 practice for wrought aluminium, is solution treatment at about 505 – 520 °C, water quench, then ageing at about 170 °C for around 10 hours. The exact cycle is fixed by the specification and qualified by the forge.

T6

The standard and almost universal temper for 4032 forgings and pistons.

O / F

Forging stock may be supplied annealed (O) or as fabricated (F) for forging and later heat treatment.

T651 and T73

Listed on some older pages for 4032 forgings, but not standard 4032 forging tempers. T73 is a 7xxx-alloy over-ageing temper. If your drawing shows them, ask the design authority to confirm.

Some piston makers apply a stabilising heat treatment so pistons do not grow when they first see engine temperature. Where this matters, state it on the order.

Inspection

Inspection and documentation

  • Chemistry and tensile tests: per heat-treat lot, reported on an EN 10204 3.1 certificate
  • Hardness: Brinell checks on each forging or by sampling plan
  • Macro-etch and grain flow: sectioned sample forgings to confirm flow lines follow the piston contour
  • Ultrasonic inspection: to AMS-STD-2154 or the customer specification for critical parts
  • Dimensional check: against the forging drawing, including machining allowance
  • Traceability: heat and lot number marked on each forging or package

Applications

Applications

  • Aircraft piston engines: forged pistons for general-aviation engines
  • Compressors: pistons and crossheads for air and gas compressors
  • Hydraulics: pump pistons, swashplates and valve parts
  • Wear parts: bushes, sleeves and bearing cages running against steel
  • Automotive and motorsport: forged pistons where low noise and tight clearance matter

4032 forgings are not used for airframe structure, fuel pipes, landing-gear hubs or similar parts that older listings mention; those use 2xxx, 6xxx or 7xxx forgings.

FAQ

4032 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) forgings: questions

Why use 4032 for forged pistons?

Its 12 % silicon gives low thermal expansion and good wear resistance, so pistons run with tight clearances, quietly and with little scuffing. Forging adds strength and fatigue resistance over casting.

Is AMS 4145 still a valid specification?

SAE lists AMS 4145 as cancelled (1996), although it still appears on legacy drawings. ASTM B247 covers 4032-T6 forgings and is the usual current reference; agree the replacement with your customer.

Are 4032 forgings available in T651 or T73?

T6 is the standard temper for 4032 forgings. T73 is a 7xxx over-ageing temper and does not apply; T651 is a stretched temper for plate and bar. Confirm any such call-out with the design authority.

4032 or 2618 for a high-output engine?

2618 is more ductile and better for very high cylinder pressures and detonation. 4032 suits engines where low noise, tight clearance and wear resistance matter more.

What inspection comes with 4032 forgings?

Chemistry, tensile and hardness results on a 3.1 certificate, with grain-flow macro-etch and ultrasonic inspection available on request.

Do you ship 4032 forgings internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies 4032 forgings and piston blanks worldwide from Mumbai, with export packing and certificates.
