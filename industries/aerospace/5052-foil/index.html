5052 Aerospace Aluminium Foil

5052 foil is the standard material for aluminium honeycomb core in aircraft floors, flaps, control surfaces and interior panels, and it is also used for insulation facings and laminated foil systems. It is stronger and more corrosion-resistant than 1xxx and 3003 foil, while still rolling to gauges below 0.02 mm. Metline supplies 5052 foil from about 0.018 mm to 0.20 mm in hard and soft tempers, slit to width with mill certificates, worldwide.

Overview

What makes 5052 foil the honeycomb alloy

Honeycomb core is made by printing adhesive lines on thin foil, stacking and curing the sheets into a block, and then pulling the block open so the cells form. The foil must be strong enough to give the core its shear and crush strength, ductile enough to expand without tearing, and durable enough to survive decades inside a sandwich panel.

5052 meets all three needs. Its 2.5 % magnesium gives roughly twice the strength of 3003 at the same gauge, so core can be lighter for the same panel stiffness, and its low copper content gives very good resistance to humidity, de-icing fluids and salt air. For these reasons, 5052 is the alloy behind most aerospace aluminium honeycomb, with 5056 used where higher strength is needed and 3003 reserved for commercial and architectural core.

Outside honeycomb, 5052 foil is chosen when a thin metallic layer must resist corrosion and mechanical damage better than pure aluminium foil: facings for thermal and acoustic insulation blankets, reflective heat-shield layers, laminated foil and film composites, foil tape backings and some fin and shutter applications. Hard tempers are used where stiffness matters; the annealed O temper where the foil is wrapped, embossed or deep-formed.

Why buyers specify 5052 foil

  • Higher strength than 1xxx and 3003 foil, allowing lighter honeycomb core
  • Good corrosion resistance for long service in humid and marine environments
  • Clean, uniform gauge suitable for adhesive printing and expansion
  • Same alloy family as 5052 sheet and tube used in aircraft structures

Honeycomb

5052 foil in aerospace honeycomb core

Core properties are set by three choices: foil gauge, cell size and whether the foil is perforated or corrosion-treated. Thicker foil and smaller cells give higher density and strength. Core is described by alloy, cell size, foil gauge and density, for example "5052, 1/8 in cell, 0.0007 in foil, 3.1 pcf".

SAE AMS 4348 covers corrosion-inhibited 5052 honeycomb core for sandwich construction with 350 °F (177 °C) adhesive systems, and it references a 5052 foil specification (AMS 4004) for the foil itself. Legacy drawings may cite MIL-C-7438. Aerospace core is then bonded between aluminium or composite skins to make floor panels, flaps, spoilers, trailing edges, engine nacelle components, bulkheads and galley and lavatory panels.

Compared with 3003 foil, 5052 gives clearly higher core strength and better corrosion resistance; compared with 5056, it is slightly weaker but more widely available and lower in cost. See our 5052 honeycomb core guide and the 3003 vs 5052 comparison for core selection in detail.

Thermal systems

5052 foil for insulation, heat shields and laminates

In aircraft, foil facings protect insulation blankets around ducts, engines and auxiliary power units, and reflective foil layers reduce radiant heat transfer to nearby structure. 5052 is chosen over pure aluminium foil where the facing must resist tearing, abrasion and corrosion from moisture and fluids during long service. It is often combined with glass-fibre cloth, polymer films or adhesives in laminated constructions, and joints are sealed with aluminium foil tape.

In industrial use the same properties suit HVAC ducting facings, pipe-insulation jackets and composite panels, where 5052 foil helps a laminate keep its integrity under vibration and temperature cycling.

  • Reflective layers: bright-rolled 5052 foil reflects most radiant heat; keep the surface clean to maintain reflectivity.
  • Laminates: specify the surface condition (degreased, primed or coated) your adhesive system needs.
  • Tapes: soft O temper conforms to joints; harder tempers resist puncture.
Durability

Corrosion behaviour of 5052 foil

The magnesium in 5052 forms a stable oxide film, and with copper held at 0.10 % maximum the alloy resists pitting in humid and salt-laden air far better than 2xxx or copper-bearing foils. That matters in honeycomb, where foil is only a few hundredths of a millimetre thick and even slight corrosion can weaken the core. For aerospace core, the foil is usually given a corrosion-inhibiting conversion treatment before adhesive is applied, which further improves durability and bond strength.

Typical failure modes to design against are moisture ingress into sandwich panels through damaged edges or fastener holes, and galvanic contact between aluminium core and carbon-fibre skins. Edge sealing, potting around inserts and an isolating layer (often a glass-fibre ply) between carbon skins and aluminium core address both.

FAQ

5052 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) foil: FAQs

How do I choose the right temper for 5052 foil?

Use hard tempers (H18 or H19) for honeycomb core, fin stock and other parts where stiffness and strength matter. Use O when the foil is wrapped, embossed or laminated and must conform without cracking; H32 or H34 sit between the two.

Why is 5052 used for honeycomb instead of 3003?

5052 foil is roughly twice as strong as 3003 and more corrosion-resistant, so the core is stronger for the same density, or lighter for the same strength. 3003 core is mainly used in commercial and architectural panels.

What thickness range is 5052 foil available in?

Typically from about 0.018 mm to 0.20 mm. Honeycomb core commonly uses foil of about 18 to 100 µm (0.0007 to 0.004 in).

Is 5052 foil suitable for insulation blankets?

Yes. It is used as a protective and reflective facing on thermal and acoustic insulation, where its tear and corrosion resistance outperforms pure aluminium foil.

What is the difference between 5052 foil and pure aluminium foil?

Pure aluminium foil (1145, 1235, 8011) is softer, more conductive and cheaper; 5052 is much stronger and more resistant to corrosion, which suits structural and long-life uses such as honeycomb.

Can 5052 foil be supplied with a corrosion-inhibiting treatment?

Yes. Conversion-coated or primed foil can be supplied on request to suit honeycomb and laminate manufacturers; specify the treatment and any qualification requirements.
