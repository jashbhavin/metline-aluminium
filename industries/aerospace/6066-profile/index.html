6066 Aerospace Aluminium Profiles (T4, T6)

6066 extrusions give the highest strength available from a standard 6xxx alloy: at least 345 MPa tensile and 310 MPa yield in T6. They are used for machined aircraft fittings, structural angles and channels in ground equipment and defence hardware, where 6061 is not strong enough and 2xxx or 7xxx alloys are more than the job needs. Metline supplies 6066 profiles in T4, T6 and stretched tempers to ASTM B221, worldwide.

Overview

What 6066 profiles are used for

6066 was developed as a high-strength extrusion alloy for American aircraft and military hardware. Its high silicon, copper and manganese give T6 strength well above 6061 while keeping the simpler heat treatment and lower cost of the 6xxx family.

The same chemistry makes the alloy stiffer to push through a die, so 6066 profiles tend to be solid, fairly chunky shapes rather than thin-walled hollows: angles, tees, channels, Z-sections, I-beams, rectangular bars and machining blanks shaped close to the final part. Machining these near-net extrusions saves metal and cutting time compared with milling fittings from plate.

Because 6066 does not weld reliably and has only fair corrosion resistance, profiles are designed into bolted or riveted assemblies and given a protective finish. Used that way, they deliver a useful step up in strength for brackets, frames and fittings in secondary aircraft structure, ground equipment and defence systems.

Typical reasons to choose 6066 extrusions

  • About 30 % higher minimum yield strength than 6061-T6 extrusions (310 vs 240 MPa)
  • Near-net shapes for machined fittings reduce waste compared with plate
  • Good machinability and forgeability in the heat-treated condition
  • Simpler, cheaper heat treatment than 2xxx and 7xxx alternatives

Tempers

6066 extrusion tempers

T4

Solution heat-treated and naturally aged. Minimum 276 MPa tensile with 14 % elongation; used where the profile is bent or stretch-formed and then aged.

T4510 / T4511

T4 profiles stretched for stress relief (T4511 with minor straightening allowed).

T6

Fully heat-treated for peak strength, the normal temper for machined fittings.

T6510 / T6511

T6 profiles stretched 1 – 3 % so that they stay straight and flat when machined.

O

Annealed stock for severe forming or forging, followed by full heat treatment.

T42 / T62

User heat-treated tempers with slightly lower minimum yield.

Working with 6066

Machining, joining and protecting 6066 profiles

Machining

Very good in T6 and T6511, with short chips. Use stretched tempers for long parts to limit distortion.

Joining

Use bolts, rivets or adhesive bonding. Fusion welding risks hot cracking and severe strength loss, so it is not used for structural joints.

Corrosion protection

Anodise, apply chromate or chromate-free conversion coating and prime; seal faying surfaces in assemblies.

Forming

Form in O or T4, then age. T6 sections tolerate only large radii.

Galvanic contact

Isolate from carbon fibre composites and stainless or titanium fasteners with sealant and wet-installed fasteners.

Inspection

Hardness and conductivity checks confirm heat treatment; ultrasonic testing can be added for critical fittings.

Applications

Typical aerospace and defence uses of 6066 profiles

  • Fittings machined from near-net shapes: brackets, clips, lugs and support angles in secondary structure
  • Ground support equipment: load-bearing frames, jacks, stands and towing hardware
  • Defence systems: launcher rails, mounts and vehicle-mounted equipment frames
  • Test and tooling: heavy rigs and fixtures that need stiffness and strength
  • Forging stock: extruded bar and shapes used as pre-forms for closed-die forgings

FAQ

6066 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) profiles: FAQs

Is 6066 stronger than 6061?

Yes. In T6 extrusions ASTM B221 requires 345 MPa tensile and 310 MPa yield for 6066, against 260 MPa and 240 MPa for 6061.

Can 6066 profiles be welded?

Not for load-bearing joints. 6066 is crack-sensitive and loses much strength near welds. Design for bolted or riveted assembly.

Does 6066 need corrosion protection?

Usually, yes. Its copper content lowers corrosion resistance, so anodising or conversion coating plus primer is normal for aerospace parts.

Which specification replaced QQ-A-200/10?

ASTM B221 is the usual replacement for 6066 extrusions on new orders; check whether your customer requires any additional aerospace inspection.

Can 6066 profiles be supplied stress relieved?

Yes, as T6510 (stretched) or T6511 (stretched with minor straightening). These keep long parts straight during machining.
