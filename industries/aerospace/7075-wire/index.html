Overview

What 7075 wire is and why aircraft use it

7075 is the classic high-strength aerospace alloy. In wire form it gives a tensile strength above 500 MPa in the T6 temper at roughly one third of the density of steel, which is why it is drawn into wire for rivets, pins, screws, cold-headed blanks and spring clips in airframes, missiles and satellites.

Wire is produced by extruding or rolling 7075 rod, then cold drawing it through successive dies with intermediate anneals. Because 7075 work-hardens quickly and is less ductile than 2xxx or 6xxx alloys, it is usually drawn in the annealed condition and heat-treated to T6 or T73 after the final draw or after the part has been cold headed. The result is a fine-grained, accurately sized wire with a clean surface suitable for heading, thread rolling and anodising.

The key design decision is temper. T6 gives the highest strength but is susceptible to stress-corrosion cracking (SCC) when loaded in the short-transverse direction or in sustained tension. The over-aged T73 family gives up about 10 – 15 % of strength in exchange for very high SCC resistance, so it is the usual choice for fasteners and parts that stay loaded for decades. For rivets, many designs have moved to 7050-T73 because it can be driven without the rivet cracking, while 7075 remains common for pins, screws and machined wire parts.

Where 7075 wire fits

  • Highest strength of the widely available aerospace wire alloys (2017, 2024, 2117, 6061, 7050, 7075)
  • Can be supplied soft (O) for heading and then aged, or ready-aged in T6 or T73
  • Certified to AMS and ASTM, so one material serves military, commercial and space programmes
  • Not weldable by fusion processes; joined mechanically or by adhesive bonding

Tempers

Which 7075 wire temper should you order?

7075-O

Fully annealed. The softest condition, used for cold heading, coiling and forming. The finished part is then solution treated and aged to T6 or T73 under AMS 2770 or the customer process.

7075-F

As fabricated, with no guaranteed properties. Bought as stock for further drawing or forging. AMS 4186 covers F-temper bar, rod and wire, with O available on request.

7075-T6

Solution heat-treated and artificially aged. Maximum strength for pins, shafts and machined wire parts. Avoid it for parts under sustained tensile load in humid or salt environments.

7075-T73

Solution treated and over-aged in two steps. Strength drops to about 470 MPa minimum, but stress-corrosion resistance is excellent. Standard for fasteners and long-life loaded parts.

7075-T7351

T73 with a controlled stretch to relieve quench stresses; applies mainly to larger rod and bar. AMS 4124 covers rolled or cold-finished T7351 bar, rod and wire up to 6 in diameter.

Electrical conductivity check

Over-aged tempers are verified by eddy-current conductivity as well as hardness, because conductivity confirms that the over-ageing step was done correctly.

Tolerances & quality

Tolerances, surface and inspection

Diameter tolerances follow the tables in ASTM B211 and ANSI H35.2 unless the order states tighter limits. Rivet and cold-heading wire to ASTM B316 has closer diameter tolerances and stricter surface requirements, because seams, laps or die scratches open up into cracks when the head is upset.

Dimensions
Diameter and ovality checked along the coil or on every bundle
Surface
Visual inspection for seams, scratches and pick-up; eddy-current testing on request
Mechanical
Tensile test per lot; shear and upset (heading) tests for rivet wire
Over-aged tempers
Electrical conductivity and hardness to confirm T73 condition
Certification
EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate with chemistry, properties and lot number
Working with 7075 wire

Cold heading, machining and finishing

Cold heading

Head in the O or freshly quenched (W) condition, then age. Heading T6 wire risks cracking. Freshly quenched wire must be headed within a short window or held refrigerated to delay natural ageing.

Machining

In T6 and T73 tempers 7075 machines very well: short chips, good finish and accurate threads. Use sharp carbide or HSS tools and plenty of coolant.

Thread rolling

Rolled threads add fatigue life. They are normally rolled after heat treatment for best fatigue performance.

Welding

Not suitable for arc welding. Fusion welds crack and lose strength. Join by fastening, swaging or adhesive bonding.

Anodising

Takes sulphuric and chromic acid anodising; aerospace fasteners are often anodised and primed. Hard anodising adds wear resistance on pins.

Corrosion protection

Always protect 7075 parts in service: anodise, apply chemical conversion coating and primer, and wet-install fasteners with sealant to avoid galvanic attack.

Applications

Where 7075 aerospace wire is used

  • Fasteners: screws, pins, lockbolt collars and special rivets made from cold-headed wire
  • Pins and shafts: hinge pins, clevis pins and small shafts in control systems
  • Spacecraft and satellites: lightweight standoffs, inserts and machined fittings
  • Missiles and UAVs: small structural pins, fin hinge pins and fasteners
  • Interiors and equipment: seat and galley hardware where strength per gram matters
  • Sporting and precision goods: spokes, archery parts and high-strength clips

A typical example is a hinge pin for an access panel: drawn 7075 wire is cut, machined and heat-treated to T73, anodised and greased. It carries the hinge load for the life of the aircraft without the stress-corrosion risk that a T6 pin would bring.

FAQ

7075 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) wire: FAQ

What is the difference between 7075-T6 and 7075-T73 wire?

Both are solution heat-treated. T6 is aged for peak strength (about 572 MPa typical tensile). T73 is over-aged in two steps, which lowers tensile strength to about 505 MPa but gives much better resistance to stress-corrosion cracking. Choose T73 for parts under sustained load.

Can 7075 wire be cold headed?

Yes, but heading is done in the annealed (O) or freshly quenched condition, and the parts are aged afterwards. Heading wire that is already in T6 usually cracks the head.

Which specification covers 7075-T6 wire?

SAE AMS 4122 covers 7075-T6 and T651 bars, rods and wire. ASTM B211 is the general commercial standard, and ASTM B316 covers rivet and cold-heading wire.

Is 7075 wire weldable?

Not by arc welding. 7075 is prone to hot cracking, and the weld zone loses most of its strength. Use mechanical fastening or adhesive bonding.

Why are 7075 rivets less common than 7050 rivets?

7075 rivets are hard to drive without cracking in the aged condition. 7050-T73 rivets can be driven as received and still offer high shear strength, so they replaced 7075 in many airframe uses.

Do you supply 7075 wire outside India?

Yes. Metline supplies 7075 wire worldwide from Mumbai, with EN 10204 3.1 certificates, export packing and sea or air shipment.
