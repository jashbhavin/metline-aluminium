5251 Aerospace Aluminium Sheet & Plate

5251 is a medium-strength aluminium alloy with about 2 % magnesium, long used in the UK and Europe for formed and welded aircraft sheet-metal parts. It is the alloy behind the British aerospace sheet specifications L80 (annealed) and L81 (hard tempers). Metline supplies 5251 sheet and plate in O, H22, H24, H26 and related tempers to BS L80, L81 and EN 485, cut to size and certified, to customers worldwide.

Overview

Where 5251 sheet fits in aircraft work

5251 (EN AW-5251, AlMg2) was one of the most common non-heat-treatable sheet alloys in British aircraft and vehicle work, and it is still specified on many UK and European drawings. It is stronger than 3003 and pure aluminium, easier to form than 5052 or 5754, and welds without loss of corrosion resistance.

In aircraft and helicopters, 5251 sheet goes into secondary parts that are folded, beaten, spun or welded: cowling liners, ducting, fairings and access panels in non-structural areas, fuel and oil tank shells, brackets, cable-trough covers, cabin trim panels and equipment boxes. It also appears in ground support equipment and in the restoration of older British aircraft, where the original drawings call L80 or L81.

Because 5251 work-hardens quickly, the annealed L80 condition is used for parts that must be formed heavily, while L81 in H16 or H26 gives the stiffer sheet wanted for flat panels and covers. Neither can be strengthened by heat treatment, so the temper must be chosen before forming.

Why engineers specify 5251 sheet

  • Excellent formability in the O and H22 tempers, including spinning and panel beating
  • Good resistance to marine atmospheres without cladding or painting
  • Readily welded by TIG, MIG and resistance methods
  • Matches existing British drawings that call L80 or L81

Tempers

Choosing a 5251 temper

O (L80)

Fully annealed. Best for deep drawing, spinning, panel beating and tight bends. Parts gain some strength from the forming itself.

H22

Strain-hardened then partly annealed to quarter-hard. Keeps more ductility than H12 at the same strength, so it folds well with modest bend radii.

H24

Half-hard, partly annealed. A good compromise for brackets and covers that need some stiffness but still have bends.

H26 (L81)

Three-quarter-hard, partly annealed. Stiff, flat sheet for panels and covers with gentle bends only.

H14 / H16 (L81)

Strain-hardened only, without partial annealing. Slightly higher proof stress than H24 and H26 but less elongation.

H32 / H34

Strain-hardened and stabilised. Similar strength to H22 and H24; often supplied when the order is written to EN or ASTM practice.

Working with 5251

Forming, welding and finishing 5251

Forming

Work-hardens quickly. For deep or repeated forming, start in O and anneal between stages (about 340 – 360 °C). H22 bends readily with radii around 1 × thickness in thin sheet.

Welding

TIG and MIG weld well with ER5356 or ER5183 filler. Resistance spot and seam welding are also common. The heat-affected zone softens to near O values in hard tempers.

Finishing

Takes sulphuric and chromic anodising and paint systems well. Chromate-free conversion coatings are used where aerospace programmes require them.

Corrosion

Very good in marine and industrial air. With magnesium below 3 %, 5251 is not prone to sensitisation, so warm service is acceptable.

FAQ

5251 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) sheet: FAQs

What is the difference between L80 and L81?

Both are 5251 sheet. L80 is the annealed O condition for heavy forming; L81 covers the harder H16 and H26 tempers for flatter, stiffer parts.

Is 5251 the same as 5052?

No. They are similar Al-Mg alloys, but 5251 has less magnesium (1.7 – 2.4 % against 2.2 – 2.8 %) and uses manganese rather than chromium. 5052 is a little stronger; 5251 is slightly more formable.

Can 5251 be heat treated to increase strength?

No. Strength comes from cold work. Heat treatment can only anneal it or partly anneal it to H2x tempers.

What is the American equivalent of 5251?

There is no exact ASTM-standard equivalent in common stock. 5052 is the closest widely available alloy in the US, though its properties and composition differ and a substitution needs design approval.

Does 5251 suffer from sensitisation like 5083?

No. With magnesium below 3 % it does not form continuous grain-boundary precipitates in normal service, so it can be used at moderately elevated temperatures.
