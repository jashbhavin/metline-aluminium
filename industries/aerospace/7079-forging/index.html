7079 Aerospace Aluminium Forgings

7079 is a high-strength Al-Zn-Mg-Cu forging alloy that was used for heavy aircraft forgings because it hardens evenly through thick sections. It is now a legacy alloy: its poor resistance to stress-corrosion cracking led designers to replace it with 7075-T73, 7175-T74 and 7050-T74. Metline supplies 7079 forgings where a legacy drawing still requires them, and helps buyers document an approved substitute where it does not.

Overview

What 7079 is, and why it is now a legacy alloy

7079 contains less zinc and more magnesium than 7075, plus a manganese addition. That balance made it much less sensitive to quench rate, so thick forgings reached nearly full strength right through the section. For large landing-gear parts, wing fittings and bulkheads made before modern alloys existed, that was a real advantage.

The weakness showed up in service. In the peak-aged T6 temper, 7079 is highly susceptible to stress-corrosion cracking (SCC), especially when residual or assembly stresses act in the short-transverse direction of a forging. Cracking in fittings and gear components led airframe makers and military agencies to stop specifying the alloy. Research in the 1970s showed that newer alloys such as 7050 combined strength, toughness and SCC resistance far better than 7075-T6 or 7079-T6, and the SAE forging specification for 7079-T6, AMS 4138, is now listed as noncurrent.

Today 7079 is ordered mainly for maintenance, repair and spares for older aircraft and equipment whose type design still names it, or for reverse-engineering programmes that need the original material to match a test or qualification. For any new part, a designer should choose a modern over-aged 7xxx forging instead.

Before you order 7079

  • Check whether the drawing or repair manual allows a substitute such as 7075-T73 or 7175-T74
  • If 7079 is mandatory, confirm the governing specification and revision and any customer test requirements
  • Plan surface protection and shot peening; 7079 parts rely on them to resist SCC
  • Expect limited availability, because few mills still cast the alloy

Properties

Mechanical and physical properties

7079-T6 reaches strength close to 7075-T6. Its real advantage was that thick sections kept that strength, while 7075 forgings lost more in the centre. Properties in forgings depend on grain direction: longitudinal (L) is strongest, short transverse (ST) is weakest and is the direction where SCC starts.

Tempers & stress corrosion

7079 tempers and the stress-corrosion problem

7079-T6

Solution heat-treated, quenched and artificially aged to peak strength. The temper named in AMS 4138. High strength, but the short-transverse SCC threshold is low.

7079-T652

Solution treated, stress-relieved by a controlled cold compression (typically 1 – 5 % permanent set) and aged. Compression reduces residual quench stresses, which lowers the SCC risk in machined parts but does not remove it.

7079-T6511 (extrusions)

A stretched temper for extruded bar and shapes, not forgings. Sometimes seen on legacy material lists alongside forgings.

Why over-ageing matters

The T73 and T74 tempers developed for 7075, 7175 and 7050 give up a little strength to raise the SCC threshold sharply. No widely used over-aged temper was established for 7079, which is one reason the alloy was dropped.

SAE ARP 823 sets out practices that reduce SCC in heat-treatable forgings: keep machining from cutting across the grain flow in highly stressed areas, avoid interference fits and clamp-up stress in the short-transverse direction, shot peen critical surfaces, and protect the part with anodising, primer and sealant. These measures are essential on any 7079 part kept in service.

7050 is the closest modern counterpart: like 7079 it is designed for low quench sensitivity, so thick forgings keep their strength, but it also offers good fracture toughness and SCC resistance in the T74 condition. Any change of material on an aircraft part needs approval from the type-certificate holder or the responsible engineering authority.

Applications

Where 7079 forgings were used

  • Landing gear: cylinders, trunnions and links on older aircraft
  • Wing and fuselage fittings: spar fittings, attach lugs and heavy bulkhead sections
  • Flight controls: bellcranks, actuator brackets and control arms
  • Helicopter and defence equipment: fittings and housings on legacy platforms

A typical current order is a short batch of closed-die fittings to replace parts on an older aircraft, delivered with full test results and heat-treat records so the operator can show the replacement matches the original material.

FAQ

7079 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) forgings: FAQ

Is 7079 aluminium still produced?

Only in small quantities for legacy requirements. Its forging specification, AMS 4138, is noncurrent, and most mills no longer cast the alloy routinely. Availability should be confirmed per enquiry.

Why was 7079 replaced?

In the T6 temper it is very susceptible to stress-corrosion cracking in the short-transverse direction. Over-aged 7075-T73, 7175-T74 and 7050-T74 forgings offer similar or slightly lower strength with far better SCC resistance.

What is the best substitute for a 7079-T6 forging?

For thick forgings, 7050-T74 or T7452 is usually the closest match because it also has low quench sensitivity. 7175-T74 and 7075-T73 suit thinner parts. The substitution must be approved by the responsible design authority.

What does T652 mean on a 7079 forging?

Solution heat-treated, stress-relieved by controlled compression and then artificially aged. The compression lowers residual quench stresses, which reduces distortion when machining and lowers SCC risk.

Is 7079 weldable?

No. Like other high-strength 7xxx alloys, it cracks and loses strength when fusion welded. Parts are joined mechanically.

Can Metline ship 7079 forgings internationally?

Yes. We supply aerospace forgings worldwide from Mumbai with EN 10204 3.1 certificates, test reports and export packing.

Replacing a 7079 forging?

Send the drawing and specification. We will confirm whether 7079 can be supplied and suggest a documented modern alternative.
