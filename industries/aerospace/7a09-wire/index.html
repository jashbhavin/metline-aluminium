7A09 Aerospace Aluminium Wire

7A09 is the Chinese-standard designation for a high-strength Al-Zn-Mg-Cu alloy whose chemistry sits almost on top of 7075. Drawn into wire and small rod, it is used for fastener blanks, pins, shafts and cold-headed parts where strength per kilogram matters more than weldability. Overview

What is 7A09 aluminium wire?

7A09 belongs to the 7xxx series, where zinc and magnesium form fine MgZn2 precipitates during ageing. That precipitation is what gives 7xxx alloys the highest strength of any common wrought aluminium, roughly twice that of 6061 in the peak-aged condition.

The grade is registered in the Chinese wrought-alloy standard GB/T 3190 and was formerly known as LC9. Its zinc, magnesium, copper and chromium ranges overlap those of 7075 so closely that engineers usually treat the two as interchangeable for design purposes, although the impurity limits are not identical and the certifying standard must still match the drawing.

In wire and small-diameter drawn rod, 7A09 is chosen for parts that are machined, headed or rolled from bar stock and then aged: shear pins, hinge pins, solid rivet blanks for heavy joints, threaded studs, small shafts and spring-loaded clips. It is not a welding wire and should never be used as filler metal; 7xxx alloys of this type crack when fusion welded.

Key points for buyers

  • Strength close to 7075-T6 in the peak-aged condition, with a T73 option for stress-corrosion resistance
  • Supplied straight-length or coiled, drawn to tight diameter tolerance for headers and automatic lathes
  • Certification can follow GB/T 3190 chemistry or, where a Western specification is required, 7075 to ASTM B211 or AMS 4122
  • Not weldable by fusion processes; join by riveting, bolting or adhesive bonding

Tempers

7A09 tempers and what each is for

O (annealed)

Softest condition, used for drawing to finer sizes and for cold heading complex shapes. Parts are then solution treated and aged to reach strength.

W / "T4" (freshly quenched)

7xxx alloys keep hardening at room temperature for months after quenching, so a true stable T4 does not exist. Wire described as T4 is solution treated and quenched, and must be formed or driven quickly, or held refrigerated, before it hardens.

T6

Solution treated and artificially aged to peak strength. Used for pins, shafts and machined parts that see high static loads in benign environments.

T73

Over-aged. Strength drops by roughly 10 – 15 % compared with T6, but resistance to stress-corrosion cracking and exfoliation improves sharply. The usual choice for fasteners in airframes.

T74 / T76

Intermediate over-aged conditions that trade a little less strength than T73 for better resistance than T6. Specified on some drawings for rod and forging stock.

H13 / F

Drawn or as-fabricated conditions with no guaranteed properties, used as feedstock for the customer's own heat treatment.

Working with 7A09

Heading, machining and finishing

Cold heading

Head in O or freshly quenched condition, then age. Heading in T6 risks cracking at the head-shank fillet.

Machining

Excellent in T6 and T73: short chips and a fine finish on Swiss-type and automatic lathes.

Heat treatment

Solution treatment at about 465 – 480 °C followed by fast water quench; ageing schedules follow AMS 2770 practice for 7075.

Joining

Not fusion weldable. Riveting, bolting and adhesive bonding are standard.

Corrosion protection

Anodise (sulphuric or chromic acid) or apply conversion coatings; specify T73 for parts exposed to sustained tensile stress.

Inspection

Diameter, surface and conductivity checks; tensile and shear tests on samples from each lot.

Applications

Where 7A09 wire is used

  • Airframe fasteners: blanks for pins, bolts and studs that are headed, rolled and aged
  • Hinge and shear pins: control-surface hinges, access panels and quick-release fittings
  • Small shafts and spindles: actuator parts, instrument mechanisms and drone frames
  • Clips and retaining rings: formed in soft temper and then aged
  • Sports and defence: lightweight high-strength pins, arrow shafts and fixings

Older material for this grade listed uses in "circuit systems" and power transmission; these are not realistic for a 7xxx alloy and have been removed.

FAQ

7A09 aluminium (aluminum) wire: frequently asked questions

Is 7A09 the same as 7075?

Chemically they are very close and are treated as equivalents for most designs. They are certified to different standards, so if your drawing calls for 7075 to ASTM or AMS, order and certify it as 7075.

Can 7A09 wire be used for welding?

No. Al-Zn-Mg-Cu alloys with copper crack when fusion welded and are not used as filler metal. For welding wire, see ER4043, ER5356 or ER5183.

Which temper should I specify for fasteners?

T73 for most airframe fasteners, because it resists stress-corrosion cracking. T6 gives more strength but should be limited to parts with low sustained tensile stress or a benign environment.

Why is "T4" 7A09 wire unstable?

7xxx alloys continue to age at room temperature after quenching, so their properties drift for months. That condition is properly called W; parts must be formed quickly or kept refrigerated.

What diameters can you supply?

Wire from about 0.5 to 12 mm, with larger sizes as drawn or extruded rod. Straight cut lengths or coils are available.

Do you ship internationally?

Yes. Metline ships worldwide from Mumbai with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and export packing.
