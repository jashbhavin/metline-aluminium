2014 Aerospace Aluminium Forgings

2014 is the classic high-strength forging alloy of the aircraft industry: in the T6 temper it reaches about 480 MPa tensile strength with good machinability, which is why it has been used for decades in landing-gear fittings, wheels, frames and engine mounts. Metline supplies 2014 die forgings, hand forgings, rolled rings, discs and forging stock to AMS and ASTM specifications, with full mill certification, to aerospace and defence buyers worldwide.

Overview

Why 2014 is used for aerospace forgings

2014 is an aluminium-copper alloy with an unusually high silicon content. The silicon makes the alloy age faster and harder than 2024, so 2014-T6 forgings reach higher yield strength than 2024 in the same section, and they keep that strength well into thick sections after quenching.

Forging refines the cast structure of the billet and aligns the grain flow with the outline of the part. For a landing-gear fitting or an engine mount, that grain flow follows the load path, which improves fatigue life and fracture behaviour compared with a part machined from plate. 2014 forges well in closed dies at roughly 420 – 460 °C, fills detailed cavities and machines to a fine finish after heat treatment.

The trade-off is corrosion behaviour. With 4 – 5 % copper, 2014 is one of the less corrosion-resistant aluminium alloys, and in the short-transverse direction of thick T6 forgings it is susceptible to stress-corrosion cracking. Designers therefore keep sustained tensile stresses out of the short-transverse grain, specify protective finishes (anodising, chromate-free conversion coatings, primers) and, for newer designs, often compare 2014 with 7050-T74 or 7175-T74 forgings.

Where 2014 forgings make sense

  • High static strength in medium-thickness parts at moderate cost
  • Complex closed-die shapes with good die fill and machinability
  • Long-established design data and approvals on existing aircraft programmes
  • Wheels, brackets, fittings and housings where finishes protect the surface

Tempers

2014 forging tempers explained

T6

Solution heat-treated at about 500 °C, quenched and artificially aged. The standard temper for 2014 die forgings and the one covered by AMS 4133 and AMS 4135.

T652

Solution treated, then stress-relieved by 1 – 5 % permanent compression before ageing. Used for hand forgings and rings that will be heavily machined, because it cuts distortion.

T4

Solution treated and naturally aged. Lower strength, higher ductility; occasionally used for parts that are formed or straightened after forging.

F and O

As-forged (F) or annealed (O) blanks, supplied when the customer heat-treats the finished or rough-machined part.

T651 and T6511

Stretched tempers of plate and extruded bar. They are not forging tempers, but buyers often compare a forging with a part machined from 2014-T651 plate or T6511 bar.

Choosing

Use T6 for closed-die parts, T652 for large hand forgings and rings, and confirm with your stress engineer which direction-specific minimums apply.

Testing & inspection

Testing, inspection and documentation

  • Chemistry by spectrometer for every melt, reported on the certificate.
  • Tensile tests from prolongations or sacrificial forgings, in the directions the specification requires (longitudinal, long-transverse, short-transverse).
  • Hardness and electrical conductivity checks to confirm correct heat treatment of every piece.
  • Ultrasonic inspection to AMS-STD-2154 or ASTM B594 classes where the drawing calls for it.
  • Macro-etch and grain-flow examination on first-article or sample forgings.
  • Dimensional inspection against the forging drawing, plus penetrant inspection on request.
  • Certification to EN 10204 3.1 with heat-treatment and test traceability.
Working with 2014

Machining, joining and surface protection

Machining

2014-T6 machines very well: chips break cleanly and the finish is bright. Use sharp carbide or PCD tools, high speeds and flood coolant. Rough-machine, then finish after any stress-relief to hold tolerances.

Welding

Fusion welding is not recommended for structural joints: the alloy is prone to hot cracking and the weld zone loses much of its strength. Parts are joined mechanically (bolts, rivets) or by friction-stir methods.

Corrosion protection

Always protect 2014: sulphuric or boric-sulphuric anodising, conversion coating plus primer, or sealant at faying surfaces. Avoid sustained short-transverse tensile stress.

Shot peening

Peening of fillets and bores puts the surface in compression and improves fatigue and stress-corrosion performance, a common step on landing-gear parts.

Temperature limit

Strength falls with prolonged exposure above about 120 – 150 °C. For hotter parts, 2618A or 2219 forgings are better choices.

Dimensional stability

T652 compression stress relief reduces residual quench stress so thin walls do not move during final machining.

Applications

Where 2014 aerospace forgings are used

  • Landing gear: torque links, brackets, trunnion fittings and actuator ends on light and transport aircraft
  • Wheels and brakes: forged wheel halves and brake housings for aircraft and helicopters
  • Airframe fittings: wing-to-fuselage fittings, engine mounts, frames and hinge brackets
  • Helicopter parts: transmission housings, mast fittings and control-system parts
  • Defence: missile bodies and fins, launcher fittings and vehicle suspension parts
  • Ground equipment: towing fittings, jacks and test-rig parts that need aircraft-grade strength

A typical wheel-half programme, for example, uses 2014-T6 closed-die forgings because the alloy gives high strength at a lower cost than 7xxx grades and machines quickly, while anodising and paint protect the surface in service.

FAQ

2014 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) forgings: FAQ

What is the difference between 2014 and 2014A forgings?

2014A is the European variant with tighter limits on silicon (0.50 – 0.9 %) and iron (0.50 % max), plus a Ti + Zr limit. Properties are very close; 2014A gives slightly better toughness. US specifications such as AMS 4133 call for 2014.

Can 2014-T6 forgings be welded?

Not for structural joints. 2014 cracks easily during fusion welding and the heat-affected zone loses strength. Use mechanical fastening or friction-stir welding, and treat any repair weld as a special engineering case.

Why do 2014 forgings need a protective finish?

The high copper content lowers corrosion resistance, and thick T6 sections can suffer stress-corrosion cracking in the short-transverse direction. Anodising or conversion coating plus primer, and sealant at joints, are standard.

What is T652 and when should I order it?

T652 is solution treated, compressed 1 – 5 % to relieve quench stresses, then aged. Order it for hand forgings, rings and blocks that will be heavily machined, so the part does not distort.

Is 2014 or 7075 better for a forged fitting?

7075-T73 has similar strength and far better stress-corrosion resistance, at higher cost. 2014-T6 is chosen where the design already uses it, where cost matters and where the stress state and finish keep corrosion risk low.

What certificates come with 2014 forgings?

An EN 10204 3.1 certificate with chemistry, tensile results in the required directions, hardness or conductivity, and heat-treatment traceability; ultrasonic and dimensional reports are added when specified.
