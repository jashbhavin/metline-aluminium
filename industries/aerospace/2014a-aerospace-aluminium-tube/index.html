2014 / 2014A Aerospace Aluminium Tube

2014 tube is a high-strength aluminium-copper tube used where a hollow section must carry high loads: struts, actuator bodies, torque tubes and machined sleeves. In T6 and T6511 it reaches about 480 MPa tensile strength, well above 6061 tube. Overview

What is 2014 aerospace tube?

Aluminium alloy 2014 contains about 4.4 % copper with silicon, manganese and magnesium. In tube form it gives one of the highest strength-to-weight ratios available in a seamless aluminium hollow, which is why it is chosen for structural tubes and thick-walled sleeves rather than for fluid lines.

Two production routes are used. Extruded seamless tube is pierced or extruded over a mandrel from a hollow billet, then heat-treated, stretched and aged to T6510 or T6511; it covers larger diameters and heavy walls. Drawn seamless tube starts from extruded tube and is cold drawn through dies over a plug to closer tolerances and a finer surface, then heat-treated to T4 or T6.

Because 2014 has limited corrosion resistance and poor fusion weldability, it is rarely used for hydraulic or fuel lines, where 6061-T6, 2024-T3 (to AMS 4086/4088) or stainless steel are standard. Its strength makes it a good fit for machined structural tube: landing-gear and actuator parts, struts and links, where the tube is protected by anodising or paint.

Where 2014 tube fits

  • Structural struts, torque tubes and links that need high yield strength
  • Thick-walled sleeves, bushings and cylinders machined from heavy-wall tube
  • Parts that will be anodised or painted, not exposed bare
  • Not recommended for welded assemblies or hydraulic lines

Inspection

Testing and inspection

  • Tensile tests per lot to the governing specification
  • Conductivity and hardness to confirm heat treatment
  • Eddy-current or ultrasonic inspection of tube on request
  • OD, ID, wall, ovality and straightness checks
  • EN 10204 3.1 certificate with chemistry and properties
Working with 2014

Fabrication guidance

Machining

Excellent. Bore and turn with sharp carbide tools and steady support for thin walls.

Bending and swaging

Use T4 or O temper and age afterwards; T6 tube cracks at tight radii.

Joining

Threaded ends, swaged fittings, pins and adhesive bonding. Avoid fusion welding.

Protection

Anodise inside and out where possible, or conversion-coat and prime. Seal ends.

Applications

Applications

  • Landing-gear and actuator sleeves and cylinders machined from heavy-wall tube
  • Structural struts, braces and torque tubes
  • Flight-control push-pull tubes where high stiffness and strength are needed
  • Missile bodies and launcher tubes
  • High-strength sporting and industrial tube parts

For hydraulic, fuel and pneumatic lines, specify a dedicated line-tube material such as 2024-T3 to AMS 4086 or 6061-T6 to AMS 4083 rather than 2014.

FAQ

2014 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) tube: FAQ

Is 2014 tube used for aircraft hydraulic lines?

Rarely. Hydraulic lines use materials with dedicated line-tube specifications, such as 2024-T3 to AMS 4086 or 6061-T6. 2014 tube is used for structural and machined parts.

What is the difference between drawn and extruded 2014 tube?

Drawn tube is cold drawn from extruded tube to tighter tolerances and a smoother surface, usually in smaller sizes. Extruded tube covers larger sizes and heavy walls, typically in T6511.

Can 2014 tube be bent?

Yes in O or T4, with generous radii, followed by ageing to T6. T6 tube has limited ductility and may crack in tight bends.

Is 2014 stronger than 6061 tube?

Yes. Typical yield strength is about 414 MPa against 276 MPa for 6061-T6, but 6061 has much better corrosion resistance and weldability.

Do you supply imperial aircraft tube sizes?

Yes. Tube can be supplied in inch OD and wall sizes, such as 1/2 in × 0.065 in, as well as metric sizes.
