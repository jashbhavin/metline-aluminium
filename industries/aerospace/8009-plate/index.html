8009 H112 Aerospace Aluminium Sheet & Plate

8009 is a rapidly solidified, powder-metallurgy aluminium alloy with about 8.6 % iron, 1.8 % silicon and 1.3 % vanadium. Unlike conventional aerospace grades it is not strengthened by ageing, so it keeps useful strength at 250 – 315 °C where 2xxx and 7xxx alloys soften. Metline sources 8009-H112 sheet to AMS 4308, and thicker plate on enquiry, for aerospace and defence customers worldwide.

Overview

What makes 8009 different from other aerospace alloys?

Most high-strength aluminium alloys rely on precipitates formed during ageing at 120 – 190 °C. Expose them to higher temperatures in service and those precipitates coarsen, so strength falls away. 8009 avoids this problem by using thermally stable iron-vanadium-silicon dispersoids that barely change below about 400 °C.

Those dispersoids can only be made fine enough by solidifying the metal extremely quickly. That is why 8009 is a powder-metallurgy product rather than a cast-and-rolled one. The alloy was developed for aero-engine and airframe parts that run hot, such as compressor-section components, nacelle structures, missile fins and skins near exhausts, where titanium was the only alternative.

An earlier version of this page gave a 7075-type chemistry (5 – 6 % zinc) and described 8009 as weldable and age-hardened. Both statements were wrong for the real alloy and have been corrected here.

Why engineers consider 8009

  • Retains a far larger share of its room-temperature strength at 200 – 315 °C than 2618, 2219 or 7xxx alloys
  • Good creep resistance and thermal stability: no over-ageing during long exposure
  • Can replace titanium in some warm-zone parts, at lower density and cost
  • Good general corrosion resistance because it contains no copper or zinc

Process

How 8009 sheet and plate are produced

  1. Rapid solidificationMolten alloy is cooled at extremely high rates, for example by planar-flow casting onto a chilled wheel or by atomisation, so the iron and vanadium cannot form coarse needles.
  2. Powder or flakeThe ribbon or droplets are broken down into flake or powder with a very fine internal structure.
  3. ConsolidationThe powder is degassed, canned and hot-pressed into a dense billet.
  4. WorkingThe billet is hot rolled into sheet or plate, extruded into rod and profiles, or forged.
  5. No age hardeningThe product is delivered in H112 (as-fabricated). Strength comes from the dispersoids, not from a solution-and-age heat treatment.

Because the route involves powder handling and consolidation, 8009 sheet comes from a small number of specialist producers and is made in campaigns. Sizes depend on billet dimensions; confirm thickness, width and length at the enquiry stage.

Properties

Mechanical and physical properties

Published research on rapidly solidified and spray-formed 8009 reports room-temperature tensile strength of roughly 410 – 460 MPa and yield strength of roughly 345 – 410 MPa, with elongation around 8 – 12 %. Values depend strongly on the processing route and product form, so use the minimums in AMS 4308 for design.

The high iron content makes 8009 denser than conventional aluminium, but its strength at temperature means specific strength above about 200 °C is still well ahead of 2xxx alloys. The iron-rich phases also raise the elastic modulus above that of ordinary aluminium alloys.

Working with 8009

Forming, joining and machining

Forming

Formability is lower than for conventional sheet. Warm forming and generous bend radii are normal; trial parts are recommended.

Joining

Fusion welding melts the rapidly solidified structure and coarsens the dispersoids, so weld zones lose much of their strength. Riveting, bolting, diffusion bonding and friction-stir welding are preferred.

Machining

Machines well with carbide tools; the hard intermetallic particles cause more tool wear than 6061.

Heat exposure

Avoid processing above about 450 °C; temperatures above about 600 °C coarsen the dispersoids and permanently reduce strength.

Surface treatment

Chemical conversion coatings and paint systems apply normally; anodising gives a darker film because of the iron content.

Inspection

Ultrasonic inspection is advisable for thicker sections to confirm full consolidation.

Applications

Typical uses of 8009 sheet and plate

  • Nacelle, pylon and auxiliary power unit panels exposed to warm air
  • Compressor-section housings and ducting in small gas turbines
  • Missile fins and skins heated by aerodynamic friction
  • Heat-exchanger and bleed-air components where weight matters
  • Research and replacement of titanium parts in the 150 – 315 °C band

An earlier version of this page suggested turbine discs and blades. Those run far above any aluminium alloy's capability and are made from titanium and nickel superalloys.

FAQ

8009 aluminium (aluminum) sheet: frequently asked questions

Is 8009 a heat-treatable alloy?

No. It is supplied in H112 and gets its strength from fine Fe-V-Si dispersoids formed during rapid solidification. Solution treatment and ageing do not apply.

What temperature can 8009 withstand in service?

It is designed for long-term use up to roughly 315 °C, well beyond the range where 2xxx and 7xxx alloys lose strength. Exposure above about 600 °C permanently degrades it.

Can 8009 be welded?

Fusion welding is not recommended because it destroys the rapidly solidified structure. Use mechanical fastening, diffusion bonding or friction-stir welding.

Which specification covers 8009 sheet?

SAE AMS 4308 covers sheet 0.25 – 6.35 mm thick in H112. AMS 4309 covers extrusions of the same alloy.

Why is 8009 denser than other aluminium alloys?

It contains about 8.6 % iron, which is much heavier than aluminium. Density is about 3.0 g/cm³, against 2.7 – 2.8 g/cm³ for conventional grades.

Can Metline supply 8009?

Yes, on enquiry. Because it is a specialist powder-metallurgy product, we confirm size, specification and availability with each quote.

Need heat-resistant aluminium sheet?
