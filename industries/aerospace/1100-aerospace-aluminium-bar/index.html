1100 Aluminium Rod and Bar for Aerospace

1100 is the commercially pure aluminium (99.00 % min, with a little copper) that US aerospace specifications name for formable, weldable bar, rod and wire. It is used for soft "A" rivets, spun and headed fittings, bonding and earthing hardware, spacers and lightly loaded machined parts. Metline supplies 1100 rod and bar in O, H12, H14, H16, H18 and F tempers to ASTM B211, AMS-QQ-A-225/1 and AMS documents, worldwide.

Overview

Why aerospace drawings still call for 1100 bar

1100 is the oldest commercial aluminium grade still in everyday aerospace use. It is not strong, but it is predictable: soft, very ductile, easy to weld and braze, highly resistant to corrosion and almost impossible to crack in forming.

The alloy is 99.00 % aluminium minimum with 0.05 – 0.20 % copper. That small copper addition is what separates 1100 from 1050 and 1060: it raises strength slightly and gives a finer, more consistent grain after annealing, which helps with cold heading and spinning. Like every 1xxx alloy, 1100 cannot be heat treated for strength; all its strength comes from cold work during rolling, drawing or forming.

In aircraft work the bar form is chosen for parts that are headed, upset, spun, swaged or lightly machined. The best-known example is the rivet: in the MS/NAS rivet code, the letter "A" denotes 1100, used for non-structural riveting of soft parts such as fairings, trim and ducting where a 2117 (AD) rivet would be too hard. Other uses include electrical bonding studs and busbar pieces, spacers and bushes that must not gall softer parts, thermal conductors and heat-spreader blocks, and seal and gasket carriers.

On ground support equipment, test rigs and tooling, 1100 bar is used for formed handles, tie-down fittings and conductive components where corrosion resistance and ductility count for more than strength.

What 1100 bar is good at

  • Cold heading and upsetting without cracking, for rivets and studs
  • Very high electrical and thermal conductivity for a general engineering alloy
  • Excellent corrosion resistance without coatings
  • Easy welding and brazing with no hot-cracking risk
  • Long, stable history in US military and aerospace specifications

Tempers

Which temper of 1100 bar to order

1100-O

Fully annealed. Lowest strength and highest ductility. Order for cold heading, upsetting, swaging, spinning and sharp bends. Standard condition for rivet stock.

1100-H12

Quarter hard. A small increase in stiffness while keeping most of the formability; suits parts that are lightly formed and then handled.

1100-H14

Half hard. The common general-purpose condition for drawn rod, with a useful balance of strength and workability for turned parts.

1100-H16

Three-quarter hard. Higher strength where only mild forming follows.

1100-H18

Full hard, usually cold-finished rod. Highest strength and best machined finish of the 1100 tempers; limited bendability.

1100-F / H112

As fabricated (rolled or extruded). No guaranteed properties for F; H112 has minimum properties. Used for larger diameters, machining stock and parts that will be annealed later. AMS 4102 covers 1100-F bar and rod.

Common ordering mistakes with 1100 bar

  • Ordering O temper for a precision turned part: soft bar machines poorly and bends in the chuck
  • Asking for 1050 or 1060 to a drawing that names AMS-QQ-A-225/1, which covers only 1100
  • Leaving out the tolerance class, so quotes are not comparable
  • Specifying F temper where minimum properties are actually needed (use H112 or a defined H temper)
Working with 1100

Machining, heading, welding and finishing

Machining

Machinability is fair at best: the alloy is soft and produces long, stringy chips. Use polished, very sharp carbide or HSS tools with high positive rake, high speeds, chip breakers and generous coolant. Harder tempers (H14 – H18) give a much better finish than O.

Cold heading

1100-O is one of the easiest aluminium grades to head. Heads form without cracking even with large upset ratios, which is why it is used for soft rivets and studs.

Welding

Weldable by TIG, MIG, resistance and gas methods, with 1100 or 4043 filler. Welds and the heat-affected zone return to annealed strength.

Brazing and soldering

Readily brazed with Al-Si filler metals, and solderable with suitable aluminium fluxes.

Anodising

Takes clear and dyed anodic films well, with a bright appearance.

Annealing

Full anneal at around 345 °C, hold until uniform, then air cool. Cooling rate is not critical for 1xxx alloys.

FAQ

1100 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) bar: FAQ

What does the "A" in an MS or AN rivet part number mean?

In the standard rivet material code, A stands for 1100 aluminium. A rivets are soft and used only for non-structural joints in soft materials; structural rivets use 2117 (AD), 2017 (D), 2024 (DD) or 7050 (E).

Can 1100 bar be heat treated to increase strength?

No. 1100 is non-heat-treatable. Strength comes only from cold work, giving the H12 to H18 tempers. Heat treatment can only anneal it back to O.

Is 1100 aluminium good for machining?

It machines, but not easily: it is soft and gummy. Order H14 or H18 for turned parts, use sharp high-rake tools and plenty of coolant, or choose 6061 or 2011 if the design allows.

What is the difference between 1100-F and 1100-H112?

Both are as fabricated. F has no guaranteed mechanical properties; H112 has minimum properties set by the specification. Order H112 if you need any property guarantee.

What is the European equivalent of 1100?

EN AW-1100 (Al99.0Cu). The common UK grade 1200 is close but has no copper requirement and is not a direct substitute where AMS or ASTM 1100 is specified.

How much does a 1 inch 1100 round bar weigh?

About 1.37 kg per metre, or 5.0 kg for a 12 ft (3.66 m) length.

Do you ship 1100 bar internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies 1100 rod and bar worldwide from Mumbai, with mill certificates and export packing.
