7010 Aerospace Aluminium Plate (T7451, T7651, T7351)

7010 is a zirconium-bearing Al-Zn-Mg-Cu plate alloy that keeps high strength, toughness and stress-corrosion resistance through thick sections. It competes directly with 7050 and is used for machined wing ribs, spars, frames and bulkheads, especially on European programmes. Metline supplies 7010 plate in T7451, T7651 and T7351 to AMS 4205, AMS 4204, AMS 4203 and EN 485, certified and shipped worldwide.

Overview

What is 7010 aluminium plate?

7010 was developed in the UK as a thick-plate aerospace alloy. Like 7050, it uses zirconium rather than chromium to control grain structure. Zirconium makes the alloy far less sensitive to quench rate than 7075, so the centre of a thick plate keeps most of the strength and toughness of the surface.

The alloy is supplied in over-aged T7-type tempers that trade a small amount of peak strength for much better resistance to stress-corrosion cracking and exfoliation. T7451 offers the best stress-corrosion resistance in thick sections, T7651 gives higher strength with good exfoliation resistance, and T7351 is the most over-aged and corrosion-resistant condition.

Because the plates are stretched after quenching (the "51" in each temper), residual stresses are low and parts machined from them distort very little. That makes 7010 a natural choice for monolithic machined parts: wing ribs, spar sections, fuselage frames, bulkheads and landing-gear support structure cut from plates 50–150 mm thick.

Why choose 7010 plate

  • Low quench sensitivity: properties hold through thick sections
  • Strength, fracture toughness and stress-corrosion resistance comparable to 7050
  • Stress-relieved by stretching, so heavily machined parts stay flat
  • Covered by both SAE AMS and EN aerospace specifications

Tempers

T7451, T7651 or T7351?

7010-T7451 (AMS 4205)

Solution treated, stretched and over-aged for high stress-corrosion resistance. The default for thick machined parts loaded in the short-transverse direction.

7010-T7651 (AMS 4204)

Less over-aged than T7451 for higher strength, with good exfoliation resistance. Used for wing skins and parts where strength governs. AMS 4204 covers 6.35–139.7 mm.

7010-T7351 (AMS 4203)

The most over-aged temper, with the highest stress-corrosion resistance and lower strength. Used where SCC risk is the deciding factor.

Electrical conductivity testing is a fast way to confirm the degree of over-ageing. Each temper has a conductivity window in the specification, and it is checked on every plate.

Working with 7010

Machining and processing

Machining

Excellent. High-speed milling with carbide tools gives fast metal removal; stretched tempers keep thin-walled parts flat.

Welding

Not fusion-welded in aerospace structures because of hot cracking and strength loss. Friction stir welding is possible but must be qualified.

Surface protection

Anodise (sulphuric or boric-sulphuric) and prime. The alloy is not clad, so finished parts always need a protective system.

Forming

Limited at room temperature in T7 tempers. Creep or age forming is used for contoured skins.

Applications

Where 7010 plate is used

  • Wing structure: ribs, spar sections, upper wing skins and stringer-integral panels
  • Fuselage: frames, bulkheads, floor beams and door surrounds machined from thick plate
  • Landing gear support: fittings and beams subject to short-transverse loading
  • Engine pylons and mounts: machined fittings where stress-corrosion resistance matters
  • Defence and space: structural parts needing thick-section strength and toughness

FAQ

7010 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) plate: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 7010 and 7050?

Both are zirconium-bearing Al-Zn-Mg-Cu thick-plate alloys with similar strength, toughness and stress-corrosion resistance. 7050 has more copper (2.0–2.6 %) and slightly less magnesium; 7010 is more common on European programmes. They are not interchangeable without design approval.

Which 7010 temper should I choose?

T7451 for thick machined parts that see short-transverse loads, T7651 where higher strength and exfoliation resistance are the priority, and T7351 where stress-corrosion resistance must be maximised.

What thickness is 7010 plate available in?

Typically from about 6 mm up to around 200 mm. AMS 4204 (T7651) covers 6.35–139.7 mm; other specifications cover thicker plate.

Can 7010 be welded?

It is not fusion-welded in aircraft structure. Parts are machined from solid and joined with fasteners.

What testing comes with 7010 plate?

Chemistry, tensile tests, electrical conductivity and ultrasonic inspection, reported on an EN 10204 3.1 certificate.
