Aerospace forgings

7014 Aerospace Aluminium Forgings (BS L 171)

7014 forgings are high-strength Al-Zn-Mg-Mn-Cu aerospace parts made to BS L 171 from BS L 172 forging stock, and used in the solution-treated and overaged condition for strength with stress-corrosion resistance. They are found mainly on UK and legacy airframe, helicopter and engine drawings. Metline sources 7014 die forgings, hand forgings and rings against the drawing and specification, or proposes approved 7xxx alternatives, with full certification and worldwide delivery.

Overview

What are 7014 forgings?

BS L 171 defines 7014 forgings as parts of an aluminium-zinc-magnesium-manganese-copper alloy, not exceeding 150 mm in diameter or minor sectional dimension, supplied as-forged or annealed and then used in the solution-treated and overaged condition. BS L 172 covers the extruded, rolled or cast stock the forgings are made from.

The section limit is significant. Like other high-strength 7xxx alloys without zirconium, 7014 is sensitive to quench rate: the centre of a very thick forging cools too slowly to reach full strength. Limiting the section to 150 mm keeps properties consistent through the part. For thicker forgings, low-quench-sensitivity alloys such as 7050 or 7010 are the usual choice.

Overageing is the other defining feature. Peak-aged 7xxx alloys (T6) are vulnerable to stress-corrosion cracking, particularly in the short-transverse direction of forgings where the grain flow ends at a machined surface. Overageing coarsens the precipitates at grain boundaries and greatly reduces that risk, at the cost of a modest drop in strength. This is the same principle behind the T73 and T74 tempers of 7075, 7050 and 7175.

Today 7014 forgings are mostly bought for continuing production and spares of existing designs. Because volumes are small, each order is planned individually around the drawing, the forging route and the test plan.

Key facts at a glance

  • Nominal composition Zn 5.7, Mg 2.7, Mn 0.5, Cu 0.5 (per BS L 171 / L 172)
  • Forgings up to 150 mm diameter or minor section under BS L 171

Chemical composition

7014 chemical composition (wt %)

7014 is defined by British aerospace specifications rather than by a widely published international data sheet. Its nominal composition, given in the titles of BS L 171 and BS L 172, is Zn 5.7 – Mg 2.7 – Mn 0.5 – Cu 0.5. The ranges below are the indicative limits most often quoted for the alloy; the governing values are those of the specification and revision on your order, and every lot is certified against it.

Heat treatment

Heat treatment and tempers

7014 forgings follow the classic three-step route for heat-treatable aluminium: solution treatment, quench and ageing. What sets the forging specification apart is the ageing step, which goes beyond peak strength into the overaged range.

  1. ForgeStock to BS L 172 is heated and forged in open or closed dies, with temperature control to avoid coarse grain and surface cracking.
  2. Solution treatThe forging is heated to the specified solution temperature so that zinc, magnesium and copper go into solid solution.
  3. QuenchRapid quench, usually in water or glycol solution. Section size and quench medium are controlled to balance properties against distortion and residual stress.
  4. OverageTwo-stage or extended artificial ageing takes the alloy past peak strength for stress-corrosion resistance.
  5. VerifyTensile, hardness and electrical conductivity checks confirm that the required condition has been reached.

Supplier listings sometimes offer 7014 forgings in T6, T651, T73 or T7351. T651 is a stretched temper for plate and bar and does not apply to forgings. The condition that matters is the one named in BS L 171 or on your drawing, and we quote only to that.

Supply range

Types of 7014 forgings

Closed-die forgings

Near-net-shape fittings, brackets, levers and housings with grain flow following the contour. Best mechanical properties and least machining.

Hand (open-die) forgings

Blocks, bars and simple shapes forged with a machining allowance. Used for prototypes and low volumes where die cost is not justified.

Rings

Seamless forged or rolled rings for flanges and mounting rings, with circumferential grain flow.

Discs and hubs

Upset forgings with radial grain flow for wheel-type parts.

Forging stock

Extruded, rolled or cast stock to BS L 172 for customers who forge in-house.

Machined forgings

Rough- or finish-machined parts with inspection to the drawing.

All sizes are confirmed against the drawing and the 150 mm section limit of BS L 171.

Quality

Inspection and approval

  • First article: dimensional layout, grain-flow macro-etch and mechanical tests on a sectioned forging when the customer requires it
  • Chemistry: cast analysis against the specification
  • Mechanical tests: in the directions and locations defined by BS L 171 and the drawing
  • Conductivity and hardness: on every heat-treated forging or lot to confirm the overaged condition
  • NDT: ultrasonic and penetrant inspection as specified
  • Traceability: cast, heat-treatment lot and forging serial or batch numbers on every part
Design

Design and machining notes

Grain flow

Locate highly stressed features along the grain flow. Avoid machining through the parting line on stressed surfaces, where end grain is exposed.

Short-transverse loads

Even in overaged condition, short-transverse properties are the lowest. Check bolt-hole and lug orientation against grain flow.

Residual stress

Quenched forgings carry residual stress. Allow for distortion when machining thin walls, or specify a compressively stress-relieved condition where the specification permits.

Surface protection

Anodise and prime finished parts; 7xxx alloys need a protective system in service.

Machining

Machines well with carbide tools. Leave balanced stock on both sides of thin features.

Joining

Not fusion-welded. Use fasteners and bushings.

Applications

Where 7014 forgings are used

  • Airframe: fittings, lugs, hinges and attachment brackets on legacy UK designs
  • Helicopters: transmission and rotor-system support fittings
  • Engines and nacelles: mounting brackets and accessory housings
  • Landing gear: secondary fittings and brackets
  • Spares and MRO: replacement forgings to original drawings

FAQ

7014 aerospace forgings: frequently asked questions

What standard covers 7014 forgings?

BS L 171 covers 7014 forgings up to 150 mm diameter or minor section, and BS L 172 covers the forging stock. Legacy MoD and purchaser specifications are also used.

Why are 7014 forgings overaged?

Overageing greatly improves resistance to stress-corrosion cracking, especially in the short-transverse direction, at the cost of a small drop in strength.

Can 7014 forgings be thicker than 150 mm?

Not under BS L 171. The alloy is quench-sensitive, so thicker sections would not reach consistent properties. For thick forgings, 7050 or 7010 is normally used.

Do you supply 7014 forgings as-forged for our own heat treatment?

Yes. BS L 171 allows forgings to be supplied as-forged or annealed for subsequent heat treatment, or we can deliver fully heat-treated parts to the drawing.

What can replace 7014 forgings?

7075-T73, 7050-T74 and 7175-T74 forgings are common approved alternatives. Any change needs design authority approval.

Which documents come with 7014 forgings?

An EN 10204 3.1 certificate with chemistry, mechanical, conductivity and NDT results, plus first-article reports when required.

Need 7014 forgings for a legacy drawing?

Send the drawing, specification and quantity. We will confirm the forging route, certification and a delivered price.
