7014 Aerospace Aluminium Sheet & Plate

7014 sheet and plate are high-strength Al-Zn-Mg-Mn-Cu aerospace products specified on UK and legacy airframe drawings, supplied bare or clad with a pure-aluminium surface layer and used in the solution-treated and overaged condition. Because volumes are small, each order is made against a named specification. Metline sources 7014 plate to the drawing, or supplies approved alternatives such as 7075-T7351, 7050-T7451 and 7010, certified and shipped worldwide.

Overview

What is 7014 aluminium plate?

7014 is a British 7xxx alloy with a nominal composition of 5.7 % zinc, 2.7 % magnesium, 0.5 % manganese and 0.5 % copper. It is best known as a forging alloy (BS L 171), but it has also been specified as sheet and plate on legacy UK airframes, sometimes in clad form.

As with other high-strength 7xxx alloys, the plate is solution heat-treated, quenched, normally stretched to relieve stress, and aged. For aerospace use it is taken into the overaged range, which gives better resistance to stress-corrosion cracking and exfoliation than peak-aged material. The manganese in 7014 controls grain structure in place of chromium, and the alloy contains no zirconium, so it is more quench-sensitive than modern thick-plate alloys and is best suited to thinner sections.

Few rolling mills produce 7014 plate as a regular product. Buyers are usually maintaining existing aircraft or producing spares to original drawings. In that situation the most useful thing a supplier can do is confirm quickly whether the specified product can be made in the required size, and if not, help the customer present an alternative to the design authority.

Before you send an enquiry

  • Note the exact specification and revision on the drawing, and whether the plate is bare or clad
  • Give thickness, size and quantity; small quantities can often be cut from a single mill run
  • Ask the design authority early whether an AMS-specified alternative is acceptable
Chemical composition

7014 chemical composition (wt %)

7014 is defined by British aerospace specifications rather than by a widely published international data sheet. Its nominal composition, given in the titles of BS L 171 and BS L 172, is Zn 5.7 – Mg 2.7 – Mn 0.5 – Cu 0.5. The ranges below are the indicative limits most often quoted for the alloy; the governing values are those of the specification and revision on your order, and every lot is certified against it.

Clad plate

Bare versus clad 7014 plate

Clad aerospace plate has a thin layer of pure aluminium or a low-alloy aluminium-zinc alloy roll-bonded to one or both faces. The cladding is anodic to the core, so it corrodes preferentially and protects the high-strength alloy beneath, even at scratches.

FeatureBare plateClad plate
Corrosion protectionRelies on anodising and paintCladding gives sacrificial protection plus coatings
Typical useMachined parts where the surface is removedSkins and panels whose surfaces remain
StrengthFull section strengthSlightly lower overall, since the cladding is soft
FatigueHigherCladding layer reduces fatigue strength slightly

Cladding only makes sense for sheet and thinner plate. On thick plate that will be machined, the cladding is removed and offers no benefit.

Tempers

Tempers and properties

Mechanical property limits for 7014 sheet and plate are set by the governing specification for each thickness and are reported on the certificate. We do not quote single typical values for this alloy because published data are limited and older supplier figures conflict.

Overaged (T7-type)

The usual aerospace condition: solution treated, stretched and overaged for stress-corrosion and exfoliation resistance.

Peak-aged (T6 / T651)

Highest strength but poor short-transverse stress-corrosion resistance; only used if the drawing calls for it.

Annealed (O)

Soft condition for forming, followed by full heat treatment by the user.

Some listings show 7014 plate in T79 for "engine casings" or "turbine blades". T79 is a slightly overaged 7xxx temper, but no aluminium alloy is used for turbine blades or hot engine sections, and the claim should be disregarded.

Working with 7014

Fabrication notes

Machining

Machines well with carbide tools. Stretched plate keeps residual stress low.

Forming

Form in O or freshly quenched condition, then age. Overaged plate has limited cold formability.

Joining

Not fusion-welded; use riveting and bolting.

Protection

Anodise and prime bare plate. Do not abrade through the cladding on clad plate.

Applications

Where 7014 plate is used

  • Airframe skins and panels: clad sheet on legacy UK designs
  • Machined parts: ribs, brackets and fittings cut from bare plate
  • Spares and MRO: replacement parts made to original drawings

FAQ

7014 aerospace plate: frequently asked questions

Is 7014 plate a standard mill product?

No. It is produced to order against legacy UK or purchaser specifications, usually in small quantities for existing aircraft.

What is clad 7014 plate?

Plate with a thin pure-aluminium or low-alloy layer bonded to its surface. The layer protects the high-strength core from corrosion by corroding preferentially.

Does AMS-QQ-A-250/13 cover 7014?

No. That specification covers clad 7075 plate and sheet. If a drawing pairs it with 7014, clarify which governs with the design authority.

What can replace 7014 plate?

7075-T7351, 7050-T7451 and 7010-T7451 plate, or Alclad 7075 sheet for skins, are common candidates, subject to design approval.

Need 7014 plate for an existing aircraft?

Send the drawing, specification and quantity. We will confirm sourcing, certification and a delivered price.
