2011 Aluminium Bar and Rod for Aerospace

2011 is the free-machining aluminium alloy: copper for strength, plus lead and bismuth that break chips into tiny fragments. It is used for high-volume screw-machine parts such as fittings, inserts, spacers, nuts and instrument components. Metline supplies 2011 round, hex and square bar in T3, T4/T451 and T8 to ASTM B211 and AMS-QQ-A-225/3, with mill certificates, worldwide.

Overview

Why 2011 is used for machined parts

2011 is rated as the benchmark for aluminium machinability. Fine, insoluble particles of lead and bismuth soften locally as the tool passes, so chips break into small pieces instead of long strings. That keeps automatic lathes running unattended and gives an excellent surface finish.

In aerospace and defence supply chains, 2011 bar is used for parts made in large numbers on automatic and CNC lathes where the material is not the primary structure: hydraulic and pneumatic fittings for low-stress service, threaded inserts and bushes, spacers, nuts, instrument and avionics hardware, connector bodies and camera or sensor parts. Its strength in T3 is comparable to 6061-T6 or better.

The compromises are corrosion resistance and weldability. The high copper content makes 2011 more prone to corrosion than 6xxx alloys, so parts are usually anodised or coated, and it is not considered weldable by fusion processes. It is also not suitable for fatigue-critical structure.

Regulation

Lead content and lead-free alternatives

Because 2011 contains up to 0.6 % lead, it falls under lead restrictions in regulations such as the EU RoHS and End-of-Life Vehicles directives, which allow lead in aluminium only within exemption limits that are periodically reviewed. Aerospace equipment is often outside the scope of these rules, but many programmes and their customers now ask for lead-free material anyway.

Lead-free 2xxx

Alloys such as 2033 replace lead with bismuth and tin and keep machinability close to 2011.

Lead-free 6xxx

6026 and 6262-type grades offer good machining with much better corrosion resistance and anodising response.

6061-T6

Machines well with good chip control on modern tooling, and is weldable; the default when machining speed is less critical.

Working with 2011

Machining, finishing and joining

  • Machining: high speeds with sharp carbide or HSS tools; small broken chips and excellent finish without chip breakers
  • Anodising: possible, but the copper content gives a duller, less uniform film than 6xxx alloys; hard anodising is limited
  • Welding: not recommended by fusion welding; join mechanically
  • Corrosion: fair; protect parts by anodising, conversion coating or painting, and prefer T8 for exposed service

FAQ

2011 aluminium (aluminum) bar: FAQ

Is 2011-T6 a standard temper?

ASTM B211 lists 2011 in T3, T4, T451 and T8. T6 appears in some European listings. For US aerospace orders, T3 or T8 is normal.

Which is better, 2011-T3 or T8?

T8 is slightly stronger and more resistant to stress-corrosion cracking; T3 is the most common stock temper. Choose T8 for exposed parts.

Can 2011 be welded?

It is not recommended for fusion welding. Join 2011 parts mechanically.

Does 2011 contain lead?

Yes, 0.20 – 0.6 % lead plus a similar amount of bismuth. Lead-free alternatives such as 2033 or 6026 are available where required.

Which AMS specification covers 2011 bar?

AMS-QQ-A-225/3. AMS 4115 and AMS 4116 are 6061 specifications and do not apply.
