5052 bar is a non-heat-treatable aluminium-magnesium alloy chosen for aircraft and ground-equipment parts that need good corrosion resistance, weldability and moderate strength rather than the high strength of 2024 or 7075. Overview

Where 5052 bar fits in aerospace work

5052 is the strongest of the commonly stocked alloys that cannot be heat treated. About 2.5 % magnesium in solid solution gives it roughly twice the strength of 3003, while 0.25 % chromium keeps the grain fine and protects against stress-corrosion cracking.

Aircraft designers rarely use 5052 bar for primary load paths; those go to 2024, 7075 and 7050. Its place is in the many parts where corrosion resistance and weldability matter more than peak strength: fittings and bosses welded into 5052 fuel and oil tanks, brackets and spacers for fluid lines, machined connectors for ducting, bushings in marine-exposed areas, and fixtures and ground support equipment that stand outdoors on flight lines and carrier decks.

Because 5052 gains strength only from cold work, bar is normally supplied in the as-extruded or annealed condition (H112, F or O) or with a light cold-finishing pass (H32). The alloy keeps those properties after welding much better than heat-treatable grades, which is the main reason it is chosen for welded tank and duct assemblies.

Why engineers specify 5052 bar

  • Very good resistance to marine and industrial atmospheres without coating
  • Readily welded by TIG and MIG with little loss of strength in the O and H112 tempers
  • Good fatigue strength for a non-heat-treatable alloy
  • Compatible with 5052 sheet, tube and honeycomb used elsewhere in the same assembly

Tempers

Choosing a 5052 bar temper

5052-O

Fully annealed. Lowest strength and highest ductility; chosen when the bar will be upset, bent, spun or cold headed into a fitting.

5052-H111

Annealed and then lightly strain-hardened by straightening. Properties are close to O, with slightly better straightness for machining.

5052-H112

As extruded with controlled properties. The most common condition for larger extruded rounds, squares and flats that will be machined.

5052-H32

Strain-hardened and stabilised to quarter-hard. Better strength and machined finish than O, with good residual ductility.

5052-F

As fabricated, with no guaranteed mechanical properties. AMS 4114 covers rolled or cold-finished 5052 bar and rod in this condition.

Note on heat treatment

5052 cannot be hardened by solution treatment and ageing. Stabilising (H3x) only prevents age-softening; strength comes from cold work.

Working with 5052

Machining, welding and finishing 5052 bar

Machining

Softer tempers are gummy and can form built-up edge. Use sharp, polished carbide or HSS tools with high positive rake, generous chip room and plenty of coolant. H32 and harder tempers give a cleaner finish than O.

Welding

Excellent with TIG or MIG. ER5356 filler is the usual choice for strength and colour match; ER5554 or ER5654 suit some service conditions. In O and H112 the weld zone keeps almost the base strength; in H32 the heat-affected zone softens to near O values.

Forming

O and H111 bar can be bent, upset and cold headed. Allow larger radii for H32 and above, and anneal between heavy forming operations.

Anodising

Takes clear and coloured sulphuric anodising well; chromic and boric-sulphuric processes are used where aerospace specifications require them.

Corrosion

Resists seawater spray and industrial atmospheres. Isolate from carbon fibre and stainless fasteners with sealant or primer to avoid galvanic attack.

Temperature

Unlike 5083, 5052 has too little magnesium to suffer sensitisation in normal service, so it is acceptable for warm locations near engines and heat exchangers below about 150 °C.

Applications

Typical aerospace uses of 5052 bar

  • Fuel and oil systems: bosses, ports, flanges and fittings machined from bar and welded into 5052 tanks and lines
  • Fluid-line hardware: spacers, clamps, blocks and connectors for low-pressure fuel, vent and drain lines
  • Ducting: flanges and couplings for environmental-control and cooling ducts
  • Cabin and interior: trim fittings, seat-track accessories and galley hardware where corrosion resistance matters
  • Ground support equipment: machined parts for towbars, stands, platforms and refuelling equipment exposed to weather
  • Rivets and pins: 5052 rod and wire for rivets in non-structural joints

A typical example is a welded auxiliary fuel tank: the shell is 5052-H32 sheet, the filler and drain bosses are machined from 5052-H112 round bar, and everything is joined with ER5356 so the whole assembly has uniform corrosion behaviour.

FAQ

5052 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) bar: FAQs

Is 5052 bar used for primary aircraft structure?

Generally not. Primary structure uses heat-treatable 2xxx and 7xxx alloys. 5052 bar is used for welded tank fittings, fluid-line hardware, ducting parts, interior hardware and ground equipment where corrosion resistance and weldability matter more than strength.

Which specification covers 5052 bar for aerospace?

AMS 4114 covers rolled or cold-finished 5052 bar and rod in the F condition. ASTM B211 (cold-finished) and ASTM B221 (extruded) are also widely used, often with added aerospace inspection requirements.

Can 5052 bar be heat treated to increase strength?

No. 5052 is strengthened only by cold work. Heating can anneal it to the soft O temper or stabilise a strain-hardened temper, but it cannot be solution treated and aged like 6061 or 2024.

What filler wire should be used to weld 5052 bar?

ER5356 is the most common choice and gives good strength and anodised colour match. ER5554 is used where the joint runs warm, and ER4043 is generally avoided on 5052 because of poor ductility in the weld.

What is the difference between H112 and O bar?

O is fully annealed, with the lowest strength and best formability. H112 is the as-extruded condition with minimum tensile and yield values guaranteed; it is the usual choice for bar that will be machined.

Do you supply 5052 bar cut to length with certificates?

Yes. Metline supplies 5052 bar cut to length or as full bars, with an EN 10204 3.1 mill certificate, to customers worldwide.
