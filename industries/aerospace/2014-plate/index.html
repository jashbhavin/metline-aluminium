2014 / 2014A Aerospace Aluminium Sheet & Plate

2014 sheet and plate gives the highest strength of the common 2xxx rolled products: about 480 MPa tensile and 410 MPa yield in T6 and T651. It is used for machined fittings, frames and heavy-duty structure where strength and machinability matter more than corrosion resistance. Overview

What is 2014 aerospace sheet and plate?

2014 is an aluminium-copper-silicon-magnesium alloy (AlCu4SiMg). In sheet and plate form it is chosen when a part needs higher yield strength than 2024 can give, and when the part will be machined or formed and then protected by cladding, anodising or paint.

Two families of product are supplied. Bare 2014 plate in T651 is the machining grade: it is stretched after quenching so it stays flat as material is removed, and it is cut into fittings, frames, ribs and test hardware. Alclad 2014 sheet carries a thin layer of 6003 aluminium alloy on each face; the cladding is anodic to the core and corrodes sacrificially, protecting the 2014 underneath. Alclad sheet is used for formed skins and panels where the surface stays exposed.

2014A is the European version of the alloy, with tighter silicon and iron limits. It is called up in European aerospace standards and British "L" specifications, while US programmes use 2014 to SAE AMS and ASTM documents. For most designs the two are interchangeable only with the approval of the design authority, so always order the designation shown on your drawing.

Key points for buyers

  • T651 plate for machined parts; T6 or T4 sheet for formed parts; O for severe forming
  • Alclad sheet for exposed surfaces; bare plate must be protected after machining
  • Higher strength than 2024-T351 plate, but lower fatigue crack-growth resistance and toughness
  • Not suited to fusion welding; joined by riveting, bolting or bonding

Tempers

Which 2014 temper fits your part?

O (annealed)

Softest condition for stretch forming, deep drawing and tight bends. The formed part is then solution treated and aged to T4 or T6.

T4 / T451

Solution treated and naturally aged. Good formability with useful strength; T451 plate is stretched to relieve stress. Often formed in T4 and aged to T6 (T62) afterwards.

T6

Solution treated and artificially aged for full strength. The usual temper for sheet in finished structure.

T651

Solution treated, stretched 1.5 – 3 % and aged. The standard plate temper for machined parts, because residual stresses are low.

T62

Aged to T6 by the user after heat treatment from O or F. Specify T62 properties when you heat-treat parts yourself.

Alclad tempers

Alclad 2014 is supplied in O, T3, T4 and T6; the core is heat-treated in the same way as bare material.

Tolerances & inspection

Tolerances, testing and documentation

Dimensional tolerances follow ANSI H35.2 for ASTM and AMS orders and EN 485-3/-4 for EN orders. Aerospace plate is commonly ultrasonically inspected to AMS-STD-2154 or ASTM B594, with the class stated on the order. Each lot is tested for tensile properties in the directions the specification requires, and electrical conductivity and hardness are used to confirm correct heat treatment.

  • EN 10204 3.1 certificate with chemistry, tensile results and lot traceability
  • Ultrasonic inspection report where specified
  • Flatness and dimensional checks; cut pieces inspected to drawing tolerance
  • Cladding thickness confirmation for Alclad sheet on request
Working with 2014

Machining, forming and protection

Machining

T651 machines cleanly with short chips. Remove material evenly from both faces of thick plate and keep coolant flowing to control heat.

Forming

Form in O or freshly quenched (W) condition, then age. T6 sheet tolerates only generous bend radii, typically 3 – 6 × thickness depending on gauge.

Joining

Riveting, bolting and adhesive bonding are standard. Fusion welding is avoided because of hot cracking and strength loss.

Corrosion protection

Anodise or conversion-coat and prime all bare surfaces after machining. Alclad sheet protects itself unless the cladding is machined away.

Heat exposure

Do not hold 2014 above about 150 °C for long periods; over-ageing reduces strength.

Inspection after forming

Check formed parts for orange peel and cracking at bends, and confirm properties after re-heat treatment.

Applications

Applications of 2014 sheet and plate

  • Airframe fittings and frames machined from T651 plate
  • Lower-wing and fuselage skins on older and light aircraft, usually Alclad
  • Ribs, brackets and stiffeners formed from O or T4 sheet and aged
  • Missile and space structure where high static strength is needed
  • Truck frames and heavy-duty structures needing high strength in bolted designs
  • Tooling and test rigs that require an aerospace-grade, high-strength plate

FAQ

2014 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQ

What is Alclad 2014?

Alclad 2014 is 2014 sheet or plate with a thin metallurgically bonded layer of 6003 alloy on each face. The cladding corrodes in preference to the core, protecting it. It slightly lowers strength, so Alclad minimums are a little below bare values.

Is 2014-T651 stronger than 2024-T351?

Yes in yield strength: about 414 MPa against about 324 MPa typical. 2024-T351 has better fatigue crack-growth resistance and toughness, so it is preferred for tension skins and damage-tolerant parts.

Can I form 2014-T6 sheet?

Only with generous bend radii. For tight forming, use O or T4 temper, or form immediately after quenching, and then age the part to T6 (reported as T62).

What is the difference between 2014 and 2014A plate?

2014A has tighter limits on silicon and iron and is the designation used in European standards. Mechanical properties are similar, but substitution needs approval from the design authority.

Does 2014 plate need corrosion protection?

Yes. Bare 2014 has low corrosion resistance. Anodise or conversion-coat and paint after machining, or use Alclad sheet for exposed formed parts.

What certification comes with 2014 plate?

An EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate with chemistry and mechanical results for the lot, plus ultrasonic and dimensional reports when the order specifies them.
