Seat track and cargo track

Aircraft Seat Track Extrusions

An aircraft seat track is a high-strength aluminium extrusion with a slotted crown, set into the cabin floor so that seats, galleys and cargo fittings can be locked at any 1-inch position. It is both a mounting rail and part of the floor structure, and must hold seats in place under crash loads. Metline supplies seat-track extrusions and machined tracks in 7075, 7050, 7150 and 6061, plus airline-style L-track for vehicles, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is an aircraft seat track?

A seat track looks simple: a rail with a row of circular openings joined by a narrow slot. Seat legs carry stud fittings that drop through the openings, slide half a pitch and lock under the lips of the crown. Because the openings repeat every inch, an operator can change seat pitch or cabin layout without drilling the floor.

In transport aircraft the tracks are much more than rails. They run fore and aft across the floor beams, carry the floor panels at their edges, and transfer seat loads in turbulence and emergency landings into the fuselage. Certification rules require seats and their attachments to withstand high static and dynamic crash loads, so the track crown, its lips and its attachment to the beams are all sized for those cases.

The same track profile, in lighter alloys, is widely used outside aviation as "airline-style" or L-track for securing cargo in vans, trailers, rail cars and race transporters, and for wheelchair tie-downs in buses. Metline supplies both the aerospace and the commercial versions; the material and certification requirements are very different, so it is important to say which you need.

Key points

  • Standard slot pitch is 1.000 in, with circular openings sized to accept MS33601-style stud fittings
  • Airliner floor tracks use high-strength 7xxx alloys in over-aged tempers for stress-corrosion resistance
  • Commercial L-track usually uses 6061-T6 or 6005A-T6, often anodised
  • Corrosion around galleys, lavatories and doors is the main in-service issue for aluminium tracks
Types

Types of seat track

Structural floor track (transport aircraft)

Deep I- or T-section tracks that act as floor members, with a slotted crown on top. Produced to the airframe maker's drawing in 7075, 7050 or 7150 over-aged tempers, or titanium in wet zones.

Surface-mounted seat track

Lower-profile track bolted on top of a floor or structure, used in business jets, helicopters, light aircraft and modifications.

Light-aircraft seat rail

Adjustable pilot and passenger seat rails with pin holes or slots, often in 6061-T6 or 2024-T3511.

Cargo and logistic track

Flush or surface-mounted track for cargo nets, tie-down rings and pallets in freighter and combi cabins.

Airline-style L-track (commercial)

Surface or flush-mount track for vans, trailers, trucks and buses; usually 6061-T6 or 6005A-T6, mill finish or anodised.

Beveled logistic track

Commercial track with sloped edges so that wheeled loads roll over it without catching.

Geometry

Standard seat-track geometry

Aerospace seat tracks are drawn to the airframe maker's or customer's drawing, but most share a common crown pattern so that standard seat fittings can be used.

Common crown features

Opening pitch
1.000 in (25.4 mm) centre to centre, with cumulative pitch tolerance controlled over the track length
Opening diameter
About 0.785 in (19.9 mm) on many standard tracks
Slot
Continuous narrow slot between openings, into which the fitting's locking plunger or shear plunger engages
Fitting compatibility
MS33601 / AS33601-style single and double stud fittings
Typical widths
About 1.1 – 2 in for commercial and light-aircraft tracks; floor tracks to drawing
Lengths
10, 12, 20 and 24 ft extrusions, cut and machined to drawing

Dimensions are typical. Always check track geometry against the seat or fitting maker's drawing; small differences in crown depth or lip width can prevent fittings from locking.

Openings can be machined in the extrusion, or the extrusion can be supplied plain for the customer to machine. Machining after extrusion and heat treatment is normal, followed by deburring and corrosion protection of the cut edges.

An earlier version of this page also listed 5083 and 6063. These are not suitable for load-rated seat track: 5083 is not normally extruded into such complex hard shapes, and 6063 is too weak for crash loads. Titanium is preferred in the wettest floor zones of some airliners because aluminium tracks there are prone to corrosion.

For installation on a certificated aircraft, the track must also be an approved part for that aircraft, through the type design, a supplemental type certificate, a parts manufacturer approval or a field approval. Raw extrusion supplied to a material specification is not in itself an approved aircraft part.

In service

Corrosion and maintenance

Seat tracks sit at the lowest point of the cabin floor, so spilled drinks, cleaning fluids and condensation collect around them. The areas next to galleys, lavatories and doors are the most common sites of seat-track corrosion found during heavy maintenance checks.

  • Specify over-aged tempers (T73, T76, T77) rather than T6 for structural floor tracks
  • Seal floor-panel edges and fastener holes; keep drain paths clear
  • Use track covers between seats to keep debris and fluids out of the slot
  • Inspect crown lips for cracks and wear at frequently used seat positions
  • Repair or replace damaged track sections according to the aircraft structural repair manual

FAQ

Aircraft seat track (aluminium / aluminum): frequently asked questions

What is the standard pitch of aircraft seat track?

1.000 in (25.4 mm) between opening centres, which lets seats and fittings be positioned in 1-inch steps.

Which alloy is used for airliner seat tracks?

High-strength 7xxx alloys in over-aged tempers, such as 7075-T73511, 7050-T76511 or 7150-T77511. Some aircraft use titanium tracks in wet areas.

Can I use aircraft seat track in a van or trailer?

Yes. Airline-style L-track in 6061-T6 or 6005A-T6 is common for cargo securing. Choose fittings and fasteners rated for the loads you need to restrain.

Is seat-track extrusion an approved aircraft part?

Not by itself. Material certified to a specification must still be made into a part approved for the specific aircraft through its type design, an STC, PMA or field approval.

Why do seat tracks corrode?

Fluids collect around them near galleys, lavatories and doors. Over-aged tempers, sealing, drainage and track covers reduce the risk.

Can you machine the openings?

Yes. Openings, fastener holes and end features can be CNC machined to your drawing, or plain extrusion can be supplied for your own machining.

Need seat track or L-track?

Tell us whether it is for aircraft or vehicle use, with the profile, alloy, length and finish. We will confirm supply and certification.
