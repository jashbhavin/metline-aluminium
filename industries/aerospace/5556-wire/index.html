5556 & 5556A Aluminium Welding Wire and Rod

5556 is the highest-strength member of the common aluminium-magnesium filler family. With about 5 % magnesium, 0.75 % manganese and a titanium grain refiner, it gives stronger welds than 5356 or 5183 in high-strength 5xxx and weldable 7xxx alloys. Overview

What 5556 filler is for

Filler choice decides the strength, ductility and corrosion behaviour of an aluminium weld. 5556 is selected when the joint must be as strong as possible in a non-heat-treated, welded condition, for example in 5083, 5456 and 5383 plate, or in weldable Al-Zn-Mg alloys such as 7005, 7020 and 7039.

In aerospace and defence work this means welded ground tanks and cryogenic equipment, armoured and protective vehicle hulls, heavy ground support equipment, shelters and naval aviation structures. 5556 is not used to weld heat-treatable airframe alloys such as 2024 or 7075, which are joined mechanically, and it is not chosen for parts that run hot.

Compared with ER5356, ER5556 gives higher as-welded tensile and shear strength. Compared with ER5183, it carries slightly more manganese and a deliberate titanium addition, which refines the weld grain and helps resist hot cracking.

Good practice

Storage and welding tips

Storage

Keep spools sealed until use, in a dry store at stable temperature. Moisture and oxide on the wire are the main causes of porosity.

Preparation

Remove oxide from joint faces with a stainless brush or solvent clean just before welding.

Feeding

Use U-groove drive rolls, a PTFE or nylon liner and the correct contact tip to avoid shaving the soft wire.

Shielding

Argon or argon-helium mixtures; helium additions give deeper penetration on thick plate.

FAQ

5556 aluminium (aluminum) welding wire: FAQs

What is the difference between 5556 and 5556A?

They are the same alloy family. ER5556 is the AWS classification; S Al 5556A is the ISO 18273 classification, with a slightly tighter magnesium and manganese range.

When should I use ER5556 instead of ER5183?

When you need the highest possible as-welded strength, for example on 5456 plate or weldable 7xxx alloys. For most 5083 work ER5183 is sufficient.

Can 5556 be used to weld 6061?

It can, and it gives good strength, but ER4043 or ER5356 is more common on 6061. ER5556 should not be used where the joint will see service above 65 °C.

Is 5556 wire covered by an AMS specification?

Aluminium filler wire is normally bought to AWS A5.10 or ISO 18273. Note that AMS 4190 covers a 5.2 % silicon wire (4043), not 5556, so it should not be cited for 5556.

How should 5556 wire be stored?

In its sealed pack in a dry, temperature-stable store. Once opened, keep spools covered and use them promptly to avoid porosity from surface moisture and oxide.
