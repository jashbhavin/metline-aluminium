7150 Aerospace Aluminium Plate

7150 is a high-strength, high-purity Al-Zn-Mg-Cu-Zr plate alloy developed from 7050 for compression-loaded aircraft structure such as upper wing skins and spar caps. Metline supplies 7150 plate in the T7751 and T6151 tempers to SAE AMS 4252 and AMS 4306, with full mill certification, to aerospace manufacturers and MRO shops worldwide.

Overview

What 7150 plate is and why it exists

Upper wing skins and stringers are loaded mainly in compression as the wing bends upward in flight. For those parts compressive yield strength matters more than damage tolerance, so the industry developed 7150: 7050 chemistry with slightly more zinc and magnesium and tighter iron and silicon limits, giving more strength without losing toughness.

7150 uses zirconium rather than chromium for grain control, like 7050. Zirconium keeps the plate unrecrystallised and makes it less sensitive to quench rate, so plate several centimetres thick still reaches high strength in the centre. Low iron and silicon reduce the coarse particles that start fatigue cracks and lower toughness.

Two tempers matter. T6151 is the peak-aged, stretched condition introduced first and used on upper-wing structure of jet airliners. T7751 came later: a three-step ageing treatment that keeps strength close to T6151 while raising exfoliation and stress-corrosion resistance. Most current designs and many replacement parts use T7751.

Typical 7150 plate parts

  • Upper wing skins and integrally machined skin panels
  • Upper spar caps and upper wing stringers machined from plate
  • Fuselage keel beams, floor beams and frames that are compression-critical
  • Repair and spares plate for aircraft whose type design names 7150

Tempers

T7751 or T6151?

7150-T7751

Solution heat-treated, stretched 1.5 – 3 % and aged by the three-step T77 process. AMS 4252 covers plate 0.250 – 3.000 in (6.35 – 76.2 mm). Choose it for new designs and for parts exposed to moisture.

7150-T6151

Solution heat-treated, stretched and artificially aged to near-peak strength. Covered by AMS 4306. Used where the drawing calls for it; exfoliation resistance is lower than T7751.

Why T77 matters

T77 tempers use a three-step ageing cycle (age, brief high-temperature retrogression, re-age). The result keeps strength close to peak-aged T6 while giving exfoliation and stress-corrosion resistance close to the over-aged T76 condition.

Tempers not used in aerospace

Old data sheets mention 7150 in O or T651 for general engineering. These are not aerospace plate tempers; for general machining plate see our 7075 or 7050 plate pages instead.

Working with 7150

Machining, forming and protection

Machining

Machines like 7050 and 7075 plate. Stretched tempers stay flat when skins and spar caps are pocketed from thick plate.

Forming

Wing skins are often shot-peen formed or age-formed to contour. Bend radii must be generous because elongation is about 10 %.

Joining

Not fusion-welded. Riveted or bolted; friction stir welding is used only in qualified applications.

Protection

Anodise or conversion coat, prime and seal. Keep heat exposure below about 120 °C to avoid further ageing.

How to order 7150 plate

  1. SpecifyAlloy and temper, thickness, width and length or cut sizes, quantity and the governing specification with its revision letter (AMS 4252 or AMS 4306, plus any OEM specification). Add any customer or prime-contractor material specification that sits on top of the AMS.
  2. ConfirmWe confirm the specification can be met, the tolerance class, any extra tests (ultrasonic class, conductivity, fracture toughness, grain flow) and the delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP).
  3. InspectMaterial is checked for dimensions, surface condition and marking; tests named on the order are carried out or arranged with an independent laboratory.
  4. DocumentEach lot ships with a mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 giving chemistry, mechanical results and heat-treatment lot, traceable to the cast number.
  5. Pack and shipExport packing in sealed, moisture-protected wooden cases or on pallets, shipped by sea or air to your port or door.

FAQ

7150 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQ

What is 7150 plate used for?

Mainly compression-critical aircraft structure: upper wing skins, upper spar caps and stringers, and some fuselage keel and floor beams.

What is the difference between 7150-T7751 and T6151?

Both are stretched and aged. T6151 is aged close to peak strength. T7751 uses a three-step ageing cycle that keeps similar strength but gives better exfoliation and stress-corrosion resistance, so it is preferred for new parts.

How does 7150 compare with 7050?

7150 has slightly higher zinc and magnesium and tighter iron and silicon limits. It is stronger, especially in compression, while 7050 offers higher toughness and SCC resistance and is used for lower wing and thick fittings.

Which specification covers 7150-T7751 plate?

SAE AMS 4252, for plate 0.250 to 3.000 in (6.35 to 76.2 mm) thick. Airframe makers often add their own material specification.

Is 7150 plate weldable?

Not by arc welding. Parts are riveted or bolted.

Can you supply cut pieces of 7150 plate for repairs?

Yes. We can supply saw-cut blanks with the original mill certificate, shipped worldwide from Mumbai.
