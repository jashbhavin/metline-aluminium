2219 Aerospace Aluminium Extrusions (T8511 & T3511)

2219 is the aluminium-copper alloy aerospace engineers choose when an extruded part must be welded, must hold its strength at up to about 300 °C, or must stay tough at cryogenic temperatures. Overview

Why 2219 is used for aerospace extrusions

2219 carries about 6.3 % copper, far more than 2024 or 2014, together with small additions of manganese, zirconium, vanadium and titanium. The copper produces a dense population of strengthening precipitates that stay stable when the metal is warm, while zirconium and vanadium hold a fine grain structure through extrusion and heat treatment.

Three properties set it apart from other high-strength aluminium. First, it is one of the few 2xxx alloys that can be fusion welded with sound, crack-free joints, using the matching 2319 filler. Second, it keeps a useful share of its room-temperature strength after long exposure at 150 – 300 °C, where 7xxx alloys over-age quickly. Third, its strength and toughness actually rise as temperature falls, which is why it has been used for decades in welded tanks for liquid oxygen and liquid hydrogen.

The price of these advantages is modest room-temperature strength compared with 7075 or 7050, a slightly higher density (2.84 g/cm³) and poor resistance to corrosion if left unprotected. In an airframe or launch vehicle, 2219 extrusions are therefore used where welding, heat or cold matter more than ultimate strength: stiffeners and rings for welded tanks, engine-bay structure, frames near hot zones, and supersonic airframe members.

When 2219 extrusions are the right call

  • The extrusion will be welded into an assembly (tank stringers, rings, frames)
  • Service temperature regularly exceeds about 120 °C, where 2024 and 7xxx lose strength
  • The part sees cryogenic service and must stay ductile and tough
  • You need stress-relieved, stretched sections that machine without distortion

Mechanical and physical properties of 2219 extrusions

The two aerospace tempers behave very differently. T3511 is solution heat-treated, stretched and naturally aged; it is the softer, more ductile condition, often bought by fabricators who will form the section and then age it themselves. T8511 is stretched and then artificially aged, which roughly adds a third to the yield strength and gives the stable structure needed for elevated-temperature service.

Working with 2219

Welding, machining, forming and finishing

Welding

GTAW (TIG), GMAW (MIG), plasma, electron-beam and friction-stir welding are all used. Fusion welds use 2319 filler, which matches the base chemistry and adds titanium to refine weld grain. Welding in T8511 and leaving the assembly as welded is common; the heat-affected zone softens, so allowable stresses are reduced locally.

Friction-stir welding

2219 was one of the first alloys welded by FSW in launch-vehicle tank production, because the solid-state process avoids porosity and keeps more joint strength than fusion welding.

Machining

Good in T8511: chips break cleanly and surface finish is fine with sharp carbide tooling. T3511 is gummier. Stretched tempers stay straight when material is removed asymmetrically.

Forming

Form in O or freshly quenched condition, then age to T62 or T81. Bending fully aged T8511 sections needs generous radii.

Corrosion protection

Like other high-copper alloys, 2219 needs protection: chromic or sulphuric acid anodising, chemical conversion coating and primer, or paint systems to your process specification.

Heat treatment

Solution treatment is around 530 – 540 °C followed by a fast quench; artificial ageing around 175 – 190 °C produces the T8 tempers. Follow AMS 2770 or your approved heat-treat specification.

Applications

Where 2219 extrusions are used in aerospace

  • Launch-vehicle propellant tanks: stringers, rings and Y-ring stock welded into liquid-oxygen and liquid-hydrogen tanks
  • Engine-bay and nacelle structure: frames and brackets near hot zones where 7xxx alloys would over-age
  • Supersonic and high-speed airframes: members that see aerodynamic heating in service
  • Fuselage frames and stiffeners: welded or machined sections that need stable stretched stock
  • Satellite and spacecraft structure: brackets, struts and interface rings that cycle between hot and cold
  • Missile bodies and fins: welded and heat-exposed sections in defence programmes
  • Cryogenic ground equipment: transfer lines and storage vessels that must remain tough below −200 °C

A typical welded tank build uses T8511 extruded stiffeners friction-stir welded to 2219 plate, with 2319 filler used for any fusion repairs, then a conversion coating and primer before final assembly.

FAQ

2219 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) extrusions: FAQ

What is the difference between 2219-T3511 and 2219-T8511?

Both are solution heat-treated, stretched to relieve quench stress and straightened. T3511 is then naturally aged and stays softer and more ductile; T8511 is artificially aged for higher yield strength and a stable structure at elevated temperature. AMS 4163 covers T3511 and AMS 4162 covers T8511.

Can 2219 extrusions be welded?

Yes. 2219 is one of the most weldable 2xxx alloys. Use 2319 filler for TIG or MIG welding; friction-stir welding is also widely used. The heat-affected zone loses some strength, so designs use reduced allowables at welds or re-heat-treat the assembly.

Up to what temperature can 2219 be used?

It keeps useful strength for long exposures up to roughly 250 – 300 °C, better than 2024 and much better than 7xxx alloys. It also performs very well at cryogenic temperatures.

Does 2219 need corrosion protection?

Yes. The high copper content makes bare 2219 susceptible to corrosion, so aerospace parts are anodised or conversion coated and painted to the programme process specification.

What is the density of 2219?

About 2.84 g/cm³, slightly heavier than 2024 (2.78) and 7075 (2.81), which is worth accounting for in weight-critical designs.

Do you supply 2219 extrusions outside India?

Yes. Metline ships 2219 extrusions worldwide with EN 10204 3.1 certificates, export packing and sea or air freight on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
