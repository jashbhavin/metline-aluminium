7050 Aerospace Aluminium Extrusions & Profiles

7050 extrusions are used for stringers, spar caps, floor beams, seat tracks and frame members where an airframe needs high strength, good toughness and resistance to stress corrosion in one long, stiff section. They are supplied stretched and overaged in T76511, T74511 or T73511 to AMS 4340, AMS 4342 and AMS 4341. Metline supplies 7050 profiles, angles, tees, channels and extruded bar with full certification to aerospace buyers worldwide.

Overview

The role of 7050 extrusions in an airframe

Long structural members in wings and fuselages, such as stringers, spar caps, frame chords, floor beams and seat tracks, are most efficiently made as extrusions: the cross-section is formed in one pass, and only local machining is needed. These members carry high axial loads and fatigue cycles for decades, so the alloy must be strong, tough and corrosion resistant along its whole length.

7050 meets these needs because its zirconium-controlled grain structure keeps strength through thick flanges and makes it far less quench sensitive than 7075. In the stretched and overaged T7xxx11 tempers it resists stress-corrosion cracking and exfoliation, which is critical for floor beams and seat tracks exposed to spills and condensation.

Compared with 7075-T6511 extrusions, 7050-T76511 gives similar or higher strength in thicker sections with much better exfoliation resistance, and 7050-T74511 gives strength well above 7075-T73511 with comparable stress-corrosion performance. That is why 7050 has displaced 7075 for many extruded parts on current aircraft.

Tempers

Understanding the 7050 extrusion tempers

In a temper such as T76511, "T76" is the ageing condition, "5" means stress relieved by stretching, the next "1" means a controlled permanent set of about 1 – 3 %, and the final "1" means the product was straightened after stretching. T76510 and T74510 are the same without straightening.

T76 / T7651 (moderately overaged)

Aged past peak to gain good exfoliation-corrosion resistance and moderate stress-corrosion resistance, with a strength penalty of only a few per cent.

T74 / T7451 (overaged)

A balance between strength and stress-corrosion resistance. The usual choice for thick 7050 plate, bar and forgings in wing and fuselage structure.

T73 / T7351 (fully overaged)

The highest resistance to stress-corrosion cracking of the 7xxx tempers, at roughly 10 – 15 % lower strength than T6. Chosen for short-transverse loaded fittings and parts in wet areas.

O (annealed) and F (as extruded) conditions are used only for material that will be heat-treated later. 7050 extrusions are not supplied in T1 or T3 tempers for aerospace use.

Working with 7050

Machining, joining and finishing

Machining

Machines well, with short chips. Stretched tempers stay straight when flanges are milled or pockets cut. Use sharp carbide and flood coolant.

Joining

Not weldable by MIG or TIG; earlier claims of good weldability for 7050 are wrong. Join with rivets, bolts or adhesive bonding.

Forming

Limited in the overaged tempers. Stringers needing contour are stretch-formed or formed before final ageing under controlled processes.

Corrosion protection

Anodise or apply conversion coating and primer; seal faying surfaces and fasteners in wet areas such as galleys and lavatories.

Applications

Where 7050 extrusions are used

  • Wing: stringers, spar caps and rib chords
  • Fuselage: stringers, frame chords and longerons
  • Floor structure: floor beams and seat tracks, often T74511 or T73511 for corrosion resistance
  • Cargo systems: cargo-floor rails and roller-track supports
  • Helicopters and business jets: frames, beams and stiffeners
  • Defence and space: missile body members and launch-vehicle stiffeners

  2. ConfirmWe confirm the specification can be met, the tolerance class, any extra tests (ultrasonic class, conductivity, fracture toughness) and the delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP).
  3. InspectMaterial is checked for dimensions, straightness or flatness and surface condition; tests named on the order are carried out or arranged with an independent laboratory.
  4. DocumentEach lot ships with a mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 showing chemistry, tensile results and conductivity where specified, traceable to the cast and lot number.

FAQ

7050 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) extrusions: FAQ

Which temper should I order for 7050 extrusions?

Use the temper on the drawing. T76511 gives the highest strength, T74511 balances strength with stress-corrosion resistance, and T73511 gives the most stress-corrosion resistance.

Can 7050 extrusions be welded?

No, not by conventional arc welding. The alloy cracks and loses strength in the weld zone. Use rivets, bolts or bonding.

What is the maximum size of a 7050 extrusion?

AMS 4340 covers sections up to 5 in (127 mm) in least thickness or diameter. The overall size of a profile is limited by the die and press used.

Why is 7050 preferred to 7075 for seat tracks and floor beams?

In T74511 or T73511, 7050 resists stress corrosion and exfoliation much better than 7075-T6511 while keeping high strength, which suits areas exposed to moisture and spills.

Do you supply standard angles as well as custom profiles?

Yes. Common angles, tees and bars can be supplied, and custom aerospace sections are produced to drawing.
