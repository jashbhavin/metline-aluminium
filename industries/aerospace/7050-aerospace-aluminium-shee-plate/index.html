7050 Aerospace Aluminium Plate (T7451 / T7651)

7050-T7451 is the standard thick plate for machined aircraft structure: wing spars and ribs, fuselage frames and bulkheads, and large fittings cut from plates up to about 150 mm thick and more. It combines high strength with good fracture toughness and stress-corrosion resistance through the full thickness. Metline supplies 7050 plate in T7451 (AMS 4050) and T7651 (AMS 4201), cut to size and certified, to aerospace buyers worldwide.

Overview

Why 7050-T7451 became the default thick aerospace plate

Modern airframes are increasingly machined from solid plate: a single wing rib or fuselage frame can start as a plate weighing hundreds of kilograms and end as a part weighing a tenth of that. The plate must be strong in the centre as well as at the surface, stay flat as most of it is machined away, and resist stress-corrosion cracking across its thickness. 7050-T7451 was designed to do all three.

Two features make it work. Zirconium, in place of the chromium used in 7075, forms fine dispersoids that keep the grain structure unrecrystallised and make the alloy far less sensitive to quench rate, so the centre of a thick plate still reaches high strength. The T7451 temper then stretches the plate to remove quench stresses and overages it to a condition that balances strength against resistance to stress corrosion and exfoliation.

Low iron and silicon limits (0.15 % and 0.12 % maximum) give 7050 better fracture toughness and fatigue performance than 7075. For these reasons 7050-T7451 plate is used on most current commercial and military aircraft for machined internal structure, while 7075 plate is now chosen mainly for thinner, less critical parts or where cost dominates.

Key properties at a glance

  • Strength close to 7075-T651 in thin plate, and higher than 7075 in thick plate
  • Good plane-strain fracture toughness, with minimum values set by thickness and direction in AMS 4050
  • Stress-corrosion and exfoliation resistance verified by testing and conductivity checks
  • Low residual stress after stretching, so plate stays flat during heavy machining

Mechanical & physical properties

Mechanical properties of 7050 plate

Typical values describe material of moderate thickness. AMS 4050 and AMS 4201 set minimum tensile, yield and elongation values in the longitudinal, long-transverse and short-transverse directions, and those minimums fall as plate thickness rises. Design allowables come from those specified values, not from typical data.

TemperTensile strength (typical)Yield strength (typical)ElongationHardness

Short-transverse properties matter most for thick plate. Strength and ductility through the thickness are lower than in the rolling direction, so parts with loads across the plate thickness (lugs, fittings, frames with integral attachments) are designed to short-transverse allowables, and the T7451 temper is chosen to keep stress-corrosion risk low in that direction.

Tempers

T7451 versus T7651 plate

7050-T7451 (AMS 4050)

Solution heat-treated, stretched about 1.5 – 3 % and overaged. The usual temper for thick plate, giving good stress-corrosion resistance in the short-transverse direction. Covers plate across a very wide thickness range.

7050-T7651 (AMS 4201)

Stretched and moderately overaged for higher strength with good exfoliation resistance. AMS 4201 covers plate 6.35 – 76.2 mm (0.250 – 3.000 in) thick. Often used for wing skins and parts loaded in the plane of the plate.

Other tempers

F and O are supply conditions for later heat treatment. T7452 belongs to hand forgings rather than plate, and older designs may call T73 or T7351 in other alloys.

Working with 7050

Machining and processing 7050 plate

High-speed machining

Machines very well with sharp carbide tools at high spindle speeds. Large pocketing operations are routine; the stretched temper keeps distortion low, though removing more than about 90 % of the plate from one side may still need balanced roughing.

Distortion control

Rough both faces symmetrically, leave stock for a finishing pass after a rest period, and clamp lightly on the final cut. These steps matter more as part thickness falls.

Joining

Not suitable for arc welding. Assemblies are fastened, riveted or bonded. Friction stir welding is used in some aerospace and space structures after qualification.

Forming

Limited cold formability in T7451. Creep forming or age forming is used for curved wing panels; small bends should use generous radii.

Surface treatment

Chromic, sulphuric or boric-sulphuric anodising, or conversion coating and primer. Shot peening is often added to fatigue-critical surfaces.

Temperature

Keep service temperature below about 120 °C to avoid further overageing and loss of strength.

Applications

Where 7050 plate is used

  • Wing structure: spars, ribs, bulkheads and integrally stiffened panels
  • Fuselage: frames, floor beams, bulkheads and door surrounds machined from thick plate
  • Fittings: wing-to-body, engine-mount and landing-gear support fittings
  • Helicopters: transmission decks, frames and rotor-support structure
  • Space: launch-vehicle rings, adaptors and machined panels
  • Defence and tooling: armour-adjacent structure and high-strength jigs and moulds

A typical example is a machined wing rib: a 100 mm 7050-T7451 plate is ultrasonically inspected, nested and saw-cut into blanks, then high-speed machined to a pocketed part with webs a few millimetres thick, shot peened and anodised before assembly.

FAQ

7050 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQ

What does T7451 mean on 7050 plate?

The plate has been solution heat-treated, stretched to relieve quench stresses (the 51) and overaged (the T74) to balance strength against stress-corrosion resistance. It is the standard temper for thick 7050 plate.

Is 7050 plate stronger than 7075 plate?

In thin plate, 7075-T651 is slightly stronger. In thick plate, and against the corrosion-resistant 7075-T7351 temper, 7050-T7451 is stronger and tougher because it is much less quench sensitive.

Which specification covers 7050-T7451 plate?

AMS 4050. AMS 4201 covers T7651 plate and ASTM B209 covers sheet and plate in general. Aircraft manufacturers often add their own specifications on top.

Can 7050 plate be welded?

Not by conventional arc welding. It is joined by fasteners, rivets or bonding. Friction stir welding is used in some structures after qualification.

How much does a 7050 plate weigh?

About 2.83 kg per m² per millimetre of thickness. A 1525 × 3660 × 50.8 mm plate weighs about 802 kg.

Do you cut 7050 plate to size?

Yes. Plate can be saw-cut into blanks to your nesting, with ultrasonic inspection and full certification, and shipped worldwide in export cases.
