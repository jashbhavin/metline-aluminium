3003 Aerospace Aluminium Sheet & Plate

3003 is the manganese-alloyed aluminium used in aircraft for formed, non-structural sheet parts: fairings, ducting, tanks, liners and interior panels. It is soft and very formable in O temper (AMS 4006), moderately stiff in H14 (AMS 4008), welds and brazes easily and resists corrosion well. Metline supplies 3003 sheet and plate to AMS and ASTM B209 with mill certificates, worldwide.

Overview

Where 3003 sheet fits in an aircraft

3003 adds about 1.2 % manganese and a trace of copper to commercially pure aluminium. The result is roughly 20 % stronger than 1100 while keeping almost the same formability, corrosion resistance and weldability.

The alloy cannot be strengthened by heat treatment. Its strength comes from cold rolling, which is why it is ordered by H temper: O is fully annealed and very soft, H14 is half hard, and H18 is full hard. That simple behaviour makes 3003 easy to work with in a sheet-metal shop. It stretches, spins, draws and bends without cracking, it does not need to be quenched after forming, and it does not age-harden on the shelf.

In aerospace, 3003 sits well away from primary structure. Its strength (about 110 MPa annealed and 150 MPa in H14) is a fraction of 2024 or 7075, so it is not used where a part carries flight loads. It is used where a part must be formed into a complex shape, survive vibration, resist corrosion and stay light: wing-to-body and flap-track fairings, engine cowl liners, air-conditioning and bleed-air ducting, fuel, oil and water tanks, galley and lavatory panels, heat-exchanger parts and the foil used for non-structural honeycomb core.

When 3003 is the right aerospace sheet

  • The part is deep-drawn, spun, stretched or bent to a tight radius
  • It will be welded or brazed and must not lose strength in a heat-treatment sense
  • Loads are low and corrosion resistance and formability matter more than strength
  • The drawing calls up AMS 4006, AMS 4008 or ASTM B209 3003

Tempers

Choosing the 3003 temper for an aircraft part

3003-O (AMS 4006)

Fully annealed. The choice for deep drawing, spinning, stretch forming and hydroforming of fairings, tank ends and duct transitions. Parts work-harden slightly as they are formed.

3003-H14 (AMS 4008)

Half hard and the most widely stocked temper. Stiff enough to hold shape for panels, covers, shrouds and brackets, while still bending to about 1 × thickness in thin gauges.

3003-H12

Quarter hard. A useful middle point when O is too soft to handle and H14 cracks on the tightest bends.

3003-H16 / H18

Three-quarter and full hard. Used for flat or lightly formed parts where denting resistance matters, and as feedstock for honeycomb foil and fin stock.

3003-H22 / H24 / H26

Rolled harder and then partially annealed back. Strength similar to H12 / H14 / H16 with more elongation, which helps when the part is bent after cutting.

3003-H112

As-rolled plate above about 6 mm, with only minimum properties specified. Used for machined or welded tank and fixture parts.

Quality

Tolerances, surface and inspection

Thickness, width, length and flatness tolerances follow the tables of the order specification: ANSI H35.2 through ASTM B209 or the AMS document, or EN 485-4 for cold-rolled sheet on EN orders. Formed aerospace parts are sensitive to surface damage, so we check sheets visually and protect them with interleaving during packing.

  • Certificate: EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate with chemistry, tensile results and temper
  • Traceability: cast or lot number marked on each pack and carried onto the certificate
  • Surface: free of rolled-in debris, corrosion stains and scratches deeper than the specification allows
  • Flatness: to the standard tolerance, or tighter by agreement for panels that are bonded
  • Third-party inspection: available on request before shipment
Working with 3003

Forming, joining and finishing 3003 sheet

Forming

O temper bends flat on itself and draws deeply. H14 accepts bend radii of about 0 – 1 × t in thin sheet, rising with gauge. Anneal at about 345 – 415 °C to restore full ductility between severe operations.

Welding

Excellent by TIG and MIG. ER1100 or ER4043 filler are usual. The weld and heat-affected zone return to O-temper strength, which designers should allow for in H14 parts.

Brazing

One of the easiest alloys to braze. 3003 is the standard core for Al-Si clad brazing sheet in heat exchangers and ducting.

Riveting and bonding

Accepts blind and solid rivets and structural adhesives. Use sealant and isolation where 3003 meets carbon-fibre or steel.

Finishing

Takes chemical conversion coatings, primers and paint well. Anodised 3003 has a slightly grey tone compared with 1xxx or 6xxx alloys.

Corrosion

Very good resistance to atmosphere, fuel, oils and fresh water. No exfoliation or stress-corrosion concerns of the kind that affect 2xxx and 7xxx sheet.

Applications

Typical 3003 parts in aircraft and ground equipment

  • Fairings and covers: non-load-bearing fairings, access-panel covers and trailing-edge closures that are stretch formed or spun
  • Ducting: environmental-control and ventilation ducts, transitions and plenums formed and welded from O or H14 sheet
  • Tanks: potable water, hydraulic reservoir and auxiliary fuel tank shells where formability and weldability lead
  • Engine bay: cowl liners, baffles and heat shields away from the hottest zones
  • Cabin interiors: galley, lavatory and cabinet panels, trim and closeouts
  • Heat exchangers: fins, headers and brazed assemblies in oil and air coolers
  • Honeycomb: 3003 foil core for non-structural interior and floor panels
  • Ground support: enclosures, steps, toolboxes and covers on tugs, carts and stands

Note what is not on the list: skins, frames, stringers and fittings that carry flight loads. Those use heat-treatable 2xxx and 7xxx alloys, or 6xxx in some newer designs.

FAQ

3003 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) sheet: buyer questions

Is 3003 aluminium used for aircraft skins?

Not for load-carrying skins. 3003 is far weaker than the 2024 and 7075 sheet used for fuselage and wing skins. It is used for formed, non-structural panels, fairings, ducts, tanks and interior parts.

What is the difference between AMS 4006 and AMS 4008?

Both cover 3003 sheet and plate. AMS 4006 is the annealed O temper for heavy forming; AMS 4008 is H14, the half-hard temper for panels and covers that must hold their shape.

Can 3003 be heat-treated to increase its strength?

No. 3003 is non-heat-treatable. Strength comes from cold work (the H tempers), and annealing returns it to O. Any listing that offers 3003 in T tempers is wrong.

How much does a 48 × 96 inch sheet of 0.063 in 3003 weigh?

About 12.6 kg (roughly 27.8 lb), using a density of 2.73 g/cm³.

What filler should I use to weld 3003?

ER1100 gives the closest colour and corrosion match; ER4043 flows better and resists cracking. Both are standard for 3003 tanks and ducts.

Do you ship 3003 aerospace sheet internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies customers worldwide from Mumbai, with EN 10204 3.1 certificates, interleaved export packing and sea or air freight.
