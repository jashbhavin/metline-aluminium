Aluminium-lithium aerospace sheet

8090 Aluminium-Lithium Sheet & Plate

8090 is a second-generation aluminium-lithium alloy with about 2.4 % lithium, 1.3 % copper and 1 % magnesium. Lithium lowers density to about 2.55 g/cm³ and raises stiffness by around 10 %, so 8090 parts weigh noticeably less than 2024 or 7075 equivalents. Overview

What is 8090 aluminium-lithium?

Lithium is the lightest metal. Every 1 % of lithium added to aluminium cuts density by about 3 % and raises the elastic modulus by about 6 %. 8090 uses roughly 2.4 % lithium, so it is around 8 – 10 % lighter and about 10 % stiffer than conventional 2xxx and 7xxx aerospace alloys.

8090 was developed in Europe in the 1980s as a drop-in replacement for 2024 and 7075 in skins, frames and stringers. It flew on helicopter and military aircraft programmes, but its anisotropy, low short-transverse toughness and high cost limited wider use. Third-generation Al-Li alloys such as 2050, 2195, 2196 and 2099, with lower lithium and better toughness, have since taken over most new designs.

Today 8090 is mainly bought for existing platforms, repairs, spares and research. Availability is limited and material is often supplied from specific mill runs, so early enquiry is important.

Corrections to earlier information

  • Density is about 2.55 g/cm³, not 2.81 g/cm³; low density is the whole reason for choosing the alloy
  • Service temperature is similar to other aged aluminium alloys; claims of 200 – 230 °C continuous use are not supported
  • Fusion welding is possible only with special care; 8090 is not a general-purpose weldable grade

Working with 8090

Forming, machining and joining

Forming

Form in T3/T4 or solution-treated condition, then age. Some 8090 variants were developed for superplastic forming of complex panels.

Machining

Similar to 2024; keep swarf separate from other alloys, because lithium-bearing scrap must be recycled separately.

Joining

Usually riveted. Fusion welding is possible with careful surface preparation to avoid porosity, but is rarely used on airframes.

Anisotropy

Properties vary noticeably with direction, and short-transverse toughness is low. Orient parts so that the main load lies in the rolling direction.

Corrosion

Generally comparable to 2024; protect with conversion coatings, primers and paint as normal for airframe parts.

Swarf safety

Fine Al-Li chips are reactive; store dry and handle under normal light-metal fire precautions.

Applications

Applications of 8090

  • Fuselage skins, frames and stringers on helicopters and military aircraft
  • Wing leading edges and secondary structures where stiffness limits design
  • Spacecraft and satellite structures needing high specific stiffness
  • Spares and repair material for platforms originally built in 8090

FAQ

8090 aluminium-lithium (aluminum) sheet: frequently asked questions

What is the density of 8090?

About 2.54 – 2.55 g/cm³, roughly 8 – 10 % lower than 2024 or 7075.

Is 8090 still used on new aircraft?

Rarely. New designs mostly use third-generation Al-Li alloys such as 2050, 2195, 2196 and 2099. 8090 is mainly bought for existing platforms, spares and research.

What does AMS 4259 cover?

Sheet in 8090-T6, solution and precipitation heat treated, with an unrecrystallised structure.

Can 8090 be welded?

It can be fusion welded with careful preparation, but porosity is a risk and airframe parts are normally riveted.

Does 8090 scrap need special handling?

Yes. Lithium-bearing scrap must be kept separate from other aluminium scrap so it can be recycled correctly.
