4032 Aerospace Aluminium Bar & Rod

4032 is a wrought aluminium alloy with about 12 % silicon plus copper, magnesium and nickel. It has the lowest thermal expansion and some of the best wear resistance of any wrought aluminium, which is why it is machined into pistons, bushes, valve parts and swashplates. Metline supplies 4032 bar to AMS 4318 (T86) and AMS 4319 (T651), worldwide.

Overview

Why 4032 bar is chosen

Most aluminium alloys expand about twice as much as steel when heated. 4032's high silicon content cuts that expansion noticeably, adds hard silicon particles that resist wear, and keeps useful strength at moderately raised temperatures.

Those three properties make 4032 the classic wrought piston alloy. A piston that expands less can run with tighter clearances in its bore, so it is quieter, seals better and wears less. The same logic applies to hydraulic and pneumatic parts, compressor components, bearing cages and bushes that run against steel or cast iron and see temperature changes.

In aerospace, 4032 bar is used for piston-engine pistons in general aviation, hydraulic pump pistons and swashplates, actuator and valve components, compressor parts and wear sleeves. It is not a structural airframe alloy, and claims that 4032 bar is used for wings, fuselages or turbine parts are not correct.

4032 in brief

  • Lowest thermal expansion of the common wrought aluminium alloys
  • Good wear resistance from primary silicon particles
  • Heat-treatable to T6-level strength: about 380 MPa tensile typical
  • Machines well with carbide or PCD tools; abrasive to high-speed steel

Working with 4032

Machining, finishing and joining

Machining

Machines to a good finish with short chips, but the silicon particles wear tools quickly. Use carbide or PCD tooling, rigid set-ups and flood coolant.

Anodising

Hard anodising is possible but the film is grey and less uniform than on low-silicon alloys; test before committing to a finish.

Welding

Not normally welded in service parts; the high silicon and heat-treated condition make fusion welding unattractive.

Dimensional stability

Specify T651 for close-tolerance parts and rough-machine before final finishing for best stability.

FAQ

4032 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) bar: questions

What is 4032 aluminium used for?

Pistons, hydraulic and pneumatic components, swashplates, bushes and other wear parts where low thermal expansion and wear resistance matter.

What is the difference between 4032-T86 and T651?

T86 includes a cold-work step before ageing and is covered by AMS 4318 for small diameters; T651 is stretched for stress relief and covered by AMS 4319 for larger diameters. Strength is similar; T651 is more stable when machined.

Why does 4032 contain nickel?

Nickel, with copper, helps 4032 keep strength at the moderately raised temperatures pistons run at.

Is 4032 easy to machine?

It gives a good finish and short chips, but its silicon is abrasive. Carbide or PCD tools are recommended.

Do you ship 4032 bar worldwide?

Yes. Metline supplies 4032 bar from Mumbai to customers worldwide with mill certificates.
