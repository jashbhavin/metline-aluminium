5056 Aerospace Aluminium Wire

5056 is a 5 % magnesium aluminium alloy drawn into wire for aircraft rivets, especially rivets used to join magnesium alloy parts, and for other cold-headed fasteners, mesh and cable armour. It is supplied annealed (5056-O) to AMS 4182 and to rivet-wire specifications such as QQ-A-430. Metline supplies 5056 wire in coils or straight lengths with mill certificates, worldwide.

Overview

What 5056 wire is used for

5056 has the highest magnesium content of the common wrought 5xxx alloys, about 5 %. That gives it high strength from cold work alone, good corrosion resistance and, importantly for aircraft builders, good galvanic compatibility with magnesium alloys.

The main aerospace use is rivet wire. Rivets headed from 5056 wire carry the code letter B in AN and MS rivet standards and are identified by a raised cross on the head. They are specified mainly for joining magnesium alloy structure, where aluminium-copper rivets such as 2117 or 2017 would set up a strong galvanic couple and corrode the surrounding magnesium. 5056 rivets are driven as received, without the solution heat treatment and ice-box storage that 2017 and 2024 rivets need.

Beyond rivets, 5056 wire is cold headed into pins and small fasteners, woven into screens, used as armour and sheathing on cables, and drawn into the foil used for high-strength honeycomb core.

Fabrication

Heading, corrosion and temperature limits

Cold heading

Annealed 5056 heads well but work-hardens quickly; keep heading speeds and die clearances matched to the wire size.

Magnesium joints

Use 5056 rivets with primed or sealed faying surfaces; never 2xxx rivets in magnesium structure.

Temperature

With more than 3 % magnesium, 5056 can become sensitised to intergranular and stress corrosion after long exposure above about 65 °C. Keep it out of hot zones.

5056 wire should not be confused with ER5356 welding wire. The two compositions are similar, but welding filler is made to AWS A5.10 with titanium additions and cleanliness requirements for welding, while 5056 rivet wire is made for heading and shear performance.

FAQ

5056 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) wire: FAQs

Why are 5056 rivets used with magnesium?

5056 contains almost no copper and sits close to magnesium in the galvanic series, so it causes far less corrosion in magnesium structure than copper-bearing 2xxx rivets.

Does 5056 rivet wire need heat treatment before driving?

No. 5056 is not heat treatable; rivets are driven as received, unlike 2017 and 2024 rivets that must be solution treated and kept cold.

How are 5056 rivets identified?

In AN and MS rivet standards the material code is B, and the head carries a raised cross.

What is the chemical composition of 5056?

Magnesium 4.5 – 5.6 %, manganese 0.05 – 0.20 %, chromium 0.05 – 0.20 %, copper 0.10 % max, balance aluminium.

Can 5056 be used at high temperature?

Avoid long service above about 65 °C, where alloys with more than 3 % magnesium can become sensitised to intergranular corrosion.
