1050A Aerospace Aluminium Sheet and Plate

1050A is 99.5 % pure aluminium sheet, known in older British documents as 1B. In aircraft it is used for parts that must be deep drawn, spun, welded or kept free of corrosion while carrying little load: ducting, liners, fairing closures, shims, heat shields and interior panels. Overview

Where 1050A sheet fits in an aircraft

1050A is not a structural aerospace alloy, and nobody chooses it for strength. It is chosen because it forms further than almost any other aluminium sheet, welds without cracking and resists corrosion on its own, without cladding or heavy coatings.

Those qualities make it useful for the many secondary parts in an airframe and its systems: hot and cold air ducts that are spun or drawn, lightly loaded liners and covers, equipment closures, baffles, shims and packers, reflective heat shields and interior trim. On ground support equipment it is used for guards, panels and enclosures. Wherever a part carries real load, a 2xxx, 6xxx or 7xxx alloy, or at least a 3xxx or 5xxx grade, is the better choice.

The British designation 1B dates from BS 1470, which preceded the European EN 485 and EN 573 standards. Drawings from UK and Commonwealth programmes still use it, and 1B, 1050A and Al99.5 all describe the same material in practice.

Working with 1050A

Forming, welding and finishing

Forming

O temper can be bent flat on itself, deep drawn and spun. Work hardening during forming raises strength; anneal between stages for very deep draws.

Welding

Excellent by TIG and MIG with 1100 or 4043 filler. The heat-affected zone returns to O-temper strength.

Machining

Soft and gummy. Use sharp, polished, high-rake tools, high speed and plenty of lubricant to avoid built-up edge and torn surfaces.

Anodising

Gives a clear, bright film and is suitable for chemical brightening; useful for reflectors and heat shields.

Corrosion

Excellent in air and fresh water. Avoid direct contact with copper alloys and carbon fibre in damp conditions.

Handling

The surface marks easily; order PVC-filmed sheet for visible parts and handle with clean gloves.

FAQ

1050A aerospace aluminium (aluminum) sheet: FAQ

Is BS 1B the same as 1050A?

Yes. 1B was the BS 1470 designation for 99.5 % aluminium sheet. It was replaced by EN AW-1050A when the EN standards took over, and the two are used interchangeably on drawings.

Can 1050A be used for structural aircraft parts?

No. Its strength is too low. Use it for formed, lightly loaded parts such as ducts, liners, shims and covers.

Which temper is best for spinning or deep drawing?

O (or H111). It has the highest elongation and the lowest strength, so it flows into the tool without splitting.

What is the difference between H14 and H24 1050A?

Both have similar tensile ranges. H24 is hardened further and then partially annealed, which gives more elongation, so it bends better without cracking.

How much does a 1250 × 2500 × 2 mm sheet of 1050A weigh?

About 16.9 kg, using 2.70 kg per m² per mm of thickness.
