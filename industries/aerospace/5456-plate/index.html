5456 Aerospace Aluminium Sheet & Plate

5456 is the strongest of the commonly used non-heat-treatable aluminium alloys: about 5 % magnesium gives welded strength above 5083 while keeping excellent seawater resistance. It is used for welded tanks, armoured and protective structures and heavy ground equipment rather than airframes. Metline supplies 5456 sheet and plate in O, H111, H112, H116 and H321 to ASTM B209, ASTM B928 and legacy QQ-A-250/9, worldwide.

Overview

Where 5456 fits

5456 is close to 5083 in composition but carries a little more magnesium and manganese. The result is roughly 10 % higher strength in both parent metal and welds, which is why designers move to 5456 when a welded 5083 structure is too heavy.

In aerospace and defence, 5456 is used for welded fuel and liquid-propellant storage tanks on the ground, rugged transport containers and shelters, armour and blast-protective panels on vehicles and equipment (where it appears alongside 5083 in MIL-DTL-46027), heavy GSE and naval aviation deck equipment. It is not used for flight structures, where heat-treatable 2xxx and 7xxx alloys give far more strength per kilogram.

Like 5083 and 5086, 5456 must not be used for long periods above about 65 °C, because its high magnesium content can sensitise. For warm service choose 5454.

Working with 5456

Welding and fabrication

Welding

Use ER5556 or ER5183 filler to reach the required as-welded strength; ER5356 gives lower joint strength. Avoid 4xxx fillers.

Forming

Moderate. Use generous bend radii in H116 and H321 and form across the rolling direction; O temper for severe forming.

Service temperature

Keep below about 65 °C in continuous service to avoid sensitisation; never use for hot tanks or engine-area parts.

Machining

Fair; tends to be gummy. Sharp carbide tools with high rake and flood coolant give the best finish.

FAQ

5456 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) sheet: FAQs

Is 5456 stronger than 5083?

Yes. With more magnesium and manganese, 5456 is roughly 10 % stronger than 5083 in comparable tempers, both as plate and after welding.

Can 5456 be heat treated?

No. It is strengthened by cold work only. H116 and H321 are strain-hardened tempers with controlled processing, not heat-treated conditions.

What filler should I use to weld 5456?

ER5556 matches the parent strength best; ER5183 is also widely used. ER5356 gives a weaker joint.

Is 5456 used for aircraft fuel tanks?

Integral aircraft fuel tanks are part of the 2xxx and 7xxx wing structure. 5456 is used for welded ground storage and transport tanks.

Why is ASTM B928 important?

It requires H116 and H321 plate to pass exfoliation and intergranular corrosion tests, giving assurance for seawater and salt-spray service.
