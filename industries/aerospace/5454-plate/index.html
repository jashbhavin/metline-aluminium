5454 Aerospace Aluminium Sheet & Plate

5454 is the aluminium-magnesium alloy engineers choose when a welded, corrosion-resistant structure will run warm. With magnesium held below 3 %, it can work continuously above 65 °C, where 5083 and 5086 risk sensitisation. Metline supplies 5454 sheet and plate in O, H111, H112, H22/H32 and H24/H34 tempers to ASTM B209 and legacy QQ-A-250/10, to customers worldwide.

Overview

Why 5454 exists, and where it is used in aerospace

The strongest 5xxx alloys contain 4 – 5 % magnesium. That magnesium gives strength, but if the metal sits at moderately raised temperatures for long periods it can precipitate along grain boundaries and make the alloy vulnerable to intergranular and stress corrosion. 5454 was developed to avoid that: about 2.7 % magnesium with 0.5 – 1.0 % manganese gives the best strength available in an Al-Mg alloy that is safe for sustained warm service.

In aerospace, 5454 is a support-and-ground material rather than an airframe alloy. It is used for welded tanks and vessels that hold heated fluids, such as de-icing fluid heaters, hot-water and fuel-conditioning tanks; for ducting and enclosures near engines or exhausts on ground power units and test cells; for heat-exchanger shells; and for wheels, chassis members and bodies on airport vehicles and ground support equipment.

Its weldability is excellent, its strength is about 20 % higher than 5052, and it keeps good toughness. Where the structure always runs cool, 5083 or 5086 gives more strength; where it runs warm, 5454 is the safer specification.

Key reasons to specify 5454

  • Approved for continuous service above 65 °C, unlike 5083, 5086 and 5456
  • Excellent weldability with ER5554 or ER5356 filler
  • Good resistance to marine and industrial atmospheres
  • Higher strength than 5052 with similar formability in the O temper

Tempers

Choosing a 5454 temper

O / H111

Annealed for deep forming, spinning and dished tank heads. H111 carries a small amount of strain from levelling.

H112

As rolled with guaranteed minimums. The usual condition for thicker plate cut into flanges, bases and machined fittings.

H32 / H22

Quarter-hard. The standard temper for welded tank shells, ducting and panels; H22 is partly annealed and slightly more ductile.

H34 / H24

Half-hard. Higher strength for flat panels and bodies with gentle bends.

Not heat treatable

5454 cannot be solution treated and aged. Claims that it can be hardened by heat treatment are wrong; strength comes from cold work.

No H116 temper

Marine-tested H116 and H321 tempers under ASTM B928 apply to 5083, 5086 and 5456, not 5454, which does not need sensitisation testing.

Working with 5454

Welding, forming and service temperature

Welding

Weld with ER5554, which has a matching magnesium level and is recommended for warm service. ER5356 is acceptable for parts that run cool, as its 5 % magnesium deposit can sensitise above 65 °C.

Forming

O temper forms like 5052. H32 bends to about 1 – 1.5 × thickness in thin sheet; allow more for H34 and for plate.

Temperature

Suitable for continuous service up to roughly 150 °C, with strength falling gradually as temperature rises.

Machining

Fair; harder tempers machine more cleanly. Sharp, polished carbide tools and good coolant prevent built-up edge.

Applications

Typical aerospace and support uses

  • Heated tanks: de-icing fluid heaters, hot-water and fuel-conditioning tanks on GSE
  • Engine-area enclosures on ground equipment: ground power unit and air-start unit housings and ducts
  • Heat-exchanger shells and welded pressure vessels for test cells
  • Airport vehicles: wheels, chassis members, tanker and catering-truck bodies

A typical example is a de-icing truck: the heated fluid tank is welded from 5454-H32 plate with ER5554 filler, because the fluid is held warm for long periods and a 5083 tank would risk sensitisation.

FAQ

5454 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQs

Why choose 5454 instead of 5083?

Because it can run warm. 5083 has about 4.5 % magnesium and can become susceptible to intergranular and stress corrosion after long exposure above 65 °C. 5454, with under 3 % magnesium, is suitable for sustained elevated-temperature service.

Is 5454 heat treatable?

No. It is strengthened by cold work only. Heat can anneal it or stabilise it, not harden it.

Which filler wire should be used for 5454?

ER5554 is recommended, especially for warm service. ER5356 can be used for parts that stay below 65 °C.

Is 5454 used in aircraft structure?

Not in primary airframe structure. It is used in heated tanks, ground equipment, enclosures and vehicles that support aircraft operations.

What does QQ-A-250/10 cover?

It is the legacy US federal specification for 5454 plate and sheet. It has been superseded by ASTM B209, but older drawings still reference it.
