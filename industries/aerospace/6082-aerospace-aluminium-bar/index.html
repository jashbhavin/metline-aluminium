6082 Aerospace Aluminium Bar (L111, T6 / T6511)

6082 is the European structural workhorse among heat-treatable aluminium alloys and, as BS L111 and EN 2326, an established aerospace bar for fittings, brackets, ground equipment and machined parts. In T6 and T6511 it gives about 310 MPa minimum tensile strength with good machinability, weldability and corrosion resistance. Metline supplies 6082 round, square, hex and flat bar in O, T4, T6, T6510 and T6511 to aerospace and EN standards, worldwide.

Overview

Why 6082 bar is used in aerospace

6082 contains more silicon and manganese than 6061 and almost no copper. The extra silicon raises aged strength; manganese controls grain structure in extrusions. The result is a bar with slightly higher strength than 6061 in most sizes and better resistance to corrosion.

British and European aerospace practice adopted 6082 long ago. The UK L-series lists it in every wrought form: L111 for bars and sections, L112 for forging stock and forgings, L113 for sheet, L114 for drawn tube and L115 for plate. EN 2326 and EN 2636 carry the same material into the European aerospace standards as AL-P6082-T6, with EN 2636 adding a limit on peripheral coarse grain.

In aircraft, 6082 bar goes into machined brackets and fittings for secondary structure, interior and cargo system components, hinge pins and bushes, actuator and linkage parts, avionics and equipment racks, and a very wide range of ground support equipment. It is not used for engine blades, turbine discs or other hot-section parts, which need nickel or titanium alloys, nor for highly loaded primary structure, where 7xxx and 2xxx alloys dominate.

Why buyers choose 6082 bar

  • Highest strength among the widely stocked European 6xxx bar alloys
  • Good machinability in T6, especially from stretched T6511 bar
  • Weldable by TIG and MIG and good corrosion resistance without cladding
  • Aerospace certification available to BS L111, EN 2326 or EN 2636

Tempers

6082 bar tempers and when to use them

6082-T6511

Solution treated, stretched for stress relief with minor straightening, and aged. The usual aerospace condition (L111 bar is normally supplied this way); parts stay true during machining.

6082-T6510

As T6511 but with no straightening after stretching. Chosen when straightness from stretching alone is enough.

6082-T6

Solution treated and aged without stretching. Common for commercial bar and smaller sizes.

6082-T4

Solution treated and naturally aged. Formable; can be aged to T6 after forming.

6082-O / H111

Annealed for cold forming or as forging and re-heat-treat stock.

6082-T651

Plate designation, often mis-applied to bar. For bar, specify T6510 or T6511.

Peripheral coarse grain

Controlling the coarse-grain ring in 6082 bar

Extruded 6082 bar can form a band of large, recrystallised grains near the surface, called peripheral coarse grain (PCG) or a coarse-grain ring. It develops where the outer layer is heavily sheared in the die and then recrystallises during solution treatment. PCG lowers strength and fatigue performance locally, can cause orange-peel on bent or anodised surfaces and may be removed when bar is turned, revealing a different structure under the skin.

  1. ChemistryKeep manganese and chromium in the upper part of their ranges, as these form dispersoids that pin grain boundaries and resist recrystallisation.
  2. Billet practiceHomogenise the cast billet correctly and add titanium-boron grain refiner during casting for a fine, even starting structure.
  3. Extrusion parametersControl billet temperature, ram speed and die design to reduce surface shear and heat build-up.
  4. Quench routePress quenching or carefully controlled separate solution treatment limits the time available for grain growth.
  5. VerificationMacro-etch a slice from each lot and measure PCG depth. EN 2636 sets limits on the depth of coarse grain; specify it when PCG matters.

For machined fittings, ask for EN 2636 or a stated maximum PCG depth, or allow enough machining stock to remove the affected layer.

Working with 6082

Machining, welding and finishing 6082 bar

Machining

Good in T6 and T6511 with sharp carbide tools; chips are slightly longer than 6061 because there is little copper. Chip breakers help on lathes.

Welding

TIG and MIG with ER4043 or ER5356 (5183 for higher strength). The heat-affected zone falls to roughly half the T6 strength unless re-heat-treated.

Forming

Bend in O or T4 and age afterwards; T6 bar needs generous radii.

Anodising

Takes protective and hard anodising well; decorative clarity is lower than 6063.

Corrosion

Good resistance to atmospheric and marine exposure; better than 6061 because of the low copper.

Temperature

Like other 6xxx alloys, strength declines with long exposure above about 150 °C.

Applications

Typical aerospace uses of 6082 bar

  • Machined fittings: brackets, clips, lugs and mounts for secondary structure and systems
  • Cabin and cargo systems: seat and cargo-handling hardware, roller and guide parts
  • Mechanisms: hinge pins, bushes, linkages and actuator end fittings with modest loads
  • Avionics and electronics: housings, chassis and heat-sinking blocks
  • Ground support equipment: towbars, jacks, maintenance stands, engine dollies and access platforms
  • Space and UAV: brackets and frames in small satellites and unmanned aircraft

A typical example is an engine-stand cradle for line maintenance: the welded frame uses 6082-T6 tube and the pick-up blocks are machined from 6082-T6511 round bar, so the entire assembly shares one alloy and finish.

FAQ

6082 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) bar: FAQs

What is BS L111?

BS L111 is the British aerospace specification for 6082-T6 bars and extruded sections. It is normally supplied in the stretched T6511 condition and has minimum tensile strengths of 295 – 310 MPa depending on size.

What is the difference between EN 2326 and EN 2636?

Both cover 6082-T6 aerospace bars and sections up to 200 mm. EN 2636 adds control of peripheral coarse grain, which matters for fatigue-sensitive or heavily machined parts.

Is 6082 used for aircraft engine parts?

Not for hot-section parts such as blades or turbine discs, which need nickel or titanium alloys. 6082 is used for brackets, housings and fittings around the engine where temperatures are moderate.

Is 6082 stronger than 6061?

In most extruded bar sizes, slightly: about 310 MPa minimum tensile in T6 for 20 – 150 mm against about 260 – 290 MPa for 6061. 6082 also has better corrosion resistance because of its low copper.

Which temper should I order for machining?

T6511. Stretching relieves quench stresses so the part stays straight and flat as material is removed.

Do you supply 6082 bar with aerospace certification?

Yes. Metline supplies 6082 bar certified to the specification on your order, with an EN 10204 3.1 certificate, to customers worldwide.
