6262 Aerospace Aluminium Bar (T6, T651, T9)

6262 is the free-machining version of 6061: the same magnesium-silicon base with lead and bismuth added so that chips break cleanly on automatic lathes. It is used for precision turned parts such as hydraulic fittings, valve bodies, pistons and connectors, and for aerospace parts made to AMS-QQ-A-225/10. Metline supplies 6262 round, hex and square bar in T6, T651 and T9, certified and shipped worldwide.

Overview

What is 6262 aluminium bar?

6262 combines the corrosion resistance and anodising response of 6061 with the machining speed of a free-cutting alloy. About 0.4–0.7 % each of lead and bismuth form tiny soft particles that melt locally at the cutting edge, so chips break into small curls instead of long strings.

This matters most on multi-spindle and Swiss-type lathes, where long chips wrap around tools and stop production. With 6262, parts can be turned at high speed with short cycle times and fine surface finishes. Unlike the older free-machining alloy 2011, 6262 keeps good corrosion resistance and takes a clear or hard anodic coating well, which is why it is favoured for anodised hydraulic and pneumatic parts.

The usual high-strength temper is T9: solution treated, artificially aged, then cold drawn to raise strength further. T6 and T651 give 6061-level strength with better machinability. In aerospace, 6262 bar is bought to AMS-QQ-A-225/10 or ASTM B211 for fittings, couplings and small turned parts in hydraulic, fuel and air systems.

Lead content and regulations

  • 6262 contains up to 0.7 % lead, which is restricted in some products by the EU End-of-Life Vehicles and RoHS directives
  • Aerospace parts are generally outside these directives, but check your customer's requirements
  • Where lead must be avoided, lead-free free-machining grades such as 6026 are the usual substitute

Tempers

6262 tempers explained

T6

Solution treated and artificially aged. Good strength, very good machinability.

T651

As T6, stretched to relieve quench stress. Stays straight and stable when turned or milled.

T6511

Extruded bar, stretched and minor-straightened; the usual form for larger diameters.

T9

Solution treated, aged, then cold drawn. Highest strength and the best chip breaking; the standard screw-machine temper.

T73 and T74 are over-aged tempers for 7xxx alloys and do not apply to 6262, even though they sometimes appear in supplier listings.

Working with 6262

Machining, finishing and joining

Machining

Rated among the most machinable aluminium alloys. Use high speeds, positive-rake carbide or HSS tools and soluble-oil coolant; expect small broken chips and fine finishes.

Anodising

Takes clear, coloured and hard anodic films; the lead-bismuth particles can leave slight speckling in very thick hard coats.

Welding

Not recommended: the low-melting lead and bismuth cause hot cracking. Join by threads, crimping or mechanical fasteners.

Corrosion

Good, close to 6061. Much better than 2011 for parts exposed to moisture.

Applications

Where 6262 bar is used

  • Aircraft hydraulic and fuel systems: fittings, couplings, union nuts and valve bodies
  • Pneumatics: cylinder end caps, pistons and manifolds
  • Connectors and electronics: anodised housings and contact bodies
  • Automotive and industrial: brake and transmission parts, camera and optics hardware
  • Fasteners: screws, nuts and inserts made on automatic lathes

FAQ

6262 aluminium (aluminum) bar: frequently asked questions

What is 6262 aluminium used for?

Precision turned parts made in high volume, such as hydraulic and pneumatic fittings, valve bodies, connectors and fasteners, where fast machining and good corrosion resistance are both needed.

What does the T9 temper mean?

T9 is solution heat-treated, artificially aged and then cold worked. The cold drawing after ageing raises strength to about 400 MPa typical and improves chip breaking.

Is 6262 the same as 6061?

The base chemistry is very close, but 6262 adds about 0.4–0.7 % each of lead and bismuth for free machining and has a narrower chromium range. It is not weldable, while 6061 is.

Is 6262 RoHS compliant?

Its lead content exceeds the RoHS and ELV limits for many products. Aerospace parts are usually outside these rules; where they apply, a lead-free grade such as 6026 is used instead.

Can 6262 bar be anodised?

Yes. It takes clear, coloured and hard anodic coatings much better than 2011, which is one of the main reasons it is chosen.
