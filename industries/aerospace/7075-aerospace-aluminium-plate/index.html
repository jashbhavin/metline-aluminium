7075 Aerospace Aluminium Sheet & Plate

7075 aerospace plate is the long-established high-strength aircraft material: in T6 and T651 it gives about 570 MPa tensile and 505 MPa yield strength, and in T7351 it adds strong resistance to stress-corrosion cracking. It is machined into fittings, frames, ribs and spars, and Alclad 7075 sheet is used for skins. Metline supplies bare and Alclad 7075 sheet and plate to AMS 4045, AMS 4078, AMS-QQ-A-250/12 and ASTM B209, cut to size and certified, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What makes 7075 plate an aerospace material

7075 was first produced in the early 1940s and quickly became the standard high-strength alloy for aircraft structure. It contains about 5.6 % zinc, 2.5 % magnesium, 1.6 % copper and 0.23 % chromium. During ageing, zinc and magnesium form extremely fine MgZn2 precipitates that block dislocation movement, which is what gives the alloy its strength.

In sheet and plate form, 7075 offers a combination that is still hard to beat for many parts: very high strength for its weight, excellent machinability, good fatigue strength and a long record in service. It is used for fuselage frames, wing ribs and spars on many aircraft types, for machined fittings, and as Alclad sheet for skins on older and general-aviation designs.

Two limitations shape how it is used. In the peak-aged T6 and T651 tempers, thick 7075 plate is susceptible to stress-corrosion cracking when stressed in the short-transverse direction (through the thickness). And because 7075 is quench sensitive, very thick plate loses strength at the centre. The overaged T7351 temper solves the first problem, and 7050-T7451 is now often chosen for the second.

Why aerospace buyers still specify 7075 plate

  • High static strength: about twice that of 6061-T6
  • Excellent machinability with short chips and a fine finish
  • T7351 temper gives strong stress-corrosion resistance where it matters
  • Long history in service, with design allowables in every major handbook
  • Widely available in sheet, plate and Alclad forms

Short-transverse properties are the key difference between tempers in thick plate. In T651 the short-transverse stress-corrosion threshold is low, so parts with through-thickness loads (lugs, fittings, frames with integral attachments) are usually made from T7351 or from 7050-T7451. Plane-strain fracture toughness of 7075 plate is moderate and depends on orientation; where a design needs guaranteed toughness, specify KIc testing or select 7475 or 7050.

Tempers

Choosing a 7075 plate temper

7075-T6 (sheet)

Solution heat-treated and artificially aged to peak strength. Normal for sheet up to about 6 mm, used flat or formed lightly, where residual stress is low.

7075-T651 (plate)

Same strength as T6, stretched 1.5 – 3 % after quenching to relieve stress. The standard high-strength machining plate. Watch the short-transverse direction in thick sections.

7075-T7351 (plate)

Stretched and overaged. Strength is about 12 % lower than T651, but stress-corrosion resistance is far better. Specified for structural fittings and parts in wet or corrosive zones.

7075-T7651 (plate and sheet)

Moderately overaged for good exfoliation resistance with less strength loss than T7351. Used for wing skins and parts loaded in the plane of the plate.

7075-O

Annealed for forming. Parts are formed and then heat-treated to T6 or T73 by the fabricator.

Alclad 7075

Clad with 7072 on both faces for corrosion protection; supplied in O, T6 and T76 tempers. Strength is slightly below bare material because the cladding is softer.

Working with 7075

Machining, forming, joining and finishing

Machining

One of the best aluminium alloys to machine. Use sharp carbide with high positive rake, high spindle speeds and flood coolant. Balance roughing between faces on thin-walled parts and leave a finishing allowance.

Forming

Limited in T6. For tight bends, form in O or in the freshly quenched (W) condition and then age. Use inside bend radii of several times thickness in T6 sheet.

Welding

Not recommended for arc welding: 7075 is prone to hot cracking and the heat-affected zone loses much of its strength. Join with rivets, bolts or adhesive bonding; friction stir welding is possible with qualification.

Anodising

Takes sulphuric, chromic, boric-sulphuric and hard anodic coatings well. Hard anodising suits wear surfaces on fittings and tooling.

Corrosion protection

Bare 7075 needs protection: anodise or conversion coat and prime. Use Alclad sheet for skins, seal faying surfaces and isolate from carbon fibre and stainless fasteners.

Temperature

Prolonged exposure above about 120 °C reduces strength. The alloy performs well at low and cryogenic temperatures.

Applications

Where 7075 aerospace plate is used

  • Fuselage: frames, longerons, door frames and floor-beam fittings
  • Wing: ribs, spars and machined fittings; T7651 for some skins
  • Fittings: brackets and lugs, in T7351 where loads act through the thickness
  • Helicopters and light aircraft: structural parts and Alclad skins
  • Space and defence: satellite structure, missile bodies and launcher parts
  • Ground equipment and tooling: jigs, fixtures, moulds and test rigs

A typical example is a fuselage frame segment cut from 7075-T7351 plate: the plate is ultrasonically inspected, saw-cut to a blank, high-speed machined to a thin-webbed profile, anodised and primed, then fastened to the skin and stringers. Where the same part sees mainly in-plane loads and no corrosion risk, a designer may choose T651 for its higher strength.

FAQ

7075 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQ

What is the difference between 7075-T651 and 7075-T7351?

T651 is peak-aged for maximum strength. T7351 is overaged, giving about 12 % lower strength but much better resistance to stress-corrosion cracking. Both are stretched to relieve stress.

Which specification covers 7075-T651 plate for aerospace?

AMS 4045 covers T6 sheet and T651 plate, AMS 4078 covers T7351 plate, and AMS-QQ-A-250/12 covers bare 7075 sheet and plate in several tempers.

What is Alclad 7075?

7075 sheet or plate with a thin layer of 7072 alloy bonded to each face. The cladding corrodes sacrificially and protects the core, which suits aircraft skins.

Can 7075 plate be welded?

Not by conventional arc welding. It cracks and loses strength in the heat-affected zone, so it is riveted, bolted or bonded.

How much does a 7075 plate weigh?

About 2.81 kg per m² per millimetre. A 48 × 144 in sheet that is 0.250 in thick weighs about 80 kg.

When should I choose 7050 instead of 7075 plate?

For plate thicker than about 75 mm, or where both high strength and stress-corrosion resistance are needed in thick sections. 7050-T7451 keeps its strength through the thickness and is tougher.

Do you supply 7075 plate cut to size?

Yes. Plate is saw-cut into blanks to your sizes, inspected and certified, and shipped worldwide in export packing.
