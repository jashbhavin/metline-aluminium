8009 H112 Aluminium Profile Extrusions

8009 extrusions are rods, bars and solid shapes pressed from consolidated powder of the Al-8.6Fe-1.8Si-1.3V alloy. They keep their strength at 250 – 315 °C, so they suit warm-zone brackets, fins, fasteners and engine-bay parts where normal aerospace extrusions soften. Overview

What are 8009 extrusions?

8009 is an aluminium alloy that cannot be made by normal casting. Its high iron and vanadium content must be frozen into a very fine structure by rapid solidification, then consolidated from powder into a billet that is extruded at high pressure. The result is a profile whose strength comes from stable silicide particles, not from ageing.

This matters for parts that sit near engines, exhausts or aerodynamically heated surfaces. A 2024 or 7075 extrusion loses strength progressively when held above about 150 °C, because its precipitates over-age. 8009 retains its structure, so a bracket designed at room temperature still has most of its capacity after thousands of hours at 250 °C.

An earlier version of this page listed a 3xxx-type chemistry (manganese 0.8 – 1.3 %), a solution-heat-treatment schedule and decorative uses such as guardrails, doors and windows. None of that applies to 8009; it is a specialist aerospace material and is not supplied for architectural profiles.

In short

  • Use 8009 when an aluminium part must keep strength in the 150 – 315 °C range
  • Expect higher cost and limited section sizes compared with conventional extrusions
  • Shapes are generally solid; hollow profiles are uncommon for powder billets
  • For general structural profiles, 6061, 2024 or 7075 remain the right choice

Process

From powder to profile

  1. Rapid solidificationMolten alloy is cooled at extremely high rates, for example by planar-flow casting onto a chilled wheel or by atomisation, so the iron and vanadium cannot form coarse needles.
  2. Powder or flakeThe ribbon or droplets are broken down into flake or powder with a very fine internal structure.
  3. ConsolidationThe powder is degassed, canned and hot-pressed into a dense billet.
  4. WorkingThe billet is hot rolled into sheet or plate, extruded into rod and profiles, or forged.
  5. No age hardeningThe product is delivered in H112 (as-fabricated). Strength comes from the dispersoids, not from a solution-and-age heat treatment.

Extrusion of 8009 requires higher pressure than 6xxx alloys and lower speeds, and die design must account for the alloy's limited hot ductility. This is why the range of shapes is narrower than for conventional alloys.

Properties

Properties at room and elevated temperature

Research on extruded spray-formed 8009 reports room-temperature tensile strength around 415 MPa and yield around 345 MPa, with both falling gradually as temperature rises. Rapidly solidified product can reach somewhat higher values. Design to the minimums in AMS 4309 and to elevated-temperature data agreed with the supplier.

Design

Design and fabrication notes

Joining

Use fasteners or solid-state joining. Fusion welds melt and coarsen the dispersoid structure, cutting strength in the weld zone.

Machining

Machines cleanly; expect higher tool wear from the hard intermetallic particles. Carbide or PCD tools are recommended.

Forming

Limited cold formability. Bend warm if needed and test first.

Temperature limits

Do not stress-relieve or process above about 450 °C. Do not heat-treat as for 2xxx or 7xxx alloys; it brings no benefit.

Corrosion

Free of copper and zinc, so general corrosion resistance is good; standard conversion coatings and paints apply.

Galvanic contact

Isolate from carbon-fibre composites and stainless steel with sealant or barrier layers, as for any aluminium alloy.

Applications

Where 8009 extrusions are used

  • Brackets, clips and fittings in engine bays and nacelles
  • Missile and projectile fins exposed to aerodynamic heating
  • Fasteners and pins for warm structures where titanium would otherwise be used
  • Compressor-section parts in small turbines and auxiliary power units
  • Heat-exchanger headers and supports in bleed-air systems

FAQ

8009 aluminium (aluminum) extrusions: frequently asked questions

What sizes does AMS 4309 cover?

Round extrusions up to 89 mm (3.5 in) diameter and rectangular or other solid shapes up to about 64.5 cm² (10 in²) cross-section, in lengths up to about 2.5 m.

Can 8009 profiles be heat treated to increase strength?

No. 8009 is not age-hardenable. It is supplied in H112 and its strength comes from dispersoids formed during rapid solidification.

Why choose 8009 over 2618 for hot parts?

2618 is a precipitation-hardened alloy that slowly loses strength with long exposure above about 150 °C. 8009 is thermally stable, so it holds strength better over long service at 200 – 315 °C.

Are hollow 8009 profiles available?

Rarely. Powder billets are usually extruded into solid sections; hollow parts are typically machined from rod.

Can 8009 be used for building profiles?

It is not suitable or economical for that. Architectural profiles use 6060, 6063 or 6005A.

Need a heat-resistant aluminium extrusion?

Share the section, service temperature and specification, and we will confirm whether 8009 or an alternative fits.
