7175 Aerospace Aluminium Forgings

7175 is the high-purity version of 7075, made for aircraft forgings that need more strength and toughness than 7075-T73 while keeping good stress-corrosion resistance. Overview

What 7175 forgings are and why they are used

7175 has the same zinc, magnesium, copper and chromium ranges as 7075, but iron is limited to 0.20 % and silicon to 0.15 %. With fewer coarse intermetallic particles, forgings show better fracture toughness, fatigue life and ductility in the transverse directions, which is where forgings are weakest.

7175 is also processed differently. A higher-temperature homogenising and solution treatment puts more of the alloying elements into solution, so after ageing the forging reaches higher strength for the same degree of over-ageing. That is why 7175-T74 is stronger than 7075-T73 while still offering good resistance to stress-corrosion cracking (SCC) and exfoliation.

The alloy is used for highly stressed closed-die forgings: landing-gear components, wing and fuselage fittings, engine mounts, flap tracks and helicopter rotor parts. For very thick forgings, 7050 is often preferred because its zirconium addition makes it less sensitive to quench rate; for thinner and medium sections, 7175 gives excellent strength and toughness.

7175 forging at a glance

  • Higher strength than 7075-T73 with good SCC resistance in T74
  • Better transverse toughness and fatigue life than 7075
  • AMS 4149 covers T74 die and hand forgings up to 6.000 in (152 mm) thick at heat treatment
  • AMS 4179 covers T7452 forgings, which are compressively stress relieved

Tempers

7175 forging tempers explained

7175-T74

Solution heat-treated and over-aged to a level between T73 and T76 (formerly called T736). Good balance of strength, toughness and SCC resistance. Covered by AMS 4149 for die and hand forgings.

7175-T7452

Solution heat-treated, stress-relieved by 1 – 5 % cold compression and over-aged. The compression lowers residual quench stress so large hand forgings and blocks stay stable during machining. Covered by AMS 4179.

7175-T66

Peak-aged after the special high-temperature solution treatment. Highest strength, but SCC resistance is low, so it is limited to parts that are not loaded in the short-transverse direction.

Choosing a temper

For most loaded aircraft fittings choose T74 or T7452. T66 needs a deliberate stress-corrosion assessment, and many current programmes do not allow it for primary structure.

Working with 7175

Machining, finishing and SCC precautions

Machining

Machines like 7075. T7452 hand forgings have low residual stress, so heavy pocketing causes little distortion.

Shot peening

Peen fillets, bores and lugs to add compressive surface stress; this improves fatigue life and SCC resistance.

Surface protection

Anodise or conversion coat, prime and seal. Wet-install fasteners and bushings with sealant.

Design

Keep sustained tension and interference fits away from the short-transverse direction (ARP 823 practices).

Joining

Not fusion-welded; joined by bolts, rivets or interference-fit bushings.

Temperature

Avoid long exposure above about 120 °C, which over-ages the forging further.

Applications

Where 7175 forgings are used

  • Landing gear: torque links, drag braces, side-stay fittings and wheels on smaller aircraft
  • Wing: flap tracks, flap and slat carriages, pylon and spar fittings
  • Fuselage: door frames, window frames and bulkhead fittings
  • Engine and nacelle: mounts, brackets and fan-case attach fittings
  • Helicopters: rotor hub components, swashplate parts and transmission fittings
  • Space and defence: launcher structure fittings and missile components

  4. DocumentEach lot ships with a mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 giving chemistry, mechanical results and heat-treatment lot, traceable to the cast number.

FAQ

7175 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) forgings: FAQ

What is the difference between 7175 and 7075?

The main alloying ranges are the same, but 7175 limits iron to 0.20 % and silicon to 0.15 %, and it is solution treated at a higher temperature. The result is better toughness and fatigue life and higher strength in over-aged tempers.

What does T74 mean on a 7175 forging?

Solution heat-treated and over-aged to an intermediate level between T73 and T76. It gives a good balance of strength and stress-corrosion resistance. The temper was previously designated T736.

When should I use 7175-T7452?

For hand forgings and blocks that will be heavily machined. The cold-compression stress relief keeps residual stresses low, so the part does not distort during machining.

Which specification covers 7175-T74 forgings?

SAE AMS 4149 covers die and hand forgings in 7175-T74, 6.000 in (152 mm) and under in thickness at heat treatment.

Is 7175-T66 suitable for landing-gear parts?

Only after a careful stress-corrosion assessment. T66 is peak-aged and has low SCC resistance, so most programmes use T74 or T7452 for loaded fittings.

Do you supply 7175 forgings outside India?

Yes. Metline supplies aerospace forgings worldwide with EN 10204 3.1 certificates, full test reports and export packing.
