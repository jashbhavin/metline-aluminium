6061 Aerospace Aluminium Sheet & Plate (T6, T651)

Aerospace-grade 6061 sheet and plate is ordinary 6061 chemistry made, tested and certified to aerospace material specifications, mainly SAE AMS 4025 (O), AMS 4026 (T4) and AMS 4027 (T6/T651). It is the weldable, corrosion-resistant workhorse for aircraft fittings, brackets, ground-support equipment and spacecraft structure where 2024 and 7075 are not needed. Overview

What makes 6061 sheet "aerospace grade"?

There is no separate aerospace alloy called 6061. What turns standard 6061 sheet or plate into aerospace material is the specification it is produced and certified to, the testing that specification demands, and the traceability that follows the metal from cast to finished part.

Commercial 6061 is normally sold to ASTM B209 or EN 485. Aerospace buyers usually call up SAE Aerospace Material Specifications instead: AMS 4025 for annealed sheet and plate, AMS 4026 for T4 and AMS 4027 for T6 and T651. These documents tighten the rules on how lots are defined and sampled, how tensile tests are taken, what grain size and heat-treatment response are acceptable, how the material is marked, and what the certificate must show. Some airframe makers add their own requirements on top, such as ultrasonic inspection of thick plate or approval of the source mill.

The chemistry and the basic mechanical property levels are the same as commercial 6061. A part designer gains confidence and documentation, not a stronger metal. That is why the first question in any 6061 aerospace enquiry is: which specification, and which revision, is on the drawing?

Why 6061 is used in aircraft and spacecraft

  • Good strength for its weight, with excellent corrosion resistance compared with 2xxx and 7xxx alloys
  • Fully weldable by TIG, MIG and friction stir welding, which most high-strength aerospace alloys are not
  • Machines cleanly in T651 and stays flat because quench stresses are removed by stretching
  • Takes Type II and Type III anodising and chemical conversion coatings very well
  • Widely produced, so lead times and cost are lower than for specialist aerospace alloys

Strength falls slowly at moderate temperature: 6061-T6 keeps most of its room-temperature strength up to about 100 °C, but long exposure above roughly 150 °C over-ages the alloy. At cryogenic temperatures strength and toughness actually increase, which is one reason 6061 is used for propellant and cryogenic hardware in launch vehicles.

Tempers

Which 6061 temper fits which aerospace part?

6061-O (AMS 4025)

Annealed for maximum formability. Used for deep-drawn, spun or stretch-formed parts such as fairings, ducts and covers that are heat-treated to T6 after forming.

6061-T4 / T451 (AMS 4026)

Solution treated and naturally aged. Moderately strong and still formable; parts can be formed in T4 and artificially aged to T6. T451 plate is stretched for flatness.

6061-T6 (AMS 4027)

Peak-aged sheet up to about 6 mm. Used flat or lightly formed for panels, doublers, brackets and enclosures.

6061-T651 (AMS 4027)

Peak-aged plate stretched 1.5 – 3 % to relieve quench stresses. The standard choice for machined fittings, mounts and frames because it stays flat when material is removed.

T62 (heat-treated by user)

The designation used when O or T4 material is solution treated and aged by the part maker rather than the mill. Properties are close to T6.

Tread / diamond plate

Patterned 6061 sheet for cabin and cargo floors, maintenance stands and access platforms where slip resistance matters.

Tolerances, testing and certification

Dimensional tolerances for AMS orders follow ANSI H35.2 (the tables referenced by ASTM B209). EN orders use EN 485-3 for hot-rolled plate and EN 485-4 for cold-rolled sheet. Cut blanks can be held tighter by sawing or machining.

Common mistakes when buying aerospace 6061

  • Ordering "aircraft grade" without naming a specification, so the certificate only shows ASTM B209
  • Specifying T6 for thick plate that will be machined; T651 is needed to avoid distortion
  • Forgetting the transverse property and ultrasonic requirements that the drawing or customer specification adds
  • Mixing imperial and metric thickness: 12 mm and 1/2 in (12.7 mm) plate are different products
Selection

Sheet, plate or another product form?

Many 6061 aerospace parts can be made from more than one starting form, and the choice affects cost, scrap and certification. A thin bracket can be bent from T4 sheet and aged, or machined from T651 plate; a long angle can be machined from plate or bought as an extrusion. The rules below help decide before the request for quotation goes out.

Part featureUsually best fromWhy
Flat or bent part under about 6 mmT6 sheet, or O/T4 sheet formed then agedLeast machining, lowest cost per part
Pocketed fitting, frame or mountT651 plateStress-relieved, stays flat during heavy machining
Long constant section (angle, channel, T)6061 extrusion to AMS 4150 or AMS 4173Far less scrap than machining from plate
Round or hollow part6061 bar or tubeMatches the shape; avoids cutting discs from plate
Highly loaded, three-dimensional part6061 forging to AMS 4127Grain flow follows the part; better ductility in all directions

When a part is machined from plate thicker than about 75 mm, check whether the drawing requires short-transverse properties or ultrasonic inspection, because both add cost and narrow the list of qualifying material. For prototype work, buyers sometimes start from commercial ASTM B209 plate and switch to AMS 4027 material for qualification and production; if you do this, make sure the production specification is fixed early so test results remain valid.

Working with 6061

Machining, forming, joining and finishing 6061 sheet

Machining

T651 machines with short chips and a bright surface. Use sharp, high-rake carbide tools, high spindle speeds and flood coolant. Remove material evenly from both faces of thick plate to keep it flat.

Forming

T6 sheet needs inside bend radii of roughly 1 – 3 × thickness depending on gauge. For tight bends, form in O or freshly quenched W condition, then age.

Welding

TIG and MIG with 4043 or 5356 filler; friction stir welding gives better joint strength. The heat-affected zone drops towards T4 strength unless the part is re-heat-treated.

Riveting and bonding

Accepts solid and blind rivets and structural adhesive bonding after suitable surface preparation (anodise or etch and prime).

Anodising and coatings

Excellent response to sulphuric (Type II), hard (Type III) and chromic or boric-sulphuric anodising, and to conversion coatings for primer adhesion.

Heat treatment

Solution treatment around 530 °C, rapid quench, then ageing around 160 – 175 °C to T6. Aerospace heat treatment is controlled to AMS 2770 with pyrometry to AMS 2750.

Applications

Where 6061 sheet and plate is used in aerospace

  • Machined fittings and brackets for secondary structure, systems mounting and equipment racks
  • Cabin and cargo floors, seat-track supports, galley and lavatory structures (tread plate for walkways)
  • Ground-support equipment: maintenance platforms, engine stands, towbars, dollies and tooling
  • Spacecraft and satellite structure: panels, frames, propellant and pressurant tank parts, antenna mounts
  • Unmanned aircraft: frames, landing gear plates and payload mounts where weldability matters
  • Avionics enclosures and heat sinks, using the good thermal conductivity and anodising response
  • Fuel and hydraulic system components machined from plate, such as manifolds and mounting blocks

6061 is rarely used for primary wing and fuselage skins on transport aircraft; those use 2024, 2524, 7075 and newer aluminium-lithium alloys for fatigue performance and strength. 6061 earns its place where corrosion resistance, weldability and machinability matter more than peak strength. Outside aerospace the same sheet is widely used in automotive, marine, tooling and machine building.

FAQ

6061 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) sheet: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between aerospace 6061 and commercial 6061?

The alloy is the same. Aerospace 6061 is produced, tested and certified to an aerospace specification such as AMS 4027, with stricter lot definition, testing, marking and traceability. Commercial 6061 is usually certified only to ASTM B209 or EN 485.

Which AMS specification covers 6061-T6 sheet?

AMS 4027 covers 6061 sheet and plate in T6 and T651. AMS 4025 covers the annealed O temper and AMS 4026 covers T4 and T451.

Should I order 6061-T6 or T651 plate for machined aircraft fittings?

T651. It is stretched after quenching to remove internal stress, so it stays flat and dimensionally stable during heavy machining. T6 is normal for sheet up to about 6 mm.

Is 6061 used for aircraft skins?

Rarely on transport aircraft, where 2024, 2524 and 7075 are preferred for fatigue and strength. 6061 is common on light aircraft, kit aircraft, drones, spacecraft and in secondary structure and fittings.

How much does a 6061 aluminium sheet weigh?

It weighs 2.70 kg per m² per millimetre of thickness. A 1220 × 2440 × 3 mm sheet weighs about 24.1 kg; a 1250 × 2500 × 10 mm plate about 84.4 kg.

Can 6061-T6 sheet be welded for flight hardware?

Yes, with qualified TIG, MIG or friction stir procedures. Welding lowers strength in the heat-affected zone, so welded designs use reduced allowables or post-weld heat treatment.

Do you supply 6061 aerospace plate with ultrasonic testing?

Yes. Plate can be ordered with ultrasonic inspection to AMS-STD-2154 or ASTM B594 at the class you specify, documented on the certificate. Metline ships it worldwide.
