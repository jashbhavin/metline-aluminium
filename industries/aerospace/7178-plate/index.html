7178 Aircraft Aluminium Sheet and Plate

7178 is one of the strongest conventional aluminium alloys, with about 7 % zinc. It was used for compression-critical upper wing skins of early jet transports and is now supplied mainly for repair and spares. Metline supplies 7178 sheet and plate in the annealed O temper for forming and in T6, bare or alclad, to AMS-QQ-A-250/14 and /15, to aircraft maintenance and manufacturing customers worldwide.

Overview

What 7178 is and where it is still used

7178 pushes the 7075 recipe further: zinc rises to 6.3 – 7.3 % and copper and magnesium are slightly higher. In the T6 temper it reaches typical tensile strengths around 600 MPa, about 6 % above 7075-T6, which made it attractive for upper wing skins and stringers that mainly carry compression.

The extra strength came with lower fracture toughness and poor stress-corrosion and exfoliation resistance in T6. As damage-tolerance rules tightened, designers moved to 7075-T76, then to 7150 and 7055, which give similar or higher strength with much better toughness and corrosion behaviour. SAE notes on the alclad 7178 specification state that the alloy is not recommended for new designs.

Demand today comes from maintenance, repair and overhaul (MRO) of older aircraft whose structural repair manuals specify 7178 for skin repairs and doublers, and from manufacturers making spare parts to legacy drawings. The annealed O temper is common for this work: it can be formed into contour and then heat-treated to T6.

Before ordering 7178

  • Confirm the structural repair manual or drawing calls for 7178, and whether bare or alclad
  • Check whether the part is to be formed in O and heat-treated, or bought ready in T6
  • Ask whether an approved alternative (for example 7075 or 7150) is allowed
  • Expect limited availability; plan quantities early

Tempers

7178 tempers: O, T6 and over-aged variants

7178-O

Annealed. Used for stretch forming, brake forming and joggling repair skins and doublers. After forming, parts are solution treated, quenched and aged to T6 under AMS 2770 or the repair manual process.

7178-T6 / T651

Solution treated and aged to peak strength; T651 plate is also stretched. Highest strength, but low resistance to stress corrosion and exfoliation.

Over-aged T76

An over-aged condition was introduced later to improve exfoliation resistance at some cost in strength. Order it only where the drawing names it.

Alclad

Alclad sheet carries 7072 cladding on both sides. It protects the core from corrosion and is standard for external skins; bare sheet is used inside the structure.

Working with 7178

Forming, heat treatment and protection

Forming

Form in O or freshly quenched (W) condition. T6 sheet cracks on tight bends; allow generous radii.

Heat treatment

Solution treat, quench quickly in water and age to T6. Control furnace uniformity to AMS 2750.

Joining

Riveted or bolted. Not fusion-welded.

Protection

Use alclad for external skins; anodise or conversion coat and prime bare parts. Seal faying surfaces.

  2. ConfirmWe confirm the specification can be met, the tolerance class, any extra tests (ultrasonic class, conductivity, fracture toughness, grain flow) and the delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP).
  3. InspectMaterial is checked for dimensions, surface condition and marking; tests named on the order are carried out or arranged with an independent laboratory.
  4. DocumentEach lot ships with a mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 giving chemistry, mechanical results and heat-treatment lot, traceable to the cast number.
  5. Pack and shipExport packing in sealed, moisture-protected wooden cases or on pallets, shipped by sea or air to your port or door.

FAQ

7178 aircraft aluminium (aluminum) sheet: FAQ

Is 7178 stronger than 7075?

Yes. In T6, 7178 typically reaches about 605 MPa tensile and 540 MPa yield, against 572 and 503 MPa for 7075-T6. Its toughness and corrosion resistance are lower.

Why buy 7178 in the O temper?

Annealed sheet can be formed into contoured skins and doublers, then solution treated and aged to T6. This is common in aircraft repair work.

What is alclad 7178?

A 7178 core with thin 7072 cladding on both surfaces. The cladding corrodes preferentially and protects the high-strength core, which suits external skins.

Is 7178 still used in new aircraft?

No. SAE notes it is not recommended for new designs. Modern upper-wing structure uses 7150 or 7055 instead.

Can 7178 be welded?

No. It is riveted or bolted.

Can you supply small quantities of 7178 for repairs?

We supply cut pieces and full sheets where material is available, with mill certificates, worldwide.

Looking for 7178 sheet?

Send the temper, cladding, thickness, size and specification. We will confirm availability and a delivered price.
