Aerospace extruded sections

2024 Aerospace Aluminium Extrusions and Profiles

2024 extrusions are the traditional choice for fatigue-loaded stringers, frames, spar caps and floor beams in aircraft, combining high strength with a slow crack growth rate. They are normally supplied in the stretched T3510 or T3511 temper, or artificially aged to T8511 for higher yield strength. Metline supplies 2024 angles, tees, channels, Z and J sections, bar and tube to ASTM B221 and AMS specifications, with full mill certification, to customers worldwide.

Overview

Why 2024 is used for aircraft extrusions

Most of the extruded length in a conventional metal airframe is in thin open sections: stringers that stiffen the skin, frame chords, floor beams and seat tracks. These parts see millions of load cycles, so fatigue crack growth often governs their design rather than static strength.

2024 answers that requirement well. In the naturally aged T3-type tempers it combines a tensile strength of around 450 – 480 MPa with fracture toughness and crack growth resistance that 7075 cannot match. That is why lower wing stringers and fuselage stringers in many transport aircraft have historically been 2024, while upper wing members loaded mainly in compression use 7xxx alloys.

The alloy is harder to extrude than 6xxx grades. It needs slower press speeds, careful billet temperature control and a separate solution heat treatment and quench, so sections are generally simpler and thicker than architectural profiles. For complex hollow shapes, designers often switch to 6xxx or 7xxx alloys or machine from bar.

Common 2024 extruded shapes

  • Equal and unequal angles, tees, channels, Z and J sections and hat sections for stringers and frames
  • Flat, square and hexagonal bar for machined fittings
  • Seamless extruded round, square and rectangular tube
  • Custom sections to drawing, subject to extrudability review

Tempers

Choosing a temper for 2024 extrusions

T3510

Solution heat-treated, stretched for stress relief and not straightened afterwards. Lowest residual stress; dimensional tolerances are slightly wider.

T3511

As T3510, with minor straightening allowed after stretching. The most common temper for 2024 stringers and bar.

T8510 / T8511

Stretched and then artificially aged. Higher yield strength and better resistance to stress corrosion and creep at moderately raised temperatures, at the cost of ductility and some toughness.

O

Annealed for forming. Parts are bent or stretch-formed, then solution treated and aged by the user.

T42

Solution treated by the user from O or F and naturally aged. Properties are slightly below T3.

T6 / T62

Occasionally specified for rings and machined parts heat treated by the user; confirm against your drawing.

Twist and straightness tolerances are particularly important for long stringers that will be riveted to curved skins. If your assembly jig is unforgiving, state tighter limits on the order rather than relying on the default tables.

Fabrication

Machining, forming and joining

Machining

T3511 and T8511 machine well with carbide tooling and give small, broken chips. Stretched tempers minimise distortion when stringer flanges are milled.

Forming

Joggling and stretch forming of stringers are normally done in O or freshly quenched condition, followed by ageing.

Joining

Riveting and bolting are standard. Fusion welding is not recommended for structural joints.

Corrosion protection

Anodise (chromic, sulphuric or boric-sulphuric) or conversion coat, then prime. Seal faying surfaces against moisture.

Applications

Typical aerospace uses

  • Fuselage and lower wing stringers where fatigue crack growth governs
  • Frame chords, floor beams and cargo floor supports
  • Seat tracks and interior structural sections
  • Machined fittings from extruded bar
  • Hydraulic and structural tube
  • Missile and UAV airframe longerons

A typical example is a fuselage stringer: a thin Z or hat section in 2024-T3511, riveted to 2024-T3 skin so that crack growth behaviour is matched across the joint.

Ordering

How to order 2024 extrusions

  1. SpecifySection drawing or standard shape and size, temper (e.g. 2024-T3511), length, quantity and governing specification, including any ultrasonic class.
  2. Confirm the specificationWe check the order against the governing AMS, ASTM or EN document, confirm tempers, sizes, tolerances and any customer flow-down clauses, and quote on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
  3. ProcessSections are cut to length, inspected for dimensions, straightness and twist, and protected for transport.
  4. DocumentEach lot ships with a mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 showing heat or lot number, chemical analysis and mechanical test results, plus any ultrasonic or other inspection reports called for on the order.
  5. Pack and shipMaterial is protected against moisture and handling damage, packed in seaworthy cases or on pallets, marked for traceability and shipped by sea or air.

FAQ

2024 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) extrusions: FAQ

What is the difference between 2024-T3510 and T3511?

Both are solution treated, stretched and naturally aged. T3511 allows minor straightening after stretching to meet straightness tolerances; T3510 does not, so it has slightly lower residual stress.

Why choose 2024 over 7075 for stringers?

2024 has better fracture toughness and slower fatigue crack growth. 7075 is stronger, so it suits members loaded mainly in compression, such as upper wing stringers.

Can 2024 extrusions be anodised?

Yes. Chromic, sulphuric and boric-sulphuric anodising are all used. The finish is less bright than on 6xxx alloys because of the copper content.

Are custom 2024 profiles possible?

Yes, subject to an extrudability review. 2024 needs simpler shapes and thicker walls than 6xxx alloys, so send the drawing early.

What certification is supplied?

An EN 10204 3.1 mill certificate with chemistry and mechanical results for each lot, plus inspection reports required by the specification.
