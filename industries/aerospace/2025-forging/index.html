2025 Aerospace Aluminium Forgings

2025 is a copper-silicon-manganese forging alloy, specified mainly as 2025-T6 die forgings under AMS 4130. It forges readily into complex shapes and offers moderate strength with good ductility, which made it the classic material for propeller blades and remains useful for intricate aircraft fittings. Overview

What is 2025 aluminium?

2025 is one of the older 2xxx wrought alloys, developed specifically for forging. Its chemistry, about 4.4 % copper, 0.85 % silicon and 0.8 % manganese, gives good metal flow in the die and a strong response to solution treatment and artificial ageing.

Compared with 2014, the other copper-silicon forging alloy, 2025 contains almost no magnesium. That lowers its peak strength but improves forgeability and ductility, so it fills thin ribs and deep cavities with less risk of laps and cracks. The alloy was widely used for aircraft propeller blades and is still found in propeller hubs, fittings and other complex, moderately stressed parts.

For new designs, many engineers now choose 2014-T6, 7050-T74 or 6061-T6 depending on strength and corrosion needs. 2025 remains relevant for legacy part numbers, spares and repairs where the drawing calls for AMS 4130, and for parts whose shape benefits from its forgeability.

Key points about 2025

  • Excellent forgeability for complex die forgings
  • Moderate T6 strength with relatively high elongation
  • Copper-bearing, so it needs corrosion protection in service
  • Mainly bought against AMS 4130 for aircraft and propeller parts

Heat treatment

Tempers and heat treatment

T6

Solution heat-treated, quenched and artificially aged. The standard delivery temper under AMS 4130.

T4

Solution treated and naturally aged. Lower strength, higher ductility; sometimes used as an intermediate condition before forming or straightening.

O / F

Annealed or as-forged. Supplied when the user will heat treat after machining or further forging.

Process control

Heat treatment of aerospace forgings follows AMS 2772 and pyrometry rules such as AMS 2750. Quench delay and quench medium affect both strength and residual stress.

Some legacy documents list T3 and T8 tempers for 2025. Those designations relate to cold-worked products and are not normal for forgings; confirm the intended condition before ordering.

Ordering

How to order 2025 forgings

  1. SpecifyForging or part drawing, alloy and temper (2025-T6), specification (AMS 4130 or ASTM B247), quantity, test coupon locations and inspection class.
  2. Confirm the specificationWe check the order against the governing AMS, ASTM or EN document, confirm tempers, sizes, tolerances and any customer flow-down clauses, and quote on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
  3. ProcessForgings are heat treated, inspected and, if ordered, machined, then marked for traceability.
  4. DocumentEach lot ships with a mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 showing heat or lot number, chemical analysis and mechanical test results, plus any ultrasonic or other inspection reports called for on the order.
  5. Pack and shipMaterial is protected against moisture and handling damage, packed in seaworthy cases or on pallets, marked for traceability and shipped by sea or air.

FAQ

2025 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) forgings: FAQ

What is 2025-T6 aluminium used for?

Mainly complex die forgings for aircraft, historically propeller blades and hubs, and today mostly legacy fittings and spares called up on AMS 4130.

How strong is 2025-T6?

Typical tensile strength is about 400 MPa and yield about 255 MPa, with roughly 19 % elongation. Design to the minimums in AMS 4130 or ASTM B247.

What is the difference between 2025 and 2014?

2014 adds about 0.5 % magnesium, which raises T6 strength to around 485 MPa. 2025 has almost no magnesium, giving lower strength but better forgeability and ductility.

Is 2025 corrosion resistant?

Like other copper-bearing alloys it needs protection. Anodise or conversion coat and prime, and follow ARP 823 guidance to limit stress-corrosion risk.

Can you supply 2025 forging stock rather than forgings?

Yes. Forging stock can be supplied for customers who forge in-house, certified for chemistry and condition.
