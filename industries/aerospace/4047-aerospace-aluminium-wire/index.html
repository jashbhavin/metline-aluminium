4047 Aerospace Aluminium Wire for Brazing and Welding

4047 is a near-eutectic aluminium-silicon alloy (11 – 13 % Si) that melts at about 577 – 582 °C. As wire it has two aerospace jobs: brazing filler to SAE AMS 4185 (AWS BAlSi-4) for heat exchangers and ducting, and ER4047 welding filler where low shrinkage and high fluidity prevent cracking. Overview

What 4047 wire does

At 12.6 % silicon, aluminium and silicon form a eutectic that melts at 577 °C. 4047 sits almost exactly on that point, so it melts over a narrow range, flows freely and solidifies with very little shrinkage.

For brazing, that low melting point is the whole point: 4047 melts about 60 – 80 °C below common base alloys such as 3003, 6061 and 6063, so joints can be filled by capillary action without melting the parts. For welding, the high silicon gives the most fluid weld pool and the lowest hot-cracking tendency of the common fillers, which is useful on crack-sensitive joints and leak-tight fillets.

4047 is not heat-treatable and is not a structural wire. Listings that offer it in T4, T6 or T6511 tempers are wrong; wire is supplied as drawn or annealed. Claims that 4047 wire is used for turbine blades, airframe structure or electrical cable are also wrong.

Brazing

Brazing with 4047 (BAlSi-4)

AMS 4185 covers 12Si brazing filler in wire, sheet, foil and other forms, and its later revisions align with AWS A5.8, where the same alloy is classified BAlSi-4. It has a long history in dip (molten flux) brazing and is used for torch, furnace and controlled-atmosphere brazing.

Base metals

1xxx, 3xxx (3003, 3103) and lower-magnesium 6xxx (6061, 6063). The base metal solidus must be comfortably above 604 °C.

Flux

Torch and dip brazing need a suitable aluminium brazing flux; remove chloride flux residues completely after brazing to avoid corrosion.

Joint design

Lap and tee joints with clearances of about 0.1 – 0.25 mm fill well by capillary flow.

Magnesium

Base metals with more than about 1 % Mg are hard to flux-braze; vacuum brazing uses Mg-bearing fillers instead.

Welding

Welding with ER4047

ER4047 is used where 4043 still cracks, where a leak-tight fillet matters more than strength, or where the base metal already contains a lot of silicon (Al-Si castings). It gives a bright, smooth bead and less distortion.

  • Use on 6xxx and 3xxx alloys and Al-Si castings; not on 5xxx alloys with more than about 2.5 % Mg
  • Expect slightly lower ductility than 4043 and a dark grey colour after anodising
  • Common in heat-exchanger, tank and duct fabrication where joints must be pressure-tight

  • Storage: keep wire sealed in its original packing in a dry, heated store; aluminium oxide hydrates and causes weld porosity
  • Open spools: return part-used spools to a sealed bag with desiccant, or to a wire cabinet
  • Cleaning base metal: degrease, then remove the oxide with a dedicated stainless-steel brush shortly before welding
  • Feeding: use U-groove drive rolls, a PTFE or nylon liner and contact tips sized for aluminium
  • Traceability: record the lot number from the label on the weld record, so the certificate links to the joint
Applications

Aerospace uses of 4047 wire

  • Heat exchangers: brazed oil coolers, fuel-oil heat exchangers and avionics cold plates
  • Ducting: brazed and welded environmental-control ducts and manifolds
  • Tanks and housings: leak-tight fillet welds on 3xxx and 6xxx parts
  • Castings: repair of high-silicon castings where low shrinkage reduces cracking
  • Ground equipment: brazed and welded cooling units and fluid systems

FAQ

4047 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) wire: questions

Is 4047 wire for brazing or welding?

Both. As BAlSi-4 to AMS 4185 or AWS A5.8 it is a brazing filler; as ER4047 to AWS A5.10 it is a welding filler for crack-sensitive and leak-tight joints.

Can 4047 wire be heat-treated to T6?

No. 4047 is not heat-treatable. Wire is supplied as drawn or annealed; T tempers do not apply.

What is the melting point of 4047?

About 577 – 582 °C. Brazing is normally done at about 582 – 604 °C.

What is the difference between 4047 and 4043?

4047 has about 12 % silicon against 5 % in 4043, so it melts lower, flows more and shrinks less. 4043 gives slightly better ductility in welds; only 4047 is used as a brazing filler.

Do you supply 4047 wire outside India?

Yes. Metline ships 4047 wire, rod and preforms worldwide with lot certificates.
