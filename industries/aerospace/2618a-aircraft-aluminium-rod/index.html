2618A Aircraft Aluminium Rod & Bar

2618A rod and bar is the heat-resistant aluminium machinists use for parts that must keep their strength at 150 – 250 °C: pistons, impellers, turbocharger wheels, valve bodies and engine-area fittings. Metline supplies 2618A and 2618 extruded, drawn and forged bar in T6511, T651, T6 and T61, certified to aerospace and engine-maker specifications and shipped worldwide.

Overview

What 2618A bar is and why it is used

2618A is an aluminium-copper-magnesium alloy with roughly 1 % each of iron and nickel. Those two elements form hard Al9FeNi particles that stay stable when hot, so bar machined from 2618A keeps a large part of its strength and resists creep where 2024, 2014 and 7075 would soften.

Bar is the most practical starting form for many 2618A parts. Turned pistons for prototype and low-volume engines, compressor wheels, pump and valve bodies, bushes, studs and brackets are all machined from extruded or forged round bar. Stretched T6511 extruded bar machines with little movement, while T61 forged bar gives the best through-thickness properties for large diameters.

The same chemistry is sold under several names: 2618 (US), 2618A (Europe), AlCu2Mg1.5Ni, Werkstoff 3.1924, RR58 in older British practice and AU2GN in French practice. The differences between 2618 and 2618A are small composition-range adjustments, and most engine programmes state which one they accept.

Typical 2618A bar parts

  • Aero, racing and high-performance pistons and connecting parts
  • Compressor impellers and turbocharger compressor wheels
  • Fuel, oil and hydraulic valve bodies that run hot
  • Engine-area brackets, bosses, spacers and fittings

Working with 2618A

Machining, heat treatment and finishing

Machining

Machines well in T6511 and T61 with short, broken chips. The Fe-Ni particles are mildly abrasive, so carbide or PCD tools last longer than HSS on production runs.

Distortion

Choose stretched tempers (T6511, T651, T851) for thin-walled or asymmetric parts. Rough-machine, then finish after a rest period on critical parts.

Heat treatment

Solution treatment around 525 – 535 °C and ageing around 190 – 200 °C. Re-heat-treating finished parts is rarely done because of distortion risk.

Welding

Not suited to fusion welding for load-bearing parts. Design for bolting, press fits or threaded joints.

Surface treatment

Hard anodising for wear surfaces such as ring grooves; conversion coating and paint for general protection.

Inspection

Ultrasonic inspection of larger bar and forged bar to AMS-STD-2154 or the customer specification.

Applications

Applications of 2618A rod and bar

  • Engines: pistons, compressor wheels, impellers and housings in aero, motorsport and high-output engines
  • Turbomachinery: small gas-turbine and auxiliary-power-unit components running warm
  • Airframe: fittings and brackets near engines and in areas heated by bleed air or exhaust
  • Space and defence: structural parts in missiles and launch vehicles that combine heat and vibration
  • Hydraulics and fuel systems: valve bodies and manifolds operating at elevated temperature

A typical racing piston programme machines prototype pistons from 2618A forged bar, then moves to closed-die forgings in the same alloy once the design is frozen, keeping the material qualification unchanged.

FAQ

2618A aircraft aluminium (aluminum) rod: FAQ

What is the difference between 2618A-T6511 and T61 bar?

T6511 is extruded bar that has been stretched and straightened before ageing, so it machines with minimal movement. T61 is the forging temper with a hot-water quench, used for forged bar and forging stock.

Are 2618 and 2618A the same alloy?

They are close variants. 2618 is the US registration and 2618A the European one; 2618A allows slightly wider iron, nickel and titanium ranges. Mechanical properties are similar, and many programmes accept either, but the specification on your drawing decides which is certified.

What is 2618A bar used for?

Machined engine and hot-running parts: pistons, compressor wheels, impellers, valve bodies and engine-area brackets in aerospace, motorsport and high-performance engines.

How is 2618A bar produced?

Billet is cast and homogenised, then extruded or forged, solution heat-treated, quenched, stretched where the temper requires it, artificially aged, and inspected and tested.

What diameters are available?

Extruded round bar from about 5 mm to 250 mm, and forged bar up to about 420 mm. Square, flat and hexagon bar are also available.

Can I get a lower price on 2618A bar?

Price depends on size, temper, testing, quantity and the aluminium market. Sending a full size list and accepting random mill lengths usually gives the most economical quotation.

Machining hot-running parts from 2618A?

Send the size list, temper and specification. We will confirm stock options, certification and a delivered price.
