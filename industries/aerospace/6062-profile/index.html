6062 Aerospace Aluminium Profiles (T6)

6062 is a heat-treatable Al-Mg-Si-Cu alloy that is chemically 6061 with chromium held to a narrow, low band. Extruded 6062-T6 profiles still appear on older aircraft, helicopter and ground-equipment drawings for moderate-strength sections that are welded or brazed. Metline sources 6062 extrusions made to order, or helps buyers document an approved alternative, and ships worldwide with full traceability.

Overview

What 6062 aircraft profiles are

6062 was registered next to 6061 and 6063 when the four-digit system was introduced; its older US name was 62S. All its alloying limits match 6061 except chromium, which is restricted to 0.04 – 0.14 % instead of 0.04 – 0.35 %.

Chromium refines grain and helps resist stress-corrosion cracking, but it also makes the alloy more quench-sensitive and stiffer to extrude. Holding it low gave extruders a 6061-strength alloy that pushed more easily through complex dies and reached full T6 strength more consistently in thin walls. That is why 6062 was used for extruded shapes, tubing and hollow sections in mid-twentieth-century aircraft and why it lives on in maintenance manuals and spare-part drawings.

Today 6062 is not a routine mill grade. When an aerospace drawing calls 6062-T6 extrusions, the practical options are a production run in 6062 chemistry, or a written engineering approval to use 6061-T6 (for example to AMS 4150) or another grade. Metline handles both routes, but never substitutes a grade without the customer's written approval.

Key facts for buyers

  • 6062 is a real registered alloy, not a misprint for 6061, 6063 or 6262
  • Strength in T6 is in the same class as 6061-T6
  • Chemistry falls inside 6061 limits, so a low-chromium 6061 cast can meet 6062, subject to approval
  • Typical uses are welded or brazed secondary structure, frames, rails and ground equipment

Tempers and specifications

6062 tempers and the documents that call them

6062-T6

Solution heat-treated (press or furnace quench) and artificially aged by the producer. The usual condition on drawings.

6062-T62

Heat-treated by the user, or by the producer from O or F to show heat-treatment response. Common where profiles are formed before ageing.

6062-T4

Solution treated and naturally aged, for stretch-forming or bending before final ageing.

6062-O

Annealed for maximum formability; full strength is restored by heat treatment.

AMS 4092 / 4093

SAE documents for 6062-T6 drawn tubing (general and hydraulic). They show the alloy is still recognised in aerospace specifications.

AMS 4150 caution

AMS 4150 is a 6061-T6 extrusion specification. It is often proposed as the replacement for 6062-T6 profiles, but it is a different alloy designation and needs engineering approval.

Working with 6062

Welding, brazing, machining and finishing

Welding

TIG and MIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler, as for 6061. The heat-affected zone drops towards T4 strength unless the part is re-heat-treated and aged.

Brazing

Like 6061, 6062 can be furnace or dip brazed with Al-Si filler alloys; the T6 condition is restored by heat treatment after brazing.

Machining

Good in T6, with short chips and a clean finish; softer O and T4 tempers are gummier.

Forming

Bend or stretch-form in O or T4, then age. T6 sections accept only generous bend radii.

Finishing

Anodises well (sulphuric, chromic or boric-sulphuric to aerospace specifications) and accepts conversion coatings and primers.

Corrosion

Good atmospheric resistance; protect with anodising or primer and isolate from carbon fibre and stainless fasteners.

Applications

Where 6062 profiles are used

  • Legacy airframes: stringers, frames, rails and doublers on older aircraft and helicopters where the drawing names 6062
  • Interior structure: seat-track supports, floor beams for light aircraft, luggage-rack and galley frames
  • Welded and brazed frames: radiator and heat-exchanger headers, ducts and brazed assemblies
  • Ground equipment: passenger stairs, baggage carts, maintenance platforms and towbars
  • Spare parts and MRO: replacement sections for repairs documented to original drawings

A common case is an MRO shop replacing a damaged floor-support extrusion on an older aircraft: the repair manual names 6062-T6, so the shop must either source 6062 with a certificate or obtain an approved repair scheme that allows 6061-T6.

FAQ

6062 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) profiles: FAQs

Is 6062 the same as 6061?

Almost. All alloying limits are the same except chromium, which is 0.04 – 0.14 % in 6062 against 0.04 – 0.35 % in 6061. T6 properties are in the same class, but the two are separate designations and cannot be swapped without approval.

Which specification covers 6062-T6 extrusions?

Check the drawing first. SAE lists AMS 4092 and AMS 4093 for 6062-T6 tubing; extrusions are often called up by older ASTM or military documents. AMS 4150 is a 6061-T6 specification, not a 6062 one.

Can a 6061 extrusion be certified as 6062?

Only if its measured chemistry meets the 6062 limits, chiefly chromium at 0.14 % or below, and the customer's quality rules allow it. This must be agreed in writing before the order.

Is 6062 still produced?

Not as a routine stock grade. It is made to order for programmes and spare parts that require it, which is why buyers should send the drawing and specification early.

Can 6062 profiles be welded?

Yes, with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. Expect the heat-affected zone to lose part of its T6 strength unless the assembly is re-heat-treated.

Have a drawing that calls 6062-T6?
