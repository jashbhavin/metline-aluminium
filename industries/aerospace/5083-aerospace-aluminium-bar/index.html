5083 Aerospace Aluminium Bar & Rod

5083 bar is the strongest non-heat-treatable aluminium bar in common supply, with excellent corrosion resistance, weldability and toughness down to cryogenic temperatures. In aerospace it is used for welded fittings, cryogenic and launch-site hardware, ground support equipment and tooling rather than airframe structure. Metline supplies 5083 round, square, hex and flat bar in O, H111 and H112 to ASTM B221 and EN 755, worldwide.

Overview

The role of 5083 bar in aerospace

5083 contains about 4.4 % magnesium and 0.7 % manganese. It is the strongest of the widely used non-heat-treatable alloys, keeps most of its strength after welding and becomes stronger and tougher as temperature falls, which is why it is a standard cryogenic alloy.

Those properties shape where it is used. 5083 is not an airframe alloy: wings, fuselages and other primary structure use heat-treatable 2xxx and 7xxx grades, and claims that 5083 is used for fuselage or wing structure should be treated with caution. Instead, 5083 bar turns up where welded corrosion-resistant hardware is needed: fittings and flanges for cryogenic propellant and liquid-gas systems at launch sites, machined parts in ground support equipment, aircraft-carrier and coastal flight-line equipment, test fixtures, transport containers and shelters, and tooling that must stay dimensionally stable.

Bar is normally extruded and supplied in the as-extruded H112 condition or annealed (O or H111). Because 5083 is strengthened by cold work, strain-hardened tempers such as H32 are only available in small sections, where the cold-finishing reduction can be applied evenly.

Why 5083 bar is chosen

  • Highest strength among non-heat-treatable bar alloys
  • Welded joints keep close to base-metal strength in O and H112
  • Excellent resistance to seawater and marine atmospheres
  • Retains toughness and gains strength at cryogenic temperatures

Tempers

5083 bar tempers

5083-O

Fully annealed. Maximum ductility and toughness; preferred for cryogenic parts and anything that will be formed.

5083-H111

Annealed and lightly strain-hardened by straightening. Slightly higher yield than O with the same corrosion behaviour.

5083-H112

As extruded with guaranteed minimum properties. The most common bar condition for machined parts.

5083-H32 (small sections)

Strain-hardened and stabilised; higher strength, available in limited sizes.

Working with 5083

Machining, welding and service limits

Machining

Gummy in O; use sharp, polished high-rake tools, high speeds and ample coolant. H112 machines a little cleaner.

Welding

TIG and MIG with ER5183 filler to match strength; ER5356 for lower-stress joints. Weld zones keep close to O-temper strength.

Service temperature

Do not use for long periods above about 65 °C: magnesium-rich phases can form on grain boundaries and cause intergranular and stress corrosion. Use 5454 or 5052 for warm service.

Cryogenic

Strength and toughness improve at low temperature, with no ductile-to-brittle transition, making 5083-O a standard choice for liquid-gas fittings.

Anodising

Takes protective anodising; the finish is greyer than on 6xxx alloys.

Galvanic contact

Isolate from stainless and carbon-fibre parts in wet environments.

Applications

Typical applications

  • Cryogenic hardware: flanges, valve bodies and fittings for liquid oxygen, nitrogen and natural gas systems at launch and test sites
  • Ground support equipment: machined parts for stands, tow equipment and refuelling systems exposed to weather and salt air
  • Welded fittings in 5083 plate structures such as containers, shelters and transport cases
  • Tooling and fixtures that need stable, weldable aluminium
  • Marine and naval aviation equipment on carrier decks and coastal airfields

FAQ

5083 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) bar: FAQs

Is 5083 used in aircraft structures?

Not in primary airframe structure, which uses heat-treatable 2xxx and 7xxx alloys. 5083 bar is used for welded fittings, cryogenic and launch-site hardware, ground support equipment and tooling.

Why is 5083 good for cryogenic service?

Its strength and toughness increase as temperature falls and it has no ductile-to-brittle transition, so it is widely used for liquid-gas storage and transfer hardware.

What filler is used to weld 5083 bar?

ER5183 is the standard match for strength; ER5356 is acceptable for lower-stress joints.

What is the maximum service temperature for 5083?

Avoid sustained service above about 65 °C, where sensitisation can lead to intergranular and stress-corrosion cracking.

Can 5083 bar be heat treated?

No. It is strengthened by cold work only; heat can anneal it but not age-harden it.
