2219 Aerospace Aluminium Sheet and Plate

2219 sheet and plate are the established material for welded high-strength aerospace structures, above all cryogenic propellant tanks, because the alloy welds reliably and keeps its strength and toughness from about −253 °C up to roughly 300 °C. It is supplied bare or Alclad in tempers from annealed O to the high-strength T851 and T87. Metline supplies 2219 sheet and plate to AMS 4031, ASTM B209 and customer specifications, cut to size and certified, to aerospace, space and defence buyers worldwide.

Overview

What makes 2219 plate special

Most high-strength aluminium alloys are difficult or impossible to fusion weld. 2219 is the exception among the high-copper alloys: with about 6.3 % copper, almost no magnesium and a zirconium addition, it resists hot cracking and can be welded into large, pressure-tight structures.

The alloy was developed in the 1950s and found its defining use in rocketry. Large launch-vehicle propellant tanks, including those of the Saturn V stages and the original Space Shuttle external tank, were welded from 2219 plate. The same reasoning still applies: 2219 is strong, its toughness rises rather than falls at cryogenic temperatures, it welds with well-understood procedures, and it is available in thick, wide plate for machined and formed tank panels.

At the other end of the temperature range, 2219 resists softening better than 2024 and much better than 7075. Its copper-rich precipitates are stable up to roughly 250 – 315 °C for limited exposures, so the alloy is also chosen for supersonic aircraft skins, engine-area structure and parts that see heat during service or processing.

The price of these advantages is moderate room-temperature strength and limited corrosion resistance. 2219-T87 is weaker than 7075-T6 or 2024-T3 in tension, and like all high-copper alloys it needs anodising, conversion coating or Alclad cladding. Third-generation aluminium-lithium alloys such as 2195 have replaced 2219 in some new tank designs because they save weight, but 2219 remains widely specified for its proven weldability, availability and lower cost.

Why buyers choose 2219 plate

  • Reliable fusion and friction stir weldability with 2319 filler or no filler
  • Strength and toughness that increase at cryogenic temperatures
  • Useful strength retained up to about 300 °C
  • Good resistance to stress-corrosion cracking in the T8 tempers
  • Available as thick plate for machined and formed structures

Each minor element has a purpose. Manganese and zirconium form fine dispersoids that keep the grain structure unrecrystallised and refine the weld fusion zone. Vanadium and titanium further stabilise the structure at temperature. The tight magnesium limit (0.02 % max.) is critical: even small amounts of magnesium increase hot-cracking sensitivity during welding.

Typical values from Aluminum Association and ASM data. Specified minimums depend on product form, thickness and test direction, and are shown in the governing ASTM or AMS document and on the mill certificate.

The T8 tempers (T81, T851, T87) differ from T6-type tempers in that the material is cold worked, by stretching or rolling, between quenching and ageing. The cold work speeds up and refines precipitation, giving higher yield strength and better resistance to stress corrosion. T87, with about 7 % cold work, is the strongest standard temper; T851 is stretched only 1.5 – 3 % for stress relief and is preferred for plate that will be heavily machined.

Service temperature

Performance from cryogenic to elevated temperatures

2219 is often described as a "wide temperature range" alloy, and the description is accurate. Aluminium alloys with a face-centred cubic structure have no ductile-to-brittle transition, but 2219 goes further: its tensile strength, yield strength and fracture toughness all increase as it is cooled towards liquid-hydrogen temperature (about −253 °C), and its weld joints keep useful toughness too.

Tempers

2219 tempers explained

2219-O

Fully annealed for forming. Parts are spun, stretched or bent and then heat treated by the user, usually to T62. AMS 4031 covers O sheet and plate.

2219-T31 / T351

Solution heat-treated, cold worked and naturally aged. Used as the starting temper for parts that will be formed and then artificially aged to T81 or T851.

2219-T37

Solution heat-treated with about 7 % cold work and naturally aged. Precursor to T87.

2219-T62

Solution heat-treated and artificially aged by the user, often after forming or welding. No cold work, so yield strength is lower than T81.

2219-T81 / T851

Solution heat-treated, stretched or cold worked, then artificially aged. T851 is stretched for stress relief and is the usual choice for machined plate.

2219-T87

Solution heat-treated, cold worked about 7 % and artificially aged. Highest strength; widely used for welded tank plate.

Product types

Bare or Alclad 2219?

Bare 2219 is the alloy as rolled and heat treated, with no cladding. It is the normal form for plate over a few millimetres, for parts machined on all surfaces, and for welded structures, because cladding would be removed by machining or mixed into the weld.

Bare plate relies on anodising, chemical conversion coating and paint for corrosion protection.

Alclad 2219 has a thin layer of 7072 alloy bonded to each face during rolling. The cladding corrodes preferentially and protects the copper-rich core, even at scratches and cut edges.

It is mainly used for thin sheet skins exposed to the atmosphere. Strength is a little lower than bare sheet, and the cladding must be accounted for in fatigue and weld design.

Fabrication

Welding, forming and machining 2219

Fusion welding

Gas tungsten arc, gas metal arc, variable-polarity plasma arc and electron beam welding are all used. Filler alloy 2319 (2219 chemistry with added titanium and vanadium for weld grain refinement) is standard.

Friction stir welding

Solid-state friction stir welding avoids melting, porosity and hot cracking, and gives higher joint strength than fusion welding. It is now common on large tank barrels and domes.

Weld preparation

Remove oxide and contamination immediately before welding: degrease, then mechanically or chemically clean. Hydrogen from moisture and hydrocarbons is the main cause of porosity.

Joint strength

The weld and heat-affected zone are weaker than T87 parent plate. Designs either accept as-welded allowables or heat treat the welded assembly to T62.

Forming

Form tank gores and panels in O or T31/T37, then age. Brake forming of T8 tempers requires generous bend radii.

Machining

Machines cleanly with carbide tools; T851 plate keeps flat when large pockets are milled.

Applications

Where 2219 sheet and plate are used

  • Launch vehicles: welded liquid-oxygen and liquid-hydrogen tank barrels, domes and rings
  • Spacecraft: pressure vessels, propellant tanks and welded structures
  • Aircraft: skins and structure in warm zones, supersonic airframe parts and engine-area fittings
  • Defence: welded structures and armour-related applications needing strength and weldability
  • Cryogenic engineering: industrial cryogenic vessels and test equipment
  • High-temperature tooling: fixtures and parts that must keep strength in heated processes

A typical example is a cryogenic tank barrel: 2219-T87 plate is machined into thin isogrid-stiffened panels, formed to the barrel radius, and joined by friction stir or plasma arc welding with 2319 filler.

FAQ

2219 aerospace aluminium (aluminum) plate: frequently asked questions

Why is 2219 used for rocket propellant tanks?

It combines moderate-to-high strength with reliable weldability and strength and toughness that increase at liquid-hydrogen and liquid-oxygen temperatures.

Which filler should be used to weld 2219?

2319 filler (AWS ER2319) is standard. It matches the parent chemistry and adds titanium and vanadium to refine the weld structure. Friction stir welding needs no filler.

What is the difference between 2219-T851 and T87?

Both are cold worked before artificial ageing. T851 is stretched 1.5 – 3 % for stress relief; T87 receives about 7 % cold work and is stronger.

Is 2219 plate corrosion resistant?

Not on its own. It is a copper-rich alloy that should be anodised, conversion coated and painted, or supplied as Alclad sheet for external skins.

How much does 2219 plate weigh?

2.84 kg per m² per millimetre of thickness. A 1250 × 2500 × 10 mm plate weighs about 88.8 kg.

Can 2219 be heat treated after welding?

Yes. Welded assemblies can be solution treated and aged to T62 to recover strength, but the process causes distortion that must be planned for.

Do you supply 2219 plate with ultrasonic testing?

Yes, to AMS-STD-2154 or ASTM B594 at the class on your order, with the inspection report included.
