7039 Aluminium Armour Plate

7039 is a heat-treatable aluminium-zinc-magnesium armour alloy, supplied in the T64 temper to MIL-DTL-46063H from 1/2 to 4 in thick. It resists armour-piercing projectiles considerably better than 5083 at similar weight, but is more sensitive to stress corrosion and harder to weld, so it is used where AP protection matters most. Overview

What 7039 armour plate is and when to use it

7039 contains about 4 % zinc and 2.8 % magnesium, with chromium and manganese for grain control. After solution treatment and ageing it is roughly 40 – 50 % stronger than 5083-H131, and that strength translates directly into better resistance to armour-piercing bullets.

7039 was introduced as a higher-performance alternative to 5083 for light armoured vehicles and has been used for hulls, turrets and add-on panels. Its Al-Zn-Mg chemistry places it among the "weldable" 7xxx alloys: unlike 7075, it contains almost no copper, so welds do not hot-crack badly and the heat-affected zone partly recovers its strength by natural ageing after welding.

The trade-off is stress-corrosion cracking. Zinc-magnesium precipitates make 7039 susceptible to cracking under sustained tensile stress in moist or salty environments, especially in the short-transverse direction and in the heat-affected zone of welds. Research programmes have looked for replacements such as 7017 and 2139 for exactly this reason. Today 7039 is chosen deliberately: where AP protection per kilogram is the deciding factor and the design keeps residual stresses and exposure under control.

Ballistic performance

How 7039 performs against AP and fragment threats

Armour-piercing (AP) bullets have hard steel or tungsten cores that are resisted mainly by plate hardness and strength. Fragments and fragment-simulating projectiles (FSP) are blunter and are resisted more by toughness and ductility. 7039 is strong, so it excels against AP threats; its fragment performance is less impressive.

Armour specifications limit the minus thickness tolerance more tightly than commercial plate, because a plate that is too thin may fail ballistic acceptance. The standard ANSI H35.2 tolerance band is kept, but part of the minus allowance is moved to the plus side. For example, if the standard tolerance is ±0.060 in and the armour specification permits only a small minus deviation, the remaining band is added on the plus side. Ask for the tolerance table that applies to your thickness when ordering.

Fabrication

Welding and protecting 7039

Welding

MIG weld with 5039 or 5183-type fillers as qualified for the programme. The heat-affected zone softens, then partially recovers by natural ageing over several weeks. Keep welds out of the short-transverse direction under tension.

Residual stress

Design joints to minimise sustained tensile stress. Shot peening of weld toes and careful sequencing help reduce SCC risk.

Corrosion protection

Use conversion coating, primer and military paint, and seal edges. Avoid long exposure to wet, salty conditions without protection.

Machining and cutting

Machines well in T64. Waterjet or saw cutting avoids heat damage at edges; plasma-cut edges should be dressed back.

Applications

Where 7039 armour plate is used

  • Light armoured vehicles: hulls, turrets and add-on panels where AP resistance per kilogram is the priority
  • Appliqué armour: bolt-on panels over a welded 5083 hull, avoiding welding of the 7039
  • Protected shelters: fragment- and bullet-resistant walls for mobile command posts
  • Ammunition and equipment containers: protected storage for sensitive loads
  • Hatches, doors and mounts: bespoke protective parts made to drawing

FAQ

7039 armour plate: frequently asked questions

What standard covers 7039 aluminium armour plate?

MIL-DTL-46063H, Armor Plate, Aluminum Alloy, 7039, which covers wrought plate from 1/2 to 4 in nominal thickness, normally in the T64 temper.

Is 7039 better than 5083 for armour?

Against armour-piercing threats, yes, by a clear margin at similar weight. Against fragments in thinner plate, 5083 does as well or better, and 5083 is easier to weld and far more resistant to stress corrosion.

Can 7039 be welded?

Yes, with qualified procedures. It is one of the weldable Al-Zn-Mg alloys, and the heat-affected zone partly recovers by natural ageing. Welded 7039 is more prone to stress-corrosion cracking, so joint design and stress control are important.

Why is 7039 supplied in T64 rather than T6?

T64 is an ageing treatment that gives up a little peak strength in return for better toughness and stress-corrosion resistance, which suits armour service.

What is the density of 7039 aluminum?

About 2.74 g/cm³ (0.099 lb/in³), slightly higher than 5083 at 2.66 g/cm³.
