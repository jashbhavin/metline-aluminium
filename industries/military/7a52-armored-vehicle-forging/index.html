Armour forgings

7A52, 7050 and 2A50 Aluminium Forgings for Armour

Forgings in 7A52, 7050 and 2A50 supply the thick, shaped parts of armoured vehicles: hatch rings, mounts, brackets and load-bearing nodes. 7A52-T6 is weldable to 7A52 armour plate, 7050-T74 gives the highest strength with good stress-corrosion resistance, and 2A50-T6 is a forgeable medium-strength alloy for complex shapes. Metline supplies all three to drawing, inspected and certified, worldwide.

Overview

Choosing an alloy for armour forgings

A forging is chosen when a part is thick, shaped and highly loaded, and when the designer wants the grain to follow the shape. In armoured vehicles that means parts such as hatch rings, turret ring sections, suspension mounts and towing eyes.

7A52-T6

Weldable Al-Zn-Mg alloy that matches 7A52 armour plate, so a forged node can be welded straight into the hull. Medium-high strength with good toughness.

7050-T74

High-strength Al-Zn-Mg-Cu aerospace alloy. The over-aged T74 temper gives much better stress-corrosion resistance than T6, with strength well above 7A52. Bolted rather than welded.

2A50-T6

Al-Mg-Si-Cu forging alloy with good hot workability, used for complex medium-strength parts. Bolted rather than welded.

Mechanical properties

Inspection

Fracture inspection and oxide-film limits

Inclusions and oxide films left from the melt are planes of weakness in a forging. Armour forgings are therefore broken open on sample pieces and the fracture surface examined. It must be free of non-metallic inclusions, and the oxide film found within a set inspection area is limited by forging category.

Applications

Typical armour forging applications

  • Hatch rings and doors: forged 7A52 welded into a 7A52 hull
  • Turret and weapon mounts: high-load 7050-T74 rings and brackets, bolted in place
  • Suspension and running gear: arms, brackets and hull nodes
  • Towing and lifting points: eyes and lugs that carry shock loads
  • Fixed protection: shaped parts for protected shelters and installations
Selection

What to check when selecting armour forgings

  1. Joining methodIf the part is welded to 7A52 plate, use 7A52. If it is bolted and highly loaded, consider 7050-T74.
  2. Load and directionIdentify the short-transverse direction of the part and check the minimum properties there.
  3. CorrosionFor 7xxx forgings in wet or salty service, prefer over-aged tempers and specify coatings.
  4. InspectionAgree forging category, ultrasonic class and first-article tests before production.
  5. DocumentationAsk for an EN 10204 3.1 certificate with chemistry, mechanical and inspection results.

FAQ

Armour forgings: frequently asked questions

Which forging alloy can be welded to armour plate?

7A52-T6 forgings are weldable to 7A52 armour plate. 7050 and 2A50 are not normally fusion welded in armour structures and are bolted.

Why is 7050 supplied in T74 rather than T6?

T74 is an over-aged temper that gives up some peak strength for much better resistance to stress-corrosion cracking and exfoliation, which matters in thick forgings.

What is the equivalent of 2A50?

2A50 has no exact AA equivalent. It is an Al-Mg-Si-Cu forging alloy; 2014 (stronger) and 6061 (weaker, more corrosion resistant) are the nearest common alternatives.

Why are oxide films checked on fracture surfaces?

Oxide films act as internal cracks under impact. Breaking sample forgings and limiting the oxide area found ensures the metal is clean enough for armour service.

Do you supply machined aluminum forgings?

Yes. Forgings can be supplied as forged, rough machined or finish machined to drawing.
