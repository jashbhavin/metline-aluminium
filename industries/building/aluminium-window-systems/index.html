Aluminium Window Systems

An aluminium window system is a family of matched extruded profiles, gaskets, hardware and glazing details that produces every window type a building needs, from sliding and casement windows to fixed lights and tilt-and-turn units, with consistent appearance and tested performance. Metline supplies the window profiles, thermal-break sections and accessories, selected from your drawings and window schedule, to fabricators for residential, hotel and commercial projects worldwide.

Overview

What makes a window a "system"?

A window is more than a frame around glass. It has to resist wind pressure, keep out driven rain, limit air leakage, insulate, reduce noise, open and lock reliably for decades and look the same across hundreds of openings. A window system achieves this with a coordinated set of profiles, thermal breaks, gaskets, corner cleats, drainage paths and hardware that has been tested together as a whole.

Aluminium is used because it delivers strength and stiffness in slim sections. A 6063-T6 profile allows narrow sightlines and large glass panes that would need much bulkier frames in PVC or timber, and it does not warp, swell or degrade in sunlight. Modern thermally broken aluminium windows, with polyamide strips separating the inner and outer shells, now reach insulation levels that meet strict energy codes.

For a building project, the real benefit of buying a system is consistency. Sliding windows in the bedrooms, casements in the kitchens, fixed lights in the stairwell and tilt-and-turn units in the living rooms can all share the same colour, glass build-up, hardware family and sightlines, and all be backed by the same test reports.

Window types

Aluminium window types and where each fits

Sliding windows

Sashes slide horizontally on rollers, so nothing projects into the room or over the façade. Two- and three-track designs suit apartments, hotels and large openings.

Casement windows

Side-hinged sashes that close against compression gaskets, giving the best air and water tightness of the common types. Inward or outward opening.

Tilt-and-turn windows

One handle tilts the sash inwards for secure ventilation or swings it fully open for cleaning. Standard across much of Europe; multi-point locking.

Awning / top-hung windows

Hinged at the top and opening outwards, so they ventilate even in light rain. Common in kitchens, bathrooms and as vents above fixed glass.

Fixed windows

Non-opening frames for maximum glass area and the simplest, most reliable seal. Often combined with opening sashes in one frame.

Single- and double-hung windows

Vertically sliding sashes, traditional in North America and in British sash-window designs.

Slimline windows

Very narrow visible frames for a minimal look and maximum daylight; popular in villas and hotel projects.

Bifold windows

Folding sashes that stack to one side to open a counter or bar area fully to the outside.

Acoustic windows

Deeper frames, laminated or asymmetric glass and extra seals to cut road, rail and aircraft noise.
Engineering

Profiles, alloys and thermal breaks

Window profiles are extruded from 6063 or 6060 alloy, aged to T5 or T6 (T66 in Europe). These alloys extrude into thin, complex multi-chamber sections with crisp detail, and their surface takes powder coating and anodising evenly.

ElementTypical specificationWhat it controls
Frame and sash profiles6063-T5/T6 or 6060-T66 to EN 755 or ASTM B221Strength, stiffness, sightlines
Wall thicknessTypically 1.2 – 2.0 mm in visible and structural walls; thicker for large or high-rise windowsWind-load resistance, screw holding, hardware fixing
Thermal breakPolyamide PA66 GF25 strips, typically 14 – 35 mm wideHeat loss and condensation on the inner frame
GasketsEPDM, often with a centre gasket in casement and tilt-turn systemsAir and water tightness, acoustic performance
Corner jointsCrimped or pinned corner cleats with corner sealantFrame rigidity, leak prevention at corners
HardwareMulti-point locks, friction stays, rollers, hinges in stainless steel or zincOperation, security, long-term reliability
GlazingDouble or triple insulated glass, Low-E, laminated or toughenedU-value, solar gain, safety, acoustics

Thermal breaks are the main difference between a basic and an energy-efficient aluminium window. Without one, the frame conducts heat straight through, making its inner surface cold in winter and prone to condensation. With a wide polyamide break, foam inserts and good glass, whole-window U-values (Uw) of about 1.0 to 1.6 W/m²K are achievable, compared with about 5 W/m²K or more for an old single-glazed, non-broken window. See thermal break aluminium windows and energy-efficient aluminium windows for detail.

Window performance standards by market

Each market tests windows to its own standards. Agree the governing standard and the required classes at enquiry stage, because they decide the profile depth, gaskets, glass and hardware.

Configuration

Configuring windows for a project

A window quotation is only as good as the information behind it. These are the decisions to make, ideally before tender:

  • Type and size of every window, usually listed in a window schedule with references to elevations
  • Opening direction and handing, plus restrictors for child safety on upper floors
  • Glass build-up: double or triple glazing, Low-E coating, argon fill, laminated or toughened panes where codes require safety glass
  • Performance: wind pressure for the building height and location, air and water classes, Uw and acoustic targets
  • Finish: colour, coating standard, and whether inside and outside colours differ
  • Hardware and accessories: handle style, lock type, insect screens, trickle vents, sill and flashing details

Quality

Where window quality is decided

Profile design

Section depth, wall thickness and chamber layout set wind-load resistance and the space available for thermal breaks and hardware.

Extrusion accuracy

Consistent dimensions mean corners close tightly, gaskets seal evenly and hardware fits without adjustment.

Surface finish

Coating thickness, pre-treatment and colour consistency across batches decide how the façade looks in ten years.

Fabrication

Accurate cutting, corner crimping, drainage slots and sealant application decide whether the window leaks.

Glazing

Correct setting blocks and glazing gaskets protect the glass edge and keep the sash square.

Installation

Fixing, packing, sill flashing and perimeter sealing are where many leaks start; good details and instructions matter.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) window systems: frequently asked questions

Do you supply finished aluminium windows or only profiles?

Profiles only. Metline supplies the extruded profiles, thermal breaks, gaskets and accessories, cut and machined on request; your local fabricator assembles and glazes the windows.

Can you quote from CAD drawings or a window schedule?

Yes. A window schedule or elevations with sizes, types, quantities and opening directions is the best starting point. Add the project location, required standard, glass and finish and we can prepare a configured quotation.

Can one project mix several window types?

Yes, and a system makes this easy. Sliding, casement, tilt-and-turn, fixed and awning windows can share the same colour, glass build-up and hardware family, so the building looks consistent.

When should a project choose thermal break windows?

Whenever the building is heated or air-conditioned and has to meet an energy code, or where condensation on frames is a concern. In mild climates with natural ventilation, non-thermally-broken windows can still be appropriate for cost reasons.

Can windows be built to EN 14351-1, NAFS or AS 2047?

Yes. Tell us the governing standard and the required air, water, wind and thermal classes. The profile system, glass and hardware are then selected to suit, and supporting test documentation is confirmed with the order.

How are windows packed for export?

Frames are protected with film and corner guards, then packed in crates or on stillages labelled by building, floor and opening, with a packing list that matches the window schedule.

Planning windows for a building project?

Send your window schedule, drawings and project location. We will propose a window profile series and quote delivered supply.
