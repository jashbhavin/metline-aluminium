Aluminium Picket Railing Systems & Component Kits

An aluminium picket railing is a guard made of extruded posts, a top rail, a bottom rail and closely spaced vertical pickets, used along balconies, decks, terraces and stairs. Extruded from 6063 or 6005A alloy and powder coated, it meets guard height and opening rules without rusting or needing repainting. Metline supplies the posts, rails, pickets and fittings to railing fabricators, installers and projects worldwide.

Overview

What is an aluminium picket railing?

A picket railing (often called a baluster or spindle railing) is the most common guard style for residential and light commercial edges. Vertical pickets sit between a top and a bottom rail, and the assembled sections are fixed to posts that carry the loads into the deck, slab or wall.

The picket format is popular for a practical reason: building codes limit the size of any opening in a guard, and evenly spaced vertical bars make that limit easy to meet and easy to inspect. Vertical pickets also give children no foothold, which is why many codes and guidance documents prefer them to horizontal rails in homes.

Aluminium suits the job because every part can be extruded to shape: hollow posts with internal screw ports, top rails with a channel that hides the picket ends, and bottom rails with drainage. The parts are light to carry onto balconies and stairs, and an architectural powder coat keeps the colour for many years with only occasional washing.

Components of a picket railing

  • Posts: end, line, corner and stair posts, with base plates or fascia brackets
  • Top rail and bottom rail, pre-punched for pickets at the required spacing
  • Pickets in square, rectangular or round section, cut to height
  • Rail brackets (fixed or swivel for stairs), post caps, skirts and end caps
  • Stainless steel fixings, nylon isolation washers and touch-up paint

Height, opening and load rules for picket guards

A railing on a raised edge is a life-safety component, so most of its geometry is set by the building code of the project location. These are the figures buyers ask about most often; amendments are common, so check the edition adopted locally.

Because picket spacing is measured as a clear gap, the centre-to-centre spacing depends on the picket size. With a 19 mm picket and a 95 mm clear gap, pickets sit at 114 mm centres, or about nine pickets per metre of rail. Fabricators usually punch rails a little tighter than the limit so that site tolerance never pushes a gap over it.

The gap under the bottom rail counts as an opening too. Set the bottom rail so the space to the deck is below the code limit, or the railing will fail inspection even if the pickets are correct.

Installation

Post mounting options and what governs them

Surface mount

A post welded or bolted to a base plate sits on top of the deck or slab. Quick to install on concrete; on timber decks the blocking under the plate, not the aluminium, is often the weak link.

Fascia mount

Brackets fix the post to the face of the slab edge or rim joist. Frees the walking surface and gives a clean balcony edge, but the edge must be strong enough to take the bolt loads.

Core-drilled / embedded

The post is grouted into a hole drilled in concrete. Very stiff and tidy; use non-shrink grout and isolate the aluminium from wet cement.

Wall mount

Rail ends fix to a wall with brackets or flanges, removing an end post where the railing meets the building.

Stair posts

Raked sections follow the stair pitch, typically 30 – 37 degrees, with swivel brackets that adjust to the actual angle on site.

Corner posts

Take rails from two directions; corners stiffen a run, so line posts between corners can sometimes be lighter.

Railing posts almost always fail by bending at the base. A 200 lb (0.89 kN) load on a 42 in (1067 mm) post creates a base moment of about 0.95 kN·m before any safety factor. A 50 × 50 × 3 mm 6063-T6 tube has a section modulus of roughly 8,300 mm³, so the stress is about 114 MPa against a specified minimum yield of 170 MPa. That margin explains why 50 – 60 mm posts are standard for homes, and why taller commercial guards move to 6005A-T6, thicker walls or internal reinforcement.

Black textured powder is the most requested colour for picket railings, followed by white, bronze and grey. Textured finishes hide small scratches and fingerprints better than gloss. For balconies within a few hundred metres of the sea, ask for a pre-treatment approved for seaside use and plan a fresh-water rinse a few times a year.

Applications

Where picket railings are used

  • Balconies and multifamily housing: 1100 mm or 42 in guards on fascia brackets, matched in colour to the window frames, with profiles often cut and numbered per floor.
  • Stairs and landings: raked picket sections with swivel brackets, plus a separate graspable handrail where the code requires one.
  • Decks, terraces and walkways: 36 in modular kits on surface-mount posts for timber or composite decks.
  • Commercial and public buildings: heavier posts and rails for schools, hotels, mezzanines and podium decks, where line loads are higher.
  • Pool surrounds: picket railing styles extend into pool fencing with self-closing gates, though pool barriers follow their own height rules.

On a typical balcony project, the architect fixes the height and colour, the engineer sizes posts and fixings for the occupancy load, and the supplier converts the elevations into a cutting list. Profiles are then labelled by unit and floor so installers do not have to sort parts on site.

For most homes and apartment blocks, pickets give the best balance of cost, compliance and durability. Glass is chosen where the view justifies the cost, and many projects combine both: glass on the view side, pickets on stairs and side returns.

Ordering

How to order picket railing profiles

  1. Share drawingsPlans and elevations, railing heights, run lengths, stair pitches, mounting method and the code that applies.
  2. Confirm the profile setWe propose post, rail and picket sections and a picket spacing that keeps every gap under the limit.
  3. Choose finishColour (RAL or wood-grain), coating standard and any matching gate or handrail.
  4. InspectDimensional checks, coating thickness and adhesion tests; mill certificates to EN 10204 3.1 for the extrusions.
  5. Pack and shipProfiles and parts labelled per elevation, protected with film or sleeves and packed in export cases on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.

Profiles can be supplied in standard modular lengths, cut to project drawings, or packed by part number with spare parts for distributors that sell their own railing kits.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) picket railing: FAQ

How far apart should railing pickets be?

Close enough that a 4 in (100 mm) sphere cannot pass between them in the US and UK, or a 125 mm sphere in Australia. In practice that means a clear gap of about 90 – 98 mm. With 19 mm pickets this works out to roughly 114 mm centres.

What height should an aluminium picket railing be?

In the US, 36 in for homes under the IRC and 42 in for other buildings under the IBC. In the UK, 900 mm on stairs and 1100 mm at balconies; in Australia, 1000 mm. Always check the local code edition.

Which alloy is used for aluminium railing posts?

6063-T6 is standard for residential posts. For tall or heavily loaded commercial guards, 6005A-T6 or 6061-T6 gives a higher minimum yield strength in the same section. Pickets and rails are normally 6063-T5 for the best coated finish.

Can picket railings be used on stairs?

Yes. Stair sections are raked to the stair pitch with swivel brackets. Codes usually allow a slightly larger opening on stair sides and a 6 in triangle at the tread and riser, and a separate graspable handrail may be required.

Are aluminium railings welded or bolted together?

Both exist. Modular systems are mechanically fastened, ship flat and install without site welding. Welded panels are stiffer and have no visible fixings but are bulkier to ship and harder to adjust on site.

Will aluminium railings corrode near the sea?

Aluminium does not rust, and a coastal-grade powder coat over a suitable pre-treatment performs well. Use stainless steel fixings with isolation washers and rinse salt off a few times a year to keep the finish in good condition.
