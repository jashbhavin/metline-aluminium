Outdoor living systems

Outdoor Aluminium Solutions

Outdoor aluminium solutions are the structures that turn gardens, terraces, roofs and car parks into usable space: pergolas, hardtop gazebos, sunrooms, screen rooms, greenhouses, carports, sheds, fences and privacy walls. All of them rely on the same strengths of aluminium extrusions: no rust, low weight, slim strong sections and durable coated finishes. Metline supplies the profiles, sheet and accessories behind outdoor systems to makers and installers, selected for each site's use, climate and target market, to customers worldwide.

Overview

Why aluminium for outdoor structures?

Outdoor structures live in the harshest conditions a building product faces: full sun, rain, coastal salt, temperature swings, wind and, in some regions, snow. Timber needs regular treatment and eventually rots; steel rusts the moment its coating is damaged; PVC softens and yellows under strong UV. Aluminium forms its own protective oxide film, keeps its strength across the outdoor temperature range and can be coated to last for decades.

Extrusion is the other reason aluminium dominates outdoor living. A single profile can combine a structural beam, a hidden gutter, a cable channel for lighting, a screw port for end caps and a track for screens. That integration gives clean, modern lines with no visible downpipes or wiring, and makes systems quick to assemble from pre-cut kits.

Weight matters too. Aluminium has about one third the density of steel (2.70 g/cm³ against 7.85 g/cm³), so a 4 × 4 m pergola frame can be carried and erected by a small crew without lifting equipment, footings are smaller, and shipping costs per unit are lower. For roof terraces and balconies, where every kilogram loads the building structure, that difference often decides the material.

What every outdoor aluminium system should deliver

  • Structure designed for local wind and, where relevant, snow loads
  • Concealed drainage that takes water away from people and footings
  • An architectural-grade finish matched to the site's UV and corrosion exposure
  • Stainless steel fasteners and isolation from dissimilar metals
  • Accessories such as lighting, screens and glass that fit the system, not added later

Shade structures

Pergolas and gazebos

Pergolas and gazebos form the largest group of outdoor aluminium systems. Both provide shade; the differences lie in the roof and in how the structure is supported.

Louvered pergola

Rotating aluminium blades adjust shade and ventilation and close to keep rain out. The most popular modern pergola type for homes and hospitality.

Motorised pergola

A linear motor turns the blades from a remote, switch or home-automation system, with optional rain and wind sensors. Suits hotels, restaurants and premium villas.

Retractable roof pergola

A fabric or slatted roof that slides back to open the sky completely and closes for shelter. Gives the widest range between fully open and covered.

Wall-mounted pergola

A ledger beam fixes to the building, halving the number of posts. Works as an extension of the house over a terrace or doorway.

Freestanding pergola

Four or more posts with no connection to a building. Placed anywhere on a site, such as a garden centrepiece or poolside.

Hardtop gazebo

A freestanding shelter with a solid fixed or louvered roof. Available in square, rectangular and hexagonal plans with flat, single-slope, gable or double-tier roofs.

Residential gazebos and pergolas are commonly sold in footprints from about 3 × 3 m (10 × 10 ft) to 5 × 5 m (16 × 16 ft). Popular intermediate sizes are 3 × 3.6 m (10 × 12 ft), 3.6 × 3.6 m (12 × 12 ft) and 3.6 × 4.9 m (12 × 16 ft). Post sections typically range from about 80 × 80 mm to 120 × 120 mm for residential units and 150 × 150 mm and larger for commercial structures and multi-bay canopies.

Enclosed spaces

Sunrooms, solariums and screen rooms

When the aim is to use outdoor space through more of the year, the structure gains walls. How far to enclose it depends on climate, budget and how the room will be used.

TypeConstructionBest for
Screen roomAluminium frame with insect mesh panels; solid or louvered roofWarm, humid climates where airflow matters and insects are a nuisance
Three-season sunroomNon-insulated or lightly insulated frame, single glazing or acrylic panelsSpring to autumn use on a moderate budget
Four-season sunroomThermal break profiles, insulated roof panels, Low-E double or triple glazingYear-round living space in cold or hot climates
SolariumGlazed walls and glazed roofMaximum daylight and views; needs strong shading and ventilation
Solar (PV) roof roomGlazed or panel roof incorporating photovoltaic modulesCombining shelter with on-site electricity generation

A sunroom performs only as well as its weakest component. For year-round rooms, specify thermally broken profiles, insulated glass with solar control, an insulated roof, cross-ventilation (opening vents low and high) and drainage that copes with heavy rain on a large glazed roof. In hot climates, shading and ventilation are more important than U-value; in cold climates, the reverse. Screen rooms usually use fibreglass mesh around 18 × 16 strands per inch, or finer 20 × 20 mesh where small biting insects are a problem; aluminium mesh resists pets and damage better.

Utility structures

Carports, sheds, greenhouses and screens

Carports

Single or double carports with flat or pitched roofs of aluminium sheet, sandwich panel or polycarbonate. Cantilever designs keep posts on one side for easy parking. Snow load and wind uplift govern the beam and post sizes; posts near driveways benefit from impact protection.

Storage sheds

Aluminium frames with coated sheet or composite panels. Unlike steel sheds, they do not rust at cut edges or fixings. Lockable doors, vents against condensation and a raised floor or base keep contents dry.

Greenhouses

Slim 6063 glazing bars carry glass or polycarbonate with maximum light transmission. Roof vents, louvre windows and gutters are built into the profile system.

ZIP and side screens

Vertical roller screens with zip-locked edges running in aluminium side tracks give wind, sun and insect protection to pergolas and gazebos while staying lighter than glass.

Privacy walls

Slatted, louvred or perforated aluminium panels on posts screen patios, hot tubs and neighbours. Solid infill catches more wind, so posts and footings must be sized for it.

Bin and utility enclosures

Small slatted enclosures hide bins, air-conditioning units, meters and pool equipment in the same colour and style as nearby fences and screens.

Boundary fences, pool fences and balcony railings use the same profile families. They are covered in detail on our fence and railing systems, slat fencing and privacy fence and gate pages.

Materials

Common alloys and tempers for outdoor systems

Nearly all outdoor profiles are 6xxx-series Al-Mg-Si alloys, which extrude well, take coatings evenly and are age-hardened to useful strength. The choice between them depends on the load a member carries and the surface finish required. Values below are EN 755-2 minimums for thin-walled profiles.

Alloy and temperMin. RmMin. Rp0.2Typical outdoor use
6063-T5175 MPa130 MPaMost pergola, fence, screen, greenhouse and sunroom profiles
6063-T6215 MPa170 MPaLonger beams and taller posts
6060-T66215 MPa160 MPaEuropean window, door and sunroom systems
6063A-T6230 MPa190 MPaSlightly stronger alternative to 6063 for architectural profiles
6005A-T6270 MPa225 MPaHeavy-duty posts, carport beams, multi-bay frames
6061-T6260 MPa240 MPaHighly loaded beams, base plates, brackets and connectors
6463-T5 / T6——Bright-anodised decorative trims where surface lustre matters

Minimum properties fall slightly for thicker walls. North American projects specify ASTM B221, where 6063-T5 is 152 MPa tensile and 110 MPa yield minimum and 6061-T6 is 260 MPa and 240 MPa.

As a rule of thumb, 6063-T5 covers the majority of outdoor members; move to 6063-T6 or 6005A-T6 when spans grow, and reserve 6061-T6 for parts where strength matters more than surface finish. Roof sheet, fascias and shed panels are usually coated 3003, 3105 or 5005 sheet; see our 5005 sheet and colour-coated sheet pages.

Surface finish

Colours and surface finishes

The finish decides how an outdoor structure looks after ten years. Select it by exposure: UV intensity, distance from the sea, industrial pollution and how often the structure will be cleaned.

Within about 1 km of the sea or near swimming pools, specify a marine-grade pretreatment (for example Qualicoat Seaside), a durable coating class and A4 (316) stainless steel fasteners. Dark colours absorb more heat and expand more; allow for movement at long runs of fence and pergola beams.

Engineering

Design, hardware and installation considerations

  • Loads: design to local codes, for example EN 1991 (Eurocode 1) in Europe, ASCE 7 in the United States, IS 875 in India and AS/NZS 1170 in Australia; check the aluminium members to EN 1999 or the Aluminum Design Manual.
  • Wind on enclosures: adding glass, screens or solid privacy panels greatly increases wind load on posts and anchors compared with an open frame.
  • Snow: in snowy regions, state the design snow load at the outset; louvre blades, polycarbonate and long spans are the usual limits.
  • Drainage: concealed gutters in beams and downpipes in posts need outlets sized for local rainfall and directed away from paths and footings.
  • Fixings and contact: use stainless fasteners, isolate aluminium from copper, bare steel and treated timber, and avoid trapping water at post bases.
  • Foundations: concrete pads or slabs with anchors sized for uplift; on timber decks, fix to the structural frame, not the boards.
  • Hardware: hinges, locks, rollers and motors should be rated for outdoor use and for the panel weights involved; preferred hardware brands can be matched on request.
Selection

How to choose the right outdoor aluminium product

  1. Define the useShade and airflow points to a pergola or gazebo; seasonal or year-round living to a screen room or sunroom; growing, parking or storage to a greenhouse, carport or shed.
  2. Check the siteFreestanding or against a wall, ground or roof terrace, available footings, and access for installation.
  3. Set the climate requirementsWind and snow loads, rainfall, UV, coastal exposure and whether heating or cooling will be used inside.
  4. Choose featuresManual or motorised roof, lighting, side screens, glass walls, insect mesh, privacy panels, heaters.
  5. Confirm finish and documentsColour and coating class, packing and labelling, and any certificates or test reports needed in the target market.

Export orders are packed with profiles protected by film and wrapping, small parts bagged and labelled by part number, and pallets or wooden crates suited to sea freight. Mixed containers combining profiles for several systems are common.

FAQ

Outdoor aluminium (aluminum) structures: FAQ

What is the difference between a pergola, a gazebo and a sunroom?

A pergola is the most open: a frame with a slatted, louvered or retractable roof, often attached to a wall. A gazebo is a freestanding shelter with a solid fixed or louvered roof. A sunroom is fully enclosed with glass and, in four-season versions, insulated for year-round use.

Are aluminium pergolas suitable for windy areas?

Yes, when the system is designed for the local wind speed. Post size, beam depth, anchor bolts and footings all scale with wind load, and closed louvre roofs or side screens increase it. Ask for the design wind speed or pressure the system has been checked for.

Should I choose 6063-T5 or 6061-T6?

6063-T5 suits most outdoor profiles because it extrudes into thin, detailed shapes with an excellent coated finish. 6061-T6 is about 40 – 50 % stronger and is used for heavily loaded beams, brackets and connectors. Many systems combine both.

Which finish lasts longest outdoors?

PVDF coatings and Class 3 powders keep colour and gloss longest under strong sun. Anodising does not peel and suits metallic looks. Standard polyester powder is economical for moderate climates. Near the sea, the pretreatment and fastener choice matter as much as the topcoat.

Is a screen room or a sunroom better?

A screen room keeps airflow and blocks insects at lower cost, ideal for warm climates. A sunroom keeps out weather and, if insulated, can be heated or cooled for year-round use. Some designs combine both with removable glass or interchangeable panels.

What information should I provide for a quotation?

The product type, project use, site location and climate, approximate dimensions, freestanding or wall-mounted, open or enclosed, features such as motorisation or lighting, colour and finish, quantity and delivery destination.
