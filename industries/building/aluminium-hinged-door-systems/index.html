Aluminium Hinged Door Systems

An aluminium hinged door system is a complete swing door: extruded frame and leaf profiles, glazing, gaskets, threshold, hinges and locks designed to work together. It is the most common door type in buildings because it seals tightly, locks securely and suits everything from apartment entrances to office corridors and hotel balconies. Metline supplies hinged door profiles, thermal-break sections and accessories to door fabricators for project door schedules worldwide.

Overview

What is an aluminium hinged door?

A hinged door, also called a swing door or side-hung door, rotates on hinges fixed to one vertical edge of the frame. When it closes, the leaf presses against continuous gaskets on all four sides and the lock draws it tight. That compression seal is why a hinged door outperforms a sliding door of the same quality on air tightness, water tightness, sound insulation and security.

In an aluminium system, the frame (outer profile fixed into the wall opening) and the leaf (the moving part) are extrusions designed as a pair. The profiles carry grooves for gaskets, chambers for hinges and lock gear, a glazing rebate, drainage paths and, in insulated systems, polyamide thermal break strips. Hardware is chosen to suit the leaf weight and traffic, and the threshold detail decides how well the door keeps water out at floor level.

Hinged doors are specified for almost every type of opening: front doors and apartment entrances, balcony and terrace doors, office and corridor doors, plant-room and service doors, and the active leaves within shopfront and curtain wall entrances. Because one system can produce all these variants with the same sightlines and colours, project teams often build a whole door schedule from a single hinged door series.

Advantages of aluminium hinged doors

  • Compression seals on four sides give the best weather and acoustic performance of any opening door
  • Multi-point locking and robust hinges provide strong security
  • Slim, stiff aluminium profiles carry large glass with narrow sightlines
  • Thermal break versions meet modern energy codes
  • No rust, no warping and low maintenance compared with steel and timber doors
Configurations

Hinged door types and configurations

Single-leaf door

One active leaf for standard openings, typically up to about 1.0 – 1.2 m wide. Used for apartment, office, side and service doors.

Double-leaf (French) door

Two leaves meeting at a central mullion or with an astragal on the inactive leaf. Gives a wide clear opening for lobbies, terraces and public entrances.

Inward-opening

The usual choice for residential entrances and balcony doors in Europe; the leaf is protected from weather when open and hinges are inside for security.

Outward-opening

Saves interior space and resists wind-driven rain well because pressure pushes the leaf into its seals. Required on many escape routes, where doors must open in the direction of travel.

With sidelights and transoms

Fixed glazed panels beside or above the leaf to bring daylight into halls and lobbies and to fill wide or tall openings. Frame and mullion design must carry the extra wind load.

Slim-frame and high-performance

Narrow-sightline leaves for modern architecture, or deep, multi-chamber thermal break systems with triple glazing for low-energy buildings.

Materials

Profile alloys, wall thickness and system depth

Door and window systems in Europe are mainly extruded in 6060, while 6063 is the usual alloy in North America, India and much of Asia. Both are low-alloy Al-Mg-Si grades with excellent surface quality for anodising and powder coating. The temper is chosen for the stiffness the leaf needs.

Performance

Performance classes and test standards

External doors are tested as complete units. In Europe, EN 14351-1 is the product standard behind CE and UKCA marking, and each property is declared as a class. In North America, swing doors are rated under the North American Fenestration Standard (AAMA/WDMA/CSA 101/I.S.2/A440) with a performance class and design pressure. Hardware

Hinges, locks, thresholds and seals

Hinges

Barrel or butt hinges clamped into the profile chambers, surface-fixed 3D-adjustable hinges, or concealed hinges for a clean look. Choose by leaf weight and size, and to EN 1935 grades for durability; use three hinges on tall or heavy leaves.

Locks

Single-point locks for internal doors; multi-point locks with hooks or rollers at top, middle and bottom for external doors, which pull the leaf evenly into its seals and resist forcing.

Handles and cylinders

Lever-lever, lever-pull or panic hardware, with profile cylinders to EN 1303 and anti-snap options on entrances.

Thresholds

Standard drained thresholds for exposed doors; low or flush thresholds (often 20 mm or less) for accessibility, which need more care with drainage and sill detailing.

Seals

Continuous EPDM gaskets in two or three lines, corner-moulded or vulcanised at joints, plus a drop seal or brush at the threshold where a low sill is used.

Closers and stays

Overhead or concealed closers for public doors, and restrictors or stays to stop wind slamming external leaves.

Hardware and profile must be compatible: hinge and lock positions are machined into specific chambers of the profile, and the leaf weight (frame plus glass) must be within the hinge rating. Double-glazed leaves of 1.0 × 2.4 m weigh roughly 60 – 80 kg, and triple-glazed or laminated leaves considerably more.

Installation

Design and installation checklist

  • Swing direction and handing: fix inward or outward and left or right hand for every door on the schedule before fabrication; escape doors normally open outward.
  • Clear width: the effective passage is the leaf width minus the frame rebates and hardware, not the structural opening. Check accessibility rules (for example 800 – 850 mm clear in many codes, 32 in under ADA).
  • Threshold and floor level: agree finished floor levels inside and out, drainage direction and whether a low threshold is required.
  • Fixing: anchor the frame at regular centres and close to hinges and lock keeps, with packers under the hinge jamb so the leaf cannot drop.
  • Perimeter sealing: use membranes, pre-compressed tape or backer rod and sealant between frame and wall; most leaks happen here, not through the door.
  • Isolation: keep aluminium away from wet mortar and steel contact; use stainless fixings.
  • Adjustment: after installation, adjust hinges so the leaf meets its seals evenly and the lock engages without force.
Applications

Typical projects

Apartments and housing

Entrance and balcony doors in thermal break systems, specified once and repeated across hundreds of units.

Offices and commercial

Glazed corridor, meeting-room and side entrance doors, with closers and access-control hardware.

Hotels and mixed use

Guest-room balcony doors with good acoustic glass, and paired lobby doors with sidelights and transoms.

Schools, hospitals and public buildings

Robust, high-cycle doors with panic or emergency hardware on escape routes and laminated safety glass.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) hinged doors: FAQ

Should an external aluminium door open inward or outward?

Inward-opening doors are common for homes and balconies because the hinges are inside and the leaf is sheltered when open. Outward-opening doors save interior space, resist wind-driven rain better and are required on many escape routes. Decide per door, taking into account threshold design and local code.

What makes a hinged door more weathertight than a sliding door?

When a hinged leaf closes, the lock pulls it against continuous compression gaskets on all four sides. Sliding doors rely on brushes or lift-slide seals at moving joints, which are inherently leakier. A good hinged door typically reaches higher air and water classes from the same system family.

Are aluminium hinged doors energy efficient?

With a polyamide thermal break, multi-chamber profiles and Low-E double or triple glazing, aluminium hinged doors typically reach door U-values of about 1.2 – 2.0 W/m²K, depending on system depth, glass and size.

How heavy a leaf can aluminium door hinges carry?

It depends on the hinge type and number. Standard clamped barrel hinges suit typical residential leaves, while heavy-duty and concealed hinges are rated for heavier glazed leaves. Always compare the leaf weight, including glass, with the hinge manufacturer's rating, and use three hinges on tall leaves.

Which alloy are aluminium door profiles made from?

Usually 6060-T66 in European systems and 6063-T5 or T6 elsewhere, extruded to EN 755 or ASTM B221. Both give a fine surface for powder coating and anodising; reinforcing profiles may use 6005A or 6061.

Do you supply assembled hinged doors to a door schedule?

No. Send the schedule with sizes, swing, handing, glazing, hardware and finish for each door, and we supply the profiles, cut and machined on request, with hardware and accessories labelled by door number; your fabricator assembles and glazes the doors.
