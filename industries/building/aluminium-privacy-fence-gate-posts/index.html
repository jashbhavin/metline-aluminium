Aluminium Privacy Fence, Gates & Posts

An aluminium privacy fence uses extruded horizontal or vertical slats held between posts to block views into gardens, patios, pool areas and side yards. Full-privacy panels interlock so no gaps remain; semi-private panels leave a small gap for light and air. Overview

What makes an aluminium privacy fence different

Most aluminium fences are open: pickets or bars that mark a boundary but do not hide anything. A privacy fence closes the view. That single change affects every part of the system, from the slat profile to the post size, the footing and the gate hardware, because a solid face catches the full force of the wind.

Timber has long been the default privacy fence, but it warps, splits, rots at ground level and needs staining every few years. Vinyl does not rot, but it can sag in heat and become brittle in cold. Steel is strong but rusts wherever the coating is chipped. Extruded aluminium avoids all of these: it does not absorb water, it keeps its shape across normal outdoor temperatures, and a properly applied powder coat protects the surface for many years.

A modern aluminium privacy system is modular. Hollow slats slide into channels fixed to the posts, so panels can be assembled on site without welding and adjusted to the actual post spacing. Matching gates, posts, caps, skirts and trim come from the same profile family and the same colour batch, so the finished boundary looks like one product rather than a collection of parts.

Why buyers choose aluminium for privacy fencing

  • No rot, rust, warping or termite damage; washing is the only routine maintenance
  • Light hollow extrusions are easy to ship flat and handle on site
  • Interlocking slats give full screening with no gaps opening up over time
  • Panels, gates, posts and accessories match in profile and colour
  • Fully recyclable, with a high scrap value at the end of its life
System selection

Full privacy, semi-private, horizontal or vertical

Full privacy

Tongue-and-groove slats interlock edge to edge, leaving no sight lines. Best for backyards, side yards, pool areas and boundaries next to roads or neighbours. Highest wind load of any fence type.

Semi-private

Slats are separated by small spacers (typically 3/8, 1/2 or 1 in, about 10, 13 or 25 mm). Screens most views at an angle while letting light and some breeze through, which suits patios, café seating and gardens.

Horizontal slats

The dominant modern look. Long slats span between posts, so post spacing is limited by slat stiffness; a 72 in span is the usual maximum without a mid-support.

Vertical slats

Slats run between a top and bottom rail. Suits sloping ground, because panels can be stepped or raked, and reads as a more traditional board fence.

Louvred

Angled blades block direct views but allow air and light through. Useful for screening equipment such as air-conditioning units and pool pumps.

Mixed panels

Solid lower section with a slatted or lattice top, giving privacy at eye level and a lighter look above.

The gap you choose changes how private the fence feels and how hard it works. A 1/2 in gap between 4 in slats leaves about 11 % of the face open. That is too little to reduce wind load by much, but enough to stop the fence feeling like a solid wall. Larger gaps start to relieve wind pressure noticeably, at the cost of privacy when viewed straight on.

Gates

Matching privacy gates and screen gates

A gate is the most heavily used and most heavily loaded part of any fence. It hangs from one post, it is opened many times a day, and in a privacy fence its solid face acts as a sail when the wind blows. Gates in a matching system share the slat profile and colour of the panels, so the entrance reads as part of the fence.

Frame and bracing

A welded or corner-keyed perimeter frame keeps the gate square. Wide gates need a diagonal brace or anti-sag cable running from the top hinge corner to the bottom latch corner.

Hinges

Adjustable heavy-duty hinges, often self-closing, let installers correct small post movements after installation. Use three hinges on tall gates.

Latches and locks

Gravity, magnetic or keyed latches. Pool gates need self-closing, self-latching hardware with the release mounted high, as pool barrier rules require.

Gate posts

Gate posts carry the gate weight plus wind on its face. Specify thicker walls or a reinforcement insert, and a larger footing, than for line posts.

Double and drive gates

Two leaves meeting at a drop rod, or a sliding gate on a track, for vehicle openings wider than about 2 m.

Swing clearance

Check the ground slope in the swing path. On rising ground, a gate may need to open outward or be fitted with a rising hinge.

Posts

Posts, mounting methods and reinforcement profiles

Posts decide how the fence is installed and how well it stands up to wind and gate loads. Privacy systems typically use 4 × 4 in (about 100 mm) square posts, larger than the 2 – 2.5 in posts used on open picket fences, because the bending moment at the base is much higher.

Reinforcement profiles slide inside the post or clip behind the panel channel to raise stiffness without changing the visible size. Common shapes are T, L (for corners), box, C, U and I/H sections, usually extruded in 6063-T6 or 6005A-T6. A box insert that fills the post bore can roughly double the bending capacity of a thin-walled post, which is often cheaper than moving to a heavier outer section.

Dig and set (in-ground)

The standard method for soil. Posts are set in concrete footings below the frost line, giving the stiffest fence and the best resistance to wind.

Surface mount (concrete mount)

Posts bolted through a base plate to an existing slab, patio or retaining wall. No digging, ideal for renovation, but the slab edge distance and anchor type must suit the loads.

Core-drilled

Posts grouted into holes drilled in an existing slab. Stiffer than a base plate and hides the fixing, but needs sound concrete of adequate depth.

Engineering

Wind load, post bending and footing size

Wind is the design load for nearly every privacy fence. A solid fence of height h and bay width s sees a wind force roughly equal to the design wind pressure times the panel area, acting at about mid-height. The resulting moment at the base of each post is what sizes the post and its footing.

Case (worked example, net pressure 0.6 kPa)Force per bayBase momentStress in 4 × 4 in post, 2 mm wall
69 in (1.75 m) panel, 72 in bayabout 1.9 kNabout 1.8 kN·mabout 70 MPa
92 in (2.34 m) panel, 72 in bayabout 2.6 kNabout 3.1 kN·mabout 120 MPa
92 in panel with box reinforcement insertabout 2.6 kNabout 3.1 kN·mroughly halved

Illustrative figures only, for unfactored loads. A net pressure of 0.6 kPa corresponds to a moderate design wind; coastal and open sites can be much higher. Design values come from ASCE 7 (freestanding walls) or EN 1991-1-4 and must be checked by an engineer for the site.

Compare these stresses with the specified minimum yield of 6063-T6 extrusion, 170 MPa, and remember that design codes add load and resistance factors. The 69 in fence on plain 4 in posts has a comfortable margin; the 92 in fence does not, which is why tall panels and gates are supplied with reinforcement inserts or heavier-walled posts. Deflection matters too: aluminium is about one third as stiff as steel, so a post that is strong enough can still sway visibly if it is too slender.

Footings must resist the same moment by bearing against the soil. A common starting point is a hole about three times the post width in diameter (around 300 mm or 12 in for a 4 in post) and deep enough to reach below the local frost line, with at least one third of the above-ground height in the ground. Gate posts and corner posts usually get a deeper or wider footing than line posts.

Black and white are the stock colours for most privacy systems, with grey, bronze and wood-grain the next most popular. Timber remains the cheapest way to build a privacy fence, but over a long service life the cost of staining, repairs and board replacement often exceeds the extra up-front cost of aluminium. Vinyl competes on maintenance but offers fewer colours and less rigidity on tall panels. Steel is stronger but heavier, and every scratch is a potential rust point. For homeowners and developers who want a fence that still looks the same in fifteen years, aluminium is usually the better long-term value.

Planning

Planning rules and common mistakes

Before ordering, check what height of fence the site allows. Many US municipalities cap rear-yard fences at 6 ft and front-yard fences lower; in England, a boundary fence over 2 m (or over 1 m next to a road used by vehicles) normally needs planning permission. Homeowner association rules may also fix colour and style. Pool barriers have their own minimum heights and gate rules.

  • Using picket-fence posts for a privacy fence. Solid infill multiplies the wind load; posts and footings must be sized for it.
  • Ignoring the gate post. Gate posts carry weight and wind and need reinforcement and a larger footing.
  • Spanning too far. Horizontal slats sag or flutter beyond their design span; add a mid-support or reduce post spacing.
  • Mixing metals carelessly. Use stainless steel fixings with nylon washers and keep aluminium away from copper-treated timber and wet concrete.
  • Forgetting drainage and ground clearance. Leave a small gap under the bottom slat so water, leaves and soil do not collect against it.
  • Not planning for slopes. Decide early whether panels will be stepped or raked, because this affects post lengths and slat orientation.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) privacy fence: FAQ

How tall can an aluminium privacy fence be?

Standard panels are 46, 69 and about 92 in high. The limit is usually set by planning rules (often 6 ft in US rear yards, 2 m in England) and by wind: panels over about 6 ft need reinforced posts and larger footings.

What size posts does a privacy fence need?

Most aluminium privacy systems use 4 × 4 in (about 100 mm) posts. Gate posts, corner posts and posts on tall or exposed fences should have thicker walls or a reinforcement insert.

Is a semi-private fence better in windy areas?

Somewhat. Small gaps between slats make the fence feel lighter but only reduce wind load slightly. Only fairly open designs (louvres or wide gaps) cut wind load substantially. In windy sites, size posts and footings for the solid case.

Can posts be mounted on an existing concrete patio?

Yes. Surface-mounted posts bolt through a base plate into the slab, and a post skirt covers the anchors. The slab must be thick enough and the anchors far enough from the edge to carry the wind moment.

How wide can a privacy gate be?

Single gates are usually 40 in wide; 72 in wide gates are common with a diagonal anti-sag brace. Wider openings are better served by double gates or a sliding gate.

Does an aluminium privacy fence need maintenance?

Very little. Wash it with water and mild detergent once or twice a year, more often near the sea, check gate hinges and latches, and touch up any deep scratches in the coating.

Can I order panels without posts, or posts only?

Yes. Metline supplies the profiles and accessories individually, including posts, replacement slats, channels, reinforcement inserts and caps in matching colours; we do not supply complete fence kits.
