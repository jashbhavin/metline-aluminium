Aluminium Storefront & Shop Front Systems

An aluminium storefront (shop front) system is a framework of extruded mullions, transoms, sills and door frames that holds large display glass and commercial entrance doors at street level. It is lighter and simpler than curtain wall, spans one storey between floor and head, and can be thermally broken for energy codes. Metline supplies storefront framing and entrance door profiles with accessories, cut and labelled to drawings, to shop front fabricators and installers worldwide.

Overview

What is an aluminium storefront system?

A storefront is the glazed front of a shop, café, showroom, office lobby or mall unit. In façade terms it is a non-load-bearing framing system installed within a single storey: it sits on the floor slab or a sill and is fixed to the structure at the head, carrying its own weight and the wind load on the glass. That is the key difference from curtain wall, which hangs from the edges of floor slabs and runs continuously past several storeys.

A complete shop front system normally includes vertical mullions and horizontal transoms, head and sill members, glazing stops (beads), gaskets and setting blocks, door frames, entrance doors with their hardware, sidelights and transom lights above the door, and the anchors and flashings that connect it all to the building. Because the framing is a family of extrusions, the same profiles can be used for a single small shop or repeated across every unit of a shopping centre.

Aluminium is the dominant storefront material because 6063 extrusions give straight, accurate members with crisp edges that anodise or powder coat to a high standard, at a weight and cost far below steel. Thermally broken versions let the same look meet modern energy codes on exterior façades.

Configurations

Storefront system configurations

Thermal storefront

A polyamide thermal break or poured-and-debridged pocket separates inner and outer aluminium. Accepts 25 mm (1 in) insulating units with Low-E glass and is required by most energy codes on exterior storefronts.

Non-thermal storefront

Simpler and lower cost, for interior mall frontages, partitions and mild climates. Usually glazed with 6 – 10 mm monolithic toughened or laminated glass.

Centre-glazed

Glass sits in the middle of the frame depth, giving equal shadow lines inside and out. The most common general-purpose layout.

Front (offset) glazed

Glass sits close to the outer face for a flatter external look, with deeper frame showing inside. Useful when matching adjacent systems or details.

Flush or structural silicone glazed

Vertical joints closed with silicone or flush covers, so glass appears to run continuously. Needs higher fabrication precision and qualified structural silicone work.

Reinforced storefront

Steel or heavier aluminium reinforcing in mullions for taller bays, heavier glass or higher wind loads.

Glazing

Choosing storefront glass

Toughened glass

Standard for doors, sidelights and low display glass; required as safety glazing in hazardous locations under EN 12600, 16 CFR 1201 and similar codes.

Laminated glass

Holds together when broken, so it resists smash-and-grab and protects people in busy streets and malls. Also cuts UV fading of displayed goods.

Insulating glass units

Typically 25 mm (1 in) overall, for exterior storefronts in hot or cold climates, with Low-E coatings for energy codes.

Low-iron clear glass

Removes the green tint of standard float glass for the truest view of products, popular for luxury retail.

Tinted and solar-control glass

Reduces glare and heat gain on sunny frontages; balance against visibility of the display.

Frosted or patterned glass

Privacy for clinics, offices and partitions while keeping daylight.

Because mullions span between sill and head, their design is normally governed by deflection under wind: typical limits are L/175 of the span, or 19 mm (3/4 in), whichever is less, so that glass edges are not overstressed. Taller bays need deeper sections or reinforcement rather than a stronger alloy. See 6063 extrusions.

For chain stores, specify the colour by RAL or a manufacturer code and approve a sample per production batch so that frontages in different cities match. Black anodised and dark bronze remain the most popular commercial colours; clear anodised suits offices and malls.

Typical projects

Where storefront systems are used

  • Retail stores: full-height display glass with a centre-glazed frame and a narrow or medium stile entrance door to maximise product visibility.
  • Shopping malls: non-thermal interior frontages with consistent framing across many tenants, often with sliding or folding entrances.
  • Restaurants and cafés: bifold or sliding shop fronts that open to outdoor seating, with laminated glass toward the street.
  • Office lobbies and showrooms: thermal storefront with Low-E IGUs and pairs of wide or medium stile doors.
  • Renovations: replacement of old steel or timber shop fronts with thermal aluminium framing fitted into the existing opening.
Cost

What affects the cost of a storefront system

  • Frontage width and height, and the number of mullions and transoms
  • Thermal or non-thermal framing, and the frame size
  • Glass type: monolithic toughened, laminated, insulating Low-E, low-iron
  • Number and type of doors and the hardware grade (closers, panic devices, automatic operators)
  • Finish: anodised, powder, PVDF or wood-grain, and colour-matching requirements
  • Wind load and any testing or certification required for the market
  • Processing scope, packing and the freight route to site

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) storefront systems: FAQ

What is the difference between storefront and curtain wall?

Storefront is installed within a single storey, supported at sill and head, with shallower frames and drainage to the sill. Curtain wall hangs from the floor-slab edges, spans several storeys and has deeper frames and zoned drainage. Storefront costs less but is limited in height and wind load.

Which alloy is used for aluminum storefront framing?

6063-T5 is standard because it extrudes accurately and anodises well. 6063-T6 or 6061-T6 can be used for members needing extra strength, and steel reinforcement is added in mullions for very tall bays.

Do I need a thermal storefront?

For exterior storefronts in most climates and under modern energy codes, yes. A thermally broken frame with Low-E insulating glass reduces heat loss and gain and prevents condensation. Non-thermal framing suits interior mall units and partitions.

What size is standard storefront framing?

Common North American sizes are 1-3/4 × 4 in, 1-3/4 × 4-1/2 in, 2 × 4-1/2 in and 2 × 6 in (face × depth). Metric projects often use similar 50 × 100 – 150 mm sections or custom profiles.

What glass is used in storefronts?

Toughened glass for doors and low glazing, laminated glass for security and safety, and 25 mm insulating Low-E units on exterior thermal storefronts. Low-iron glass is chosen where the clearest view of products matters.

Can Metline supply cut and labelled framing for site installation?

Yes. Framing can be cut, drilled, notched and labelled by elevation or position, with packing lists, photographs and mill certificates, ready for installers to assemble.
