Aluminium Bifold Doors

An aluminium bifold door is a row of glazed panels hinged together that fold and stack to one or both sides, opening almost the full width of a wall to a garden, terrace or street. Aluminium is the preferred frame material because its strength keeps sightlines slim and panels true at heights up to about 3 m. Metline supplies bifold door profiles and thermally broken sections in 6063 and 6061, with matching hardware, accessories and finishes, to door fabricators and project buyers worldwide.

Overview

How an aluminium bifold door works

Each panel of a bifold door is joined to the next by hinges on alternate edges, so the panels fold like a concertina. Rollers on every second hinge line run in a track at the top or bottom of the frame and carry the weight, while guides keep the panels aligned. When fully open, the panels stack neatly at the side of the opening, typically occupying 70 – 100 mm of stack width per panel.

There are two main carrying principles. In a bottom-rolling system the weight runs on a stainless-steel or engineered-polymer track in the threshold, so the head of the frame need only guide the panels; this puts very little load on the lintel and is the usual choice for heavy glazed panels. In a top-hung system the panels hang from rollers in the head track, so the threshold can be almost flush and free of debris, but the structure above must carry the full door weight without deflecting more than a few millimetres.

Aluminium dominates the premium end of this market for three reasons. First, extruded 6063 profiles are stiff and strong enough to hold large double- or triple-glazed panels with frame-and-sash sightlines of around 110 – 140 mm where two panels meet, far slimmer than timber or uPVC. Second, aluminium does not swell, warp or creep, so the many hinges and rollers stay aligned for decades. Third, powder-coated or anodised aluminium needs little maintenance, even on coastal and high-UV sites.

Where bifold doors make sense

  • Openings where you want the whole width to open, not just one sliding panel
  • Kitchens, living rooms and garden rooms leading onto a terrace or patio
  • Restaurants, cafés, bars and hotel lounges that open to outdoor seating
  • Shopfronts and showrooms that open fully in good weather
  • Interior room dividers that fold away when the spaces are joined
Types and configurations

Types of aluminium bifold doors

Exterior and patio bifolds

Thermally broken, fully weather-sealed doors for the building envelope, with drained thresholds and multi-point locking. The most common type.

Slim-frame bifolds

Narrower interlocks and sashes for maximum glass. Usually limited to lighter panels or interior use unless the system is specially reinforced.

Interior glass bifolds

Non-thermal frames with single toughened or laminated glass to divide rooms, offices or hotel suites without losing light.

Commercial bifolds

Heavier profiles, high-cycle hardware, panic or anti-trap features and robust thresholds for restaurants, bars, shops and public buildings.

Corner bifolds

Two runs meeting at a corner with no fixed post, so the whole corner opens. Needs careful structural design above the opening.

Heavy-duty concealed-hinge bifolds

Deeper frames (often 100 mm+) with hidden hinges and tall panels, for villa and hospitality openings with large glass.

Bifold doors are also described by how the panels fold. They may open inward (panels stack inside the room, protected from weather, but taking floor space) or outward (panels stack on the terrace, keeping the room clear and making the door more weather-resistant because wind presses the sash onto its seals). They may fold all to one side, or split to both sides. Layouts are written as the number of panels folding to each side: 3L means three panels folding left, 2L2R means two to each side, 4L1R means four left and one right.

An odd number of panels allows a traffic door: the end panel works like an ordinary hinged door with its own lever handle, so you can step outside without opening the whole run. With an even number of panels folding to one side, the last panel is attached to a carrier and there is no independent door; a split layout such as 3L1R gives a traffic door on the single right-hand panel.

Sizes

Popular bifold door sizes and panel counts

Bifold doors are almost always made to measure, but a few overall widths are very common. The number of panels follows from the width: most systems allow panels from about 600 mm to 1,000 – 1,200 mm wide, with 700 – 900 mm the comfortable range for weight, appearance and stacking.

Heights of 2.1 – 2.4 m are standard; many aluminium systems allow up to about 2.7 – 3.0 m. Maximum panel size and weight (commonly around 100 – 150 kg per panel) are set by the system's hinges and rollers, not by the aluminium.

More panels are not automatically better. Each extra panel adds a vertical sightline, another set of hinges and rollers, and more stack width. Fewer, wider panels look cleaner but are heavier and need stronger hardware. The right answer balances opening width, single panel size, glass weight, opening direction and how much space the stack may take inside or outside.

Aluminium profiles

Profile alloys, thermal breaks and frame depth

Alloy & temperTypical tensile / yield (EN 755-2 min.)Use in bifold doors
6063-T5175 / 130 MPa (wall ≤ 3 mm)Standard residential and light-commercial frames and sashes
6063-T6215 / 170 MPaTaller panels, wider panels and higher wind loads
6060-T66215 / 160 MPaEuropean alternative to 6063 for window and door systems
6061-T6 / 6005A-T6260 / 240 MPa; 270 / 225 MPa (thin walls)Reinforcing sections, heavy-duty tracks and posts for very large panels

6063 is the default alloy for door systems because it extrudes into complex thin-wall, multi-chamber shapes with precise tolerances (EN 12020-2 for precision profiles) and a smooth surface that powder coats and anodises evenly. Main frame walls are typically 1.6 – 2.5 mm, with thicker walls at hinge and roller fixings. 6061 or 6005A is used only where calculations show 6063 is not stiff or strong enough, as in heavy-duty tracks, corner posts or steel-replacement reinforcements.

For exterior doors, the inner and outer aluminium shells are joined by polyamide thermal break strips (glass-fibre reinforced PA66), commonly 20 – 34 mm wide, inserted and rolled in place. The break interrupts the heat path through the metal, raises the inside surface temperature and prevents condensation on the frame. Frame depths of about 60 – 80 mm are typical for double glazing in temperate climates; 90 – 120 mm frames carry triple glazing and wider breaks for cold climates and low-energy buildings.

  • Corner joints: mechanically crimped or pinned with aluminium corner cleats and sealed with structural adhesive, so the sash stays square under the weight of the glass.
  • Multi-chamber sections: extra internal chambers raise bending stiffness and lengthen the heat path.
  • Interlock design: the meeting profiles between panels carry gaskets and are where most air leakage and water entry happen, so their design matters as much as the frame depth.

The product standard EN 14351-1 brings these results together into the declaration of performance needed for CE or UKCA marking. In England, Approved Document L requires replacement glazed doors in existing dwellings to reach a U-value of 1.4 W/m²K or better, and Approved Document M limits threshold upstands for level access on new homes, which affects the choice between a flush and a weathered threshold.

Glass is specified separately: insulating glass units to EN 1279, toughened safety glass to EN 12150, laminated glass to EN 14449 and safe glazing in critical locations (doors and low glazing) per BS 6262-4 or local codes. In the US, glazing in doors must meet the safety-glazing rules of 16 CFR 1201.

Glass weight is the main driver of panel weight: each square metre of 4 + 20 + 4 mm double glazing weighs about 20 kg, and a triple unit with three 5 mm panes about 37.5 kg. A 900 × 2,400 mm triple-glazed panel therefore carries around 80 kg of glass before the frame is counted, so heavy glass must be matched to hinges and rollers rated for it.

Hardware, thresholds and seals

Hardware, thresholds, seals and drainage

Hinges

Stainless-steel or aluminium hinges, surface-mounted or concealed, rated for panel weight. Adjustable hinges allow fine alignment after installation.

Rollers and tracks

Twin-wheel or four-wheel stainless-steel or polymer roller carriages on a stainless track; sealed bearings for coastal sites.

Locks and handles

Multi-point locks with hook bolts or shoot bolts into head and threshold; key-locking lever handle on the traffic door; flush handles on intermediate panels.

Safety features

Anti-finger-trap gaskets between panels, soft-close or damped stops, and panel restraints for windy sites.

Seals

Co-extruded EPDM gaskets in the frame, sash and interlock; brush seals only where the threshold must be very low.

Insect screens

Pleated or retractable screens that slide across the opening without blocking the stack.

On exposed sites, combine a weathered threshold with an outward-opening layout and a linear drain outside the door. Low thresholds work well under a canopy, veranda or pergola where wind-driven rain is unlikely. Whichever is chosen, the sill must be laid perfectly level and supported along its full length; most bifold problems in service trace back to an uneven threshold or a deflecting lintel.

Ordering

What affects the price and how to order

Main cost drivers

  • Size: taller and wider openings need deeper profiles, larger glass and higher-rated hardware
  • Panel count: each panel adds profiles, hinges, rollers, locks and fabrication time
  • Glass: triple, laminated, solar-control and acoustic units cost more than standard double glazing
  • Finish: PVDF, dual colour, wood grain and special textures cost more than standard powder
  • Hardware: concealed hinges, high-cycle rollers and security-tested locks add cost
  • Packing and freight: full-length profiles versus cut-to-size sections, with boxed hardware for your fabricator
  1. MeasureGive the structural opening width and height, wall construction, floor finish level and whether the lintel can carry a top-hung system.
  2. ConfigureChoose panel count and layout (e.g. 3L or 3L2R), opening direction, traffic door position and threshold type.
  3. SpecifyGlazing build-up, colour inside and out, hardware finish, screens and any performance class, U-value or security standard required.
  4. ConfirmWe return a profile and accessory list, section data for the chosen series and a quotation on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
  5. DeliverProfiles are film-protected, edge-protected and bundled or crated, with hardware packed and labelled by opening for your fabricator.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) bifold doors: FAQ

How many panels should my bifold door have?

Divide the opening width by a comfortable panel width of about 700 – 900 mm. A 3 m opening usually takes three or four panels; 4 m takes four or five. Choose an odd number if you want a traffic door on one side.

Should bifold doors open inward or outward?

Outward-opening doors are generally more weather-resistant and keep the stack out of the room. Inward opening keeps panels away from rain and terrace furniture and suits balconies. Choose based on the space on each side and exposure to wind and rain.

What is the maximum size of an aluminium bifold door?

Runs of 6 – 8 m or more are possible by adding panels or splitting to both sides. Individual panels are usually limited to about 1.0 – 1.2 m wide and 2.7 – 3.0 m high, depending on the system hardware.

Are aluminium bifold doors secure?

Good systems use multi-point locks, internally beaded or bonded glass, anti-lift features and laminated glass. In the UK, look for PAS 24 test evidence; in Europe, EN 1627 resistance class RC2 or above.

Which aluminium alloy is used for bifold door profiles?

6063 in T5 or T6 temper for frames and sashes, sometimes 6060-T66 in Europe, with 6061-T6 or 6005A reinforcement for very large or heavy panels.

Do low thresholds leak?

A flush threshold has lower watertightness than a rebated one. It works well under a canopy or with an external drain; on exposed elevations, a weathered threshold is the safer choice.

Does Metline supply complete bifold doors?

No. We supply thermally broken bifold profiles, hardware and accessories, cut and machined to drawing on request; your fabricator glazes and assembles the doors. Export packing is available to any destination.

Send the openings, panel layout, glazing and finish. Our team will propose a profile series and quote profiles and hardware delivered to your site.
