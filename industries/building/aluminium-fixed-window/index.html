Aluminium Fixed Windows

An aluminium fixed window is a glazed unit that does not open: the glass sits in a single aluminium frame with no sash, hinges or rollers. That makes it the simplest, most airtight and usually the cheapest window per square metre, and it allows larger glass and slimmer sightlines than any opening window. Metline supplies fixed window profiles in 6063, thermal-break sections and accessories to window fabricators for residential, commercial and façade projects worldwide.

Overview

What is an aluminium fixed window?

A fixed window (also called a fixed light, fixed lite or, when large, a picture window) is a frame and a glazing unit, held in place by glazing beads or structural sealant, with nothing that moves. Its job is to admit daylight, frame a view and keep weather out. Ventilation and escape are provided by other windows or doors in the same opening or room.

Because there is no opening joint, a fixed window has only one weather line to seal: the glass to the frame. Air leakage is typically far lower than for a sliding or casement window of the same size, and the frame can be much narrower because it does not have to carry a sash, lock or hinge. This is why fixed lights are used wherever the designer wants the most glass and the least frame: stair towers, double-height living rooms, shop fronts, hotel lobbies and the upper panes of window walls.

Aluminium suits fixed windows particularly well. A 6063 extrusion is stiff and strong enough to carry heavy insulating glass units over long spans with a frame face of 40 – 60 mm, it holds tight dimensional tolerances, and it can be powder coated or anodised in any colour. With a polyamide thermal break, an aluminium frame also reaches the thermal values required by modern energy codes.

Where fixed windows make sense

  • Large openings where views and daylight matter more than ventilation
  • High or hard-to-reach glazing where an opening sash would never be used
  • Combination units: a fixed centre pane flanked by casement or awning vents
  • Projects with strict air-tightness, acoustic or security targets
  • Budget-sensitive jobs: no operating hardware means lower cost per square metre
Types

Types of aluminium fixed window

Direct-set fixed window

Glass is glazed straight into the outer frame without a sash profile. Gives the narrowest sightline and the largest glass area for a given opening.

Fixed panel (sash-set)

Glass is set in a fixed sash profile that matches the operable vents beside it, so all panes in a combination look the same from outside.

Picture window

A single large pane, often 2 – 3 m wide, chosen to frame a view. Glass weight and wind load usually govern the frame section and fixings.

Thermal break fixed window

Inner and outer aluminium shells joined by glass-fibre-reinforced polyamide strips, glazed with Low-E double or triple units for energy-code compliance.

Fixed window wall

Several fixed frames mulled together, or a stick-built ribbon of fixed lights between floor slabs, forming a continuous glass wall on low- and mid-rise buildings.

Shaped fixed window

Arched, round, triangular, trapezoid or raked frames made by bending or mitring the extrusions; only possible because the unit does not need to open.

Materials

Frame alloys, profiles and thermal breaks

Window frames are extruded almost exclusively from 6063 aluminium. It extrudes at high speed through complex hollow dies, produces a fine surface that powder coats and anodises evenly, and reaches adequate strength after ageing. Where a long mullion or a very large pane needs more stiffness, the section is made deeper or a 6005A or 6061 reinforcing profile is inserted, rather than changing the alloy of the whole system.

Glazing

Glass options for fixed windows

In a fixed window the glass is around 80 – 90 % of the area, so it decides most of the thermal, acoustic and safety performance. Flat glass weighs about 2.5 kg/m² for every millimetre of thickness, which is why large triple-glazed picture windows need heavier frames and careful handling.

Finishes

Surface finishes and colours

Powder coating

Polyester powder in any RAL or custom colour, 60 – 80 µm, to AAMA 2603/2604 or Qualicoat. The standard finish for residential and most commercial windows.

Super-durable and PVDF

AAMA 2604 / 2605 or Qualicoat Class 2 and 3 coatings keep colour and gloss longest in strong sun and coastal air. Specified for façades and high-rise work.

Anodising

Clear, bronze or black anodic film to EN ISO 7599 / Qualanod, class 15 – 25 µm. Gives a metallic look and hard surface that does not chalk.

Wood-grain

Sublimation printed on a powder base coat to look like oak, walnut or teak, for villas and heritage-style projects.

Dual colour

Thermal break frames can be coated in a different colour inside and outside, for example black outside and white inside.

Coastal pre-treatment

Near the sea, specify a high-performance pre-treatment and minimum coating thickness, and plan regular washing.

Most real elevations combine both. A typical approach is a large fixed pane for the view, with a casement or awning vent beside or above it for fresh air, or a fixed transom above a sliding door. Building codes also require an openable emergency escape window in some bedrooms, which a fixed light alone cannot provide.

Design and installation

Design and installation points

  • Size the frame for glass weight and wind: a 2.4 × 2.4 m triple-glazed unit can weigh over 200 kg; the sill and setting blocks must carry it without rotating the profile.
  • Glass bite and edge clearance: keep the glazing rebate deep enough for the unit thickness and its sealant; too little bite is a common cause of seal failure.
  • Drainage: even a fixed light needs a drained and ventilated glazing rebate, with weep slots, so water that gets past the outer gasket can escape.
  • Thermal movement: allow for frame expansion of about 2.3 mm per metre for a 100 °C range; fix through slotted holes on long frames.
  • Perimeter seal: use a compatible backer rod and sealant, or pre-compressed tape and membranes, between frame and wall; this joint fails more often than the window.
  • Isolation: separate aluminium from wet cement, steel and copper with tape or coating to prevent corrosion and staining.
  • Access for cleaning and replacement: plan how the outside of high fixed panes will be cleaned and how a broken pane would be replaced from inside or outside.
Applications

Typical applications

Homes and villas

Picture windows in living rooms, gable glazing, stair windows and clerestories above head height.

Apartments and hotels

Fixed panes beside opening vents in guest rooms, and full-height fixed glass in lobbies and corridors.

Offices and retail

Ribbon windows, fixed shop-front glazing and transoms above entrances, often in thermal break frames.

Schools and hospitals

Laminated fixed glazing in corridors and halls, with acoustic glass on noisy façades.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) fixed windows: FAQ

Are fixed windows more energy efficient than opening windows?

Generally yes. With no opening joint they leak less air, and the narrower frame means more of the area is high-performance glass. With a thermal break frame and Low-E argon double glazing, an aluminium fixed window can reach whole-window U-values around 1.4 – 1.8 W/m²K.

How large can an aluminium fixed window be?

Size is limited by glass manufacture, handling, wind load and frame stiffness rather than by the window type. Single panes of 2.5 × 3 m are routine, and larger units are possible with deeper frames, laminated glass and lifting equipment on site.

What alloy are aluminium window frames made from?

6063 in T5 or T6 temper, extruded to ASTM B221 or EN 755. It gives the best surface for powder coating and anodising. Long mullions may use 6005A-T6 or internal reinforcement for extra stiffness.

Can a fixed window be combined with an opening window?

Yes. Fixed lights are often mulled to casement, awning, tilt-turn or sliding vents in one frame, or set as transoms above doors, so the elevation has both a large view and ventilation.

Do aluminium fixed windows need maintenance?

Very little. Wash the frames and glass periodically, keep drainage slots clear, and inspect the perimeter sealant every few years. There is no hardware to lubricate or adjust.

Which glass should I choose for a picture window?

For most climates a Low-E double glazed unit with argon and a toughened or laminated inner pane. Choose triple glazing in cold climates, solar-control coatings on sunny façades, and laminated glass where safety, security or noise control is required.
