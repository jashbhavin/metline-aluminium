Aluminium Cladding Panels for Facades and Walls

Aluminium cladding panels are the outer skin of a ventilated facade: flat or folded metal panels hung on a sub-frame in front of the insulated wall. Solid aluminium sheet, honeycomb and corrugated-core panels give durable colour, low weight and, unlike polyethylene-cored composites, non-combustible or limited-combustibility performance. Metline supplies panel sheet, coated coil, honeycomb panels and cassette panels to drawing, worldwide.

Overview

What aluminium cladding panels do

Cladding protects the building from weather and gives it its appearance, but in a modern rainscreen facade it is not the waterproof layer. The panel sheds most rain, shields the insulation from sun and wind, and leaves a ventilated cavity behind it so any water that gets past drains away and the wall dries out.

Aluminium suits this role because it is light (a 3 mm solid panel weighs about 8.1 kg/m², roughly a third of a steel panel of the same stiffness), forms easily into cassettes and curves, does not rust, and takes long-life coatings in any colour. It is also fully recyclable at end of life. The main design questions are which panel type to use, how thick, how it is fixed, which finish suits the exposure, and what fire classification the building regulations demand.

Options

Types of aluminium cladding panel

Solid aluminium panels

Single sheets 2 – 4 mm thick, folded into trays or cassettes with returns and stiffeners. Very durable, easy to shape into corners, curves and column covers, and non-combustible when uncoated. The default choice for high-rise and public buildings.

Honeycomb panels

Two thin aluminium skins bonded to an aluminium honeycomb core (commonly 3003, 5052 or 5056 foil). Very flat and stiff at low weight, so large modules of 1.5 × 4 m and more stay flat. Used for large-format facades, soffits and ceilings.

Corrugated-core panels

Skins bonded to a corrugated aluminium core, typically 4 – 6 mm thick. A non-combustible-core alternative to composite panels that keeps good flatness and can be routed and folded.

Sandwich panels

Aluminium faces with an insulating or acoustic core, used for walls, roofs and partitions where the panel itself must insulate.

Corrugated and profiled sheet

Roll-formed trapezoidal or sinusoidal profiles for industrial buildings, roofs and textured facade bands.

Perforated panels

Solid sheet with round, square, slot or image-based perforation patterns for screens, car-park facades, sunshades and balcony infills.

Aluminium composite material (ACM) with a polyethylene core is no longer accepted on the external walls of high-rise residential buildings in many countries. Where composite panels are used, specify a mineral-filled core with a tested classification of A2-s1,d0 or better, or use solid or metal-cored panels.

Fire classes for coated and bonded panels depend on the coating and adhesive and must be taken from the product's own classification report, not assumed from the metal. 3 mm solid sheet is the usual choice for facades; 2 mm suits smaller modules, soffits and interiors, and 4 mm is used for very large flat panels or high wind loads.

Systems

Panel forms and fixing systems

Hook-on cassettes

Panels with folded returns hang on pins or hooks on vertical rails. Secret fixing, easy replacement of single panels, and open or gasketed joints.

Rivet or screw fixed

Flat panels fastened through the face to the sub-frame with colour-matched rivets. Economical and robust; fixings visible.

Interlocking planks

Long tongue-and-groove planks for horizontal or vertical linear facades and soffits.

Standing seam

Long trays joined by folded seams; used on roofs, curved walls and feature volumes.

Folded and pressed forms

Three-dimensional panels, fins and faceted shapes for expressive facades.

Sub-frame

Aluminium T and L rails (typically 6063 or 6005A extrusions) on adjustable brackets with thermal isolator pads, designed for wind load and movement.

Allow for thermal movement: a 4 m aluminium panel grows by around 4 mm over a 40 °C temperature change. Slotted holes, sliding brackets and open joints of 10 – 20 mm let panels move without buckling. Design wind loads to EN 1991-1-4 or ASCE 7 and check panel deflection, fixings and brackets together.

Applications

Where aluminium panels are used

  • Commercial facades: offices, retail and mixed-use buildings with cassette or honeycomb panels around curtain walls.
  • Public buildings: airports, stations, hospitals and schools where non-combustible, low-maintenance cladding is required.
  • Residential towers: solid aluminium rainscreen panels meeting A1 or A2 fire requirements.
  • Soffits, canopies and entrances: flat honeycomb panels that stay true across large spans.
  • Column covers and fascias: rolled and folded solid sheet.
  • Industrial buildings: profiled sheet walls and roofs.
  • Interiors: feature walls, lift lobbies and ceilings with anodised or decorative finishes.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) cladding panels: frequently asked questions

What thickness of aluminium cladding panel should I use?

3 mm solid sheet is the standard for exterior facades. Use 2 mm for small modules, soffits and interiors, and 4 mm or a honeycomb panel for very large flat panels or high wind loads. The final choice comes from a wind-load and deflection check of the panel and its fixings.

Are aluminium cladding panels fire safe?

Uncoated solid aluminium is classified A1 (non-combustible) under EN 13501-1. Coated solid panels and metal-cored honeycomb or corrugated-core panels are tested and typically reach A1 or A2-s1,d0. Polyethylene-cored composite panels are the products restricted on tall buildings; avoid them where regulations call for limited combustibility.

What is the difference between solid aluminium panels and ACP?

A solid panel is one sheet of aluminium, folded into a cassette. ACP (aluminium composite panel) is two thin skins bonded to a core. Solid panels are more robust and fire-safe; ACP is flatter for its weight and cheaper, but its fire performance depends entirely on the core.

PVDF or powder coating for facade panels?

PVDF gives the best long-term colour and gloss retention and metallic effects, and is the usual choice for prestige and coastal projects. Super-durable powder coating is a good, economical alternative for many climates and is applied after fabrication.

How much does a 3 mm aluminium panel weigh?

About 8.1 kg/m², since aluminium weighs 2.70 kg/m² per millimetre of thickness. A 1.5 × 3 m panel in 3 mm sheet weighs about 36 kg before folding returns and stiffeners.

Which alloy is used for anodised aluminum cladding?

5005 in H14 or H24 temper, because it anodises to an even, clear finish that matches 6063 extrusions. 3003 can show streaks or a darker tone when anodised.

Planning an aluminium facade?
