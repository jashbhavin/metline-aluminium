Aluminium Curtain Wall Systems

An aluminium curtain wall system is a complete, tested facade kit: extruded framing, glass and panel infills, gaskets, thermal breaks, brackets and anchors that together keep weather out and carry wind load to the floor slabs. Choosing between stick, unitised and structurally glazed systems sets the cost, programme and appearance of the building. Metline supplies curtain wall extrusions, panels and accessories to facade contractors and system companies, to project drawings, worldwide.

Overview

What a curtain wall system includes

The word "system" matters. A curtain wall is tested and certified as an assembly, so mullions, transoms, gaskets, pressure plates, thermal isolators, drainage and anchors are designed to work together. Mixing parts from different systems, or changing glass build-ups beyond the tested range, voids that evidence.

  • Framing extrusions: mullions, transoms, pressure plates, cover caps and adaptors, usually in 6063-T6 or 6060-T66.
  • Infills: vision glass (double or triple insulating units), spandrel glass, solid aluminium, honeycomb or insulated panels.
  • Seals and thermal breaks: EPDM or silicone gaskets, polyamide or PVC isolators, structural silicone in SSG systems.
  • Anchors and brackets: slab-edge brackets allowing three-way adjustment, with fixed and sliding points for movement.
  • Openings and interfaces: top-hung or parallel-opening vents, doors, slab-edge fire-stopping, copings and interfaces with cladding.

Performance

How curtain wall performance is classified

In Europe, EN 13830 is the product standard behind CE and UKCA marking. It calls on separate test methods for each property. In North America, specifications quote ASTM test methods, usually run in a mock-up chamber to AAMA 501.

Thermal performance is dominated by the glass and by the spacing of the frame. A wall with triple glazing and wide-spaced mullions can reach Ucw values around 1.0 W/m²K; tight grids with double glazing are typically higher. Spandrel areas need insulation behind the infill.

Engineering

Design considerations for aluminium curtain walls

Wind load and deflection

Mullion size comes from the design wind pressure (EN 1991-1-4, ASCE 7 or local code) and the deflection limit. Stiffness is a function of depth because all 6xxx alloys share the same modulus.

Building movement

Floors deflect and sway. Stack joints, sliding brackets and splice sleeves absorb inter-storey drift and thermal expansion; aluminium moves about 2.3 mm per metre per 100 °C.

Drainage

Pressure-equalised, drained transoms send water out through weep slots at each level; nothing relies solely on the outer seal.

Thermal bridging

Isolators under the pressure plate, insulated spandrels and warm-edge spacers control heat loss and condensation risk on the inner frame.

Fire

Slab-edge fire-stopping, non-combustible spandrel insulation and, where required, fire-rated glazing; spandrel panels should meet local external wall rules.

Corrosion and finish

Isolate aluminium from steel brackets with pads or coatings; specify PVDF, super-durable powder or class 20 – 25 anodising for exposed parts.

Applications

Where curtain walls are used

  • Office buildings: stick or unitised walls with 1.35 – 1.5 m modules, vents and spandrel panels.
  • High-rise towers: unitised systems with tested wind, water and drift performance.
  • Hotels and apartments: acoustic glazing, opening vents and balcony door interfaces.
  • Retail and malls: large glazing and feature cladding at podium level.
  • Public buildings: schools, hospitals, airports and stations with robust, low-maintenance facades.

A typical mid-rise office specification might call for a stick system with 60 mm faces, 150 – 180 mm deep 6063-T6 mullions at 1.5 m centres, double Low-E glazing, air class A4, watertightness class RE 750 and super-durable powder coating, verified by a project mock-up test.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) curtain wall systems: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between stick and unitised curtain walls?

A stick wall is assembled piece by piece on site, which keeps material cost down but needs more site labour and access. A unitised wall arrives as factory-glazed units that are lifted in and interlocked, giving faster enclosure and better quality control at a higher design and logistics cost.

Which aluminium alloy is used for curtain wall systems?

Mostly 6063-T6 and 6060-T66, which extrude into complex sections with a good surface finish. 6005A-T6 or 6061-T6 is used for highly loaded mullions and brackets.

What performance classes should I specify?

For most commercial buildings in Europe, air class A4 and watertightness R7 or RE (for example RE 750 or higher on tall buildings), with a wind design load from the structural calculation. In North America, specify ASTM E283, E331 and E330 results tied to the project design pressure, verified by mock-up testing.

Is a curtain wall load-bearing?

No. It carries its own weight and the wind load on it, and transfers both to the building structure through brackets at each floor. It does not support floors or roofs.

Can you supply aluminum curtain wall components for an existing system design?

Yes, from profile drawings or a system reference. We extrude, finish and machine the profiles and supply infill panels and accessories, with export packing and mill certificates; the facade contractor assembles, glazes and installs.
