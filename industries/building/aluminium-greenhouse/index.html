Aluminium Greenhouse Kits, Frames & Profiles

Aluminium is the standard frame material for glass and polycarbonate greenhouses because it does not rust in a warm, humid growing climate, and because thin, strong extrusions shade less of the crop than timber or steel. Metline supplies greenhouse frame profiles such as glazing bars, gutters, ridge sections and panel channels, cut and drilled to drawing, to garden-product brands, greenhouse builders and growers worldwide.

Overview

Why greenhouses are built in aluminium

A greenhouse frame lives in the harshest climate of any building component: constant humidity, condensation running down every bar, fertiliser and pesticide sprays, and daily temperature swings. It also has to be as slender as possible, because every square centimetre of frame is a shadow on the crop.

Aluminium meets both demands. Its natural oxide film protects it in humid air without paint, so frames stay sound for decades and do not stain glass with rust. Extrusion lets one profile combine a structural web, a glazing rebate, a condensation channel and a screw port, which a rolled steel section cannot do. And at a density of 2.70 g/cm³ the frame is light enough to ship as a flat-packed kit and assemble with hand tools.

For small garden greenhouses aluminium is almost universal. In commercial horticulture, the classic Dutch Venlo greenhouse combines a galvanised steel truss and column structure with aluminium gutters, ridges and glazing bars, while all-aluminium and hybrid structures are used for retail garden centres, research glasshouses and conservatory-style buildings.

Benefits of an aluminium greenhouse frame

  • No rust, rot or repainting in a constantly humid environment
  • Slim glazing bars maximise light reaching plants
  • Integrated drainage and condensation channels in the profile
  • Lightweight, pre-cut kits that assemble with bolts and clips
  • Recyclable at end of life, and easily extended or re-glazed
Types

Types of aluminium greenhouse

Freestanding garden greenhouse

Apex-roof kits from about 1.8 × 1.2 m (6 × 4 ft) up to 3 × 4 m and more, glazed with toughened glass or twin-wall polycarbonate, with a sliding or hinged door and roof vents.

Lean-to greenhouse

A single-pitch structure fixed against a house or garden wall. The wall stores heat, which helps in cooler climates; the wall plate and flashing details matter most.

Mini and wall greenhouses

Compact frames for patios and balconies, where light weight and easy assembly are the main requirements.

Commercial Venlo glasshouse

Multi-span houses with small roof pitches, aluminium gutters spanning between columns and aluminium glazing bars carrying horticultural glass.

Wide-span and polycarbonate houses

Larger arched or peaked spans clad in multi-wall polycarbonate, often with aluminium end walls, doors and vent frames.

Film greenhouses

Steel hoops covered in polyethylene film, using aluminium wire-lock channels, roll-up bars and door frames for fastening and ventilation.

Most profiles are extruded from 6063 aluminium in T5 or T6 temper, which gives clean, thin sections with good surface quality. Gutters and long-span beams that carry snow and crop-hanging loads are often made in 6063-T6 with deeper webs, or in 6005A-T6 or 6061-T6 where higher strength is needed. Fasteners are stainless steel or zinc-plated with plastic isolation.

The aluminium profile must suit the covering. Glass sits on glazing tape or gaskets and is held with clips or caps; polycarbonate needs H and U channels and room to expand, because it moves about three times more than aluminium with temperature. Film is clamped with wire-lock channels so it can be re-tensioned and replaced.

Design

Structural design: wind, snow and crop loads

Even a small garden greenhouse is a lightweight structure with a large surface area, so wind is usually the governing load; in cold regions snow on the roof and in valleys takes over. Commercial greenhouses also carry hanging crops, thermal screens, lighting and irrigation, which the gutters and trusses must support.

Surface finish

Mill finish, anodising and powder coating

Mill finish

The lowest-cost option. The frame weathers to a dull grey and stays structurally sound. Common on commercial glasshouses where appearance is secondary.

Anodised

A clear or bronze anodic layer, typically 10 – 20 µm, improves corrosion resistance and keeps the frame looking clean. Popular for premium garden greenhouses.

Powder coated

Green, black, grey or any RAL colour, 60 – 80 µm. Gives a furniture-like look for garden and orangery projects; specify a durable coating for strong sun.

Reflective white

A white coating reflects light back into the crop and reduces frame heating; often used on commercial gutters and bars.

Fertiliser solutions and some crop-protection chemicals can attack bare aluminium if they collect in crevices. Good drainage in the profile, regular rinsing and an anodised or coated finish all extend service life in intensive growing.

Many commercial projects use both: a galvanised steel primary structure for columns and trusses, and aluminium for the gutters, ridges, glazing bars, vents and doors where corrosion, light and precision matter most.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) greenhouses: FAQ

Is an aluminium greenhouse better than a wooden one?

For durability and light, usually yes. Aluminium does not rot, needs no treatment and uses slimmer bars, so more light reaches the plants. Timber insulates slightly better and suits traditional designs, but needs regular maintenance.

Will an aluminium greenhouse frame corrode?

Aluminium forms a protective oxide layer and resists humid greenhouse air very well. Problems arise only where it touches dissimilar metals, sits in standing fertiliser solution, or is in permanent contact with wet concrete; use stainless fixings, good drainage and an anodised or powder-coated finish for intensive growing.

Which is better for an aluminium greenhouse: glass or polycarbonate?

Glass gives the highest light transmission and a long life; toughened glass is safest. Twin-wall polycarbonate insulates better, is almost unbreakable and diffuses light, at the cost of some transmission and a shorter surface life. Many growers use glass in mild regions and polycarbonate where heating costs or hail are a concern.

What alloy are greenhouse profiles made from?

Mainly 6063 in T5 or T6 temper, because it extrudes into thin, complex sections with a good surface. Gutters and long-span members may use deeper 6063-T6 sections or 6005A-T6 and 6061-T6 for extra strength.

Can you supply replacement greenhouse parts?

Yes. Send a drawing, sample or clear photographs with dimensions, and we can supply glazing bars, H and U channels, gutters and connectors to match, or develop a new die if no existing profile fits.

Can greenhouse profiles be supplied pre-cut?

Yes. Profiles can be cut, drilled and labelled by part number to your parts list, so the greenhouse brand or builder can pack its own kits or assemble on site. We do not supply complete greenhouse kits.
