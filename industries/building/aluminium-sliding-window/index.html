Aluminium Sliding Windows

An aluminium sliding window has sashes that move sideways on tracks inside an extruded frame, so nothing swings into the room or out over a walkway. It is simple, economical, easy to fit with insect screens and well suited to apartments, hotels, schools and kitchens. Metline supplies sliding window profiles in 6063 and 6060, thermal-break sections and hardware to window fabricators and project buyers worldwide.

Overview

What is an aluminium sliding window?

A sliding window (a horizontal slider) consists of an outer frame with two or more parallel tracks and sashes that roll on nylon or stainless rollers in the sill track. Where two sashes overlap, interlocking profiles hook together to resist wind and keep out rain. Because the sashes stay within the frame depth, sliders are the natural choice wherever an opening sash would hit furniture, curtains, a balcony railing or people walking outside.

Sliders are the most widely used window type in much of South and South-East Asia, the Middle East, Africa and Latin America, and remain common in North American and Australian housing. The reasons are practical: simple hardware, low cost, easy cleaning of inner sashes, straightforward insect screens on an extra track and wide horizontal openings that suit modern façades.

Their weakness is sealing. A sliding sash cannot be pressed onto a compression gasket the way a casement or tilt-turn sash is, so it relies on brush pile, fins and drainage. Well-designed modern systems close much of the gap with deeper interlocks, multiple seal lines and pressure-equalised sills, but in very exposed, windy or cold locations a casement window still seals better. Choosing well means matching the slider to the building, not using it everywhere.

Where sliders work best

  • Balconies, corridors and kitchens where an opening sash would obstruct space
  • Wide, low openings and continuous horizontal window bands
  • Hot and humid climates that need ventilation and mosquito screens
  • Repetitive housing, hotel and dormitory projects where cost and simple maintenance count
Configurations

Types of aluminium sliding window

Standard two-track slider

Two sashes on two tracks, either both sliding or one fixed. The workhorse for apartments, kitchens and dormitories; opens about half the width.

Three-track slider

Either three glass sashes for a wider opening, or two glass sashes plus an insect screen on the third track. Common in Indian, Gulf and South-East Asian housing.

Thermally broken slider

Polyamide strips separate inner and outer aluminium, combined with Low-E insulating glass and extra seals for energy-efficient buildings and air-conditioned interiors.

Slider with integrated screen

A fly screen on its own track, as a pleated screen or as a separate fixed or sliding screen. See window screens.

Slim-frame and large-glass slider

Narrow sightlines and heavier sashes for floor-to-ceiling glazing, using stiffer interlocks and higher-capacity rollers. See slimline windows.

Fixed + sliding combinations

Sliding sashes combined with fixed lights, transoms or awning vents above, to balance daylight, ventilation and cost.

How it is built

Tracks, rollers, interlocks and drainage

Sill track and rollers

The sill carries all sash weight. A stainless or aluminium running rail and adjustable rollers give smooth movement; rollers must be rated for the glass weight, which is about 2.5 kg/m² per mm of glass.

Interlock

Where sashes meet, hooked interlock profiles join them when closed. Their depth controls wind deflection and rattle; tall windows need deeper or reinforced interlocks.

Anti-lift blocks

Blocks fitted in the head stop a closed sash from being lifted out of its track from outside, an important and cheap security detail.

Drainage

Water that passes the outer brush is collected in the sill and released through weep slots with baffles or flap valves. A raised inner sill upstand keeps it from blowing inward.

Sealing

Fin-seal pile on the sash edges and interlocks, gaskets around the glass and sealed frame corners. Corner joints are a frequent leak path and should be sealed during assembly.

Locking

Crescent locks for basic windows; hook or multi-point locks for security and better seal pull-in on large sashes. Opening restrictors limit opening width on upper floors.

Materials

Alloys and profiles for sliding windows

Window profiles are extruded almost entirely from the soft 6xxx alloys 6063 and 6060. Both extrude at high speed through complex hollow dies, give a smooth surface for anodising or powder coating and reach adequate strength after ageing. In a window the governing design check is usually deflection of the interlock and mullions under wind load, so section depth and wall thickness matter more than the small strength differences between tempers.

A sensible approach on many projects is mixed: casement or tilt-turn windows on exposed, windward upper floors and bedrooms that need high sealing, and sliders on balconies, kitchens and sheltered elevations. See casement window systems.

Typical projects

Where aluminium sliding windows are used

  • Apartment and housing developments: repeated two- or three-track sliders built to a window schedule, delivered labelled by flat and floor.
  • Balconies and kitchens: sliders that save space and integrate fly screens and grilles.
  • Hotels, schools and dormitories: many identical windows with restrictors, acoustic glass on road-facing sides and simple, replaceable rollers.
  • Commercial and mixed-use buildings: sliders in offices, shops and service buildings, often combined with fixed lights.
  • Coastal and humid regions: powder coat with seaside pre-treatment, stainless hardware and generous drainage.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) sliding windows: FAQ

Are aluminium sliding windows good for apartments and hotels?

Yes. They save space, cost less than casements, take insect screens easily and use simple, replaceable hardware, which suits large numbers of identical windows. On exposed high floors, check that the chosen system has tested air, water and wind classes suitable for the height.

Do sliding windows leak more than casement windows?

They generally achieve lower air and water tightness because the sash cannot compress a gasket. A well-designed slider with deep interlocks, fin-seal pile and a drained, raised sill performs well in most climates; for very exposed or cold locations, casement or tilt-turn windows are better.

Two-track or three-track: which should I choose?

Two-track for most openings. Choose three-track when you need a wider opening or want a fly screen on its own track behind two glass sashes, as is common in hot, humid regions.

Can aluminum sliding windows be soundproof?

They can be improved considerably with laminated acoustic glass, better seals and a thermally broken frame, reaching around 30 – 35 dB. For heavy traffic, rail or airport noise, a dedicated acoustic window with compression seals is more effective.

Which alloy is used for sliding window profiles?

6063-T5 is used for most frames and sashes worldwide; 6063-T6 or 6060-T66 for interlocks and larger windows where more strength is needed.

Can I get a quote without full drawings?

Yes. A window schedule with sizes, quantities, type, glass and finish is enough for a first quotation; details are confirmed during shop-drawing approval.
