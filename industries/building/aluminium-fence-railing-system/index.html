Aluminium Fence & Railing Systems

An aluminium fence or railing system is a set of extruded posts, rails, pickets or panels, brackets and fixings that assemble into boundary fences, pool barriers, balcony guards and stair rails. Built from 6063 or 6005A extrusions and finished with architectural powder coat, it does not rust, needs no repainting and weighs about a third as much as an equivalent steel system. Metline supplies the fence and railing profiles, accessories and fixings to fabricators, installers and projects worldwide.

Overview

Fence systems and railing systems: what is the difference?

The two products share the same materials and finishes but answer different questions. A fence marks or secures a boundary at ground level; a railing (called a guard in building codes) stops people falling from a raised edge such as a deck, balcony, landing or stair. That difference decides how each is engineered.

Fences are mainly designed for wind on the infill, impact, security and, around pools, child safety. Posts are set in concrete footings or bolted to base plates, and spans of 1.8 to 2.4 m between posts are common. Railings are life-safety components. Building codes set a minimum height, a maximum opening size and a load that the top rail must resist, and the post-to-structure connection is usually the critical detail.

Because both are assembled from extrusions, one supplier can provide matching profiles, gate sections and colours for the boundary fence, the pool enclosure and the balcony guards of the same project, which keeps the appearance consistent and simplifies ordering.

Why aluminium for fencing and railings

  • Forms a protective oxide film, so it does not rust like steel and does not rot or split like timber
  • Density of 2.70 g/cm³: posts and panels are light to ship, carry and install
  • Extrusion allows hollow posts with screw ports, channels and hidden fixing grooves in one profile
  • Powder coat or anodising gives long colour life with occasional washing as the only maintenance
  • Fully recyclable at the end of its life without loss of properties
System types

Common aluminium fence and railing styles

Picket fence and railing

Vertical square or round pickets between top and bottom rails. The classic choice for gardens, pool barriers and balcony guards, because picket spacing is easy to keep under the code opening limit. See picket railing.

Slat fencing

Horizontal or vertical slats held in channel posts with spacers. Gives a modern look and adjustable privacy depending on the gap. See slat fencing.

Privacy fence and gates

Interlocking boards or solid panels that close the view completely. Wind load on solid infill is far higher than on pickets, so posts and footings must be sized for it. See privacy fence, gate and posts.

Glass railing

Toughened laminated glass in a base shoe or held by posts and clamps. Keeps views open from balconies and terraces; the glass, not the aluminium, often governs the design.

Deck and stair railing

Post, top rail, bottom rail and infill kits for timber or composite decks and for stairs, with raked sections that follow the stair pitch. See deck railing components.

Commercial and pool fencing

Heavier posts, rails and gates for schools, parks, plant perimeters and public pools, where security, vandal resistance and barrier codes matter. See commercial fence and railing.

Which alloys are used for which parts

Almost all fence and railing components are extruded from 6xxx-series alloys, which extrude easily into complex hollow shapes and are then aged to the required temper. The choice between them is a trade-off between surface finish and strength.

Design and fabrication

Design details that decide performance

Post size and wall

Railing posts usually fail by bending at the base. A 50 × 50 mm post with a 3 mm wall is common for residential guards; commercial guards and tall privacy fences often need 70 – 100 mm posts or internal steel or aluminium reinforcement.

Base connection

Surface-mounted posts depend on the base plate, anchor bolts and the substrate. Many failures happen in the timber or concrete, not the aluminium, so the fixing must be designed with the deck or slab.

Wind on infill

Pickets let wind through; solid privacy panels catch all of it. A 1.8 m privacy fence can see several times the post moment of a picket fence of the same height, so check footings.

Thermal movement

Aluminium expands about 23 µm/m per °C. A 6 m rail can grow by 7 mm between a cold night and a hot afternoon, so long runs need slotted holes or expansion joints.

Galvanic contact

Use stainless fixings with nylon washers, and separate aluminium from copper-treated timber, wet concrete and steel with tape, bitumen paint or plastic sleeves.

Assembly method

Most systems are mechanically assembled with screw ports and hidden brackets, so they ship flat and go up without site welding. Welded panels are stiffer but need care so welds do not show through the coating.

Examples

How the systems are typically specified

  • Residential pool enclosure: 1200 – 1250 mm picket panels in 6063-T5 with 50 × 50 mm 6063-T6 posts, black powder coat, and a self-closing gate with a magnetic latch mounted high on the gate post.
  • Apartment balconies: 1100 mm guards with 6005A-T6 posts on side-mounted brackets, 6063 top rails and laminated glass or vertical bar infill, all in one RAL colour to match the window frames.
  • Garden boundary: 1.8 m horizontal slat fence with 65 × 16 mm slats, 10 – 20 mm gaps and channel posts in concrete footings, plus a matching pedestrian gate.
  • School or park perimeter: 2.0 – 2.4 m commercial picket fence with heavy posts, pointed or flush tops, and double swing or sliding vehicle gates.
  • External stair: raked picket railing with a separate graspable handrail profile, both anodised for wear resistance.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) fence and railing systems: FAQ

Is aluminium fencing strong enough compared with steel?

For residential and most commercial fencing, yes. Aluminium is less stiff than steel, so posts are made larger or with thicker walls to compensate, but a correctly sized 6063-T6 or 6005A-T6 post carries code guard loads. For high-security perimeters that must resist cutting or vehicle impact, steel is still normally used.

Which alloy is best for aluminium railing posts?

6063-T6 suits most residential posts. For tall or highly loaded guards, 6005A-T6 or 6061-T6 gives a higher minimum yield strength in the same section. Pickets and slats are usually 6063-T5, which gives the smoothest powder-coated finish.

How long does a powder-coated aluminium fence last?

The aluminium itself does not rust, so service life is governed by the coating. A properly pre-treated AAMA 2604 or Qualicoat Class 2 powder coat keeps its colour and gloss for many years in normal climates; coastal sites benefit from regular fresh-water rinsing.

Can aluminium fencing be used as a pool barrier?

Yes, it is one of the most common pool fence materials. The system must meet the local barrier rule, for example at least 48 in high with openings under 4 in in the US, or 1200 mm under AS 1926.1 in Australia, with self-closing, self-latching gates.

Do aluminium fence systems need welding on site?

Usually not. Most systems use screw ports, brackets and pre-punched rails so panels are assembled with fasteners. Where extra stiffness is wanted, the fabricator can weld panels, which must be done before coating.

What should I send for a quotation?

The system type, heights, total length, number of posts, corners and gates, infill style, colour and coating standard, any code or test requirement, quantity and delivery destination. Drawings or site plans make the quote faster and more accurate.
