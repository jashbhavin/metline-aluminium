System Aluminium Windows

A system aluminium window is a complete, engineered package in which the profiles, thermal break, gaskets, glass rebate, hardware grooves and drainage were designed together and tested together as one window. That is what separates it from a window assembled from whatever profiles and fittings happen to be available. Metline supplies system window profiles in 6063 and 6060, thermal break and co-extruded sections and accessories, to window fabricators and project buyers worldwide.

Overview

What makes a window a "system" window

The word "system" describes how the window was developed, not how it looks. A system designer starts from a performance target (an air-tightness class, a wind load, a U-value) and then designs every section, gasket, cleat and hardware groove to reach it. The finished window is tested at a laboratory, and the test report defines the sizes, glass and hardware that the performance applies to.

A conventional window is built the other way round: profiles, glass and fittings are chosen by price or availability and the result is whatever it turns out to be. Such windows can look identical on the facade, but their air and water tightness depend heavily on the skill of the fabricator and installer, and there is no test evidence to show a building inspector or energy assessor.

For a project buyer, the practical value of a system window is predictability. The same frame depth, gasket layout and drainage detail appear on every unit across hundreds of openings, the fabrication drawings already exist, and the performance claimed in the tender can be traced back to a test report. That is why most energy codes, facade consultants and main contractors now ask for a tested system rather than a generic window.

What a genuine system package includes

  • A family of matching frame, sash, mullion, transom and bead profiles with fixed dimensions
  • Defined gasket, thermal break, cleat and hardware components, each with a part number
  • Fabrication manuals: cutting lists, machining positions, corner jointing and glazing rules
  • Laboratory test reports for air, water, wind and thermal performance at stated sizes
Profile structures

Thermal break versus co-extruded system windows

Two profile structures dominate insulated system windows. Both keep aluminium on the outside for strength and durability; they differ in how the cold outer shell is separated from the room.

CriterionThermal break (polyamide strip)Aluminium-PVC co-extruded
ConstructionInner and outer aluminium shells joined by glass-fibre-reinforced polyamide (PA66 GF25) strips rolled into the profileAluminium core with a co-extruded or clipped rigid or micro-foamed PVC layer on the room side
Main strengthStructural aluminium on both faces; high stiffness for large sashes and high wind loadsWarm, quiet interior surface with good insulation for its depth
FinishesPowder coating, PVDF, anodising, wood-grain transfer, different colours inside and outAluminium face coated as usual; PVC face foiled or coloured, often wood-grain
Typical useOffices, schools, hotels, apartments, high-rise and public buildingsHousing, villas, hotels and refurbishment where comfort and appearance lead
Points to checkStrip width and shear rating to EN 14024; foam inserts for higher insulationLong-term bond and thermal movement between PVC and aluminium; fire and recycling requirements

Non-insulated system windows still have a place in warm climates and for internal glazing, where condensation and heat loss are not a design issue. They are cheaper and slimmer, but a non-insulated aluminium frame conducts heat several times faster than a thermally broken one, so check local energy rules before specifying them. Opening types

Opening types within one system family

A good system family covers several opening types with shared outer frames, colours and sightlines, so a facade can mix them without looking patched together.

Casement

Hinged sash closing onto compressed gaskets. The best air, water and acoustic performance of any opening type; inward or outward opening.

Tilt-turn

One handle gives secure tilt ventilation and full inward opening for cleaning. Standard in European housing, schools and hotels.

Sliding and lift-slide

Saves space on balconies and wide openings. Brush or lift-slide seals; lift-slide closes onto gaskets and performs much better than a plain slider.

Bi-fold

Folding sashes that clear almost the whole opening. Needs careful threshold and drainage design.

Awning and louvre

Top-hung or bladed openings for kitchens, bathrooms, plant rooms and continuous ventilation; awnings shed rain when open.

Fixed and combination

Fixed lights joined with opening sashes by mullions and transoms, for daylight and views with a controlled opening ratio.

Components

The four subsystems that decide performance

SubsystemWhat it doesSelection points
GlazingCarries most of the thermal, solar and acoustic performance; the glass usually covers 70 – 85 % of the window areaDouble or triple insulating units, Low-E coatings, argon fill, warm-edge spacers, toughened or laminated safety glass, acoustic laminates; stay within the system's glass thickness range
HardwareOpens, locks and carries the sash; compresses the gaskets evenlyRated sash weight per hinge set, number of locking points, restrictors and child-safety catches, corrosion class for coastal sites
SealingForms the air and water lines and dampens soundEPDM gaskets with moulded or vulcanised corners; two or three sealing lines; centre gasket to separate a drained chamber from a dry inner zone
DrainageRemoves water that passes the outer sealSlotted drainage with baffled covers, pressure-equalised chambers, concealed drainage for a cleaner face

Swapping any of these components for a non-system part, for example a cheaper gasket or a different hinge, can invalidate the test report. Agree substitutions in writing before fabrication.

Material

Profile alloys and surface finishes

System window profiles are extruded from 6xxx alloys because they flow through intricate dies, harden by ageing and give a smooth surface for coating. 6063-T5 is the standard frame and sash material; 6063-T6 or 6060-T66 is used where higher strength is needed, and 6005A-T6 for heavily loaded mullions. Tight architectural tolerances to EN 12020-2 keep gasket grooves and corner joints consistent from one extrusion batch to the next. See our 6063 aluminium guide for the alloy data.

Polyester powder coating

Wide RAL colour range and good weathering; specify Qualicoat class 1 or AAMA 2603/2604 for standard exposure.

Super-durable powder and PVDF

Better gloss and colour retention for coastal, high-UV and high-rise facades; AAMA 2605 or Qualicoat class 2/3.

Anodising

Hard, metallic oxide surface; use 20 µm or thicker (Qualanod class 20 or 25) for exterior exposure.

Wood-grain transfer

Sublimated wood pattern on a powder base, popular for villas and hotels.

Dual colour

Thermal break profiles allow a different colour inside and outside at no loss of performance.

Black and dark colours

Absorb more heat; check expansion allowances and use dark-colour-rated thermal strips on large frames.

Applications

Choosing a system by building type

  • Housing and apartments: thermally broken casements or tilt-turns with Low-E double glazing; focus on condensation resistance, insect screens and consistent colour across many floors.
  • Hotels: acoustic laminated glass, triple sealing and restrictors; consistent appearance across hundreds of identical rooms.
  • Schools and public buildings: robust hinges, restrictors, safety glazing and tilt ventilation for occupied classrooms.
  • Offices and commercial buildings: fixed lights with top-hung vents, matched to a curtain wall or ribbon window sightline.
  • Plant rooms and service areas: louvre and awning windows for continuous ventilation with drainage.
  • Renovation: systems that fit existing openings, colours and opening types, often with flush or slim sashes.

A typical mid-rise residential tender might ask for a 70 mm thermally broken system, Uw ≤ 1.4 W/m²·K with 6-16-6 Low-E argon glass, air class 4, water class 7A, wind class C4 and a super-durable grey finish, all evidenced by test reports for the chosen system and size.

FAQ

System aluminium (aluminum) windows: frequently asked questions

What is a system aluminium window?

A window built from a matched family of profiles, thermal breaks, gaskets, hardware and drainage parts that were designed and laboratory-tested together. The test report states the air, water, wind and thermal performance for defined sizes and glass, so the result is predictable on site.

How is a system window different from a standard aluminium window?

A standard window combines profiles and fittings chosen by price or stock, with no test evidence for the combination. A system window has fixed components, fabrication rules and test reports, so every unit performs the same and the performance can be proven to inspectors and consultants.

Thermal break or co-extruded: which should I choose?

Thermal break profiles suit commercial, high-rise and high-wind projects because both faces are structural aluminium. Co-extruded aluminium-PVC profiles suit housing, hotels and refurbishment where a warm, decorative interior surface and acoustic comfort are priorities.

Are system aluminium windows energy efficient and quiet?

Yes, when correctly specified. A thermally broken system with Low-E double glazing typically reaches Uw values around 1.2 – 1.6 W/m²·K, and with laminated acoustic glass and triple seals sound reduction of 40 dB or more is achievable.

Can system windows include insect screens and restrictors?

Yes. Most systems have matching screen frames, opening restrictors and child-safety catches designed to fit the profiles without affecting the seals.

What information do you need for a quotation?

The window schedule or drawings with sizes and quantities, opening types, performance targets, glass and hardware preferences, colour and finish, whether you need profile lengths or cut-to-size profiles, and the delivery destination.
