Insulated Aluminium Windows System

An insulated aluminium window is a complete system in which every heat path is interrupted: a thermally broken frame, an insulating glass unit with a warm-edge spacer, continuous gaskets, compression hardware and a sealed installation joint. Get one link wrong and the window loses heat or forms condensation, however good the rest is. Metline supplies thermal break window profiles and accessories to window fabricators, selected for each project's climate, window schedule and target standards, worldwide.

Overview

What makes an aluminium window "insulated"?

Aluminium conducts heat roughly 700 times better than the polyamide used in thermal breaks. A plain aluminium frame therefore acts as a bridge between the warm room and the cold outside, with a frame U-value (Uf) of around 5.5 – 7 W/m²K. In winter its inner face can drop below the dew point and run with condensation. An insulated system cuts that bridge so the inner and outer aluminium shells are joined only by low-conductivity material.

The thermal break is necessary but not sufficient. Heat also leaves through the glass centre, through the metal spacer at the glass edge, through gaps where the sash meets the frame, and through the joint between frame and wall. A window specification that only says "thermal break frame and double glazing" leaves most of these open, which is why two windows sold under the same description can differ in whole-window U-value (Uw) by a factor of two.

Thinking of the window as a continuous insulation line, running from the wall insulation through the installation joint, the thermal break, the gasket plane and the glazing, is the most useful way to configure an insulated system. The sections below follow that path.

Benefits of a properly insulated aluminium window

  • Lower heating and cooling energy and smaller HVAC plant
  • Warmer inner surfaces, so no condensation or mould at the frame in normal conditions
  • Better comfort near the glass: less cold radiation and fewer draughts
  • Compliance with energy codes that set maximum Uw or U-factor values
  • Keeps the slim sightlines, strength and durability of aluminium

Each of these must be at least as good as its neighbours. A 34 mm thermal break combined with an aluminium spacer and an uninsulated wall gap behaves much like a cheaper system, because heat simply takes the easier route.

Frame

Thermal break profiles: strip width, depth and alloy

Most insulated windows use the "crimped strip" method: two aluminium extrusions are knurled and rolled onto polyamide strips that run the full length. The composite profile must transmit shear between the shells, which is tested to EN 14024. The North American alternative is "pour and debridge", where liquid polyurethane fills a channel and the aluminium bridge is cut away; it is common in storefront and commercial windows.

The profiles themselves are extruded in 6060-T6/T66 (common in Europe) or 6063-T5/T6 (common in North America and Asia). These alloys give the clean surface needed for anodising and powder coating and enough stiffness for normal sash sizes. Typical wall thickness is 1.4 – 2.0 mm, increasing for large sashes, high wind loads and heavy triple glazing.

Glazing

Choosing the insulating glass unit and spacer

Glass is usually 70 – 85 % of the window area, so the IGU sets most of the result. Low-emissivity coatings reflect long-wave heat back into the room; argon in the cavity reduces conduction; a third pane adds a second insulating cavity at the cost of weight and some light.

The spacer is the strip that separates the panes at the edge. A traditional aluminium spacer adds a significant linear heat loss (Ψ around 0.08 – 0.11 W/m·K in an aluminium frame), while warm-edge spacers of stainless steel or reinforced polymer typically achieve 0.03 – 0.05 W/m·K. On a normal residential window, switching to warm edge lowers Uw by roughly 0.1 – 0.2 W/m²K and, just as important, raises the glass-edge temperature to prevent condensation along the bottom of the pane.

In hot climates the priority shifts from U-value to solar control. A solar-control Low-E coating with a low g-value (solar heat gain coefficient) keeps cooling loads down while maintaining daylight.

Configuration

Basic, enhanced and high-performance configurations

Basic insulated

Thermal break of about 20 – 24 mm, double Low-E IGU, standard spacer, two gasket lines and standard hardware. Typical Uw about 1.8 – 2.2 W/m²K. Suits apartments and homes in mild climates.

Enhanced insulated

Thermal break of about 28 – 34 mm, double Low-E argon IGU with warm-edge spacer, centre gasket and multi-point locking. Typical Uw about 1.3 – 1.6 W/m²K. Suits hotels, villas and urban residential towers.

High performance

Wide thermal break with foam-filled chambers, triple IGU, warm-edge spacer, three gasket lines and high-compression hardware. Typical Uw about 0.8 – 1.1 W/m²K. Suits cold regions and low-energy buildings.

Uw values above are indicative for a standard reference window of about 1.23 × 1.48 m; large fixed lights achieve better figures and small, frame-heavy windows worse ones. Ask for the Uw calculated for your actual sizes.

Energy codes then set the targets: for example, national building regulations across Europe set maximum Uw values for new and replacement windows, while in the United States the IECC and ENERGY STAR use NFRC-rated U-factors by climate zone. Note that 1 Btu/h·ft²·°F equals 5.678 W/m²K, so a U-factor of 0.30 is about 1.7 W/m²K.

Selection

What to prioritise by climate and building type

  • Cold climates: wide thermal break, triple or high-grade double Low-E glazing, warm-edge spacer and an insulated, airtight installation joint. Check the frame temperature factor to rule out condensation.
  • Hot climates: solar-control glass with a low g-value, good air tightness to keep humid air out, and a standard thermal break to stop the frame heating the room.
  • Coastal sites: marine-grade pretreatment and Qualicoat Class 2 or anodising to at least 20 µm, A4 stainless or coated hardware and drained, ventilated glazing rebates.
  • High-rise buildings: wind resistance and watertightness classes rise with height; deeper frames, more locking points and tested pressure equalisation matter as much as U-value.
  • Hotels, schools and roadside housing: combine thermal and acoustic needs; laminated acoustic glass in asymmetric build-ups adds sound reduction without hurting Ug.
  • Replacement projects: survey the existing opening, wall build-up and insulation position so the new frame sits in line with the insulation rather than in front of it.

FAQ

Insulated aluminium (aluminum) windows: FAQ

If a specification only says "insulated aluminium windows", what must be confirmed?

At least the target Uw or U-factor, the thermal break width or system, the glass build-up (double or triple, Low-E, gas), the spacer type, gasket and locking arrangement, and the air, water and wind classes. Without these, quotations from different suppliers cannot be compared.

Is a thermal break frame with double glazing enough?

Often not. A narrow thermal break with clear double glazing and an aluminium spacer gives a Uw of around 2.5 – 3 W/m²K, which fails many current energy codes. Low-E coatings, argon, warm-edge spacers and a wider break typically bring Uw down to 1.3 – 1.6 W/m²K.

Does triple glazing need different frames and hardware?

Yes. Triple IGUs are thicker (typically 36 – 48 mm) and about 50 % heavier than double units, so the sash needs a deeper glazing rebate and the hinges or rollers must be rated for the extra weight. Check maximum sash weight before choosing triple glazing for large opening windows.

Can aluminium windows reach passive-house levels?

Yes. High-performance systems with wide thermal breaks, foam-filled chambers and triple glazing achieve Uw values below 0.8 – 1.0 W/m²K, and several aluminium systems hold component certification from the Passive House Institute.

Will insulated aluminium windows stop condensation?

They greatly reduce it by keeping inner frame and glass-edge surfaces warmer. Indoor humidity still matters: in very humid rooms or very cold weather, some condensation on glass can occur with any window. Warm-edge spacers specifically help the bottom edge of the glass.

Are insulated aluminium windows also soundproof?

Thermal insulation and sound insulation depend on different things. For noise, use laminated acoustic glass, panes of different thickness, wider cavities and continuous seals. Both can be combined in one window when the requirements are set together.
