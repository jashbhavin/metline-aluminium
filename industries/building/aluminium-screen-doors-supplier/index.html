Overview

What an aluminium screen door does, and why the frame matters

A screen door has one job: allow fresh air and light through an open doorway while blocking insects, leaves and, in some designs, pets and intruders. How well it does that job over the years depends far more on the frame and hardware than on the mesh, which is the easiest part to replace.

Cheap screen doors are built from thin roll-formed strip that bends at the corners and twists out of square after a season of slamming. A good aluminium screen door uses extruded 6063 frame sections with proper wall thickness, reinforced corner joints and a spline groove that holds the mesh evenly. The frame stays flat, the rollers or hinges stay aligned, and the door keeps sealing against the opening.

Aluminium is the standard frame material because it does not rust in humid, coastal or poolside air, it is light enough for a sliding screen to glide with a fingertip, and it can be powder coated in any colour to match the main door or window system. Unlike timber screens, it does not swell, rot or need repainting.

What buyers should look for

  • Extruded frame sections rather than thin roll-formed strip on doors that see daily use
  • Corner joints that are keyed, crimped or screwed, not just pressed together
  • Adjustable rollers or heavy-duty hinges and a door closer on hinged doors
  • A mesh chosen for the site: insects, pets, security or airflow
  • Bug sweeps and wool-pile weatherstrip to close gaps at the edges
Screen door types

Types of aluminium screen doors

Sliding screen doors

Panels run on a track beside a sliding glass door, needing no swing space. Suit homes, apartments, hotels and balconies. Specify adjustable rollers, reinforced corners and, where useful, self-closing or self-locking hardware.

Hinged screen doors

Swing in or out, left or right hand, in front of a hinged entry door. Best for porches, back doors and high-traffic entries. Pair with a closer, latch and kick plate.

Patio screen doors

Sized to match large sliding or glass patio doors, often extra tall or wide, keeping airflow to patios, balconies and outdoor living areas.

Screen doors with pet door

A built-in pet flap sized to the animal and fitted at the right height, usually with pet-resistant mesh, a reinforced bottom rail and a kick plate.

Mosquito net and mesh doors

The same product under the names used in different markets: fly screen doors, insect screen doors or mosquito net doors. Popular for kitchens, balconies and lanais in warm climates.

Retractable screen doors

Mesh rolls or pleats into a side housing when not needed, leaving the doorway clear. Single or double for wide openings, with a slim guide track.

Heavy-duty screen doors

Heavier frames, stronger hinges or rollers, tougher mesh and protective bottom panels for rentals, frequent use, pets and light commercial entries.

French screen doors

Two hinged leaves for French doors and double entries, with flush bolts or a removable centre stile so both leaves open for full clear width.

Security screen doors

Rigid frames with stainless steel woven mesh or perforated aluminium sheet and multi-point locks, combining insect screening with intruder resistance.

Measure the opening width at the top, middle and bottom and the height at both sides, and use the smallest figure. Note whether the screen will sit inside the frame, on the face of the frame or on its own track, because each method changes the door size needed. For sliding screens, measure the track length and the height between the upper and lower tracks.

Mesh

Choosing the screen mesh

Mesh is described by its count, the number of openings per inch in each direction. A higher count blocks smaller insects but lets less air and light through. Wire or strand diameter decides strength.

Fibreglass insect screening is commonly specified to ASTM D3656. In North America, the Screen Manufacturers Association publishes SMA 1201 for insect screens on windows, sliding doors and swinging doors; security screen doors in Australia are tested and rated under AS 5039 and AS 5041.

In coastal areas, choose fibreglass, coated polyester or 316 stainless rather than bare aluminium mesh, and pair stainless mesh with an insulating spline or frame isolation to limit galvanic corrosion between the mesh and the aluminium frame.

Applications

Where aluminium screen doors are used

  • Patios and balconies: sliding or patio screen doors beside sliding glass doors keep airflow without taking up swing space.
  • Porches and entries: hinged screen doors with closers, kick plates and latches for front and back doors.
  • Lanais and pool enclosures: humid, insect-prone semi-outdoor spaces where aluminium frames and fibreglass or polyester mesh last longest.
  • Garages and utility openings: large custom screens for ventilation and insect control in garages, workshops and storerooms.
  • Apartments, hotels and rentals: uniform colour, mesh, hardware, packing and labelling across hundreds of openings.
  • Mobile homes: light, replaceable doors in standard mobile-home sizes such as 32 × 75 in.
  • Kitchens and food areas: fly screen doors that keep insects away from food preparation while allowing ventilation.

A typical apartment block order combines sliding screens for balcony doors with window screens in the same colour and mesh, so residents see one consistent system. A typical lanai uses tall hinged screen doors with heavy-duty mesh, a closer and a kick plate, matched to the screen enclosure framing.

Choose sliding screens for patio and balcony doors, hinged screens for front and back entries where they will be used all day, and retractable screens where the screen is only needed in insect season or where the view through an open doorway matters most.

Common mistakes to avoid when specifying screen doors:

  • Ordering by nominal size. Openings are rarely square; measure in three places and size to the smallest figure.
  • Light frames on busy doors. A thin frame on a back door used fifty times a day will rack out of square; specify extruded sections and a closer.
  • Wrong mesh for the site. Standard fibreglass will not survive a large dog, and bare aluminium mesh is a poor choice on the coast.
  • No bottom seal. Insects enter through the gap under the door; fit a bug sweep or adjustable bottom rail.
  • Ignoring the main door hardware. Check that the screen door handle and closer do not clash with the main door lever or storm door.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) screen doors: FAQ

What is the most common screen door size?

36 × 80 in (about 914 × 2032 mm) is the most common residential size, followed by 32 × 80 in. Sliding patio screens and French screen doors are larger, and many projects order custom sizes measured from each opening.

Which mesh is best for a screen door?

Standard 18 × 16 fibreglass suits most homes. Choose 20 × 20 no-see-um mesh for very small insects, pet-resistant polyester for cats and dogs, and woven stainless steel for security screen doors.

Are extruded frames better than roll-formed frames?

For doors used daily, yes. Extruded 6063 sections are stiffer and keep the door square, so rollers, hinges and latches stay aligned. Roll-formed frames are lighter and cheaper but suit light-duty use.

Can a pet door be built into an aluminium screen door?

Yes. The flap is sized to the animal and positioned at the right height, and the door is usually fitted with pet-resistant mesh, a reinforced bottom rail and a kick plate.

Do aluminium screen doors corrode near the sea?

The powder-coated frames perform well. Avoid bare aluminium mesh on the coast; use fibreglass, coated polyester or 316 stainless mesh, and rinse salt off from time to time.

Can screen doors be matched to existing window and door colours?

Yes. Frames can be powder coated to a RAL code or matched to the colour of the main door or window system, which is standard practice on apartment and hotel projects.

Do you supply complete screen doors?

No. Metline supplies frame profiles, aluminium mesh, hardware and accessories, cut to size on request, in mixed-size and repeat orders for screen door makers, distributors, contractors and multi-unit projects, who assemble the doors.
