Aluminium Door Systems

Aluminium door systems cover every way a door can open: hinged single and double leaves, sliding and lift-slide panels, bifolds, pivots and commercial entrance doors. The right choice depends on the opening size, traffic, weather exposure, insulation target and accessibility rules. Overview

Why aluminium for door systems

A door is the hardest-working part of the building envelope. It opens thousands of times a year, carries heavy glass on its hinges or rollers, and still has to seal against wind and rain. Extruded aluminium suits this duty: it is stiff for its weight, does not warp or swell, and can be shaped into multi-chamber profiles with gasket grooves, hardware channels and thermal breaks in one section.

Aluminium also allows much slimmer frames than timber or uPVC for the same glass size, which is why it dominates large glazed doors, shopfronts and minimal-frame designs. With a polyamide thermal break, modern aluminium doors meet the energy requirements of most climates, and coated or anodised finishes keep their appearance for decades with only washing.

Selection

How to choose a door system

Opening size and weight

Large glass panels can weigh 100 – 400 kg. Check the hinge, roller or pivot rating and the profile depth before fixing the panel size.

Thermal performance

Exterior doors in cold or hot climates need a thermal break and insulated glass; ask for the calculated UD value at your size.

Weather exposure

Upper floors and coastal sites need tested watertightness and wind classes, deeper sills and drained thresholds.

Traffic

Commercial doors need durable hinges, closers and lock cases tested for high cycle counts.

Accessibility

Low thresholds (for example 15 mm in UK Approved Document M, ½ in under US ADA rules) and suitable handle and closer forces.

Security and fire

Multi-point locks and tested security classes (PAS 24, EN 1627 RC2/RC3) where needed; fire and smoke doors certified separately under EN 16034 or local rules.

Material

Profiles, alloys and finishes

Door frames and leaves are extruded mainly in 6063-T5/T6 and 6060-T66, with 6005A-T6 for heavy sections such as lift-slide sashes or tall pivot stiles. Door profiles usually have thicker walls than windows, commonly 1.8 – 2.5 mm around hinge and lock zones, because screws and hardware carry repeated loads there. Thermal breaks of 20 – 40 mm polyamide are typical for insulated exterior doors.

  • Powder coating in any RAL colour, with super-durable grades for sun and coast.
  • PVDF for long colour retention on prestige facades.
  • Anodising (class 20 – 25) for hard-wearing metallic finishes on high-traffic doors.
  • Dual colour with different interior and exterior shades on thermally broken profiles.
  • Wood-grain transfer for a timber look without the maintenance.

Applications

Matching door types to projects

  • Shops, restaurants and offices: storefront framing with medium or wide stile entrance doors, closers and panic hardware.
  • Hotel lobbies: double or pivot entrance doors, with automatic sliding doors at main entrances.
  • Villas and terraces: lift-slide or bifold doors with thermal breaks, flush thresholds and insect screens.
  • Apartments and balconies: sliding doors with drained tracks and tested watertightness for upper floors.
  • Interiors: slimline glazed partitions and doors with minimal frames.
  • Energy-efficient buildings: insulated hinged or lift-slide doors with triple glazing.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) door systems: frequently asked questions

Should I choose a sliding, bifold or hinged door?

Choose hinged doors for the best sealing and security in normal openings, sliding or lift-slide doors where swing space is limited or panels are very large, and bifolds when you want to open almost the full width to a terrace.

When does an aluminum door need a thermal break?

Whenever it separates heated or cooled space from outside in a climate with real heating or cooling demand, or where energy codes set a door U-value. Without one, aluminium frames lose heat quickly and can show condensation on the inside.

What is the difference between entrance doors and storefront systems?

An entrance door is the operating leaf and its hardware, designed for frequent use. A storefront system is the whole ground-floor glazed frontage, including fixed glass, framing and the doors set into it.

How thick are aluminium door profiles?

Main walls are commonly 1.8 – 2.5 mm, thicker at hinge and lock zones, which is heavier than typical window profiles. The actual thickness comes from the system design and test evidence.

Do you supply finished doors to our door schedule?

No. Metline supplies door profiles, hardware and accessories to your schedule, cut and machined on request, with finish confirmed before production; your fabricator assembles and glazes the doors.

Planning doors for a project?

Send the door schedule, opening types, performance needs and finish. We will propose profile series and quote profiles and hardware.
