Aluminium Sunroom Systems & Prefab Glass Sunroom Kits

An aluminium sunroom system is a kit of extruded posts, beams, rafters, glazing bars, doors, windows, roof panels and drainage parts that assembles into a glazed room attached to a house, hotel or restaurant. Aluminium carries long spans with slim sections, does not rot or rust, and can be thermally broken for year-round use. Metline supplies the sunroom profiles, glazing bars and aluminium components to sunroom builders, dealers and contractors worldwide.

Overview

What is an aluminium sunroom system?

A sunroom is a single-storey, mostly glazed room built against an existing building. In North American codes it is usually defined as a room where glazing makes up more than about 40 % of the exterior wall and roof area. A sunroom system is the engineered frame and enclosure that makes such a room repeatable: profiles, connectors, seals, glazing and roof panels designed to work together, with tested load and weather performance.

Aluminium dominates this market for practical reasons. A 6063-T6 extrusion is stiff enough to span 3 to 5 m as a roof beam in a section that would look heavy in timber, it can carry internal screw ports, gasket grooves and drainage channels in a single shape, and its oxide film plus a powder or PVDF coating keeps it looking new with nothing more than washing. Steel needs constant protection against corrosion in a room full of condensation, and timber moves with humidity, which breaks glazing seals.

The word "system" matters. A sunroom fails at the joints: where the roof meets the house wall, where rafters meet the front beam, where glazing meets frame and where water leaves the roof. Buying matched profiles, gaskets, flashings and gutters from one source avoids the leaks and condensation problems that come from mixing parts that were never designed together.

Why aluminium for sunroom frames

  • High stiffness and strength for its weight, so posts and beams stay slim and let in more light
  • Extrusion builds gasket grooves, screw ports, thermal-break pockets and drainage into one profile
  • Does not rot, warp or rust; coatings such as AAMA 2604/2605 or Qualicoat Class 2 last for decades
  • Thermal-break profiles with polyamide strips cut heat loss for four-season rooms
  • Profiles ship in compact bundles and are bolted together on site, which suits export and remote installations
Types

Common types of aluminium sunroom

The right type depends on how many months of the year the room will be used, whether it will be heated or cooled, and how much it must keep out insects, rain and noise. North American building codes go further and assign every sunroom to one of five categories under AAMA/NPEA/NSA 2100, which then decides the performance the frame and glazing must prove.

Screen room

An aluminium frame with insect mesh instead of glass. It keeps out insects and leaves while letting air through. Light, low-cost and suited to warm climates; equivalent to Category I.

Three-season sunroom

Glazed with single toughened glass, basic double glazing or polycarbonate, on a non-thermally-broken frame. Comfortable from spring to autumn but not normally heated; Category II or III.

Four-season sunroom

Thermally broken frames, Low-E insulated glass and an insulated or high-performance glass roof. Built to be heated and cooled and used all year; Category IV or V.

Glass sunroom / solarium

Glass walls and a glass roof for maximum daylight and sky view. Needs careful solar control: Low-E or tinted roof glass, blinds or ventilation to avoid overheating.

Porch or patio enclosure

Panels and doors fitted into an existing roofed porch or terrace. The structure already exists, so the system is mainly framing, glazing and sliding or folding doors.

Retractable glass room

Sliding or stacking glass walls and sometimes a sliding roof section, so the room can be opened completely in good weather. Common in cafés, hotels and resorts.

Building codes

Sunroom categories under AAMA/NPEA/NSA 2100

The International Residential Code (IRC section R301.2.1.1.1) requires sunrooms to comply with AAMA/NPEA/NSA 2100 and requires the designer or owner to state the category on the construction drawings. Even outside North America, these categories are a useful way to agree what a sunroom must achieve before choosing profiles and glass.

The category has a direct effect on cost. A Category V room is treated much like part of the house: it needs thermally broken framing, insulated glass that meets the local energy code and tested air and water performance. A Category I or II room can use simpler profiles and single glazing. Agree the category at the start, because changing it later means changing almost every component.

Frame engineering

Which aluminium alloys and profiles go into a sunroom?

Sunroom frames are extruded almost entirely from 6xxx alloys, which combine good strength after ageing with excellent extrudability and a surface that coats and anodises well. The table shows typical choices; final sections are sized by structural calculation for the local wind and snow loads.

Wall thicknesses for sunroom profiles are typically about 1.4 to 3.0 mm, with the thicker sections reserved for beams, posts and screw-port areas. Section sizes and gauges should come from the structural calculation, not a catalogue default.

Deflection, not strength, usually controls sunroom design. Aluminium has about one third of the stiffness of steel, so roof beams are sized to limit sag under snow and to keep glass and seals from being overstressed. Deeper sections or internal steel reinforcement are used where spans are long or snow loads are high.

Roofs

Roof shapes and roofing materials

The roof decides most of a sunroom's comfort: it receives the most sun, carries the snow and handles all of the rain. Pick the shape for the house and the drainage, and the material for the climate.

Lean-to (single-slope)

The most common form: the roof falls away from the house wall to a front gutter. Simple to flash and drain, and suits single-storey and two-storey houses.

Gable (double-pitch)

Two slopes meeting at a ridge, giving a higher, more spacious interior. Needs a ridge beam and two gutters, and suits detached or garden-facing rooms.

Flat or low-slope

A modern box look, often with an insulated panel roof and a parapet or concealed gutter. Must still have a fall for drainage; never truly flat.

Curved roof

Bent rafters and curved glass or polycarbonate. Sheds snow and water well and gives a soft profile, but bending and curved glazing add cost.

Glass roof

Maximum daylight and sky view. Overhead glass should be laminated on the inner pane, and solar-control or Low-E coatings are needed to limit heat gain.

Skylight or vent sections

Opening roof vents release the hot air that collects at the top of the room; they can be manual, motorised or linked to rain sensors.

Glazing usually covers most of a sunroom's envelope, so it controls heat loss in winter, overheating in summer, glare and safety. These are the common choices and where each fits.

Performance

Drainage, sealing, insulation and ventilation

Most sunroom complaints are about leaks, condensation, heat and cold. Each one has a design answer that should be built into the system rather than added on site.

  • Roof drainage: a continuous fall to an integrated front gutter, with downpipes sized for local rainfall intensity and concealed drainage inside the posts where appearance matters
  • Wall connection: a wall plate or wall beam fixed to the house, with a flashing chased into the masonry or lapped under cladding; this is the joint most likely to leak
  • Glazing seals: EPDM gaskets in two lines inside and outside the glass, with a drained and ventilated rebate so any water that passes the outer seal escapes
  • Condensation channels: glazing bars with internal gutters that collect condensate and lead it to the outside
  • Thermal breaks: polyamide strips separating inner and outer aluminium, needed for heated rooms to stop cold frames and condensation
  • Ventilation: roof vents, high-level windows and opening doors to purge hot air; a fully glazed room in sun can overheat quickly without them
  • Shading: internal blinds, external awnings or aluminium sunshade systems on south- or west-facing rooms

Finishes

Colours and surface finishes

Powder coating

Polyester powder in any RAL or custom colour. Specify AAMA 2603, 2604 or 2605, or Qualicoat Class 1, 2 or 3, according to exposure. Coastal and high-UV sites justify the higher classes.

PVDF coating

Fluoropolymer liquid coating with outstanding colour and gloss retention, common on high-end and coastal projects; meets AAMA 2605.

Anodising

Clear, bronze or black anodic film grown from the metal itself. Specify a minimum film of 20 – 25 µm for exterior architectural use (Qualanod Class 20 or 25).

Wood-grain finish

A sublimation print over powder coat that gives the look of timber without its maintenance, popular on residential sunrooms.

Dual colour

A different colour inside and outside, possible with thermally broken profiles because the inner and outer shells are coated separately.

Common colours

White, black, anthracite and dark grey, bronze, sandstone and textured finishes are the usual choices; custom colours to sample.

Applications

Where aluminium sunrooms are used

  • Homes and villas: garden rooms, breakfast rooms and lounges built onto the rear of a house
  • Hotels and resorts: glass dining rooms, lobbies and lounges that give guests a view in any weather
  • Restaurants and cafés: conservatory dining areas and enclosed terraces that add covers all year
  • Pool enclosures: glass rooms over or beside a pool, needing corrosion-resistant finishes and good ventilation
  • Porch and balcony enclosures: renovation projects that turn an open porch or terrace into usable space
  • Care homes, schools and clinics: bright day rooms and waiting areas with good daylight

A typical villa garden room might use a lean-to roof with insulated aluminium panels and two glass strips, Low-E double-glazed walls on thermally broken 6063 frames and a three-panel sliding door, giving daylight without overheating. A café terrace, by contrast, might choose a retractable glass roof and stacking glass walls so it can open fully in summer.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) sunrooms: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a three-season and a four-season sunroom?

A three-season room uses standard aluminium frames with single or basic double glazing and is not designed to be heated, so it is comfortable from spring to autumn. A four-season room uses thermally broken frames, Low-E insulated glass and an insulated or high-performance roof, so it can be heated and cooled and used all year.

Which aluminium alloy is used for sunroom frames?

Mostly 6063 in T5 or T6 temper, which extrudes into complex sections and takes coatings well. Long-span roof beams may use 6061-T6 or 6005A-T6 for extra strength, and brackets are often machined from 6061-T6.

Do aluminium sunrooms get too hot in summer?

They can if the roof is clear glass facing the sun with no ventilation. Solar-control or Low-E roof glass, insulated roof panels, opening roof vents and blinds or external shading keep temperatures manageable. Orientation matters most: west-facing glass roofs need the most care.

Can a sunroom roof carry snow?

Yes, if it is designed for it. Roof beams, rafters and glass are sized for the local snow load from EN 1991-1-3, ASCE 7 or the national code. In heavy snow regions this means deeper beams, closer rafter spacing and thicker laminated roof glass.

Do I need a thermal break in an aluminium sunroom?

For any room that will be heated or air-conditioned, yes. Without a thermal break the aluminium frame conducts heat out of the room and its inner face gets cold enough to collect condensation. Screen rooms and unheated three-season rooms do not need one.

Can you supply only the aluminium profiles for a local fabricator?

Yes, that is what we supply. Metline provides the extrusions, cut and machined to drawing and finished in the chosen colour, together with gaskets and accessories, for local glazing and assembly. We do not supply complete kits with glass and doors.

Planning an aluminium sunroom project?

Send the room size, roof style, glazing and location. We will propose frame and roof profiles and quote delivered supply.
