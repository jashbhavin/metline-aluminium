Commercial Aluminium Fence & Railing Systems

Commercial aluminium fence and railing systems secure building perimeters, protect people at raised edges and divide outdoor spaces on offices, apartments, hotels, schools and public sites. Extruded 6063 and 6005A posts, rails, pickets, slats and glass channels are built into welded panels or modular systems, sized to meet building-code guard loads and finished to resist years of weather. Metline supplies the profiles and components to fabricators for commercial projects worldwide.

Overview

Commercial versus residential fence and railing

Commercial projects ask more of a fence or railing than a private garden does. Guards on balconies, walkways and stairs in public and multi-occupancy buildings must resist higher design loads, are inspected against building codes, and see far more use and abuse. Runs are long, so consistency of colour and dimensions across hundreds of panels matters, and the system must be quick to install on a busy site.

Aluminium meets these needs well. A powder-coated aluminium fence does not rust where it is cut, drilled or scratched, unlike painted steel, so it keeps its appearance on exposed and coastal sites with very little maintenance. It weighs about a third as much as steel, which reduces handling, crane time and load on balcony slabs. And because profiles are extruded, posts can include screw ports, glass channels and concealed fixing grooves that make modular assembly fast and tidy.

System types

Commercial fence and railing styles

Vertical picket fence

Square or round pickets between top and bottom rails. Hard to climb when rails are spaced correctly, open to view, and the standard for secure perimeters, schools and pool areas.

Horizontal slat fence

Slats of 50 – 150 mm with adjustable gaps for a modern look and controllable screening. Used on courtyards, terraces and plant enclosures.

Privacy screens

Close-spaced slats, louvres or perforated panels for hotels, apartments and bin or plant areas.

Safety railings and guards

Post-and-rail or picket guards for walkways, platforms, ramps and stairs, designed to code loads and opening limits.

Glass railings

Laminated glass in aluminium base shoes, posts or frames, preserving views on balconies, atriums and high-rise terraces.

Balcony and deck railings

Railings of posts, top rails, infill and stair sections, matched across a whole building.
Building codes

Guard heights, openings and design loads

Railings that protect a drop are guards, and every code sets three requirements for them: a minimum height, a maximum opening size, and the load the guard must resist. The values below are common reference points; always confirm the code that applies to your project.

Loads for crowd areas such as stadiums, shopping centres and stations are much higher than for offices or apartments. Glass balustrades usually require laminated toughened glass so that the guard stays in place if a pane breaks.

Meeting these loads is mostly about the post and its fixing. A 1.1 m post carrying a horizontal load at the top places a large bending moment on its base, so post sections, wall thickness, base plates and anchors are sized by calculation. In aluminium, 6005A-T6 or 6061-T6 posts, or internally reinforced 6063 posts, are common for higher loads. Fixings into concrete use tested anchors with adequate edge distance; side-fixed posts on slab edges are checked carefully for both the anchor and the slab.

Welded panels are assembled and welded in the factory before coating. They are rigid, consistent and quick to install, which suits long perimeter fences with regular spacing. The trade-off is bulk in shipping and less flexibility on site.

Modular systems use pickets and slats fixed into punched or grooved rails with screws or concealed clips. They ship flat, can be cut and adjusted on site for slopes and corners, and damaged parts can be replaced individually. Most balcony and stair railings are modular.

Finishes

Finishes for long outdoor life

For commercial exterior work, specify the coating standard as well as the colour. Powder coating to AAMA 2604 or Qualicoat Class 2 is a good default; AAMA 2605 or Qualicoat Class 3 suits coastal, high-UV and prestige sites. Anodising (at least 20 µm, Qualanod Class 20) gives a durable metallic look, and wood-grain transfer finishes are popular on slat and privacy screens. Black, anthracite and bronze are the most common colours for perimeter fences.

FAQ

Commercial aluminium (aluminum) fences and railings: FAQ

What height must a commercial railing be?

Under the International Building Code, guards in commercial buildings must be at least 42 in (1067 mm) high. In the UK, 1100 mm is typical for balconies and edges. Check the code for your country and use.

Which aluminium alloy is used for commercial fences?

6063-T5 or T6 is standard for pickets, slats and rails. Posts that carry code guard loads often use 6005A-T6 or 6061-T6, or reinforced 6063 sections.

Welded or modular: which is better?

Welded panels are rigid and quick to install on long, regular runs. Modular systems ship flat, adapt to slopes and corners on site and allow individual parts to be replaced. Many projects use welded fences and modular railings.

Are aluminium fences suitable for coastal sites?

Yes. Aluminium does not rust, and with a coating to AAMA 2605 or Qualicoat Class 3 and stainless steel fixings isolated from the aluminium, fences and railings last well in salt air with periodic washing.

Do you supply complete gates?

No. We supply gate-frame profiles, matching posts, hinges, latches, base plates, end caps and fixings with the fence or railing profiles; your fabricator builds the gates.

Can glass railings be supplied with aluminium base shoes?

Yes. Aluminium base shoes, posts and top rails can be supplied for laminated glass balustrades, with the glass and fixing system selected for the required guard load.

Planning a commercial fence or railing project?
