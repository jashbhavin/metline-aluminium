Slimline Aluminium Doors

Slimline aluminium doors reduce the visible frame to a thin line, so almost the whole opening is glass. They work because aluminium is stiff and strong enough to carry heavy glass in very narrow sections, something timber and uPVC cannot match. Metline supplies narrow-profile extrusions and accessories for slimline sliding, bifold, hinged, pivot and interior doors to door fabricators, matched to each project's opening sizes, glass weight and performance targets.

Overview

What makes an aluminium door "slimline"?

The term describes sightlines: the width of frame you see when looking at the closed door. A standard aluminium sliding door might show 80 – 120 mm of frame where two panels meet; a slimline system brings that interlock down to about 20 – 50 mm. Outer frames are often recessed into the floor, walls and ceiling so they almost disappear, leaving the glass to define the opening.

Slim sightlines are an engineering challenge, not just a styling choice. The narrower the profile, the less material is available to resist bending from wind and from the glass weight. Slimline systems compensate with deeper sections (stiffness rises with the cube of depth), thicker walls, high-strength tempers, steel or stainless reinforcement inside some interlocks, and hardware designed for very heavy panels. That is why genuine slimline systems cost more than standard doors that are simply cut thinner.

Typical applications are villa and apartment terraces, hotel rooms and suites facing a view, restaurants that open onto a garden, showrooms and interior partitions where daylight must pass between rooms. In each case, the door is chosen as much for the view and light it lets through as for access.

What slimline aluminium doors offer

  • Up to about 95 % glass in large sliding panels, for maximum view and daylight
  • Clean modern lines that suit minimalist architecture
  • Flush or near-flush thresholds that connect indoor and outdoor floors
  • Strength to carry panels of several hundred kilograms on suitable hardware
  • No warping, swelling or rot, and long-lasting coated finishes
Configurations

Types of slimline aluminium door

Slimline sliding door

Two, three or more tracks; panels slide past each other or into wall pockets. The slimmest interlocks of any operable door and the most popular slimline type for terraces and balconies.

Lift-and-slide door

Turning the handle lifts the panel off its seals onto rollers; lowering it compresses the gaskets. Gives better sealing and supports very large, heavy panels.

Slimline bifold door

Panels fold and stack to one or both sides, opening almost the full width. Sightlines are wider than sliding doors because each fold has two stiles.

Slim hinged / French door

Single or double leaf, inward or outward opening, for entrances, balconies and side doors. Narrow leaf profiles with concealed hinges keep the look minimal.

Slim pivot door

A large single panel turning on an offset or centre pivot set into floor and head. Used for feature entrances, often 2.4 – 3 m tall.

Slim interior door and partition

Non-insulated narrow frames with clear, frosted or laminated glass to divide offices, kitchens and studies while sharing light.

Glazing

Glass choice and panel weight

Glass weighs about 2.5 kg per square metre for each millimetre of thickness. A 2.5 × 3 m sliding panel with a 6 / 16 / 6 double-glazed unit carries 12 mm of glass, which is roughly 30 kg/m² or about 225 kg of glass alone before adding the frame. Triple glazing or thick laminated glass can push one panel well over 300 kg, which is why the roller and track system must be chosen together with the glass.

Glass typePurposeWhere to use it
Toughened (tempered) glassBreaks into small blunt pieces; required in doors by safety codesAll door panels unless laminated is specified
Laminated glassInterlayer holds fragments; adds security and sound reductionGround-floor and accessible doors, balustrade-height glazing, acoustic needs
Low-E insulating glass unitReduces heat loss and solar gainExterior doors in heated or cooled buildings

Acoustic laminated glassSound reduction with asymmetric panesHotels, apartments on busy roads
Fire-rated or security glassFire resistance or attack resistanceOnly in systems tested with that glass

Safety glazing rules apply to all door glass: EN 12600 impact classes and national building regulations in Europe, and CPSC 16 CFR 1201 and ANSI Z97.1 in the United States. For North American projects, glass is usually certified through the SGCC or IGCC programmes; ask your glass supplier for the relevant labels and documents.

Selection advice

When the slimmest frame is not the right choice

  • Very large or heavy panels: if the glass weight exceeds the roller or hinge rating, panels sag and become hard to operate. Choose a heavier system or smaller panels.
  • High wind loads: on high-rise floors, coastal fronts and cyclone regions, interlock stiffness and locking points matter more than a few millimetres of sightline.
  • Exposed flush thresholds: a level threshold with no step is vulnerable to wind-driven rain; it needs a drainage channel outside and a sheltered position or a raised sill.
  • Strict thermal, security or fire ratings: select a system tested to the rating, even if its frames are slightly wider.
  • Tight budgets: standard narrow-frame doors often give most of the visual benefit at noticeably lower cost.

FAQ

Slimline aluminium (aluminum) doors: FAQ

What are slimline aluminium doors?

They are aluminium doors with very narrow visible frames, usually 20 – 50 mm at the meeting stiles of sliding panels, so the opening is almost entirely glass. They are used for views, daylight and minimalist design.

Are slimline doors strong enough for large glass panels?

Yes, when the system is chosen for the panel size. Deep profiles, thick walls, reinforced interlocks and heavy-duty rollers let slimline sliding panels carry several hundred kilograms. Always check the system's maximum panel size and weight against your design.

What frame thickness is recommended?

There is no single figure. Main load-bearing profiles in quality systems generally have walls of at least 2.0 mm, with sizes increasing for large panels, high wind loads and heavy glazing. The system's structural data, not the visible width, decides suitability.

Are slimline doors energy efficient?

Thermally broken slimline systems with Low-E double or triple glazing achieve good door U-values. Non-insulated minimal-frame systems are intended for interiors or mild climates and perform noticeably worse, so check which version is offered.

What are the disadvantages of slimline aluminium doors?

They cost more than standard doors, need careful installation to tight tolerances, and very low thresholds need good drainage. Interlocks are also more exposed to impact. Choosing a tested system and an experienced installer avoids most problems.

Are slimline doors suitable for coastal villas?

Yes, with a coastal-grade finish (Qualicoat Class 2 or 3, PVDF or anodising with marine pretreatment), stainless or coated hardware, and a threshold and drainage detail designed for wind-driven rain.
