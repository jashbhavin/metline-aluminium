Powder Coated Aluminium Windows

Powder coated aluminium windows have frames finished with a baked-on layer of polyester or fluoropolymer powder, giving any RAL colour in matt, satin, gloss, textured, metallic or woodgrain effects. The finish lasts as long as its specification: the pretreatment, powder class and film thickness decide whether a frame still looks right after five years or after twenty-five. Metline supplies powder coated window profiles to Qualicoat and AAMA grades to window fabricators for residential, hotel and commercial projects worldwide.

Overview

What is a powder coated aluminium window?

Powder coating is a dry finishing process. Finely ground resin, pigment and additives are sprayed onto the aluminium profile through an electrostatic gun, so the charged particles wrap around the earthed metal and cling to it. The profile then passes through an oven, where the powder melts, flows into a continuous film and cross-links into a hard, durable coat. No solvents are used, overspray can be recovered, and a single coat typically builds 60 – 80 µm, far thicker than a liquid paint coat.

For windows, the profiles are normally coated as straight extrusions before they are cut, machined and assembled into frames and sashes. Coating the full length gives even colour along every face, while the cut ends are concealed inside corner joints and sealed. Thermal break profiles can be coated before or after the polyamide strips are rolled in; coating before assembly allows a different colour inside and outside, a popular option where the facade calls for anthracite but the interior suits white.

Powder coating is the most widely used finish for aluminium windows today because it combines a near-unlimited colour range with good durability and a low cost per square metre. Its main competitor is anodising, which suits projects that want a metallic appearance; the two are compared later on this page.

Why specify powder coated aluminium windows

  • Any RAL, NCS or custom colour, plus textured, metallic and woodgrain effects
  • Thick, continuous film that resists chipping, UV and corrosion when correctly specified
  • Dual colours possible on thermally broken profiles
  • Easy cleaning with water and mild detergent; no repainting for many years
  • Solvent-free process with recoverable overspray

Durability

Coating grades: Qualicoat and AAMA explained

Two specification systems dominate architectural coatings on aluminium. Qualicoat, based in Switzerland, is used across Europe, the Middle East, India and much of Asia. The AAMA voluntary specifications, published by FGIA, are standard in North America. Both grade coatings by how much colour and gloss they retain after outdoor exposure in Florida, the benchmark test site for high UV and humidity.

GradeOutdoor exposure testMain retention requirementTypical use
AAMA 2603Optional 1 year, South FloridaBasic colour and chalk limitsInterior and low-exposure exterior parts
AAMA 26045 years, South FloridaGloss retention at least 30 %, colour change ΔE no more than 5Residential and commercial windows
AAMA 260510 years, South FloridaGloss retention at least 50 %, colour change ΔE no more than 5, plus severe humidity and salt-spray testsHigh-rise, monumental and coastal projects
Qualicoat Class 11 year, FloridaGloss retention at least 50 %Standard architectural use
Qualicoat Class 23 years, FloridaGloss retention at least 50 % with tighter colour limitsHigh-UV climates, dark and bright colours
Qualicoat Class 310 years, FloridaHigh gloss and colour retention, comparable to PVDFPremium and long-life facades

Summarised from published AAMA and Qualicoat requirements; consult the current edition of each specification for the full test list and limits.

As a working guide, Class 1 or AAMA 2604-level polyester powder suits most homes in temperate climates. Specify Class 2 or AAMA 2604 super-durable powder in strong sun, at altitude or for dark colours, and Class 3 or AAMA 2605 for high-rise buildings, prestige projects and coastal sites where repainting would be expensive or impossible.

Process

How aluminium window profiles are powder coated

  1. PretreatmentProfiles are degreased, etched and given a conversion coating, today usually chrome-free (zirconium or titanium based). For coastal work, a deeper etch or anodic pre-treatment is used to reduce filiform corrosion.
  2. DryingThe profiles are rinsed in demineralised water and dried so no moisture is trapped under the coating.
  3. Electrostatic applicationAutomatic and manual guns apply powder evenly to visible faces, with attention to corners and recesses where the electric field is weaker.
  4. CuringIn the oven, the metal is held at the powder maker's cure schedule, typically around 180 – 200 °C for 10 – 15 minutes at metal temperature, so the film fully cross-links.
  5. Inspection and testingEach batch is checked for colour, gloss, film thickness, adhesion and appearance before profiles go on to fabrication.

The extrusion itself affects the result. Die lines, scratches, pick-up and inconsistent surface alloy chemistry show through a thin film, so window profiles use alloys with tightly controlled iron and silicon and are handled to protect visible faces. Undercured powder looks fine on day one but chalks and loses gloss early, which is why cure verification (for example a solvent rub test) is part of routine quality control.

Window types

Window types our coated profiles suit

Sliding windows

Sashes slide horizontally on rollers, saving space on balconies, in apartments and in hotel rooms. Suits wide openings; multi-track and lift-and-slide versions available.

Casement windows

Side-hung sashes open fully for ventilation and seal tightly on gaskets. Good air, water and sound performance for homes, villas and offices.

Tilt and turn windows

Tilt inwards for secure ventilation, turn inwards for cleaning and full opening. Common in Europe and high-rise housing.

Awning and hopper windows

Top-hung or bottom-hung sashes for kitchens, bathrooms, schools and corridors, ventilating even in light rain.

Fixed windows

Non-opening frames for large views, stairwells and feature glazing; the slimmest sightlines and lowest cost per square metre.

Matching door profiles

Sliding, hinged, bifold and lift-and-slide door profiles in the same colour and system family, for a consistent facade.

  • Match the hardware: handles, hinges and visible screws should be ordered in the same or a deliberately contrasting colour.
  • Consider heat: dark frames reach surface temperatures 20 – 30 °C higher than white ones in direct sun, which increases thermal movement; good systems allow for it.
  • Texture hides wear: fine-textured powders conceal minor scratches and fingerprints better than smooth gloss finishes.
  • Dual colour: on thermal break profiles, inside and outside colours can differ at little extra cost.

Choose powder coating when the facade needs a specific colour, when doors, windows and cladding must match, or when budget favours it. Choose anodising when a metallic appearance and very hard surface are the priority. Some projects combine both: anodised trims and curtain wall caps with powder coated window frames.

DefectWhat you seeUsual causePrevention
Filiform corrosionThread-like worm tracks under the film, starting at cut edges or holesWeak pretreatment combined with salt and humiditySeaside-grade pretreatment, sealed machined edges, regular washing

Poor adhesionCoating flakes or lifts in sheetsContaminated or inadequately pretreated surfaceControlled pretreatment line, cross-cut testing on each batch
Orange peel and runsUneven, dimpled or thickened surfaceExcess film build or poor gun settingsThickness control within specified range

Most failures seen on site are not caused by the powder itself but by what happened before and after coating: inadequate cleaning and conversion coating, scratches during fabrication left unrepaired, or mortar, cement and acidic cleaners splashed onto frames during construction. Keep the protective film on until wet trades are finished, then remove it promptly, since film left for months in strong sun becomes difficult to peel and can leave adhesive residue.

Applications

Where powder coated aluminium windows are used

  • Housing and apartments: white, grey or black casement and sliding windows, often with insulated glass and dual colours.
  • Villas and premium homes: slim-frame and large fixed windows in anthracite, black or bronze, with matching sliding doors.
  • Hotels and resorts: uniform colour across hundreds of rooms; coastal resorts need Class 2 or 3 coatings and marine pretreatment.
  • Offices and commercial buildings: ribbon windows and punched openings matched to curtain wall and cladding colours.
  • Schools and hospitals: durable textured finishes that hide wear, with restricted-opening hardware.
  • Shopfronts: fixed and door frames coated to brand colours.

In coastal locations, the coating class matters less than the pretreatment and the detailing. Filiform corrosion starts at cut edges and drilled holes, so seal machined areas, specify a seaside-grade pretreatment and wash frames periodically to remove salt deposits.

Cost

What affects the price of powder coated aluminium windows?

Size and type

Larger windows need deeper profiles, thicker glass and stronger hardware; opening sashes cost more than fixed lights.

Profile system

Thermal break and slim-frame systems cost more than basic non-insulated sections.

Glass

Low-E, laminated, acoustic and triple glazing raise cost but also performance.

Coating grade

Super-durable powders and Class 3 / AAMA 2605 systems cost more than standard polyester.

Colour and effect

Standard white, black and grey are economical; special RALs, metallics and woodgrain add cost, especially in small quantities.

Hardware, quantity and packing

Hardware brand, number of locking points, order volume and export packing all affect the delivered price.

FAQ

Powder coated aluminium (aluminum) windows: FAQ

How long does powder coating on aluminium windows last?

A correctly pretreated and cured standard polyester coating typically keeps an acceptable appearance for 15 – 20 years in moderate climates; super-durable and Class 3 / AAMA 2605 systems last longer and are often backed by manufacturer warranties of 20 years or more. Coastal and high-UV sites shorten life unless the grade is chosen for them.

Are black powder coated windows suitable for hot climates?

Yes, if you specify a super-durable or fluoropolymer powder and a system designed for thermal movement. Black frames get much hotter than white in direct sun, so standard polyester can fade faster, and long frames expand more.

Which is better, powder coating or anodising?

Neither is better in all cases. Powder coating offers unlimited colours and textures and is easy to touch up. Anodising gives a hard, metallic surface that cannot peel. Choose by appearance, budget and exposure.

Can powder coated aluminium windows be recoated?

Yes. The surface must be cleaned, lightly abraded and checked for adhesion, then recoated with a compatible liquid coating on site or stripped and powder coated again in a workshop. Painting directly over a dirty or chalked surface fails quickly.

How should powder coated frames be cleaned?

Wash with clean water and a mild, pH-neutral detergent using a soft cloth or sponge, then rinse. Avoid abrasive pads and solvents. Twice a year is enough inland; near the sea, clean every three months to remove salt.

Can inside and outside be different colours?

Yes, on thermally broken profiles the inner and outer aluminium shells are coated separately before assembly, so any combination of colours is possible.

Can I get a quotation without complete drawings?

Yes. Building type, approximate window sizes and quantities, colour and site location are enough for a budget price; final pricing follows once drawings and a window schedule are available.
