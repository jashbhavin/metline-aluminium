Aluminium Facade Systems

An aluminium facade system is the engineered outer skin of a building: glazed curtain walls, ventilated rainscreen cladding, window walls, sunshades and louvres, built from extruded profiles and aluminium panels. Choosing the right combination balances appearance, weather and fire performance, energy use, cost and construction speed. Metline supplies the extrusions, sheet, honeycomb panels and machined components behind these systems, to project drawings, worldwide.

Overview

Why aluminium dominates modern facades

Most commercial and high-rise facades built today use aluminium somewhere: in the framing of the glass, in the panels between it, in the sub-frames that carry the cladding, or in the fins and louvres that shade it. The reasons are practical.

  • Light weight: about a third of the density of steel, which reduces the load on slab edges and the size of brackets.
  • Extrudability: complex multi-chamber profiles with gasket grooves, screw ports and thermal breaks are made in one pass.
  • Durability: a natural oxide film resists corrosion; anodised and PVDF finishes last for decades with only washing.
  • Formability: sheet folds into cassettes, curves and three-dimensional shapes.
  • Fire behaviour: uncoated aluminium is non-combustible (class A1); solid aluminium panels avoid the risks of combustible cores.
  • Recyclability: aluminium can be recycled repeatedly without loss of properties, and facade scrap has high value; recycled-content and low-carbon primary billet can cut embodied carbon substantially.

Selection

How to choose the right facade system

Building height and programme

Unitised systems enclose tall buildings faster with less site labour; stick systems suit lower buildings and irregular geometry.

Glass ratio and energy

High glazing ratios need high-performance glass and shading to meet energy codes; spandrel and solid areas can carry more insulation.

Fire regulations

Many countries now require external wall materials of class A2-s1,d0 or better on tall residential buildings, which favours solid aluminium and metal-cored panels over polymer-cored composites.

Climate and exposure

Coastal and polluted sites need PVDF, super-durable powder or thicker anodising, and isolation of dissimilar metals.

Wind and movement

Design wind pressures, inter-storey drift and thermal expansion set profile depths, joints and bracket details.

Maintenance and access

Plan for cleaning and glass replacement: building maintenance units, removable panels and accessible gaskets.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) facade systems: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a curtain wall and cladding?

A curtain wall is a complete glazed wall that keeps out weather on its own and spans between floors. Cladding is an outer skin of panels fixed over a separate, insulated and weather-tight wall, usually with a ventilated cavity behind.

Is aluminium cladding safe in a fire?

Solid aluminium sheet is non-combustible when uncoated (class A1) and coated panels typically achieve A1 or A2-s1,d0. The products restricted on tall buildings are composite panels with polyethylene cores. Always check the classification of the actual product and the whole wall build-up.

Which aluminium alloy is used for facades?

6063 and 6060 for most extrusions, 6005A or 6061 for heavily loaded members, and 3003, 5005 or 5754 sheet for panels. 5005 is preferred for anodised panels to match anodised extrusions.

How long does an aluminum facade last?

Well-designed aluminium facades routinely serve for decades. Coating warranties for PVDF and super-durable powders are typically long-term, and gaskets and sealants may need renewal during the building's life.

Can you supply all aluminium materials for a facade?

Metline supplies curtain wall and window extrusions, cladding sheet and honeycomb panels, louvre profiles, brackets and machined components to project drawings, with finishing, export packing and mill certificates. The facade contractor fabricates and installs.
