Aluminium Sliding Glass & Patio Doors

An aluminium sliding glass door, usually called a patio door, is a large glazed door whose panels slide sideways in an extruded aluminium frame to connect a room with a garden, terrace or balcony. The frame stays slim while carrying heavy insulating glass, and with a thermal break and Low-E glazing it meets modern energy rules. Metline supplies sliding patio door profiles, thermal-break sections, tracks and hardware to door fabricators for residential and commercial projects worldwide.

Overview

What makes a good aluminium sliding glass door?

A patio door is mostly glass, often 80 – 90 % of its area, so buyers tend to judge it by the view. In service, however, its performance is decided by the parts you do not see: the stiffness of the interlock, the rollers and track, the drainage path under the sill, the gaskets and the thermal break. These decide whether the door still slides easily, stays dry in driving rain and keeps the room comfortable ten years after installation.

Aluminium is well suited to this because 6063 extrusions combine stiffness, accuracy and a durable finish in sections narrow enough to keep sightlines slim. A modern thermally broken aluminium patio door can carry triple glazing on sashes weighing a few hundred kilograms, which uPVC frames struggle to do without steel reinforcement and much wider profiles.

A complete patio door package contains the outer frame (head, sill and jambs), the sashes, the interlock profiles where the sashes meet, the bottom track and running rail, rollers, locks and handles, gaskets and brush seals, glazing beads and setting blocks, the glass itself and, often, an insect screen. Metline supplies the aluminium profiles, tracks and hardware to your drawings; your fabricator adds the glass, assembles and glazes the doors.

Five checks before you choose a patio door

  • Is the frame thermally broken, and what whole-door U-value (Uw or U-factor) is declared for your size?
  • Is the glass toughened or laminated safety glass, as building codes require in doors?
  • What sash weight are the rollers rated for, and are they adjustable and replaceable?
  • How does the sill drain, and what water-tightness class has the system been tested to?
  • Is the finish to a recognised standard (Qualicoat, Qualanod or AAMA 2603/2604/2605)?
Product options

Types of sliding patio and glass door

Two-track sliding door

One sliding and one fixed panel (or two sliders). The most common patio and balcony door: simple, economical and quick to specify. Opens about half the width.

Three-track patio door

Three panels on three tracks so two can stack behind the third, opening about two-thirds of the width. A good compromise between opening area and cost.

Multi-track / multi-slide door

Four to six panels on as many tracks, stacking to one side or parting in the middle. Used in villas, hotels and restaurants where most of the wall should open.

Pocket sliding glass door

Panels slide into a cavity in the wall and disappear, leaving the full opening clear. The wall must be designed for the pocket, with access to the concealed track and drainage.

Corner sliding door (90° or 135°)

Two runs meet at a corner without a post, so the whole corner opens. Needs careful structural design because the corner loses the post that would normally resist wind and roof loads.

Slim-frame sliding door

Very narrow interlocks and concealed frames for an almost frameless look. See slimline doors.

Motorised sliding door

An electric operator moves heavy sashes at the touch of a switch or remote, useful above about 300 kg and for accessible design.

Oversized custom door

Panels up to 3 m tall or more, sized to the project; glass weight, roller capacity and wind load are checked before quotation.

Lift-and-slide door

Sash lifts off its seals to move and clamps down to close, giving better sealing for large panels. See sliding door systems.

Patio doors can be made to almost any opening, but in North America many residential openings follow nominal sizes in inches, and replacement doors are often ordered to them. Glazing

Choosing the glass

Toughened (tempered) glass

About four to five times stronger than annealed float glass and breaks into small blunt pieces. The minimum for doors under EN 12600, 16 CFR 1201 / ANSI Z97.1 and AS 1288.

Laminated glass

Two panes bonded with a PVB or ionoplast interlayer that holds fragments if broken. Adds security, cuts noise and blocks almost all UV, which protects floors and furniture from fading.

Low-E insulating glass

A thin metal oxide coating reflects long-wave heat. Use a high solar gain coating in cold climates and a solar-control (low SHGC) coating in hot ones.

Argon fill and warm edge

Argon in the cavity and a plastic or stainless "warm-edge" spacer reduce heat loss and condensation at the glass edge.

Triple glazing

Needed to reach very low U-values in cold climates; heavier, so check roller capacity and frame depth.

Decorative glass

Tinted, frosted, patterned or reeded glass for privacy in bathrooms, street-facing rooms and offices.

Construction

Construction details that separate good doors from poor ones

Stiff interlock

The meeting stiles carry wind load over the full door height. Deeper or reinforced interlocks stop the panels bowing and whistling in strong wind.

Drained sill

Water that passes the outer seals must drain out through weep slots and baffles, not into the floor. Concealed drainage keeps the sill neat.

Multiple seal lines

EPDM gaskets around the glass and fin-seal brush pile or gaskets at the interlock and sash edges limit air leakage, water ingress and dust.

Thermal break

Polyamide strips separate the inner and outer aluminium, reducing heat flow and preventing condensation on the inside frame in winter.

Adjustable rollers

Tandem stainless or sealed-bearing rollers that can be adjusted after installation keep the sash running true as the building settles.

Slim sightlines

Narrow frames and interlocks increase the glass area and daylight, but only if the profiles are stiff enough for the panel size.

A flush threshold looks clean and suits wheelchairs and pushchairs, but it moves the weather line to floor level. Use it where the door is sheltered by a canopy or recessed in the façade, provide a drainage channel outside, and expect a lower water-tightness rating than a raised sill.

For a small standard balcony door on a tight budget, uPVC is a reasonable choice. For large openings, multi-track layouts, slim frames, strong sunlight or commercial use, aluminium is usually the better long-term option.

Replacement

Replacing an existing sliding glass door

  1. MeasureWidth and height at three points and wall depth; use the smallest values and check that the opening is square.
  2. Inspect the sillLook for a damaged, corroded or blocked track and rot or water damage in the floor below; decide on raised, low or recessed track.
  3. Choose the layoutTwo, three or multi-panel, which panel slides, and from which side it is operated, viewed from outside.
  4. Select glassSafety glass in all cases, then thermal, solar and acoustic performance for the climate and location.
  5. Match hardwareRollers rated for the sash weight, locks, handles and screen.
  6. Install and testFix level and plumb, seal to the wall and floor membrane, then check smooth sliding, locking and even seal contact.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) sliding glass doors: FAQ

Which is better, aluminium or uPVC sliding glass doors?

Aluminium is better for large panels, slim frames, multi-track layouts, hot climates and commercial use, because it is stiffer and dimensionally stable. uPVC costs less and insulates well in standard residential sizes. A thermally broken aluminium door with Low-E glass closes most of the thermal gap.

What are the disadvantages of aluminum patio doors?

Aluminium conducts heat, so frames without a thermal break lose heat and can show condensation in cold weather. The initial cost is higher than uPVC. Both points are addressed by choosing a thermally broken system with insulating glass.

What is the standard size of a sliding patio door?

In North America the most common sizes are 60, 72 and 96 inches wide by 80 inches high, with 96-inch tall doors increasingly popular. Metric projects are usually made to the measured opening.

How heavy is a sliding glass door panel?

Glass weighs about 2.5 kg/m² per mm. A 1.5 × 2.4 m sash with a 6 + 16 + 6 mm unit carries about 108 kg of glass, plus the frame. Larger or triple-glazed panels easily exceed 200 kg and need heavy-duty or lift-and-slide hardware.

Do sliding glass doors have to use safety glass?

Yes. Building codes require safety glazing in doors: toughened or laminated glass that passes EN 12600 in Europe, 16 CFR 1201 or ANSI Z97.1 in the US, or AS 1288 in Australia.

Can a sliding patio door have a flush threshold?

Yes, with a recessed track. It works best where the door is sheltered, with an external drainage channel, because a flush sill gives lower water tightness than a raised one.

What information do you need to quote?

Opening sizes and quantity, project location, layout and opening direction, glass and performance targets, frame colour and finish, hardware preference and any drawings or photos.
