Aluminium Stick Welding Rods (Covered Electrodes)

Aluminium stick welding rods are flux-covered electrodes for shielded metal arc welding (SMAW). They let you weld aluminium with an ordinary DC stick welder and no shielding gas, which makes them useful for site repair, castings and maintenance where MIG or TIG equipment is impractical. The standard classes under AWS A5.3 are E4043, E1100 and E3003; Metline supplies these and Al-Mg type electrodes in common diameters, worldwide.

Overview

What an aluminium stick electrode is

An aluminium stick electrode is a length of aluminium alloy core wire covered with a thick flux coating. When the arc is struck, the core melts to become weld metal while the coating melts and partly vaporises. The molten flux dissolves the aluminium oxide film, shields the pool from air and leaves a slag layer over the bead.

The process is simple and portable: a DC power source, an electrode holder and a ground clamp are enough. That is why stick electrodes remain in use for field repairs, agricultural and plant maintenance, emergency work and casting repair, even though gas metal arc (MIG) and gas tungsten arc (TIG) welding produce better aluminium welds and dominate fabrication shops.

Stick welding aluminium is not the same as stick welding steel. The electrodes burn off very quickly, the arc is harder to restart, and the flux residue is corrosive and must be removed. Understanding these limits is the key to getting sound welds, and to knowing when another process is the better choice.

Classes

Electrode classes and how to choose

AWS A5.3/A5.3M, the specification for aluminium and aluminium-alloy electrodes for SMAW, defines three classifications. Each is named after the wrought alloy of its core wire.

E4043 (Al-Si 5 %)

The most widely used aluminium stick electrode. Silicon makes the weld pool fluid and reduces hot cracking, so it suits 6xxx alloys such as 6061 and 6063, 3xxx alloys and many silicon-bearing castings. Welds are grey after anodising.

E1100 (commercially pure)

For 1xxx alloys such as 1100 and 1350 electrical conductor grades, where conductivity and corrosion resistance matter more than strength.

E3003 (Al-Mn)

For 3003 and 1100 base metals, giving slightly higher strength than E1100. Used on tanks, ducting and general sheet work.

Al-Mg types (5356 / 5183 core)

Some makers offer electrodes with Al-Mg core wire for 5xxx alloys. These are manufacturer classifications rather than AWS A5.3 classes, and availability is limited. Check the datasheet and qualify the procedure.

For structural 5xxx work such as 5083 or 5086 marine plate, the normal practice is MIG or TIG with ER5356 or ER5183 wire. Stick electrodes are best kept for repair and non-critical joints on these alloys.

The flux covering is not standardised by composition. It is typically a blend of alkali chlorides and fluorides, similar in chemistry to aluminium welding and brazing fluxes, which is why the residue is hygroscopic and corrosive.

The AWS minimums are measured on groove welds in the test base metal, so they partly reflect the annealed strength of that base metal. On heat-treatable alloys the joint strength is governed by the heat-affected zone: welded 6061-T6 typically drops to roughly 165 – 205 MPa ultimate across the weld regardless of filler, which design codes such as the Aluminum Design Manual and EN 1999-1-1 account for.

Avoid E4043 on alloys containing more than about 3 % magnesium, such as 5083 and 5456. Magnesium and silicon combine to form brittle Mg2Si in the weld, reducing ductility.

Technique

How to stick weld aluminium

  1. Set up DCEPUse direct current with the electrode positive (reverse polarity). AC is not suitable for most aluminium electrodes.
  2. Clean the jointRemove oil with solvent, then remove oxide with a stainless steel brush used only on aluminium. Weld soon after cleaning.
  3. Preheat thick or cold partsAluminium conducts heat away fast. Preheating sections over about 10 mm to roughly 100 – 150 °C helps fusion; keep preheat modest on heat-treatable alloys to limit softening.
  4. Strike and hold a short arcStrike firmly and keep the arc short. Travel faster than you would with steel, with the electrode angled slightly in the direction of travel.
  5. Restart cleanlyThe flux glazes over the tip when the arc stops. Snap or file off the glazed end before restriking, and restart slightly ahead of the crater.
  6. Remove all slagChip and brush the slag between passes, then wash the finished weld with hot water and brush to remove flux residue that would otherwise cause corrosion.

Welding is best done in the flat and horizontal positions. Vertical and overhead welding with aluminium electrodes is possible but demanding because the pool is very fluid.

Handling

Storage and moisture control

The covering on aluminium electrodes absorbs moisture readily, and hydrogen from that moisture is the main cause of porosity in aluminium welds. Keep electrodes in their sealed packs until use, store them in a dry, warm area, and return unused rods to a heated holding oven or sealed container.

Electrodes that have picked up moisture can often be reconditioned by baking, but temperatures and times vary by maker, and excessive baking can damage the coating. Follow the manufacturer’s instructions and discard rods with cracked, swollen or flaking covering.

Applications

Where aluminium stick electrodes are used

  • Repair of cracked aluminium castings such as pump housings, gearbox cases and engine parts (E4043)
  • Field repair of aluminium tanks, chutes, truck bodies and trailer components
  • Maintenance welding of 1xxx busbar and conductor parts in electrical plants (E1100)
  • Repairs to 3003 ducting, hoppers and sheet-metal equipment (E3003)
  • Outdoor and remote-site work where shielding gas is unavailable or wind makes it unreliable
  • Building up worn or damaged areas before machining

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) stick welding rod: frequently asked questions

Can you weld aluminium with a stick welder?

Yes, using flux-covered aluminium electrodes on DC electrode positive. Results are adequate for repair and general work, but MIG or TIG gives cleaner, stronger and more consistent welds.

Which aluminium stick electrode is most common?

E4043. Its 5 % silicon core gives a fluid, crack-resistant weld and suits 6xxx alloys, 3xxx alloys and most silicon-bearing castings.

Are E5356 or E5183 stick electrodes standard?

No. AWS A5.3 covers only E1100, E3003 and E4043. Al-Mg electrodes exist from some makers as their own grades, but for 5xxx alloys MIG or TIG with ER5356 or ER5183 wire is the usual route.

Why do my aluminium stick welds have porosity?

Usually moisture in the electrode covering, oil or heavy oxide on the joint, or an arc held too long. Use dry electrodes, clean thoroughly and keep the arc short.

Do I need to clean the weld afterwards?

Yes. The flux residue is corrosive, so chip off the slag and wash the weld with hot water and a brush. Leaving it causes corrosion later.

What is the minimum thickness I can stick weld?

About 3 mm in practice. Thinner material tends to burn through; TIG welding is better for thin aluminium.
