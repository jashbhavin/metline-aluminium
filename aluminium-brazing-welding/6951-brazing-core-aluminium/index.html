6951 Core Aluminium Brazing Sheet and Clad Coil

6951 is the heat-treatable core alloy used in brazing sheet when a brazed heat exchanger must be stronger than one built from 3003. Clad with an aluminium-silicon filler layer, usually magnesium-bearing 4004 or 4104 for vacuum brazing, it brazes at about 590 – 605 °C and can then be aged to recover strength. Metline supplies 6951-core clad sheet, coil, strip and bare or clad fin stock to your build-up, worldwide.

Overview

What is 6951 brazing sheet?

Brazing sheet is a roll-bonded composite: a core alloy that provides strength and a thin cladding of aluminium-silicon filler that melts in the furnace and forms the joints. Most brazing sheet uses a 3003 or modified 3xxx core. 6951 is chosen when the finished exchanger needs more strength or pressure capacity than a non-heat-treatable core can give.

6951 can do this because it is an age-hardening Al-Mg-Si-Cu alloy with low silicon, so its solidus (around 615 °C) stays above the brazing temperature. The brazing cycle itself acts as a solution treatment; if the assembly is cooled quickly and then aged, much of the T6 strength returns. A 3003 core, by contrast, is fully annealed after brazing and cannot be strengthened again.

The magnesium in 6951 (0.4 – 0.8 %) is an advantage in vacuum brazing, where magnesium helps break down the oxide film, and a drawback in flux (CAB) brazing, where magnesium reacts with standard fluoride flux. For that reason 6951-core sheet is mainly a vacuum-brazing product, used in plate-fin exchangers, oil coolers, cold plates and aerospace thermal hardware.

Why specify a 6951 core

  • Heat-treatable: strength can be recovered after brazing by ageing
  • Higher post-braze strength and pressure capacity than 3003-core sheet
  • Well suited to fluxless vacuum brazing with Mg-bearing clad layers
  • Copper content makes the core slightly noble to the clad and liner, helping corrosion protection

Properties

Strength before and after brazing

The mechanical properties of brazing sheet depend on the core, the clad ratio and the condition. Because the clad layer is weak, a thicker clad lowers the overall strength of the composite. The figures below are for the 6951 core alloy itself and show why post-braze ageing matters.

Condition of 6951Typical tensile strengthTypical yield strengthComment
O (annealed, as supplied for forming)≈ 110 MPa≈ 40 MPaBest formability for stamping plates and fins
After brazing, slow-cooledLow; close to annealedLowStrength not recovered
After brazing, fast-cooled and aged (≈ T6)Up to ≈ 270 MPaUp to ≈ 230 MPaRequires quench from braze temperature and ageing around 160 °C
3003 core after brazing (for comparison)≈ 110 MPa≈ 40 MPaCannot be strengthened by heat treatment

In practice, vacuum furnaces cool more slowly than a water quench, so post-braze strength is usually below full T6. Gas-fan quenching after the braze and a controlled ageing step bring the assembly closer to it. Ask us for the clad ratio and temper that suit your cycle.

Engineering

Design and process advice

Match filler to furnace

For vacuum brazing use 4004 or 4104 clad. For CAB lines, discuss flux and core options before choosing 6951.

Mind the sag

Heat-treatable cores can sag more than 3003 at brazing temperature. Support large thin parts and avoid excessive peak temperature.

Control erosion

Long soak times let silicon diffuse into thin cores and fins. Keep time at temperature short and clad ratios appropriate to gauge.

Plan the quench

Post-braze strength depends on cooling rate. Gas quenching and a separate ageing step give the best results.

Protect the waterside

For coolant or condensate exposure, add a 7072 liner or design for a sacrificial fin system.

Mixed materials

Seal plates and side bars are often 3003 or 6951 bar; check that all parts survive the same braze cycle.

Applications

Where 6951-core brazing materials are used

  • Plate-fin heat exchangers: parting sheets and fins for aircraft environmental-control systems and industrial coolers
  • Oil and fuel coolers: stacked-plate coolers that must withstand pressure pulsation
  • Liquid cold plates: vacuum-brazed cooling plates for power electronics, batteries and radar
  • Military and aerospace thermal hardware: lightweight exchangers built to customer specifications
  • Industrial and marine coolers: multi-clad build-ups with sacrificial liners for corrosive service

FAQ

6951 aluminium (aluminum) brazing sheet: frequently asked questions

Why use 6951 instead of 3003 as a brazing core?

6951 is heat-treatable, so after brazing it can be quenched and aged to regain strength. 3003 is left in the annealed condition after brazing and cannot be strengthened again.

Which clad alloy goes with a 6951 core?

For vacuum brazing, 4004 or 4104, which contain magnesium to help break the oxide film. 4045 or 4343 can be used in some cases, but the magnesium in 6951 makes standard CAB flux brazing difficult.

What brazing temperature is used for 6951 brazing sheet?

Typically about 590 – 605 °C for 4004 / 4104 clad in vacuum. The exact cycle depends on part mass and fixture.

Can 6951 brazing sheet be CAB brazed?

It is possible with special fluxes or careful trials, but it is not the normal route. High-volume CAB lines generally use low-magnesium 3xxx cores.

What clad ratios are available?

Commonly 5 – 15 % of total thickness on each side, chosen to suit gauge and joint size.

Do you supply 6951 fin stock?

Yes, bare or clad 6951 fin stock in thin gauges, along with clad sheet and coil, shipped worldwide.

Designing a vacuum-brazed exchanger?

Send your core and clad build-up, gauges, widths and quantities. We will confirm supply and certification.
