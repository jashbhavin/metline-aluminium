Forged Aluminium Bar: Round, Square and Rectangular

Forged aluminium bar is made by working a cast ingot between open dies until the whole cross-section is wrought, giving sound metal and even properties in sizes larger than extruded or rolled bar can reach. It is the usual starting stock for heavy shafts, aircraft fittings, landing-gear parts and large machined components. Metline supplies forged bar in 6061, 7075, 7050, 2024, 2014, 2219 and other alloys, heat treated and certified to AMS, ASTM or EN, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is forged aluminium bar?

Forged bar is a hand (open-die) forging in a simple bar shape. A homogenised ingot is heated and repeatedly pressed, rotated and drawn out between flat or V-shaped dies until it reaches the required section. The process is sometimes called cogging.

Why not simply buy extruded or rolled bar? For small and medium diameters, extruded rod is cheaper and perfectly adequate. Forged bar earns its place in three situations: when the diameter is beyond the practical range of extrusion presses (large rod is commonly limited to roughly 300 – 400 mm); when the specification calls for a forging and its specific test directions; and when the part needs good properties through the whole section, not only along the length. Forging works the centre of the ingot as well as the surface, closing porosity and breaking up the cast structure where extrusion of very large sections can leave it under-worked.

Forged bar also avoids a problem common in extruded 2xxx, 7xxx and 6082 rod: a coarse recrystallised grain ring near the surface, which can lower fatigue strength and spoil the appearance of anodised parts. A properly forged and heat-treated bar has a more uniform grain structure from surface to core.

Choose forged bar when

  • The diameter or section is larger than extruded bar can supply
  • The drawing or specification calls for a forging (for example AMS 4127 or AMS 4126)
  • Short-transverse properties or centre soundness matter
  • You need a stepped or tapered blank to save machining
Product types

Forged bar shapes and blanks

Forged round bar

The most common form; for shafts, axles, piston blanks, rollers and rotating parts. Supplied black (as forged), rough turned or peeled.

Forged square bar

For tooling bases, fixtures and blocks that will be milled on all faces.

Forged rectangular bar

For long fittings, rails, spars and machine members where width and thickness both matter.

Stepped and tapered bar

Shafts and blanks forged with different diameters along the length to reduce machining and keep grain flow continuous.

Large-diameter bar

Heavy sections for hubs, rollers and structural parts where the whole section carries load.

Cut lengths and slices

Bar sawn into blanks or discs to the customer's finished-part envelope.

The 7xxx choice deserves care. 7075-T6 is the strongest but is prone to stress-corrosion cracking when loaded across the grain; for structural parts, T73 or T7352 is normally specified. In sections thicker than about 75 – 100 mm, 7050 keeps more of its strength at the centre because it is less quench-sensitive.

Processing & condition

How forged bar is made and delivered

  1. Ingot preparationHomogenised ingot is scalped to remove surface segregation and cut to weight.
  2. Open-die forgingThe ingot is heated and drawn out on a hydraulic press, often with upsetting to work it in more than one direction. A minimum reduction ratio is commonly specified to break down the cast structure.
  3. Heat treatmentSolution treatment, quench and ageing; T652, T7352 and T7452 add controlled compression to relieve quench stress so the bar stays stable during machining.
  4. ConditioningSawing to length, rough turning or peeling, centre drilling and end marking as required.
  5. Inspection and dispatchTesting, certification and export packing.

FAQ

Forged aluminium (aluminum) bar: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between forged and extruded aluminium bar?

Extruded bar is pushed through a die and has strongly directional properties; it is economical in small and medium diameters. Forged bar is worked between open dies from an ingot, reaches larger sizes and has more uniform properties through the section.

What sizes of forged aluminium bar can you supply?

Forged bar is made to order from the finished-part size. Round bar typically starts around 100 mm diameter and goes up to large heavy sections; send your dimensions and we will confirm feasibility and the forged size.

Which temper should I order for 7075 forged bar?

T7352 for structural parts that will be heavily machined or loaded across the grain, because it resists stress-corrosion cracking and is stress relieved. T6 or T652 only where maximum strength is needed and SCC risk is low.

Is forged bar supplied machined?

It can be supplied as forged, rough turned or peeled, cut to length or rough machined to your drawing.

Which specification covers forged aluminium bar?

ASTM B247 and EN 586 for general use; AMS specifications such as AMS 4127 (6061-T6) and AMS 4126 (7075-T6) for aerospace.

Is ultrasonic testing included?

It can be. Ultrasonic inspection to ASTM B594 or AMS-STD-2154 is specified per order, with the class stated on the purchase order.

Send the alloy, temper, size and specification. We will confirm the forged size, testing and delivered price.
