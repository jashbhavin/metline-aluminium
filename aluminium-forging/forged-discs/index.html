Upset-forged discs and blanks

Forged Aluminium Discs and Custom Blanks

A forged aluminium disc is a round blank made by upsetting a billet between flat or shaped dies, so its grain spreads out radially and the metal is dense right through. That gives better strength, toughness and fatigue life in the plane of the disc than a circle cut from plate, which is why forged discs are used for wheels, gear blanks, flanges, turntables and pressure covers. Metline supplies forged discs, hubs and blanks in 6061, 7075, 2024, 7050, 5083 and other alloys, as forged, heat treated or machined, worldwide.

Overview

What is a forged aluminium disc?

Forged discs (also called pancakes or upset blanks) start as a length of cast or forged bar. The billet is heated and pressed along its axis so that it shortens and spreads into a disc. Further operations can add a raised hub, a recess or a pierced centre hole before heat treatment and machining.

Upsetting changes the metal in a useful way. Grain that ran along the billet is turned outward, so the disc has similar properties in every radial and circumferential direction, which is what a rotating or pressure-loaded part needs. Porosity left from casting is closed, and the cast structure is broken down. A disc sawn or cut from thick plate, by comparison, has the rolling direction running straight across it, so its properties vary around the circumference and the centre of very thick plate is the least worked metal.

Forged discs also save metal. A disc cut from a square plate wastes the corners, and a disc machined from a large bar wastes the outside. A forged disc can be made close to the finished diameter and thickness with only a machining allowance, and features such as hubs can be formed rather than machined from solid.

Forged discs are the right choice for

  • Rotating parts: wheels, gear blanks, pulleys, flywheels, rotors
  • Pressure parts: end covers, blind flanges, vessel heads, valve discs
  • Thick discs where plate would lack through-thickness properties
  • Diameters larger than standard bar or plate can supply
Product types

Types of forged discs and blanks

Solid forged disc

A plain disc with no bore, for gear blanks, covers and wheels that will be fully machined.

Disc with centre hole

Pierced or pre-bored to reduce machining for bearing seats, flanges and couplings. Larger bores become rings.

Disc with raised hub

Hub and web forged in one piece with continuous grain flow, for wheels, pulleys and drive flanges that carry torque through the hub.

Recessed or stepped disc

Recesses, steps and grooves forged close to shape to save metal and machining time.

Forged hub

A thick, compact disc for shafts, wheels and rotating assemblies, with bores and splines machined afterwards.

Large-diameter disc

Heavy flanges, turntables and base plates where cut plate would be too thin or lack through-thickness properties.

Process

How forged aluminium discs are made

  1. BilletCast and homogenised or pre-forged bar is sawn to the weight of the disc plus allowance. To avoid buckling, the starting height is normally kept below about two to three times the billet diameter.
  2. HeatingThe billet and dies are preheated; forging temperatures are roughly 380 – 480 °C depending on alloy.
  3. UpsettingThe billet is pressed along its axis to the target thickness. Large reductions, sometimes with intermediate draw-out and re-upset, give a well-worked structure.
  4. ShapingHubs, recesses and steps are formed in shaped dies; centre holes are pierced. Wide-bore parts continue to ring rolling.
  5. Heat treatmentSolution treatment, quench and ageing; compression stress relief for T652, T7352 or T7452.
  6. Machining and inspectionFacing, turning and boring as ordered, followed by testing and certification.

For high-volume parts such as vehicle wheels, discs are forged in closed dies that form the spokes and hub directly. See aluminium die forgings for that route.

FAQ

Forged aluminium (aluminum) discs: frequently asked questions

Why use a forged disc instead of cutting a circle from plate?

A forged disc has radial grain flow and is worked through its thickness, so strength and fatigue properties are consistent around the circumference and through the section. A plate-cut disc has the rolling direction running across it and weaker centre properties in thick sizes.

Which alloy is best for a forged wheel or gear blank?

6061-T6 or 6082-T6 for most vehicle and industrial wheels, 7075 for motorsport and high-load gear blanks, and 2014 or 2024 for aircraft wheels to the relevant specification.

What sizes of forged discs can you supply?

Discs are forged to order from the finished-part size, from small blanks a little over 100 mm diameter to large flanges and turntables. Send the drawing and we confirm the forged size.

Can forged discs be supplied with a centre hole?

Yes. Discs can be pierced or bored; when the bore becomes large relative to the outside diameter, a rolled ring is usually the more economical route.

Which temper limits distortion during machining?

Stress-relieved tempers such as 6061-T652, 7075-T7352 or 7050-T7452, in which the disc is compressed after quenching to remove most residual stress.

Are forged discs ultrasonically tested?

They can be, to ASTM B594 or AMS-STD-2154. Testing after rough machining allows a finer acceptance class.

Need forged aluminium discs?

Send the drawing, alloy, temper and quantity. We will propose forged dimensions, an inspection plan and a delivered price.
