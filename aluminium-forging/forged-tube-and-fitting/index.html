Forged Aluminium Pipe, Tube and Fittings

Forged aluminium pipe and fittings are pressure and structural parts made from forgings rather than from extruded tube: thick-walled hollows, flanges, socket-weld and threaded elbows, tees, couplings and caps. Metline supplies them in 6061, 6082 and 5083, made to the dimensions of ASME B16.11, B16.9 or your drawing, with ASTM B247 or B361 certification, to customers worldwide.

Overview

When is aluminium pipe or a fitting forged?

Standard aluminium pipe and tube are extruded or drawn. Forging takes over where extrusion runs out: walls too thick or diameters too large for an extrusion press, short thick hollows that are cheaper to forge and bore than to extrude, and small fittings whose shape (a tee, an elbow with a socket, a boss) is best pressed in a closed die from a solid slug.

Forged fittings are made by pressing a heated slug of bar in a closed die so that the metal flows into the body, branches and bosses of the part. The bore, sockets and threads are then machined. Compared with a fitting machined entirely from bar, a forging uses less metal, its grain follows the branches and its internal soundness is better, which matters for parts that hold pressure.

Forged pipe hollows are made by upsetting and piercing a billet, then drawing it over a mandrel or ring rolling it, or by forging a solid round and deep-hole boring it. They are used for thick-walled pressure shells, hydraulic cylinders, actuator housings and cryogenic vessel nozzles where an extruded tube of the required wall does not exist.

Important: pressure ratings are not interchangeable

  • ASME B16.11 pressure classes (2000, 3000, 6000) were derived for forged steel fittings
  • An aluminium fitting made to B16.11 dimensions does not automatically carry the steel rating
  • Rate aluminium piping with the allowable stresses for the alloy and temper in the governing code, such as ASME B31.3
  • Welding lowers the strength of 6061-T6 and 6082-T6 near the weld; use welded-condition allowables
Fittings

Forged aluminium fittings we supply

Socket-weld fittings

90° and 45° elbows, equal and reducing tees, crosses, couplings, half couplings and caps, made to ASME B16.11 socket dimensions for small-bore lines.

Threaded fittings

Elbows, tees, couplings, unions, hex bushings, plugs and nipples with NPT (ASME B1.20.1) or BSP (ISO 228 / ISO 7) threads. Use thread sealant suited to aluminium to prevent galling.

Butt-weld fittings

Elbows, reducers, tees and stub ends to ASME B16.9 dimensions for larger bores, normally formed from seamless pipe or machined from forgings to ASTM B361.

Flanges

Weld-neck, slip-on, blind and lap-joint flanges forged in 6061-T6 or 5083, machined to ASME B16.5 or EN 1092 dimensions by agreement.

Branch outlets

Weld-on branch bosses and outlets for headers, machined from forgings to drawing.

Special fittings

Manifold blocks, adaptor bodies, swivels and vacuum flanges forged close to shape and finished to drawing.

ASTM B361 is the specification for factory-made wrought aluminium welding fittings. It allows the fitting to be made from forgings, bar, plate or seamless pipe, refers to ASME B16.9 and B16.11 for dimensions, and requires the fitting material to conform to the ordered alloy and temper. For flanges, ASME B31.3 contains an appendix with pressure–temperature ratings for 6061-T6 aluminium pipe flanges; check its current edition for the rating that applies to your design.

Forged hollows are normally supplied with machining allowance on the outside and inside diameters and on the ends. Wall uniformity and concentricity are corrected by boring and turning; the achievable finish tolerance depends on length-to-bore ratio. Hollows for hydraulic and pneumatic cylinders are honed or roller-burnished internally after machining.

For standard pipe sizes and lengths, seamless extruded pipe to ASTM B241 (6061, 6063, 5083 and others) is far more economical than a forging and should be the first choice. Choose forged hollows when the wall thickness or diameter is beyond extrusion limits, when the specification calls for a forging, or when the part needs the soundness and grain flow of forged material.

Applications

Where forged aluminium pipe and fittings are used

  • Cryogenic and LNG: 5083 and 6061 flanges, nozzles and fittings that stay tough at −196 °C and below
  • Compressed air and inert gas: 6061 fittings and manifolds that do not rust or shed scale into the line
  • Vacuum and semiconductor: forged 6061 flanges and chamber ports machined to a fine sealing finish
  • Chemical processing: high-purity and 3xxx/5xxx fittings for media such as concentrated nitric acid, ammonia and some organic chemicals that aluminium resists
  • Marine: 5083 piping components above and below deck, with appropriate isolation from steel and copper alloys
  • Hydraulics and aerospace: machined 7075 and 2024 fitting bodies and actuator cylinders where weight matters

Aluminium is not suitable for high-temperature steam or hot-oil service: most alloys lose strength rapidly above about 150 °C. Always check chemical compatibility, because aluminium is attacked by strong alkalis, mercury and chloride-rich acids.

FAQ

Forged aluminium pipe and fittings: frequently asked questions

Can aluminium fittings be made to ASME B16.11?

Yes, aluminium fittings can be made to B16.11 dimensions, and ASTM B361 refers to B16.11 and B16.9 for welding fittings. However, the B16.11 class ratings were derived for steel, so the pressure rating of an aluminium fitting must be checked against the aluminium allowable stresses in your piping code.

Which aluminium alloy is best for pipe fittings?

6061-T6 is the most common: strong, weldable and corrosion resistant. 5083 is preferred for seawater and cryogenic service. 7075 and 2024 are only used for threaded or bolted fittings because they are not suitable for fusion welding.

What is the difference between forged and wrought fittings?

Forgings are a type of wrought product. "Forged fitting" usually means a closed-die pressing that is then machined, while other wrought fittings are formed from seamless pipe or machined from bar or plate. Forging gives dense metal with grain following the shape.

When should I use a forged hollow instead of extruded pipe?

Use extruded seamless pipe (ASTM B241) for standard sizes. Use a forged hollow when the wall is thicker or the diameter larger than extrusion allows, when the drawing calls for a forging, or for short heavy-wall cylinders.

Can aluminium pipe be used for steam?

No. Aluminium alloys lose much of their strength above about 150 °C, so they are unsuitable for steam and hot-oil lines. They are excellent for compressed air, vacuum, inert gases and cryogenic service.

What documents come with forged fittings?

An EN 10204 3.1 certificate with chemistry and mechanical results, dimensional reports and heat-treatment records. NDT reports, hydrostatic test results and 3.2 certification are available on request.
