Forged Aluminium Blocks and Hand Forgings

A forged aluminium block is a rectangular hand forging: a cast ingot that has been upset and drawn on an open-die press in all three directions, then heat treated and stress relieved. It gives thick parts sounder centres and better through-thickness properties than very thick rolled plate. Metline supplies forged blocks in 6061, 6082, 2014, 7075 and 7050, rough or finish machined, UT tested and certified, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is a forged aluminium block?

ASTM B247 calls it a hand forging: a forging shaped between flat or simple dies without a part-shaped cavity. A forged block is upset (compressed along its length), turned, and drawn out again several times, so that the cast structure of the ingot is broken down and the metal is worked in every direction. The result is a rectangular block, often called a forged billet or forged slab, from which a large or thick part is machined.

Why not simply use thick plate? Rolling squeezes metal mainly from the two broad faces. In very thick plate the centre receives the least work, so shrinkage porosity from the ingot can be stretched rather than closed, and properties in the short-transverse (through-thickness) direction are the weakest in the plate. Open-die forging compresses the block from several sides, closing centre porosity and spreading the work more evenly. Forged blocks are therefore specified for thick, highly stressed parts such as aircraft bulkhead fittings, landing-gear and wing-attachment fittings, and heavy machine and mould components loaded through their thickness.

Forged blocks also allow sizes that no plate mill can roll: very thick sections, square cross-sections, and blocks whose length, width and height are similar. For moderate thickness and ordinary loads, however, rolled plate or cast tooling plate is cheaper and has a better surface and thickness tolerance, so a forging should be chosen for a reason, not by default.

Choose a forged block when

  • The part is thicker than standard plate, or its height is close to its width
  • The drawing loads the part through its thickness, or specifies short-transverse properties
  • A specification calls for a forging and ultrasonic class AA or A
  • Fatigue and toughness matter more than material cost

Note the temper designations: the "51" suffix used for plate (T651, T7451) means stress relief by stretching, which is not possible on a forged block. Forgings are stress relieved by a controlled cold compression of 1 – 5 %, shown by the suffix "52" (T652, T7352, T7452). If a drawing calls for 7075-T651 or 2024-T351 and you want a forging, ask your engineer to approve the equivalent forging temper.

Weight of a block is its volume multiplied by density: about 2.70 g/cm³ for 6061 and 6082, 2.80 g/cm³ for 2014 and 7075, and 2.83 g/cm³ for 7050. A 500 × 300 × 200 mm block of 6061 weighs about 81 kg; the same block in 7075 about 84 kg. Remember to add the forging allowance when estimating the as-delivered weight.

Quality

Ultrasonic inspection and documentation

  • Ultrasonic testing to ASTM B594 or AMS-STD-2154, class to order (AA and A are usual for aerospace blocks); performed on a rough-machined sonic shape
  • Tensile tests in the longitudinal, long-transverse and short-transverse directions as the specification requires
  • Hardness and electrical conductivity checks, essential for confirming T73 and T74 tempers of 7xxx alloys
  • Dye-penetrant inspection (ASTM E1417) of machined surfaces on request; magnetic-particle testing does not apply to aluminium
  • Heat-treatment records to AMS 2770 or the customer specification, including quench delay and water temperature
  • EN 10204 3.1 certificate with chemistry, tested properties and lot traceability; 3.2 with third-party witness on request
Applications

Applications for forged aluminium blocks

Aerospace

Bulkhead, frame and wing-attachment fittings, landing-gear and engine-mount brackets machined from 7050-T7452 or 7075-T7352 blocks.

Defence

Launcher and turret components, armour-system brackets and equipment bases where shock and fatigue loads are high.

Moulds and tooling

Large blow-moulding and rubber moulds, and press tooling, where a sound block avoids porosity breaking out on cavity surfaces.

Machinery

Hydraulic manifolds, robot-arm nodes, gantry joints and spindle housings loaded in several directions.

Semiconductor and vacuum

Thick 6061 chamber bodies, where internal soundness reduces the risk of leak paths through the wall.

Marine and cryogenic

5083 and 6061 blocks for deck-equipment bases, cryogenic valve bodies and LNG-system components.

FAQ

Forged aluminium blocks: frequently asked questions

Is a forged aluminium block better than thick plate?

For thick, highly stressed parts, usually yes: open-die forging closes centre porosity and gives better short-transverse properties. For moderate thickness and ordinary loads, rolled plate is cheaper and has a better surface and thickness tolerance.

What does T652 mean on a forged block?

T652 is solution heat treated, stress relieved by cold compression of about 1 – 5 %, then artificially aged. It is the forging equivalent of T651 plate, which is stress relieved by stretching. T7352 and T7452 are the over-aged 7xxx equivalents.

Can I order a forged block in 7075-T651?

T651 is a plate and bar temper. For a forging, the equivalent is 7075-T652 for maximum strength or 7075-T7352 for stress-corrosion resistance. Ask your design authority to approve the forging temper.

Which ultrasonic class should I specify?

Class A or AA to AMS-STD-2154 is usual for aerospace and other fatigue-critical parts; class B suits general engineering. Tighter classes cost more and require a rough-machined sonic shape.

How much does a forged aluminium block weigh?

Multiply volume by density: about 2.70 g/cm³ for 6061 and 2.80 g/cm³ for 7075. A 500 × 300 × 200 mm 6061 block weighs about 81 kg before machining allowance.

Do you supply machined parts from forged blocks?

Yes. Blocks can be supplied as forged, rough machined for inspection, or CNC machined to your drawing, with dimensional reports and certificates.
