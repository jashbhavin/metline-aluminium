7075 Aluminium Forgings

7075 aluminium forgings deliver the highest strength of the common forging alloys, with grain flow that follows the part for better fatigue life and toughness than machined plate. They are used for aircraft fittings, landing-gear and wing components, defence hardware and high-performance motorsport parts. Metline supplies 7075 die forgings, hand forgings, rolled rings and discs in T6, T73 and T7352, certified to AMS, ASTM and EN, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What are 7075 aluminium forgings?

7075 contains about 5.6 % zinc, 2.5 % magnesium and 1.6 % copper. In the peak-aged T6 condition it reaches tensile strengths above 500 MPa, so a 7075 forging can replace a steel part at roughly a third of the weight in many moderately loaded applications.

7075 is harder to forge than 6061 or 2014. Its forging window is narrow (roughly 380 – 440 °C), it needs higher press loads and it is sensitive to cracking if the metal cools on the dies. In exchange, forging gives what 7075 most needs: a refined, unrecrystallised grain structure aligned with the part, which improves fatigue strength and greatly reduces the short-transverse weakness that makes thick 7075 plate vulnerable to stress-corrosion cracking.

The temper decision matters more for 7075 forgings than for almost any other product. T6 gives maximum strength but poor resistance to stress-corrosion cracking (SCC) when residual or assembly stresses act across the grain. That is why most structural aircraft forgings in 7075 are ordered in the over-aged T73 family, and why newer designs often move to 7050 or 7175 in T74. Our job is to make sure the temper on the order matches how the part will actually be loaded and exposed.

Specify 7075 forgings carefully

  • Use T73 / T7352 for parts with sustained tensile stress in the short-transverse direction
  • Ask for electrical conductivity checks to verify over-aged tempers
  • Do not fusion weld; design for bolting or bonding
  • Name the specification revision, test directions and ultrasonic class on the order

Tempers & heat treatment

T6, T73, T7352 or T74?

7075-T6

Solution treated at about 465 – 480 °C, quenched and aged near 120 °C. Maximum strength; acceptable for thin, simple forgings without sustained short-transverse stress.

7075-T652

T6 with 1 – 5 % cold compression before ageing to relieve quench stress; for hand forgings to be heavily machined.

7075-T73

Two-step over-ageing (lower then higher temperature). About 10 – 15 % less strength than T6 but high resistance to SCC and exfoliation. Standard for aircraft die forgings.

7075-T7352

T73 with compressive stress relief. The usual temper for 7075 hand forgings and rolled rings that will be machined deeply.

7075-T74

An intermediate over-aged temper with better strength than T73 and good SCC resistance; also used for 7175 and 7050 forgings.

Conductivity check

Over-aged tempers are verified by eddy-current conductivity together with hardness, because over-ageing raises conductivity measurably.

Working with 7075 forgings

Machining, joining and protection

Machining

Excellent machinability with sharp carbide tools; stress-relieved T652 and T7352 forgings move least during deep pocketing.

Joining

Not fusion weldable. Use fasteners, interference fits or adhesive bonding.

Surface treatment

Sulphuric or chromic acid anodising, hard anodising for wear surfaces, conversion coating and primer for aircraft parts.

Shot peening

Commonly applied to fatigue-critical forgings to introduce compressive surface stress.

Applications

Where 7075 forgings are used

  • Aircraft structure: wing and fuselage fittings, frames, landing-gear components, actuator housings
  • Helicopters: rotor hub parts and transmission housings (to the OEM's specification)
  • Defence: weapon mounts, missile bodies, armoured-vehicle components
  • Motorsport and cycling: wheels, uprights, cranks and suspension parts
  • Industrial: high-speed machine parts, robotic arms and lightweight tooling

FAQ

7075 aluminium (aluminum) forgings: frequently asked questions

Should I order 7075 forgings in T6 or T73?

Choose T73 (or T7352 for hand forgings and rings) whenever the part sees sustained tensile stress across the grain or a corrosive environment. T6 gives about 10 – 15 % more strength but is prone to stress-corrosion cracking in thick sections.

What is the difference between T73 and T7352?

Both are over-aged for SCC resistance. T7352 forgings are also cold-compressed after quenching to relieve residual stress, so they stay stable during machining.

Why are 7075 forgings stronger than parts machined from plate?

Peak strength is similar, but the forging's grain flow follows the part, which improves fatigue life, toughness and short-transverse properties. That often allows a lighter design.

Can 7075 forgings be welded?

No, not by conventional fusion welding. Design joints for fasteners or bonding.

When should I use 7050 instead of 7075 forgings?

For thick sections, usually above about 75 – 100 mm, where 7050's lower quench sensitivity gives higher strength and toughness at the centre of the part.

Can you supply 7075 forgings with ultrasonic inspection?

Yes. Ultrasonic inspection to AMS-STD-2154 class AA, A or B can be specified, with reports supplied alongside the mill certificate.
