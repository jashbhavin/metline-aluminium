5052 Aluminium Coil, Strip and Sheet

5052 aluminium coil is rolled from a 2.5 % magnesium alloy that is the strongest of the commonly used, easily formed non-heat-treatable grades, with excellent resistance to seawater and industrial atmospheres. It is the standard coil for fuel tanks, truck and marine panels, electronics housings, honeycomb core and pressure-formed parts. Metline supplies 5052 coil, slit strip and cut sheet in O to H38 tempers, to ASTM B209 and EN 485, worldwide.

Overview

What makes 5052 coil different

5052 sits in the middle of the 5xxx family: enough magnesium (2.2 – 2.8 %) for real strength, but not so much that formability or long-term stability suffer. In H32 temper it is roughly 50 % stronger than 3003-H14 and still bends tightly.

Magnesium strengthens the aluminium by solid solution, and cold rolling adds more strength; chromium controls grain structure and improves resistance to stress corrosion. The H3x tempers used for 5052 are strain-hardened and then thermally stabilised, which prevents the gradual softening ("age softening") that unstabilised Al-Mg alloys show at room temperature.

Unlike the higher-magnesium alloys 5083 and 5456, 5052 is not prone to sensitisation, so it can be used at moderately elevated temperatures. It has very good fatigue strength for a non-heat-treatable alloy, which is why it is chosen for vibrating parts such as fuel tanks, fan blades and vehicle panels.

Why buyers choose 5052 coil

  • Higher strength than 1xxx and 3xxx coil with good formability
  • Excellent corrosion resistance in seawater, salt spray and industrial air
  • Good fatigue strength for parts that vibrate
  • Weldable, with weld strength close to the annealed parent metal

Tempers

Choosing between O, H32, H34 and harder tempers

5052-O

Fully annealed for deep drawing, spinning and severe forming.

5052-H32

Quarter-hard and stabilised; the most popular temper. Bends to about 1 – 1.5 × thickness and suits most sheet-metal work, tanks and enclosures.

5052-H34

Half-hard; higher strength for panels and parts with gentler bends.

5052-H36 / H38

Three-quarter and full hard for stiff, flat parts, springs and honeycomb foil.

Tolerances & inspection

Tolerances, flatness and inspection for 5052 coil

Thickness and width tolerances for 5052 coil follow ANSI H35.2 (referenced by ASTM B209) or EN 485-4 for cold-rolled strip. Because 5052 is harder than 1xxx and 3xxx alloys, the standard tolerance bands are the same but rolling to the tighter end takes more passes, so state the tolerance you actually need rather than asking for "tight" by default.

Slit strip is supplied with burr height controlled to a fraction of the thickness and with edge camber limited per metre of length. For stamping and roll-forming lines, tell us the maximum camber and coil set your tooling can tolerate; tension levelling before slitting removes most of the residual shape from hard tempers such as H34 and H36.

Working with 5052

Forming, welding and finishing 5052

Forming

H32 bends well across and along the rolling direction. Allow more springback than with 3003 and use generous radii for H34 and harder.

Welding

TIG and MIG with ER5356 (stronger) or ER5554 for elevated-temperature service. Avoid ER4043 where the weld will be anodised.

Machining

Gummy in soft tempers; harder tempers machine acceptably with sharp, high-rake tools and lubricant.

Finishing

Anodises well with a clear, protective film; paints and powder-coats well after chromate-free conversion coating.

Applications

Where 5052 coil is used

  • Transport: fuel tanks, truck bodies, trailer panels, bus and rail interior panels
  • Marine: small-boat hulls and superstructures, deck fittings, marine enclosures
  • Electronics: laptop and appliance housings, enclosures and chassis
  • Honeycomb core: thin 5052 foil expanded into honeycomb for panels and energy absorbers
  • Pressure and storage: tanks, pressure vessels and hydraulic tube
  • Industrial: fan blades, signage, appliance parts and road signs

FAQ

5052 aluminium coil: frequently asked questions

What does H32 mean for 5052?

H32 means strain-hardened to quarter-hard and then thermally stabilised. Stabilising prevents the slow softening at room temperature that unstabilised aluminium-magnesium alloys show.

Is 5052 marine grade?

5052 has excellent seawater resistance and is widely used on small boats and marine fittings. For large welded hull structures, 5083 or 5086 to ASTM B928 is normally specified.

Can 5052 be heat-treated to increase strength?

No. It is non-heat-treatable; strength comes from magnesium and cold work. Annealing softens it to O temper.

How much does a 5052 coil weigh?

Weight = width × π/4 × (OD² − ID²) × 2.68 g/cm³. A 1250 mm wide coil with 1200 mm OD on a 508 mm core weighs about 3.1 tonnes.

Is 5052 better than 6061 for sheet-metal parts?

Usually, yes. 5052-H32 bends much more easily than 6061-T6 and resists corrosion better. 6061 is chosen when higher strength or machining is needed.
