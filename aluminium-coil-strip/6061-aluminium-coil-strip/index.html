6061 Aluminium Coil, Strip and Sheet

6061 coil is the heat-treatable aluminium-magnesium-silicon alloy in rolled, coiled form: soft enough in O and T4 temper to stamp and form, and strong after ageing to T6. Buyers use it where a part needs more strength than 5052 can give, or where formed blanks are aged after forming. Metline supplies 6061 coil, slit strip and cut-to-length sheet in O, T4 and T6 to ASTM B209, AMS and EN 485, to customers worldwide.

Overview

6061 in coil form: what it is and why the temper matters

Most 6061 is sold as plate, bar and extrusions. Coil and strip are a narrower market, but an important one: they let a press shop stamp, roll-form or deep-draw a heat-treatable alloy and then age the parts to near-T6 strength.

6061 gets its strength from magnesium silicide (Mg2Si) precipitates formed during solution heat treatment and ageing, with a small copper addition that raises strength further and chromium that controls grain growth. In coil, the temper you order decides how the material behaves on your line far more than the alloy number does.

O-temper (annealed) coil is the softest and forms almost like 5052-O, though it is weaker. T4 coil has been solution-treated and naturally aged; it is noticeably stronger but still bends well, and it will reach T6-level strength if your formed parts are artificially aged at about 160 – 175 °C. T6 coil is fully aged and strongest, but it bends poorly and is mostly used flat or with generous radii. The certification of a part that you age yourself is normally T62 or T42, which tells the inspector that the temper was produced by the user rather than the mill.

Why choose 6061 coil over a 5xxx coil

  • Higher strength after ageing than any common non-heat-treatable coil alloy
  • Form in O or T4, then age to strength: shape first, strength afterwards
  • Machines cleanly in T6, unlike gummy soft 5xxx alloys
  • Same alloy as your 6061 extrusions and plate, so one weld procedure and one finish

Tempers

Choosing O, T4 or T6 coil

6061-O

Annealed. Best for deep drawing, spinning and severe stamping. Parts must be solution-treated and aged afterwards to gain strength, which adds a furnace step and some distortion risk.

6061-T4

Solution-treated and naturally aged. The usual choice for formed parts that will be aged later: it bends to roughly 1 – 2 × thickness in thin gauges and ages to T62 at about 160 – 175 °C for 8 – 18 hours.

6061-T6

Fully aged; highest strength. Use for flat blanks, gentle bends and machined strip. Minimum bend radii are much larger than in T4, and cracks appear on tight bends across the grain.

T42 / T62

Tempers produced by the user from O or T4 material. Specify these when your drawing or inspector needs to know who did the heat treatment.

A practical tip: T4 strip continues to age slowly at room temperature, so plan to form it within a reasonable time of receipt. If a long gap is unavoidable, ask us about storage and test the bend radius on a sample before running production.

Tolerances & inspection

Tolerances, flatness and inspection

Thickness, width and flatness tolerances follow ANSI H35.2 when ordered to ASTM B209, or EN 485-4 for cold-rolled strip in Europe. Heat-treated strip carries more residual stress and shape than non-heat-treatable coil, because it has been quenched; tension levelling after heat treatment improves flatness and is worth requesting for blanks that must lie flat on a press bed or fixture.

Working with 6061 coil

Forming, welding, machining and finishing

Forming

Form in O or T4 and age afterwards when strength matters. In T6, use bend radii of several times thickness and bend across rather than along the rolling direction where possible.

Welding

TIG and MIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. The heat-affected zone of T6 drops towards T4-level strength, so design welded joints with welded-condition allowables or re-heat-treat the assembly.

Machining

T6 machines well with sharp carbide tools; O and T4 tend to smear and form built-up edge.

Finishing

Anodises well (clear and colour), and takes paint and powder coat after conversion coating. Avoid 4043-welded areas on parts that will be anodised, as they darken.

Applications

Where 6061 coil and strip are used

  • Transport: stamped brackets, reinforcements and trim that are aged after forming
  • Electronics: laptop, tablet and device housings machined or stamped from T6 or T4 sheet
  • Electrical: busbar strip and connector blanks where strength and conductivity are both needed
  • Machinery: guards, covers, spacers and machined flat parts
  • Aerospace and defence: secondary structure and fittings to AMS 4025-4027
  • Consumer goods: bicycle parts, sports equipment and outdoor gear

FAQ

6061 aluminium coil: frequently asked questions

Is 6061 aluminum available in coil, or only in sheet and plate?

It is available in coil and slit strip, mainly in thinner gauges and in O and T4 tempers. T6 is supplied in coil for thin gauges, but much T6 material is sold as cut sheet because the fully aged alloy is stiff to recoil.

Should I buy 6061-T4 or T6 strip for stamped parts?

If the part has tight bends, buy T4 and age the formed parts to T62 at about 160 – 175 °C. If the part is flat or only gently bent, T6 saves you a heat-treatment step.

What is the difference between T6 and T62?

Both are solution-treated and artificially aged. T6 is produced by the mill; T62 is produced by the user from O or T4 material, and the minimum properties are similar.

How much does a 6061 coil weigh?

Weight = width × π/4 × (OD² − ID²) × 2.70 g/cm³. A 1000 mm wide coil with a 1200 mm OD on a 508 mm core weighs about 2.5 tonnes.

Can 6061 strip be welded?

Yes, with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. Expect lower strength next to the weld in T6 material unless the assembly is heat-treated again.

What certificate comes with 6061 coil?

An EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate with chemical analysis and tensile results for the lot, traceable to the coil numbers.
