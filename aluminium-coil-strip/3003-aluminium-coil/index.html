3003 Aluminium Coil and Strip

3003 aluminium coil is rolled from the aluminium-manganese alloy that is about 20 % stronger than pure 1100 aluminium while keeping its excellent formability, corrosion resistance and brazeability. It is the standard coil for heat-exchanger fins and tubes, cookware, roofing, ductwork and insulation cladding. Metline supplies 3003 coil and slit strip from soft O to full-hard H18 tempers, to ASTM B209 and EN 485, worldwide.

Overview

What 3003 aluminium coil is and why it is so widely used

3003 is the most widely used of the non-heat-treatable 3xxx alloys. About 1.2 % manganese, with a little copper, strengthens the aluminium by solid solution and fine dispersoids, without sacrificing the ductility that makes pure aluminium easy to draw, spin and bend.

The result is a coil that forms almost as easily as 1100 but is noticeably stronger, resists atmospheric and fresh-water corrosion, and takes paint, anodising and roll-coating well. Because its melting range (about 643 – 654 °C) sits comfortably above aluminium brazing temperatures, 3003 is also the classic core alloy for brazed heat exchangers and the base for clad brazing sheet.

Strength comes from cold rolling, so the temper you order sets the balance between strength and formability: O is fully soft for deep drawing, H14 is half-hard for general sheet-metal work and fins, and H18 is full-hard for stiff, flat parts that are not bent tightly.

Why buyers choose 3003 coil

  • About 20 % stronger than 1100 with almost the same formability
  • Good corrosion resistance in air, fresh water and many foods
  • Brazeable and weldable; the standard core alloy for heat exchangers
  • Widely stocked and economical, from foil-gauge fin stock to 6 mm strip

Tempers

H1x versus H2x tempers

In H1x tempers (H12, H14, H16, H18) the coil is cold-rolled straight to the required hardness. In H2x tempers (H22, H24, H26) it is rolled harder and then partially annealed back to the same strength. H2x coil has a little more elongation at the same strength, so it bends and stretches better; H1x is slightly flatter and cheaper. For fins and roll-formed profiles, both are used; if you have had cracking at bends with H14, try H24.

Working with 3003

Forming, joining and finishing

Forming

O temper can be bent flat on itself; H14 bends to about 0 – 1 × thickness for thin gauges. Spinning, deep drawing and roll forming are all routine.

Brazing

Melting range is well above the 577 – 600 °C used for Al-Si brazing, so 3003 is the standard core alloy and tube alloy for brazed heat exchangers.

Welding

Readily welded by TIG and MIG with ER1100 or ER4043 filler. The heat-affected zone softens to O-temper strength.

Finishing

Paints and roll-coats well. Anodised finishes are protective but slightly grey because of the manganese; use 5005 or 1050 for decorative anodising.

Applications

Where 3003 coil is used

  • Heat exchangers: fin stock, tube strip and headers for radiators, condensers and evaporators
  • Cookware and kitchen equipment: pots, pans, baking trays and food containers
  • Building: roofing, siding, gutters, ceilings and insulation jacketing
  • HVAC: ducting and air-handling components
  • Transport: truck and trailer panels, fuel tanks
  • Packaging and general fabrication: containers, signs, reflectors, chemical equipment

FAQ

3003 aluminium coil: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 3003-H14 and 3003-H24?

Both have about the same strength. H14 is cold-rolled directly to half-hard; H24 is rolled harder and then partially annealed, which gives a little more elongation and better bendability.

Is 3003 coil suitable for cookware?

Yes. 3003-O is widely used for pots, pans and baking trays because it draws and spins well, is stronger than pure aluminium and resists corrosion from most foods.

Can 3003 be anodised?

Yes, for protection, but the film is slightly grey and less uniform than on 1050 or 5005. For decorative anodising choose an anodising-quality grade.

How much does a 3003 coil weigh?

Weight = width × π/4 × (OD² − ID²) × 2.73 g/cm³. A 1000 mm wide coil with 1200 mm OD on a 508 mm core weighs about 2.5 tonnes.

Is 3003 the same as 3A21?

3A21 is a Chinese grade close to 3003 but without the copper addition. The two are often interchangeable in non-critical uses; check the specification.

Can 3003 be used in marine environments?

It resists marine atmospheres reasonably well, but for immersion or structural marine use 5052 or 5083 is better.
