7075 Aluminium Block

7075 aluminium block gives the highest strength and hardness of the common machining alloys: about 570 MPa typical tensile strength and 150 HB in T651, close to many mould and structural steels at a third of the weight. It is cut from stretched thick plate or made as forged block, and machined into aerospace fittings, moulds, tooling and high-performance parts. Metline supplies 7075 block in T651, T7351 and forged tempers, cut to size and certified, worldwide.

Overview

What is 7075 aluminium block?

7075 block is thick 7075 material sold as machining stock. Most blocks are saw-cut from 7075-T651 or T7351 plate, which is stretched after quenching so that it stays stable during heavy machining. Larger sections are open-die forged and compression stress-relieved (T652 or T7352).

The appeal is strength per kilogram. A 7075-T651 part can often replace a steel part of similar section at about one third of the weight, and it machines several times faster than steel. That is why 7075 block dominates aerospace fittings, high-end fixtures, and blow and injection moulds for medium production runs.

There are trade-offs. 7075 is not suited to arc welding, has lower corrosion resistance than 6061, and in the peak-aged T6/T651 temper it can suffer stress-corrosion cracking when stressed across the short-transverse direction of thick plate. Thick-section aerospace parts therefore often specify T7351, which is over-aged to resist that risk.

Why buyers choose 7075 block

  • Highest strength and hardness of the common machining alloys
  • Excellent machinability with fine surface finish
  • Stretched or compressed tempers for stable machining
  • Hard-anodises well for wear surfaces

Working with 7075

Machining and finishing 7075 block

Machining

Among the best-machining aluminium alloys. Carbide tools at high speed with coolant give excellent finishes; chips break well. Plan roughing and finishing passes to manage residual stress in thick blocks.

Mould making

Used for blow moulds, prototype and bridge injection moulds, and thermoforming tools. Hardness of around 150 HB gives reasonable wear life for medium runs; hard anodising or nickel coating extends it.

Welding

Avoid arc welding. Repairs on moulds are done with specialist procedures or inserts.

Finishing

Responds well to hard anodising, clear anodising and chromate or trivalent conversion coatings.

Corrosion

Protect finished parts. Use T7351 where sustained stress meets humidity or salt.

Thermal stability

Avoid long exposure above about 120 °C, which over-ages the T651 temper and reduces strength.

Applications

Where 7075 block is used

  • Aerospace: bulkhead and wing fittings, landing-gear parts, actuator housings
  • Moulds and tooling: blow moulds, injection moulds, rubber and foam moulds, high-strength jigs
  • Defence: mounts, receivers and structural components
  • Motorsport and cycling: uprights, wishbone mounts, cranks and billet parts
  • Robotics and automation: high-load arms and grippers where stiffness-to-weight matters

FAQ

7075 aluminium (aluminum) block: frequently asked questions

Is 7075 block as strong as steel?

7075-T651 has a typical tensile strength of about 570 MPa, comparable to many medium-carbon structural steels, at about one third of the density. Its stiffness is lower, so deflection-limited parts need a larger section.

What is the difference between 7075-T651 and T7351 block?

T7351 is over-aged. It is about 10 – 15 % weaker but much more resistant to stress-corrosion cracking, which is why it is preferred for thick aerospace fittings.

Is 7075 good for moulds?

Yes, for blow moulds and prototype or medium-run injection moulds. It machines fast, conducts heat well and can be hard-anodised for wear resistance.

How do I calculate the weight of a 7075 block?

Multiply thickness × width × length in mm by 2.81 and divide by 1,000,000. A 100 × 200 × 300 mm block weighs about 16.9 kg.

Can 7075 block be welded?

Arc welding is not recommended because of cracking and strength loss. Use mechanical fastening or design the part from one piece.

Do you supply 7075 block worldwide?

Yes. Metline supplies 7075 blocks cut to size, with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and export packing, to customers worldwide.
