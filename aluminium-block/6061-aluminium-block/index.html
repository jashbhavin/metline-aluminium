6061 Aluminium Block

6061 aluminium block is the default stock for machined parts that need good strength, stable machining, weldability and corrosion resistance at a reasonable price. It is cut from stretched 6061-T651 thick plate or, for very large sections, made as compression-stress-relieved T652 forged block. Overview

What is 6061 aluminium block?

A 6061 block is a thick rectangular piece of 6061 cut from plate or forged to shape, bought as raw stock for CNC machining. Because it is the most widely produced heat-treatable aluminium alloy, 6061 block is easy to source in most sizes, machines predictably and can be welded or anodised after machining.

For blocks up to about 150 – 200 mm thick, the usual source is 6061-T651 plate. The "51" means the plate was stretched by roughly 1.5 – 3 % after quenching, which removes most of the quench stress. Without that step, a thick block would bow as material is removed from one side. Above the practical plate range, open-die forged blocks are used; they cannot be stretched, so they are compressed instead (T652) to achieve the same effect.

A third option, cast and precision-milled tooling plate, is sometimes offered as a "6061 alternative". Cast plate is flatter and more stable but weaker. Choose it for flat base plates and fixtures where strength is secondary, and choose wrought 6061-T651 when the part carries real load.

Why buyers choose 6061 block

  • Balanced strength (typical 310 MPa tensile) at moderate cost
  • Stress-relieved T651 / T652 tempers for stable machining
  • Weldable and takes clear, coloured and hard anodising
  • Good corrosion resistance; suitable for vacuum and clean environments

Quality

Tolerances, flatness and inspection

  • Saw-cut blocks carry a small positive allowance (commonly +1 to +3 mm) for clean-up; milled blocks to ±0.1 mm or as specified
  • Parent-plate thickness and flatness per ASTM B209 / ANSI H35.2 or EN 485-3
  • Ultrasonic testing to AMS-STD-2154 or EN 10308 for critical parts, on request
  • Hardness or conductivity check to confirm temper
  • EN 10204 3.1 mill certificate traceable to the plate or forging lot

Tip: if you are machining deep pockets from one face of a thick block, ask for the block to be cut with the rolling direction along the longest dimension of the part. It does not change strength much in 6061, but it does make distortion more predictable.

Working with 6061

Machining, welding and finishing 6061 block

Machining

Use sharp, polished carbide tools with high positive rake, high spindle speeds and flood coolant or mist. 6061-T651 forms short chips; O temper is gummy and should be avoided for machining.

Distortion control

Rough all faces, let the part rest, then finish. Balance pockets on both sides where possible.

Welding

TIG or MIG with 4043 or 5356 filler. Expect strength loss in the heat-affected zone.

Anodising

Excellent response, including hard anodising for wear surfaces and black anodising for optical parts.

Vacuum use

Common for vacuum chambers and semiconductor parts because it machines cleanly and has low outgassing once cleaned.

Corrosion

Good in air and fresh water; anodise or paint for marine exposure.

Applications

Where 6061 block is used

  • Automation and machinery: manifolds, housings, base plates, robot grippers and fixtures
  • Semiconductor and vacuum: chamber bodies, lids and wafer-handling parts
  • Moulds: prototype and short-run injection moulds, blow moulds and vacuum-forming tools
  • Transport: brackets, suspension parts and EV components machined from solid
  • Optics and electronics: camera bodies, heat-spreading housings and instrument frames
  • Racing and sport: billet parts for motorsport, cycling and firearms accessories

FAQ

6061 aluminium (aluminum) block: frequently asked questions

What temper should I order for a 6061 block?

T651 for plate-cut blocks and T652 for forged blocks. Both are stress-relieved, so the block stays stable during machining. Plain T6 thick sections can distort.

How strong is a 6061-T651 block?

Typical values are about 310 MPa tensile and 276 MPa yield. Specified minimums fall slightly with thickness; the certificate gives the tested results.

How do I calculate the weight of a 6061 block?

Multiply thickness × width × length in mm by 2.70 and divide by 1,000,000. A 100 × 200 × 300 mm block weighs about 16.2 kg.

Is 6061 block suitable for moulds?

Yes, for prototype and short-run moulds. For longer runs or harder surfaces, 7075-T651 or mould steel is a better choice.

Can you mill blocks to finished size?

Yes. Blocks can be saw-cut with an allowance or milled on all six faces to tighter tolerances on request.

Do you ship 6061 blocks internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies 6061 blocks worldwide with mill certificates and export packing.
