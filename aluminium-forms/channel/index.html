Aluminium Channel (C, U and Structural Channel)

Aluminium channel is an extruded section with a flat web and two flanges on the same side, sold as structural (Aluminum Association) channel, square-cornered U channel and custom C sections. The usual grades are 6061-T6 for load-bearing work, 6063-T5/T6 for trim and frames, and 6082-T6 in Europe and India. Metline supplies all three in standard and custom sizes, cut to length and certified, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is aluminium channel?

A channel is the extruded equivalent of a folded sheet edge: one web and two flanges forming a "C" or "U". Because the shape is pushed through a steel die rather than bent, the corners can be thickened, the flanges tapered and the root radii controlled, which gives far better stiffness per kilogram than a press-braked strip of the same weight.

Buyers usually mean one of three things when they ask for channel. Structural channel (often called AA or Aluminum Association channel) has slightly tapered or thickened flanges and filleted roots, and its section properties are published in the Aluminum Association design manual (ADM) so engineers can use it directly in calculations. U channel (also called square-corner or architectural channel) has parallel flanges of uniform thickness and sharp or small-radius inside corners; it is used for edge trims, glazing, sliding tracks and light frames. Custom C sections add lips, ribs, screw ports or T-slots to suit one design.

Aluminium channel weighs roughly one third of a steel channel of the same dimensions, does not rust, needs no painting in most atmospheres and can be anodised or powder coated. Its lower elastic modulus (about 69 GPa against 200 GPa for steel) means an aluminium channel deflects about three times as much under the same load, so structural sections are normally deeper or thicker than the steel parts they replace.

Choosing the right channel type

  • Load-bearing members, trailer and truck frames, platforms: structural channel in 6061-T6 or 6082-T6
  • Edge trim, glazing, shower and cabinet tracks: U channel in 6063-T5 or T6, usually anodised
  • Sections that need integral fixings or cable runs: a custom C profile designed to your drawing
  • Seawater splash zones: 6061-T6 or 6082-T6, anodised or painted; avoid copper-rich 2xxx alloys

Tolerances for channels depend on the circumscribing circle of the section and on the wall thickness. Precision 6060/6063 profiles for windows and fittings are commonly ordered to EN 12020-2, which is tighter than EN 755-9. For channels that must slide or nest into other parts, mark the critical dimensions on the drawing and agree measuring points with the extruder, because flange tips can spread or close slightly after ageing.

Working with channel

Cutting, welding, bending and finishing

Cutting and machining

Saw with carbide-tipped blades and a lubricant; 6061-T6 and 6082-T6 machine cleanly, 6063 is softer and can burr. Support thin flanges close to the cut to avoid chatter.

Welding

MIG or TIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. The heat-affected zone of 6xxx tempers drops to well below T6 strength, so welded frames are designed to reduced allowables in the ADM or Eurocode 9.

Bending

Channel can be roll-bent the easy way or the hard way, but flanges tend to buckle or flare. Allow larger radii in T6, or bend in T4 and age afterwards.

Anodising

6063 gives the most uniform anodised colour and is preferred for visible trims. 6061 and 6082 anodise well but can look slightly grey or show streaks.

Fastening

Use stainless or aluminium fasteners with isolating washers in wet areas to avoid galvanic corrosion against carbon steel or copper.

Joining to steel

Separate aluminium channel from steel with gaskets, tape or paint when the joint will be wet or salty.

Applications

Where aluminium channel is used

  • Transport: trailer cross members, truck body frames, bus and rail interiors, ramp edges
  • Building and façades: glazing channels, curtain-wall subframes, sunshade supports, cladding rails
  • Marine: dock and gangway framing, boat seating and deck structures
  • Electrical and solar: cable trays, panel mounting rails, enclosure frames, busbar supports
  • Machinery: guards, conveyors, linear guide carriers, equipment skids
  • Interiors and furniture: shelving, display systems, shower screens, cabinet and drawer tracks

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) channel: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between C channel and U channel?

In everyday use the terms overlap. Structural "C" channel usually has thicker, slightly tapered flanges and filleted inside corners, with published section properties. "U" channel normally has parallel, uniform-thickness flanges and square or small-radius corners, and is used for trims, tracks and light frames.

Which aluminium grade is strongest for channel?

Among common weldable grades, 6082-T6 and 6061-T6 are the strongest, with minimum yield strengths around 240 – 260 MPa. 7075-T6 channel is much stronger but is not suitable for fusion welding and costs more.

How much does aluminium channel weigh?

A 4 × 2 × 0.15 in Aluminum Association channel weighs 1.738 lb/ft (about 2.59 kg/m), and a 6 × 2.5 × 0.17 in channel 2.834 lb/ft (about 4.22 kg/m). For other sections, multiply the cross-sectional area in cm² by 0.27 to get kg per metre.

Can aluminum channel replace steel channel?

Often, but not size for size. Aluminium is about one third the weight of steel and has about one third the stiffness, so a replacement channel is usually deeper or thicker to keep deflection within limits. The weight saving is still typically 40 – 50 %.

Is 6063 channel strong enough for structural use?

6063-T6 is used for light structures such as frames and handrail parts, but its minimum yield is only about 170 MPa. For load-bearing members, specify 6061-T6 or 6082-T6.

Can you supply channel cut to length and anodised?

Yes. Metline supplies channel cut to length, with anodising, powder coating or machining on request, and ships worldwide with a mill test certificate.
