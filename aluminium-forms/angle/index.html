Aluminium Angle: Equal and Unequal Leg

Aluminium angle is an L-shaped extruded section used for frames, edge trims, brackets and bracing. It is made in equal-leg and unequal-leg forms, with sharp or radiused corners, mostly in 6063 for architectural work and 6061 or 6082 for structural work. Metline supplies standard and custom angles cut to length and certified to ASTM B221, B308 or EN 755, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is aluminium angle?

An angle has two legs at 90°. It is described by its outside leg lengths and its thickness, for example 50 × 50 × 5 mm for an equal angle or 75 × 50 × 6 mm for an unequal angle. Because it is open, light and easy to cut, drill and join, it is one of the most widely used aluminium sections in construction, transport and machine building.

Almost all aluminium angle is extruded: a heated billet is pushed through a steel die with an L-shaped opening, then cooled, stretched straight and aged to temper. Extrusion allows features that rolled steel angle cannot have, such as a sharp outside corner with a small inside radius, rounded leg tips, a bevelled toe, or grooves for fixings. Two broad families exist: architectural angle in 6063 with sharp corners and a fine surface for trims, and structural angle in 6061-T6 with radiused fillets and tapered or square toes, dimensioned to the Aluminum Association standard shapes.

Specify these on every angle order

  • Equal or unequal legs, and whether sizes are outside dimensions
  • Thickness of each leg (they can differ) and inside and outside corner radii
  • Alloy and temper, and the standard (ASTM B221, B308 or EN 755)
  • Length, surface finish (mill, anodised, powder coated) and protective film

Minimums follow ASTM B221 for 6063 and 6061 and EN 755-2 for 6082 and 6005A. EN 755-2 values vary by wall thickness: for example 6082-T6 is 310/260 MPa above 5 mm wall and slightly lower in thin walls. Typical values are higher than these minimums.

All these alloys have a density of about 2.70 g/cm³ and an elastic modulus of about 69 GPa, so stiffness is the same whichever you choose; only strength, finish and cost differ. For electrical earthing and busbar supports, 6101-T6 angle offers higher conductivity at moderate strength.

Sizes and weights

Tolerance standards

Standard mill lengths, typically 6 m (or 12 / 20 ft), or cut to length
Inspection
Dimensional check, hardness or Webster test, mill certificate to EN 10204 3.1

Leg thickness tolerance depends on the circumscribing circle diameter and the wall thickness, so a thin angle with long legs has a looser absolute tolerance than a short, thick one. If your angle must fit closely against another part, give the critical dimension and its tolerance on the drawing rather than relying on the standard table.

Fabrication

Cutting, joining and finishing angle

Cutting and drilling

Saw with carbide-tipped blades and lubricant; 6061 and 6082 machine more cleanly than soft 6063-T5. Deburr holes to avoid fatigue starters.

Welding

MIG or TIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. 6061-T6 and 6082-T6 lose strength near the weld (to roughly T4 level), which design codes such as Eurocode 9 take into account.

Bolting and riveting

Use stainless or coated fasteners with insulating washers where angles meet steel, to prevent galvanic corrosion.

Bending

T6 angle bends poorly; bend in the O or T4 condition, or use generous radii. Bending around the heel distorts the legs.

Anodising

6063 gives the most uniform clear and coloured anodised finish; 6061 and 6082 anodise well but can look slightly darker.

Powder coating

All grades powder coat well after pretreatment; specify the RAL colour and coating standard (for example Qualicoat).

Applications

Applications of aluminium angle

  • Building: edge trims, stair nosings, corner guards, curtain-wall and cladding sub-frames, roof edges
  • Transport: truck bodies, trailer frames, bus and rail interiors, container corner strips
  • Machinery: frames, guards, shelving, conveyor sides and brackets
  • Marine: deck fittings, hatch frames and stiffeners, using 6061 or 6082 for corrosion resistance
  • Solar and electrical: panel mounting frames, cable-tray supports and earthing strips
  • DIY and furniture: shelf supports, display frames and edge protection

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) angle: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 6061 and 6063 angle?

6061-T6 has more than twice the minimum yield strength of 6063-T5 and is used for structural frames and brackets. 6063 has a finer surface and anodises more uniformly, so it is chosen for trims and architectural work.

How much does aluminium angle weigh per metre?

Multiply the area by 2.70 g/cm³. A 50 × 50 × 5 mm equal angle has an area of 475 mm² and weighs about 1.28 kg/m; a 25 × 25 × 3 mm angle weighs about 0.38 kg/m.

Are angle sizes measured inside or outside?

Leg lengths are normally outside dimensions, measured from the back of the heel to the leg tip. Always confirm on the drawing, especially for custom sections.

Can I get a sharp-cornered angle?

Yes. Architectural 6063 angles are commonly extruded with a sharp outside corner and a small inside radius. Structural 6061 angles have fillet radii, which reduce stress concentration.

Can aluminium angle be welded?

Yes, 6061, 6063 and 6082 angles weld well with MIG or TIG using ER4043 or ER5356 filler. Expect lower strength in the heat-affected zone and design accordingly.

Which standard covers structural aluminium angle?

ASTM B308 covers 6061-T6 standard structural profiles, ASTM B221 covers extruded profiles generally, and in Europe EN 755-2 and EN 755-9 cover properties and tolerances.
