Aluminium Rectangular Tube

Aluminium rectangular tube is a hollow section with two unequal pairs of flat sides, sized by height × width × wall thickness. It gives high bending stiffness about its deeper axis at low weight, which makes it the standard section for frames, trailers, façades and machine guards. Metline supplies extruded and drawn rectangular tube in 6063, 6061, 6082 and 5052, cut and certified, worldwide.

Overview

What is aluminium rectangular tube?

A rectangular tube is a closed hollow section, usually extruded, with flat walls of uniform thickness. Because more metal sits far from the neutral axis in the deeper direction, a rectangular tube resists bending in that direction much better than a square tube of the same weight. The closed section also gives good torsional stiffness, which open channels and angles lack.

Most aluminium rectangular tube is extruded through a porthole (bridge) die, which splits the billet around a mandrel and welds it together again inside the die. The resulting longitudinal seam welds are solid-state bonds and perform well in structural service. Seamless rectangular tube and precision cold-drawn rectangular tube exist for pressure and precision uses, and thin-wall rectangular tube is also roll-formed and welded from strip.

Order rectangular tube by the outside height, outside width and wall thickness, plus the corner style. Say which face is "height" if orientation matters, for example for straightness or surface finish on a visible face, and state the alloy and temper together.

Specify rectangular tube by

  • Outside height × width × wall (e.g. 50 × 100 × 3 mm or 2 × 4 × 1/8 in)
  • Alloy and temper (e.g. 6063-T6, 6061-T6, 6082-T6)
  • Corner style: sharp or radiused, with radius if critical
  • Length, surface finish and any visible face
Alloys

Rectangular tube alloys and properties

6063 gives the best surface finish and the most economical extrusion, so it dominates architectural and general-purpose tube. 6061 and 6082 are stronger and are used for load-bearing structures. 5052 and 3003 are used in drawn or welded tube where formability and corrosion resistance matter more than strength.

Tolerance standards control outside dimensions, wall thickness, corner radii, straightness, twist, convexity or concavity of the flat faces and squareness of cut ends. Convexity and concavity can be significant on wide, thin walls, so if a face must lie flat against glass, panels or seals, state a flatness requirement on the drawing.

For structural design, use the minimum properties of the specified alloy and temper and the reduced heat-affected-zone strength near welds. Thin, wide walls can buckle locally before the section yields; design codes handle this with width-to-thickness limits.

Working with rectangular tube

Cutting, welding, bending and finishing

Cutting

Cut with carbide-tipped saws at high speed; mitre cuts are common in frames. Deburr the inside edges for safety and fit.

Welding

6061, 6063 and 6082 weld readily by MIG or TIG with 4043 or 5356 filler. The heat-affected zone loses strength, so place welds away from peak stress where possible.

Bending

Rectangular tube wrinkles or collapses on the inner wall if bent without support. Use mandrel bending, bend the softer T4 or O temper and age afterwards, or choose larger radii.

Joining

Mechanical joints with corner cleats, inserts, rivets and bolts avoid weld softening and suit modular frames and façades.

Anodising

6063 gives the most uniform anodised finish; 6061 and 6082 anodise well but may show more surface variation.

Powder coating

Common for architectural and outdoor tube. Pre-treat with a chromate-free conversion coating for adhesion and corrosion resistance.

Applications

Where rectangular tube is used

  • Transport: trailer chassis rails, truck bodies, bus and rail interior frames
  • Building: curtain-wall mullions and transoms, window and door frames, pergolas and canopies
  • Machinery: frames, guards, conveyors, workbenches and automation enclosures
  • Solar: mounting rails, purlins and support frames
  • Marine: boat railings, T-tops and deck frames in 6061 or 6082
  • Furniture and fit-out: table and shelving frames, display stands and signage

Rectangular tube is often the best compromise when a member must resist bending, twisting and occasional side loads, and when a clean, closed appearance matters. For two-way loading see square tube.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) rectangular tube: FAQ

How is aluminium rectangular tube measured?

By outside height × outside width × wall thickness, for example 50 × 100 × 3 mm. Corner radius and length are specified separately.

How much does aluminium rectangular tube weigh?

For sharp corners, weight in kg/m = [2t(H + W) − 4t²] × 0.0027 with dimensions in mm. A 50 × 100 × 3 mm tube weighs about 2.33 kg/m, and a 40 × 80 × 3 mm tube about 1.85 kg/m.

Which alloy is best for rectangular tube?

6063-T5 or T6 for architectural and general use with a fine finish, and 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 for structural members such as trailers and machine frames. 5052 suits drawn or welded tube that must be formed.

Is extruded rectangular tube seamless?

Usually not. Most rectangular tube is extruded through a porthole die, which creates solid-state seam welds along the length. These are sound for structural use. Seamless tube is available when pressure service requires it.

Can aluminium rectangular tube be bent?

Yes, with mandrel support or in a softer temper, and with a generous radius. Without support the inner wall buckles. Bend 6061 in T4 and age to T6 afterwards if full strength is needed.

Do you cut rectangular tube to length?

Yes. Metline supplies rectangular tube in standard or cut lengths, mill finish, anodised or powder coated, with certificates, to customers worldwide.
