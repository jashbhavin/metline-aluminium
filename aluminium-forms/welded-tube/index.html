Welded Aluminium Tube

Welded aluminium tube is made by roll-forming flat strip into a tube and joining the seam, usually by high-frequency (HF) welding. It gives very thin, uniform walls, long lengths and low cost in volume, and it can be made from clad strip for brazed heat exchangers. Metline supplies HF and laser welded round, oval and flat tube in 3003, clad brazing alloys, 5052 and 6063, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is welded aluminium tube?

Welded tube starts as a coil of slit strip rather than an extrusion billet. The strip is progressively formed by rolls into a round, open tube; its edges are heated to welding temperature by high-frequency current and forged together by squeeze rolls, without filler metal. The outside weld bead is trimmed, the tube is sized to the final shape and cut to length or coiled.

Because the wall thickness comes straight from rolled strip, welded tube can have thinner and more uniform walls than extruded tube, often down to about 0.2 – 0.3 mm. That makes it the standard product for automotive radiators, condensers, heater cores and charge-air coolers, where flat or oval tubes made from clad strip are brazed to fins. It is also used for furniture, ladders, irrigation, antennas and other light structures where cost per metre matters.

The trade-off is the longitudinal seam. A well-made HF weld is a solid-state bond as strong as the parent strip in non-heat-treatable grades, but it is still a feature that the designer and inspector must account for, particularly in pressure service and tight bends.

Welded or seamless?

  • Choose welded tube for thin walls, long lengths, clad brazing tube and high-volume, cost-sensitive parts
  • Choose seamless or drawn tube for high pressure, critical safety parts, or heat-treated high-strength alloys
  • Choose porthole extruded tube for thicker walls and structural 6xxx sections in moderate volume
Manufacturing

How welded aluminium tube is made

  1. Strip preparationCoil is slit to the exact width that gives the tube circumference, with clean, burr-free edges essential for a sound weld.
  2. Roll formingA series of forming stands bends the strip into an open round section with the edges meeting at the weld point.
  3. HF weldingHigh-frequency induction or contact welding heats the edges, and squeeze rolls forge them together. Oxide is expelled into the bead, leaving a clean bond.
  4. Bead removalThe external bead is scarfed flush; the internal bead can be removed or controlled to a set height where the bore must be smooth.
  5. Sizing and shapingSizing rolls bring the tube to final OD, or reshape it into oval, flat or rectangular forms for heat exchangers.
  6. Testing and cuttingIn-line eddy current testing checks the seam continuously, then the tube is cut to length or level-wound into coils.

Laser welding is also used for very thin or high-precision tube. Large-diameter welded aluminium pipe is made separately, by arc welding of formed plate or by spiral welding.

Typical properties follow the parent strip: 3003-H14 is about 150 MPa tensile and 145 MPa yield; 5052-H32 about 228 MPa and 193 MPa. In brazed heat exchangers the tube is softened by the brazing cycle, so designers use post-braze (near O temper) properties, roughly 110 – 130 MPa tensile for 3003, or choose long-life core alloys with higher post-braze strength.

Working with welded tube

Bending, brazing and joining

Bending

Position the seam on the neutral axis (side of the bend) to reduce strain on the weld. O and H1x tempers in 3003 bend tightly.

Brazing

Clad tube is brazed with fins and headers in controlled-atmosphere (flux) or vacuum furnaces; keep surfaces clean and free of heavy oils.

End forming

Flaring, beading, reducing and expanding are routine; flaring tests confirm the seam can take it.

Fusion welding

3003 and 5052 welded tube can be TIG welded into assemblies; avoid welding directly across the seam where possible.

Corrosion protection

Zinc-diffused or clad outer layers sacrificially protect thin walls in road-salt environments.

Coating

Powder coating and anodising suit furniture and consumer tube in 5052 and 6063.

Applications

Where welded aluminium tube is used

  • Automotive thermal systems: radiator, condenser, heater core, charge-air cooler and oil cooler tube
  • HVAC and refrigeration: refrigerant lines, evaporator and condenser tube
  • Furniture and consumer goods: chairs, tables, ladders, strollers, umbrellas and tent frames
  • Agriculture: irrigation pipe and sprinkler lines
  • Electrical: antennas, conduit and lighting tube
  • Construction: lightweight frames, curtain rods and handrails

FAQ

Welded aluminium (aluminum) tube: frequently asked questions

Is welded aluminium tube as strong as seamless tube?

In non-heat-treatable alloys such as 3003 and 5052, a sound HF weld is close to parent-metal strength and passes flattening and flaring tests. For high-pressure or safety-critical service, seamless or drawn tube is still the safer specification.

What is HF welding?

High-frequency welding uses high-frequency current to heat the strip edges to welding temperature in a fraction of a second; rolls then forge them together without filler metal. It is fast, clean and suited to thin aluminium walls.

Why is welded tube used in car radiators and condensers?

It can be made with very thin, uniform walls from clad strip whose outer layer melts in the brazing furnace to bond the fins, giving light, efficient heat exchangers at low cost per metre.

Can the internal weld bead be removed?

Yes. The internal bead can be removed or controlled to a specified maximum height. State the requirement if the bore must be smooth for fluid flow or inserts.

How is the seam inspected?

Usually by continuous in-line eddy current testing, plus flattening, flaring and pressure or leak tests on samples, as agreed in the order.

Do you supply welded tube in coils?

Yes. Small-diameter welded tube for refrigerant and heat-exchanger use can be supplied level-wound in coils as well as in cut lengths.
