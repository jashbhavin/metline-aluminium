Inner-Grooved Aluminium Tube

Inner-grooved aluminium tube is a thin-wall round tube with fine helical or straight fins formed on its bore. The grooves enlarge the inner surface and stir the refrigerant, raising heat transfer in fin-and-tube condensers and evaporators. It lets manufacturers build all-aluminium coils that are lighter, cheaper and free of copper–aluminium galvanic corrosion.

Overview

What is inner-grooved aluminium tube?

In a round-tube plate-fin (RTPF) heat exchanger, refrigerant flows inside the tubes while air passes over thin fins pressed onto them. Most of the thermal resistance on the refrigerant side sits in a thin liquid film on the tube wall. Grooves on the inside break up that film, increase wetted area and promote mixing, so the same tube carries more heat during condensation and evaporation.

Copper inner-grooved tube has been the industry norm for room air conditioners and heat pumps for decades. Aluminium versions have grown as manufacturers look to cut material cost and weight, avoid copper price swings, and make coils from a single metal. An all-aluminium coil has aluminium fins on aluminium tubes, so there is no galvanic couple between dissimilar metals, and aluminium tubes are not attacked by the formicary ("ant-nest") corrosion that affects copper tubes in some indoor environments.

The trade-off is that aluminium is softer and weaker than copper, more sensitive to groove damage during expansion, and harder to join. A successful switch depends on the tube geometry, the alloy, the expansion tooling and the brazing method being designed together, not on substituting one tube for another.

Why coil makers move to aluminium grooved tube

  • Lower and more stable material cost than copper
  • About one-third of the weight of an equivalent copper tube
  • Single-metal coil: no copper–aluminium galvanic cell, easier recycling
  • No formicary corrosion, a known failure mode of copper evaporator tubes
Tube geometry

The geometry parameters you need to specify

An inner-grooved tube is defined by more than its outside diameter. Each parameter below affects heat transfer, pressure drop, burst strength and how the tube behaves when it is expanded into the fin collars. Agree a drawing that shows all of them, with tolerances.

Outside diameter (OD)Commonly 5, 7, 7.94 (5/16 in) or 9.52 mm (3/8 in) to match fin collar sizesMust suit the fin die and the expansion bullet

Aluminium grooved tube is produced either by extruding the grooves directly with a profiled mandrel or by drawing over a grooved plug; helical grooves may be added by twisting. The route affects achievable geometry, so confirm it with us at enquiry stage.

Alloys & composition

Alloys for inner-grooved tube (wt %)

Tube alloys need high formability in the annealed state, good brazeability and adequate corrosion resistance. Manganese-bearing 3xxx alloys are the most common; leaner 3102 extrudes more easily into fine internal detail. Long-life alloys, which are 3xxx variants with tuned copper, manganese and zinc, are also used to shift corrosion into a lateral, non-perforating mode.

Design & fabrication

Designing a coil around aluminium grooved tube

Pressure rating

Modern refrigerants such as R32 and R410A run at high condensing pressures. Size the bottom wall so the tube meets the burst and fatigue requirements of your safety standard with margin, allowing for the lower strength of annealed aluminium.

Mechanical expansion

Tubes are expanded into fin collars with a bullet. Aluminium grooves flatten more easily than copper ones, so bullet size, expansion ratio and lubricant must be tuned to keep groove height after expansion.

Hairpin bending

Hairpins are bent from O-temper tube. Tight bends can thin the outer wall and collapse grooves; tooling designed for aluminium and correct bend radius are essential.

Joining

Return bends and headers are flame or induction brazed with Al-Si filler and a non-corrosive flux, or joined with brazing ring preforms. Copper-to-aluminium transition joints, where needed, require careful design and protection.

Corrosion protection

Fins are usually made slightly anodic to the tube so they corrode first. A zinc layer on the tube outside or a long-life alloy gives further sacrificial protection in aggressive air.

Cleanliness

Residual drawing oil and particles inside the tube can contaminate the refrigerant circuit. Agree an internal cleanliness limit and a dehydrated, capped delivery condition.

Inspection

Inspection and testing

Grooved tube for refrigerant circuits is checked more closely than general-purpose tube, because a single pinhole means a field leak.

Typical inspection plan

Dimensions
OD, bottom wall, groove height, groove count and helix angle by sectioning and optical measurement
Eddy-current testing
In-line testing of the full coil length to detect wall defects and pinholes
Pressure / leak test
Pressure or burst testing on samples; helium leak testing on assembled coils
Mechanical tests
Tensile strength, elongation and expansion or flare test on samples from each lot
Cleanliness
Residual oil and particulate inside the tube, against the agreed limit
Certification
Mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 with chemistry and mechanical results

FAQ

Inner-grooved aluminium (aluminum) tube: FAQ

Can aluminium grooved tube replace copper in an air-conditioner coil?

Yes, in many round-tube plate-fin designs, but not as a straight swap. Wall thickness, groove geometry, expansion tooling and the brazing of return bends must be redesigned for aluminium. Prototype coils should be tested for capacity, pressure and corrosion before production.

Which alloy is used for inner-grooved aluminium tube?

Mostly 3xxx alloys such as 3003 and 3102, plus 1100 and proprietary long-life alloys. All are supplied in the annealed O temper so they can be bent into hairpins and expanded into the fins.

Why do aluminium grooves need special expansion tooling?

Aluminium is softer than copper, so the internal fins flatten more easily when a bullet expands the tube into the fin collars. Bullet size, expansion ratio and lubrication must be tuned to preserve groove height and heat transfer.

Does aluminium tube suffer formicary corrosion?

No. Formicary or ant-nest corrosion is a copper problem caused by organic acids in indoor air. Aluminium tubes are not attacked this way, which is one reason all-aluminium coils are used for indoor units.

How are aluminium hairpins joined?

Return bends and headers are brazed with aluminium-silicon filler, by flame or induction, using non-corrosive flux or flux-containing ring preforms. Where a copper line must connect, a purpose-made copper–aluminium transition joint is used.

In what format is grooved tube supplied?

Usually as level-wound coils that feed hairpin benders directly, or as pancake coils, straight lengths or pre-bent hairpins, cleaned and end-capped. Metline supplies these formats with certificates to customers worldwide.
