Mirror & Reflector Aluminium Sheet

Mirror reflector aluminium sheet is high-purity aluminium rolled bright and then anodised or vacuum-coated so it reflects light efficiently, either as a sharp mirror or as a soft, diffuse surface. It is the standard material for luminaire reflectors, solar concentrators, daylight tubes and decorative trim. Overview

What is reflector aluminium sheet?

Aluminium is one of the best reflectors of visible light, infrared and especially ultraviolet. A clean, smooth high-purity aluminium surface reflects about 90 % of visible light, and it keeps reflecting well into the UV, where silver falls away. Reflector sheet turns that property into a stable industrial product by controlling purity, rolling, brightening and a protective surface layer.

Two families dominate. Anodised reflector sheet is bright-rolled high-purity aluminium that is chemically or electrochemically brightened and then given a thin, clear anodic layer to protect it. Its total reflectance typically peaks in the mid-80s per cent, because the oxide layer absorbs and scatters a little light. PVD-enhanced sheet adds a vacuum-deposited layer of very pure aluminium (or silver) on an anodised base, topped by transparent oxide layers that boost reflection, reaching about 95 % for enhanced aluminium and higher for silver-based systems.

Brightness to the eye is not a reliable guide. What matters is total reflectance, the share of it that is specular or diffuse, and how the surface behaves under the light source, temperature and humidity of the application. For that reason reflector sheet is specified by measured optical class, not just "mirror finish".

Specify reflector sheet by

  • Total reflectance (DIN 5036-3) and the required specular or diffuse share
  • Surface type: specular, semi-specular, matt, or structured/hammered
  • Base metal, temper and thickness for the forming method
  • Protective film suited to the cutting process (laser, punching, roll forming)

Base metal

Base alloys, purity and temper

Reflectance and the quality of the brightened surface rise with purity, because iron- and silicon-rich particles scatter light and leave pits after brightening. Reflector sheet therefore uses 99.85 % aluminium or purer; 5005 and other bright-trim alloys are used where some strength and a decorative anodised finish matter more than peak reflectance.

Working with reflector sheet

Cutting, forming and handling

Cutting

Shear, punch or laser cut with the protective film left on. Laser cutting needs a film rated for lasers so it does not melt into the surface.

Bending

Anodic and PVD layers are thin and hard; tight bends can craze them. Respect the supplier's minimum bend radius and bend with the film on.

Forming cones

Spinning and deep drawing are done on soft, often unanodised sheet, with brightening and anodising afterwards, or on special formable reflector grades.

Handling

Wear clean gloves. Fingerprints and oils reduce reflectance and can etch anodised surfaces over time.

Cleaning

Use mild, neutral cleaners and soft cloths. Avoid alkaline or abrasive cleaners, which attack the anodic layer.

Heat

Check the maximum service temperature of the surface system, especially near high-intensity lamps.

Applications

Where reflector aluminium is used

  • Lighting: louvres, troffer and high-bay reflectors, downlight cones, street-light optics
  • Solar energy: concentrators, booster reflectors and solar thermal collectors
  • Daylighting: tubular daylight devices, light shelves and light pipes
  • UV technology: UV curing and disinfection reflectors, where aluminium reflects UV far better than silver
  • Horticulture: grow-light reflectors and greenhouse lighting
  • Decorative and display: trims, signage, appliance panels and backlit displays
Quality

How reflectance is measured

Total reflectance is measured with an integrating-sphere instrument to DIN 5036-3 and is the headline figure on most datasheets. Diffuse reflectance is measured with the specular component excluded; the difference between the two describes how mirror-like the surface is. ISO 7668 covers specular reflectance and gloss of anodised aluminium at defined angles, and solar applications use solar-weighted reflectance, for example to ASTM E903.

For purchasing, agree the measurement method, the acceptance value and the sampling frequency, and ask for the measured result per coil or batch. A datasheet maximum is not a delivery guarantee.

FAQ

Mirror reflector aluminium (aluminum) sheet: FAQ

How reflective is mirror aluminium sheet?

Anodised specular reflector sheet typically reaches about 83 – 87 % total reflectance (DIN 5036-3). PVD-enhanced aluminium reaches about 95 %, and silver-based PVD systems about 97 – 98 %. Always ask for the measured value for the finish you buy.

What alloy is used for reflector sheet?

High-purity aluminium such as 1085 (99.85 %), 1090 or 1199 (99.99 %), because fewer impurity particles give a brighter, clearer surface after brightening. 5005 and similar alloys are used for decorative anodised trim where strength matters more than peak reflectance.

What is the difference between specular and diffuse reflector sheet?

Specular sheet reflects light like a mirror, at a defined angle, for precise beam control. Diffuse or semi-specular sheet spreads the reflected light to soften glare and hot spots. Both can have similar total reflectance; the difference is the direction of the reflected light.

Can mirror aluminium sheet be bent without damage?

Yes, within the minimum bend radius for the surface system. The anodic or PVD layer can craze at tight bends, so bend with the film on, along the recommended direction, and test the bend on a sample first.

Is aluminium better than silver for UV reflectors?

Yes. Silver reflects visible light better but its reflectance drops sharply in the ultraviolet, while aluminium stays highly reflective. That is why UV curing and disinfection reflectors use aluminium surfaces.

Do you supply reflector sheet cut to size?

Yes. Metline supplies reflector aluminium in coils, slit strip and cut sheets with protective film, packed flat for export to customers worldwide.
