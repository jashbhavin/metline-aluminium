Electrical Aluminium Wire Rod (EC Grade)

Electrical aluminium wire rod is continuously cast and rolled rod, most often 9.5 mm in diameter, that cable makers draw into wire for overhead conductors, power cables and building wire. The standard grade is EC (electrical conductor) aluminium, registered as 1350, with at least 61 % IACS conductivity; alloy rods in 6101/6201 and 8xxx series serve higher-strength and building-wire applications. Metline supplies rod to ASTM B233, IS 5484 and EN 1715 to cable plants worldwide.

Overview

What is electrical aluminium wire rod?

Wire rod is the semi-finished link between molten metal and finished conductor. It is produced on a continuous casting and rolling line: liquid aluminium is cast into a bar on a rotating wheel or between belts, and the hot bar goes straight into a multi-stand rolling mill that reduces it to round rod, which is coiled.

Electrical rod is made from high-purity aluminium with tight limits on the elements that hurt conductivity. Titanium, vanadium, chromium and manganese are the worst offenders, so the melt is often treated with boron, which ties them up as insoluble borides. The result is EC grade aluminium, registered by the Aluminum Association as 1350, with 99.5 % minimum aluminium and conductivity of about 61.8 % IACS in the annealed state.

By weight, aluminium conducts about twice as well as copper: it has around 61 % of copper's conductivity but only 30 % of its density. That is why almost all overhead transmission lines and a large share of power cables use aluminium conductors drawn from rod like this.

What makes good wire rod

  • Consistent chemistry with low Ti + V + Cr + Mn for high conductivity
  • Tensile strength within a narrow range so it draws without breaks
  • Clean, oxide-free surface with no laps, slivers or inclusions
  • Well-wound coils that pay off smoothly on the wire-drawing machine

Unalloyed 1350 dominates by volume. Alloy rods are chosen when the conductor must carry more mechanical load (6201 for AAAC spans), resist creep at terminations (8xxx in building wire), or run hotter without annealing (zirconium-alloyed rod).

Properties

Tempers, tensile ranges and conductivity of 1350 rod

ASTM B233 classifies 1350 drawing stock by temper, each with a tensile strength band and a minimum conductivity. Harder tempers are drawn with fewer passes; softer tempers are used where the finished wire must be soft or will be annealed.

ASTM B233 covers rod from 9.52 mm to 25.40 mm diameter; the H14 and H16 ranges apply up to 12.7 mm. Indian IS 5484 grades rod by similar tensile bands with a maximum resistivity of 0.028264 Ω·mm²/m at 20 °C (61.0 % IACS).

The standard you name should match the downstream product. A cable plant making IEC conductors may still buy rod to ASTM B233 or IS 5484, but the conductivity and tensile limits for the finished wire are set by the conductor standard, so both should be stated on the order.

Processing

Drawing, annealing and quality tips

Matching temper to the die schedule

Harder rod (H14, H16) needs fewer passes to reach H19 hard-drawn wire for overhead conductors; softer rod suits drawing to fine or annealed wire.

Conductivity after drawing

Cold work lowers conductivity slightly. Starting rod with margin above 61 % IACS keeps finished H19 wire within its minimum.

Breaks and inclusions

Oxide films and inclusions cause breaks at fine diameters. Clean rod, proper filtration and good degassing in casting are the main controls.

Joins

Cold-pressure or butt welds join coils; the agreed number and strength of welds should be stated.

Storage

Keep coils dry and covered; condensation causes surface oxidation that can carry into the wire.

Ageing alloys

6101/6201 rod is solution-treated and the drawn wire is artificially aged to reach strength and conductivity together.

Applications

What wire rod becomes

  • Overhead lines: AAC, ACSR, AAAC and ACAR conductors for transmission and distribution
  • Power cables: stranded and solid aluminium cores for LV, MV and HV cables
  • Building wire: 8000-series alloy conductors where codes allow aluminium
  • Winding wire: enamelled and paper-covered conductors for transformers and motors
  • Other: earthing and lightning protection conductors, cable armour and shielding wire

FAQ

EC grade aluminium (aluminum) wire rod: FAQ

What does EC grade aluminium mean?

EC stands for electrical conductor. It is high-purity aluminium (registered as 1350) with 99.5 % minimum Al and strict limits on titanium, vanadium, chromium and manganese, giving conductivity of at least 61 % IACS.

What is the standard diameter of aluminium wire rod?

9.5 mm (3/8 in) is the standard. Rod from about 7.6 mm up to 25 mm is also made for specific drawing lines and conductor types.

What is the difference between 1350 and 6201 rod?

1350 is unalloyed and has the highest conductivity (about 61.8 % IACS) but low strength. 6201 contains magnesium and silicon, and after drawing and ageing the wire is roughly twice as strong, with conductivity around 52.5 % IACS. It is used for AAAC conductors.

Which temper of 1350 rod should I order?

For hard-drawn overhead conductor wire, H14 or H16 rod is common because it reaches H19 with fewer passes. For soft or annealed wire and cable cores, O or H12 rod is often preferred. Match the temper to your die schedule.

How much rod is in a coil?

A metre of 9.5 mm rod weighs about 0.192 kg, so a 2-tonne coil contains roughly 10 km of rod.

Do you supply wire rod with test certificates?

Yes. Each lot is supplied with a mill test certificate showing chemistry, tensile strength, elongation and resistivity or conductivity.
