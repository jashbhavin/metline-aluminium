Aluminium Plate: Grades & Sizes

Aluminium plate is rolled aluminium more than 6 mm thick, supplied in grades from soft commercial-purity 1050 to aerospace 7075 and 7050. Choosing the right plate means matching the alloy and temper to strength, corrosion, welding and machining needs. Metline supplies every common plate grade, cut to size and certified to ASTM, AMS and EN standards, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What counts as aluminium plate?

Rolled aluminium is called plate once it is thicker than about 6 mm. ASTM B209 draws the line at 0.250 in (6.35 mm); European practice under EN 485 treats material above 6 mm as plate. Below that it is sheet, and very thin rolled material is foil. The distinction matters because plate is hot rolled, often stress-relieved by stretching, and supplied to thicker-section property limits and tolerances.

Plate is the starting material for parts that are machined, welded into structures or cut into heavy blanks: ship hulls and decks, tank and pressure-vessel shells, machine bases, moulds, aircraft frames and wing ribs, armour, truck and rail bodies, and semiconductor equipment. In each case the alloy is chosen for a different reason, which is why aluminium plate is offered in so many grades.

The single most useful question when choosing plate is "what will happen to it next?" Heavy machining points to a stress-relieved temper such as T651 or a cast tooling plate. Welding into a structure points to 5xxx or 6xxx alloys. Exposure to seawater points to 5083 or 5086 in H116 or H321. High stress and fatigue point to 2xxx or 7xxx aerospace alloys. See our plate vs sheet article for the full definitions.

Four questions that pick the plate grade

  • How strong must it be, and is the load static or fatigue?
  • Will it be welded, and must welds keep their strength?
  • What environment will it see: indoor, outdoor, seawater, chemicals or cryogenic?
  • How much will be machined away, and how flat must the part stay?

Tempers

Plate tempers you will see on a quotation

T651

Solution heat-treated, stretched to relieve quench stress and artificially aged. Standard for 6061, 6082, 7075 and 2xxx plate that will be machined.

T7351 / T7451

Over-aged 7xxx tempers. Slightly lower strength than T651 but far better resistance to stress-corrosion cracking; standard in aircraft structure.

T351

Solution treated, stretched and naturally aged. The usual temper for 2024 plate, giving high fatigue resistance.

H116 / H321

Marine tempers for 5083, 5086 and 5456 tested for resistance to exfoliation and intergranular corrosion (ASTM B928). Required by ship classification societies.

H111 / O / H112

Annealed or lightly worked 5xxx and 3xxx plate for forming, welding and general fabrication.

Cast tooling plate

Cast and precision-milled plate (often 5083-based) with very low internal stress and tight flatness; chosen for jigs and machine tables rather than strength.

Quality

Tolerances, flatness and inspection

Thickness, width, length and flatness tolerances follow ANSI H35.2 for ASTM orders and EN 485-3 (hot-rolled plate) for EN orders. Saw-cut blanks are typically held to about ±0.5 – 1 mm, and precision-milled plate to a few hundredths of a millimetre in thickness, when ordered.

  • Residual stress: order stretched tempers (T651, T7351, T7451) or cast plate when removing a lot of metal, or parts will distort
  • Ultrasonic testing: specify class (e.g. AMS-STD-2154 Class A or B) for thick aerospace or vacuum-chamber plate
  • Surface: state cosmetic requirements and PVC film if the plate face will be visible
  • Grain direction: mark the rolling direction on blanks where properties or bending direction matter
  • Traceability: heat and lot numbers on every plate, linked to the mill certificate

How to order aluminium plate

  1. SpecifyGrade and temper (e.g. 5083-H321), thickness, width and length or a cutting list, quantity, the governing standard (ASTM B209, B928, EN 485, AMS) and any class approval or ultrasonic requirement.
  2. QuoteWe confirm alloy, temper, tolerances, certification, packing and delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP) for your destination.
  3. ProcessPlates are cut to size, protected with film where needed and inspected for dimensions, flatness and surface.
  4. DocumentEach delivery carries a mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 with the cast analysis and mechanical results; third-party inspection can be arranged on request.
  5. ShipPlates are packed on seaworthy pallets or in wooden cases and shipped by sea or air on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQ

What thickness is aluminium plate?

Plate starts above about 6 mm. ASTM B209 defines plate as 0.250 in (6.35 mm) and thicker; EN practice treats material over 6 mm as plate. Thinner rolled product is sheet.

Which aluminium plate is best for marine use?

5083 in H116 or H321 temper, or 5086, tested to ASTM B928 for resistance to exfoliation and intergranular corrosion. These grades are accepted by classification societies for hulls and decks.

Which aluminium plate is strongest?

Among common grades, 7075-T651 (typical tensile strength about 572 MPa) and 7050-T7451 (about 524 MPa). 2024-T351 is lower in strength but has excellent fatigue resistance.

What is the difference between 6061 and 6082 plate?

They are close substitutes with similar strength, machinability and weldability. 6061 is the standard in the Americas and Asia; 6082 is more common in Europe and slightly stronger in thick sections.

How much does an aluminium plate weigh?

Thickness in mm × 2.70 gives kg per m² for 6061 or 6082. A 1,250 × 2,500 × 10 mm plate weighs about 84 kg; a 1,500 × 3,000 × 20 mm plate about 243 kg.

Do you supply plate cut to size?

Yes. Metline supplies aluminium plate in full sizes or cut to your list by saw, waterjet or CNC, with mill test certificates, to customers worldwide.
