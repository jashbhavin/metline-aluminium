Aluminium Remelt Ingot

An aluminium remelt ingot is unalloyed primary metal, cast into a stackable shape, that a foundry, casthouse or steel plant melts as charge. The grade is defined by its silicon, iron and trace-element limits, most often P1020A (99.70 % Al minimum), not by the shape of the ingot. Metline supplies remelt ingots in standard and higher purities with lot-traceable analysis certificates to buyers worldwide.

Overview

What is an aluminium remelt ingot?

A remelt ingot is aluminium in its most basic commercial form: metal tapped from reduction cells, adjusted for cleanliness and cast into open moulds so that it can be shipped, stored and charged into a furnace. It is a raw material, not a semi-finished product. Nobody machines or forms a remelt ingot; it is always melted again.

Buyers fall into three groups. Alloy producers and extrusion or rolling casthouses use unalloyed ingot to dilute recycled scrap and to hit tight impurity limits in their own alloys. Foundries add it to correct iron and silicon in a melt, or to make alloys in-house. Steel plants use it as a deoxidiser, where purity matters less than consistent weight and cleanliness.

The same physical shape is cast in many purities, so the key buying question is chemistry. A 22 kg ingot that looks identical to a P1020A ingot may carry far more iron or a different trace profile. The grade must be fixed by a named designation and confirmed by a lot analysis, never by appearance or by the brand stamp alone.

Remelt ingot is not the same as

  • Foundry alloy ingot: already alloyed (A356, A380, ADC12 and similar) for direct casting
  • Extrusion billet: DC-cast, homogenised alloy logs for extrusion presses
  • Rolling slab: large DC-cast alloy ingots scalped and hot rolled into plate and coil
  • Forging stock: cast or wrought alloy feed for forging
Grades

Remelt ingot grades and how they are named

North American and international practice names unalloyed remelt grades with a P designation, for example P1020A. The four digits are the maximum silicon and iron contents in hundredths of a per cent: P1020 means Si ≤ 0.10 % and Fe ≤ 0.20 %. A suffix letter adds limits on other elements. Europe uses EN 576 and the ISO system uses ISO 115, both of which classify ingots by minimum aluminium content.

Silicon and iron limits for the P grades follow directly from the designation. Nominal aluminium content for grades other than P1020A is shown as an approximate guide; the governing limits are those in the registration record or standard named on your order.

For electrical conductor feed, silicon and iron matter less than the transition elements. Titanium, vanadium, chromium and manganese each cut conductivity sharply even at a few hundredths of a per cent, so conductor-grade orders specify their combined maximum, and casthouses often treat the melt with boron to precipitate them.

Chemical composition

P1020A is the reference chemistry for high-grade primary aluminium traded on the London Metal Exchange, which also accepts the Al99.70 grade of GB/T 1196. The two are very close, as the table shows.

Two consequences for buyers. First, P1020A does not cap copper or magnesium as named elements; they fall under "others each". If your process is sensitive to a particular element, such as sodium and calcium in aluminium-silicon foundry alloys, or boron in conductor alloys, add an explicit limit to the purchase order. Second, iron is the impurity that most often decides whether an ingot is suitable for dilution, because iron cannot be removed economically once it is in the melt.

Physical forms

Ingot, T-bar and sow formats

Remelt aluminium is cast in a few standard shapes chosen to suit the buyer's furnace door, charging equipment and melt rate. Exchange rules for high-grade primary aluminium allow ingots of 9 to 26 kg each and T-bars or sows of up to 788 kg, delivered in 25 tonne lots with a 2 % tolerance.

FormatTypical unit weightHow it is handledBest for

Each bundle is strapped with steel or PET bands and marked with the producer's brand, grade, cast or lot number and net weight. Bundles load efficiently into 20-foot containers or break-bulk holds. Ask for net weight per bundle on the packing list; strapping and any timber skids should be excluded from the invoiced metal weight.

The theoretical melting energy is a floor, not a forecast. Real fuel or power consumption depends on furnace type, burner efficiency, heat recovery and the charge mix, and is typically several times the theoretical figure. Large sows and T-bars reduce handling and door-open time but melt more slowly per piece than standard ingots, which present more surface area to the furnace atmosphere.

Handling and melting

Safe charging and melt quality

Keep it dry

Moisture trapped between stacked ingots can flash to steam under molten metal and cause an explosion. Store under cover, and preheat or dry ingots before charging into a liquid heel.

Watch for hydrogen

Damp or corroded surfaces add hydrogen to the melt, which shows up later as gas porosity. Degas with argon or nitrogen rotary degassing where quality demands it.

Control dross

Charge clean ingots quickly into a hot furnace, avoid stirring that breaks the oxide skin, and skim before tapping. Fluxing helps separate oxides.

Sample the melt

Confirm the chemistry with optical emission spectrometry after melting, even when a certificate is supplied, before adding alloying elements.

Dilution maths

When diluting scrap, calculate the final iron and silicon from the ingot analysis and scrap analysis; the ingot grade is chosen to bring the blend inside the target alloy limits.

Steel deoxidation

For deoxidation, consistent unit weight and low moisture matter more than purity; notched ingots allow accurate additions.

Quality

Certificates, sampling and receiving inspection

A remelt ingot certificate should identify the producer's brand, the cast or lot number, the grade designation, the analysis method and the measured content of every limited element. Metline supplies lot-traceable analysis certificates with each delivery and can arrange independent inspection at loading on request.

  • Check that bundle markings match the certificate lot numbers and the packing list
  • Weigh a sample of bundles and compare with declared net weight
  • Look for heavy surface corrosion, water staining, inclusions or foreign material in the stacks
  • Take drillings or melt a sample for spectrometric analysis against the order limits
  • Confirm the extra limits you added (Na, Ca, B, Ti + V and so on) are reported, not just the standard set

Buyers increasingly also ask for the source of the metal and its carbon intensity, which differ greatly between smelters depending on their electricity supply. If your customers require that information, state it at enquiry so that suitable metal and documentation can be quoted.

Most melt shops use both: recycled metal to cut cost and carbon, and primary ingot to keep impurities inside the alloy limits. The proportion is set by the tightest limit in the target alloy, usually iron.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) remelt ingot: FAQ

What does P1020A mean?

P1020A is a registered unalloyed aluminium grade with at most 0.10 % silicon and 0.20 % iron, plus limits on zinc, gallium, vanadium and other elements, giving about 99.70 % aluminium minimum. The digits are the Si and Fe maximums in hundredths of a per cent.

How much does one aluminium ingot weigh?

Standard remelt ingots usually weigh about 20 – 25 kg, and exchange rules allow 9 to 26 kg. They are strapped into bundles of roughly one tonne. T-bars and sows weigh several hundred kilograms each, up to 788 kg under exchange rules.

Is Al 99.7 ingot the same as P1020A?

They are very close. Both cap Si at 0.10 % and Fe at 0.20 % with 99.70 % Al minimum, but the trace-element lists differ slightly (for example GB/T 1196 Al99.70 limits copper and magnesium, while P1020A limits vanadium). Name the exact standard on your order.

Can remelt ingot be used for casting directly?

Unalloyed ingot can be cast, but pure aluminium has poor fluidity and low strength as a casting. Foundries normally melt it with silicon, magnesium or master alloys, or buy pre-alloyed foundry alloy ingot.

Why must ingots be dry before charging?

Water trapped in stacks turns to steam instantly under molten aluminium and can cause a violent eruption. Damp metal also adds hydrogen, which causes porosity. Store ingots under cover and preheat them before adding them to a molten bath.

What documents come with a remelt ingot shipment?

A certificate of analysis per lot showing brand, cast number and measured composition, a packing list with net bundle weights, and the usual commercial and shipping documents. Metline can arrange third-party inspection on request.
