Aluminium Extrusion Billet

Aluminium extrusion billet is a round, direct-chill cast and homogenised log that extruders cut to length, heat and press through a die to make profiles, bars and tubes. Most billet is 6060, 6063, 6005A, 6061 or 6082, with 2xxx and 7xxx billet for high-strength work, in diameters from about 6 to 16 inches. Metline supplies full logs and cut billets to EN 486 and agreed chemistry windows for extruders worldwide.

Overview

What is an extrusion billet?

Every extruded profile starts life as a billet. Its chemistry decides the final strength and finish of the profile, and its internal structure decides how fast it can be pressed, how much pressure it needs and how many surface defects the extruder will fight. Good billet is one of the cheapest ways for an extrusion plant to raise output.

Billet is produced by direct-chill (DC) casting: molten aluminium, refined, degassed, filtered and grain-refined with Al-Ti-B, is poured into short water-cooled moulds, and the solidifying logs are lowered into a pit while water sprays the surface. Modern hot-top and air-slip moulds reduce the shell zone and inverse segregation at the surface, giving a smoother log that needs no scalping.

The cast logs are then homogenised: held at a high temperature, typically in the range of about 560 – 590 °C for 6063, for several hours. This evens out segregation, dissolves coarse Mg2Si, and transforms plate-shaped β-AlFeSi particles into rounded α-AlFeSi, which deforms more easily. Controlled cooling afterwards leaves the magnesium and silicon in a form that redissolves quickly in the extrusion press. Logs are finally ultrasonically or visually inspected, cut to length and stamped for traceability.

What a well-made billet gives the extruder

  • Higher extrusion speeds without tearing or pick-up
  • Lower breakthrough pressure and longer die life
  • Fewer surface streaks, die lines and blisters on anodised profiles
  • Consistent mechanical properties from lot to lot

Alloy selection

Which billet alloy for which profile?

6060

Fastest-pressing alloy for windows, doors and thin decorative sections. Lower strength than 6063.

6063

The global workhorse for architectural, furniture, heat sink and general profiles with an excellent anodised finish.

6005A

Thin-walled structural and transport profiles, solar frames and rail sections that need more strength than 6063.

6061

Structural profiles, tube and bar in North America and Asia; harder to press than 6063.

6082

Structural bar, tube and profiles in Europe and India; manganese gives fine grain and higher strength.

2xxx and 7xxx

2014, 2024, 7020, 7075 and similar billets for aerospace, defence and transport parts; slower pressing and closer inspection.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) extrusion billet: FAQ

Why must extrusion billet be homogenised?

As-cast logs have segregated alloying elements and needle-like β-AlFeSi particles. Homogenising evens out the chemistry and turns those particles into rounded α-AlFeSi, so the billet extrudes faster, at lower pressure and with a better surface.

What is the difference between a log and a billet?

A log is the full-length cast bar, often 6 – 7 m long. A billet is a shorter piece cut from the log to the length one press cycle needs. Some plants buy logs and cut them hot with a shear; others buy billets ready cut.

Which billet alloy is used most?

6063 is by far the most widely extruded alloy worldwide, followed by 6060 in Europe and 6061, 6082 and 6005A for structural profiles.

How much does an 8-inch billet weigh?

An 8 in (203 mm) billet weighs about 87 kg per metre at 2,700 kg/m³, so a 1-metre billet is about 87 kg.

Do you supply 7075 extrusion billet?

Yes, 2xxx and 7xxx billet such as 2014, 2024 and 7075 can be supplied, normally with ultrasonic inspection and tighter chemistry control.
