Aluminium Sheet

Aluminium sheet is flat rolled aluminium from about 0.2 mm to 6 mm thick, supplied cut to standard or custom sizes from coil. The right sheet depends on three choices: the alloy series, the temper and the surface finish. Metline supplies every common sheet grade, from soft 1050 to aerospace 7075, in mill, coated, anodised and patterned finishes, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is aluminium sheet?

Sheet sits between foil and plate. ASTM B209 defines sheet as 0.006 – 0.249 in (about 0.15 – 6.3 mm) thick; European practice under EN 485 treats material up to 6 mm as sheet and strip. Thinner material is foil, thicker is plate. Sheet is cold rolled, which gives it a smoother surface and tighter thickness tolerance than hot-rolled plate, and it is cut into flat pieces from coil.

Because sheet is cold rolled, its strength is set either by the amount of cold work (the H tempers of 1xxx, 3xxx and 5xxx alloys) or by heat treatment (the T tempers of 2xxx, 6xxx and 7xxx alloys). That is why "aluminium sheet" can mean anything from a soft, deep-drawable 1050-O blank to a 7075-T6 aircraft skin five times stronger.

Most sheet is ordered in a handful of sizes, with 1220 × 2440 mm (4 × 8 ft) and 1250 × 2500 mm the most common worldwide. Coil-fed and cut-to-length lines also make any length to order, and slitting gives narrow strip. For definitions of each flat product, see our article on coil, strip, sheet and plate.

Choosing sheet in three steps

  • Alloy: strength, corrosion, weldability and formability needs
  • Temper: how much forming the sheet must survive versus the strength it must keep
  • Finish: mill, brushed, anodised, painted, embossed, perforated or mirror

Tempers

Sheet tempers: what the letters mean

O

Fully annealed: softest and most formable. Used for deep drawing, spinning and severe bends.

H12 / H14 / H16 / H18

Strain-hardened only, from quarter-hard (H12) to full-hard (H18). H14 (half-hard) is the most common general sheet temper.

H22 / H24 / H26

Strain-hardened then partially annealed. Same strength as H1x but with better elongation, so they bend more easily.

H32 / H34

Strain-hardened and stabilised, used for magnesium alloys such as 5052 and 5005 to prevent age softening.

H111 / H116 / H321

Special 5xxx tempers; H116 and H321 are tested for resistance to exfoliation and intergranular corrosion in marine use.

T4 / T6

Heat-treated tempers for 2xxx, 6xxx and 7xxx sheet. T4 forms better; T6 gives full strength.

Tolerances & quality

Thickness tolerance, flatness and surface quality

Cold-rolled sheet is supplied to EN 485-4 or ASTM B209 (ANSI H35.2) tolerance tables. Thickness tolerance tightens for narrower and thinner sheet; flatness is specified as maximum deviation over a set length. Surface quality is agreed separately: decorative, anodising-quality and lithographic sheet carry stricter limits on streaks, roll marks and scratches than general engineering sheet.

Working with sheet

Forming, joining and finishing aluminium sheet

Bending

Minimum bend radius depends on alloy and temper: 1050-O and 3003-O bend flat on themselves, 5052-H32 typically to about 1 × thickness, 6061-T6 needs about 1.5 – 3 × thickness. Bend across the rolling direction where possible.

Deep drawing and spinning

Use 1xxx, 3003 or 5052 in O or H2x tempers. Earing and orange peel are controlled by grain size and texture.

Welding

1xxx, 3xxx, 5xxx and 6xxx sheet weld by TIG, MIG or laser. Thin sheet benefits from pulsed processes. 2024 and 7075 are normally riveted or bonded.

Cutting

Shearing, laser, waterjet and CNC routing all work; see our guide to cutting aluminium sheet.

Anodising

5005 and 1050 give the most consistent architectural anodised colour; 6061 anodises well for protection. Order anodising-quality sheet for visible parts.

Painting

Coil-coated PE or PVDF sheet avoids post-painting. Bare sheet needs a chromate-free conversion coating before powder coating.

Applications

Where aluminium sheet is used

  • Building: roofing, facades, composite panels, ceilings, flashing and gutters
  • Transport: truck and trailer bodies, bus panels, automotive closures, aircraft skins
  • Marine: boat hulls and superstructures in 5052 and 5083
  • Electrical and electronics: enclosures, heat spreaders, transformer windings, device casings
  • Signage and printing: road signs, number plates, lithographic plates
  • Food and household: cookware, appliance panels, packaging
  • Industrial: ducting, insulation cladding, tanks and machine guards

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) sheet: frequently asked questions

What thickness counts as aluminium sheet rather than plate?

Under ASTM B209, sheet is 0.006 – 0.249 in (about 0.15 – 6.3 mm) thick and plate is 0.250 in and above. European practice treats material up to 6 mm as sheet. Below about 0.2 mm the product is generally called foil.

Which aluminium sheet is best for bending?

3003-H14 and 5052-H32 are the usual choices for bent sheet-metal parts: they form well and hold their shape. For very tight bends use O or H2x tempers. 6061-T6 is stronger but needs larger bend radii.

How much does a 4 × 8 aluminium sheet weigh?

A 1220 × 2440 mm sheet weighs about 8.04 kg per millimetre of thickness. A 1 mm sheet is about 8.0 kg, a 2 mm sheet about 16.1 kg and a 3 mm sheet about 24.1 kg.

What is the most corrosion-resistant aluminium sheet?

For seawater, 5083 and 5052 (and 5086) are the standard marine grades. 1xxx and 3003 also resist general atmospheric corrosion very well. 2024 and 7075 need cladding or coating in corrosive service.

Is aluminium sheet gauge the same as steel gauge?

No. Aluminium sheet gauges follow the Brown & Sharpe (AWG) scale, while steel uses the Manufacturers' Standard Gauge. A 16 ga aluminium sheet is 1.29 mm, but 16 ga steel is 1.52 mm. Always order by decimal thickness.

Do you cut aluminium sheet to size?

Yes. Sheets can be supplied in standard sizes or cut to custom blanks by shearing, laser or waterjet, with mill certificates, and shipped worldwide.
