Aluminium Honeycomb Core

Aluminium honeycomb core is a lightweight cellular material made from thin aluminium foil bonded into hexagonal cells. Bonded between two skins it forms a sandwich panel that is very stiff for its weight. Metline supplies 3003, 5052 and 5056 honeycomb in standard cell sizes and densities, as expanded slices, unexpanded HOBE blocks or finished panels, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is aluminium honeycomb core?

A honeycomb sandwich works like an I-beam: two thin, strong skins carry the bending loads and the core holds them apart and carries shear between them. Doubling the core thickness makes a panel several times stiffer while adding only a little weight, which is why honeycomb panels are used for aircraft floors, train interiors, building facades, clean-room walls and machine guards.

Most aluminium honeycomb is made by the expansion process. Lines of adhesive are printed on aluminium foil, the sheets are stacked with the lines offset, and the stack is cured under heat and pressure into a solid block called HOBE (honeycomb before expansion). Slices are cut from the block and pulled open to form the hexagonal cells. Very high-density cores and cores for high-temperature use are made instead by corrugating the foil and bonding or brazing the corrugated sheets together.

Honeycomb is anisotropic. The L direction (ribbon direction) runs parallel to the continuous foil ribbons and the double-thickness bonded walls; the W direction is perpendicular to the ribbons, the direction in which the core was expanded; the T direction is the cell depth, i.e. the core thickness. Shear strength and stiffness are higher in L than in W, so the ribbon direction must be shown on the panel drawing.

Why choose aluminium honeycomb

  • Highest stiffness- and strength-to-weight of common core materials at moderate cost
  • Non-combustible metal core; does not absorb moisture like paper or some foams
  • Conducts heat and can be used as an airflow straightener, EMI vent or energy absorber
  • Fully recyclable, and compatible with aluminium skins for a single-material panel
How to specify

Reading a honeycomb core designation

Aluminium core is described by four numbers, in a form widely used in North American specifications, for example 3/16-5052-.001 (3.1):

Cell size3/16Diameter of a circle inscribed in the hexagon, in inches (3/16 in ≈ 4.8 mm)
Alloy5052Foil alloy: 3003, 5052 or 5056
Foil gauge.001Foil thickness in inches (0.001 in ≈ 25 µm)
Density(3.1)Nominal core density in lb/ft³ (3.1 pcf ≈ 50 kg/m³)

Cell size and foil gauge together fix the density: smaller cells or thicker foil give a denser, stronger core. Metric buyers often specify cell size in millimetres (for example 6 mm cell, 0.06 mm foil), and the supplier converts to the equivalent designation. Specify the slice thickness (T), the L × W size of the sheet, and whether the core is perforated for venting or has a corrosion-resistant coating.

For aerospace programmes, 5052 and 5056 cores are usually ordered with a corrosion-resistant treatment of the foil and to AMS-C-7438 (formerly MIL-C-7438). For architecture and transport, 3003 or 5052 is normally chosen with a lower density, since the facing skins rather than the core usually govern the design. Properties

Mechanical properties and how they are tested

Core properties are measured on the core itself, with standardised specimens, and are always tied to a density. The main values on a core data sheet are:

Compressive strength

Measured flatwise (in the T direction) to ASTM C365, either bare or "stabilised" with skins bonded on. Stabilised values are higher and are the ones used for panel design.

Shear strength and modulus

Measured in the L and W directions to ASTM C273. L-direction values are typically about one and a half to two times the W-direction values.

Crush strength

The plateau stress after initial buckling, used to design energy absorbers that crush at a near-constant force.

Flatwise tension

Tested on a bonded sandwich to ASTM C297; mainly checks the adhesive bond between skin and core.

Density

Measured to ASTM C271; all strength values scale steeply with density.

Panel properties

A sandwich panel must be tested as built (skins, adhesive and core together), for example in flexure to ASTM C393.

Core properties do not prove panel performance. The facing skins, adhesive, edge closures and inserts all fail in their own ways: face wrinkling, core shear, core crushing at supports, and skin-to-core disbonding. Each needs checking for the actual panel design and loads.

Applications

Applications of aluminium honeycomb

  • Aerospace: cabin floors, galley and lavatory panels, control surfaces, satellite structures (5052, 5056)
  • Rail and road transport: interior walls, floors, doors and truck-body panels
  • Marine: bulkheads, decks and furniture on yachts and fast craft, where weight saving raises speed or payload
  • Architecture: facade and cladding panels, ceilings and large flat doors that stay flat over wide spans
  • Clean rooms and equipment: wall panels, machine guards, optical tables and flat work surfaces
  • Airflow and EMI: flow straighteners in wind tunnels and HVAC, and shielded vent panels for electronics
  • Energy absorption: crash barriers and impact attenuators that crush at a controlled load

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) honeycomb core: frequently asked questions

What does 3/16-5052-.001 mean?

It is a honeycomb core with a 3/16 in (about 4.8 mm) cell size, made of 5052 foil 0.001 in (about 25 µm) thick. Its nominal density, often added in brackets, is 3.1 lb/ft³ (about 50 kg/m³).

Which is better, 3003 or 5052 honeycomb?

5052 is stronger and is the standard for aerospace and demanding industrial panels. 3003 costs less and is adequate for facades, partitions and airflow straighteners where the core is lightly loaded.

How do I calculate the weight of honeycomb core?

Multiply the core volume by its nominal density, not by the density of solid aluminium. A 1 m² slice 20 mm thick at 50 kg/m³ weighs about 1 kg. Add the skins and adhesive for a panel weight.

What are the L and W directions?

L (ribbon) runs along the continuous foil ribbons; W is the expansion direction at right angles to it. The core is stiffer and stronger in shear in the L direction, so it should be aligned with the main span of the panel.

Can aluminium honeycomb be curved?

Standard hexagonal core bends easily in one direction but forms a saddle shape when bent. For compound curves, use over-expanded or flexible cell configurations, or machine the core to contour.

Do you supply finished honeycomb panels?

Yes. Besides bare core, we supply aluminium-skinned sandwich panels bonded to size, with edge closures and inserts on request.
