Aluminium Tooling & Precision Plate

Aluminium tooling plate is plate made for dimensional stability rather than maximum strength: cast and stress-relieved, or rolled, stretched and precision milled, so that it stays flat when machined. It saves facing operations on jigs, fixtures, base plates and machine parts. Metline supplies cast tooling plate, precision-milled rolled plate and high-strength mould plate, cut to size, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What makes a plate "tooling plate"?

Ordinary rolled plate is supplied to standard thickness and flatness tolerances and still carries some residual stress, even after stretching. When a machinist removes a lot of metal from one side, those stresses rebalance and the part can bow. Tooling plate is designed to avoid that problem and to arrive already close to final thickness with a finished face.

There are two main routes. Cast tooling plate is cast as a thick slab, stress-relieved by heat treatment and then milled on both faces to a tight thickness tolerance; because it was never rolled or quenched, it has almost no residual stress and stays exceptionally flat. Precision rolled plate is standard rolled plate, usually 6061-T651, 7075-T651 or 5083, that has been fly-cut or milled on both faces to a precise thickness, combining a fine surface with the full strength of a wrought alloy.

A third family, mould plate, uses high-strength 7xxx alloys (7075, 7021, 7022 and similar) or 2xxx and 6xxx thick plate, rolled or forged in thick gauges for plastic-injection and blow moulds, where strength, polishability and thermal conductivity matter.

When tooling plate pays for itself

  • Large, thin or heavily pocketed parts that must stay flat after machining
  • Base plates and fixtures where facing both sides of a standard plate would take hours
  • Vacuum chucks, inspection plates and assembly jigs that need a reliable reference face
  • Short lead time: plate arrives ready to drill, tap and pocket

Tolerances

Thickness tolerance, flatness and surface finish

Tooling plate is bought on its tolerances. Typical values from producer data sheets for milled cast plate are a thickness tolerance of about ±0.1 mm across most gauges, and flatness of roughly 0.4 mm for thin plate (under about 12.7 mm), tightening to about 0.13 mm for thicker plate, measured over a defined length. Surface roughness on the milled faces is typically below about Ra 0.4 µm.

Machining

Machining tooling plate

Clamping

Clamp lightly and evenly; heavy clamping on a flat plate can induce distortion that springs back when released.

Balanced removal

Even on low-stress plate, remove material symmetrically where possible and finish in a final light pass.

Cast plate

Cuts freely with sharp carbide; chips are short and the plate does not move. Keep tools sharp to avoid smearing on the softer surface.

6061 and 7075

Produce a bright finish at high speeds; 7075 gives the best surface for precision features and threads.

Coolant

Flood or mist coolant controls heat; thermal growth is about 23 µm per metre per °C, which matters on large plates.

Protection

Leave the protective film on until final machining to avoid scratching reference faces.

Applications

Where tooling and precision plate is used

  • Jigs and fixtures: drilling, welding, assembly and inspection fixtures
  • Machine building: base plates, gantry plates, linear-axis carriages, covers
  • Vacuum and semiconductor: vacuum chucks, wafer-handling plates, chamber lids
  • Metrology: inspection plates, gauge plates and CMM fixtures
  • Moulds: blow moulds, vacuum-forming tools, prototype and short-run injection moulds
  • Packaging and printing machinery: plates that must stay flat across wide machines
  • Electronics: test fixtures, heat-spreader plates and enclosure bases

How to order tooling plate from Metline

  1. SpecifyPlate type (cast or precision rolled) and alloy, thickness, blank sizes, thickness and flatness tolerance with the measuring length, surface finish, quantity and any machining.
  2. QuoteWe confirm alloy, temper, tolerances, certification, packing and delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP) for your destination.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) tooling plate: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between cast tooling plate and 6061-T651?

Cast tooling plate is cast, stress-relieved and milled, so it has almost no residual stress and stays very flat when machined, but it is only about half as strong as 6061-T651 in yield. 6061-T651 is stronger and anodises better but can move more under heavy one-sided machining.

Is cast aluminium tooling plate weldable?

Cast 5083-type tooling plate can be welded, mainly for repairs and attachments, with 5xxx filler wire. Welding adds heat and stress, so finish-machine critical faces after welding.

How flat is aluminium tooling plate?

Producer data sheets for milled cast plate typically quote around 0.13 mm flatness for thicker plate and about 0.4 mm for thin plate, with thickness tolerance of about ±0.1 mm. State the required figures and measuring length on the order.

Can tooling plate be anodised?

Yes, but cast plate tends to give a less uniform, sometimes mottled anodised appearance. For decorative or colour-critical anodising, use precision-milled 6061-T651.

Which plate is best for injection moulds?

High-strength 7xxx plate such as 7075-T651 or dedicated 7021/7022-type mould plate offers the strength, hardness and polishability moulds need, with much better heat conduction than tool steel.

Do you cut tooling plate to size?

Yes. Tooling plate is supplied as full plates or saw-cut blanks, and can be CNC-profiled, drilled and tapped on request, with export packing.
