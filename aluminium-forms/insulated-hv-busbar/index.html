Insulated High-Voltage Aluminium Busbars

An insulated high-voltage aluminium busbar is a rigid aluminium conductor, formed to shape and covered with an insulating layer except at its contact faces. It carries high current in EV battery packs, inverters, energy storage and switchgear at about half the weight of copper for the same resistance. Overview

What is an insulated high-voltage aluminium busbar?

A busbar is a solid conductor that distributes current between components. In compact high-voltage systems, such as an electric vehicle pack running at 400 or 800 V, bare bars would need large air gaps to prevent flashover. Insulating the bar lets designers route conductors closely and safely, protects against accidental contact and keeps debris from bridging phases.

An insulated busbar is a system with three parts that must be specified together: the conductor (alloy, temper, cross-section and shape), the insulation (material, thickness, temperature class and dielectric rating), and the connections (exposed contact faces, plating, holes, welds or fasteners). A bar that fits the space is not yet a busbar; it also needs a thermal and electrical review for the current, voltage and environment it will see.

Aluminium is increasingly chosen for these parts because weight and cost matter in vehicles and large installations. For the same DC resistance an aluminium bar needs about 1.6 times the cross-section of copper, yet weighs only about half as much, and it costs considerably less per ampere carried. The penalty is a larger section and more careful joint design, because aluminium forms a hard oxide and creeps under sustained bolt load.

Where insulated aluminium busbars are used

  • EV and hybrid battery packs: module-to-module links and pack-to-inverter connections
  • Battery energy storage systems and solar inverters
  • Rail traction, charging stations and power conversion equipment
  • Low- and medium-voltage switchgear, distribution boards and data-centre power

Insulation systems

Insulation options and how to choose

Epoxy powder coating

Applied by electrostatic spray or fluidised bed and cured. Hard, adherent and conformal over bends, with high dielectric strength. Sharp edges coat thinner, so edge radii must be specified.

Polyamide (PA11 / PA12) coating

Thermoplastic coating applied by fluidised bed. Tough and flexible, resists cracking under vibration and thermal cycling, and tolerates later bending better than epoxy.

Heat-shrink tubing

Polyolefin or other sleeves shrunk over straight and gently bent bars. Low tooling cost and easy to change; less suited to complex 3D shapes and tight bends.

Laminated film

PET, PI or similar films laminated around flat conductors, often in stacked laminated busbars that cut inductance in inverters.

Over-moulded or extruded sleeves

Silicone, TPE or other polymers moulded or extruded onto the bar. Thick, robust insulation for high voltage and harsh environments.

Partial coverage

Insulation only where clearance is tight, with bare sections elsewhere. Saves cost but needs careful creepage and clearance design.

Select insulation on four properties: dielectric withstand at the system voltage with margin, continuous temperature class above the bar's hot-spot temperature, flame rating (often UL 94 V-0 for battery packs), and mechanical durability against vibration, abrasion and thermal cycling. Ask for the tracking index (CTI) where the bar sits in a humid or contaminated environment.

Aluminium needs particular attention at joints. Clean and abrade the contact surface, use a contact compound or plating, and keep bolt preload steady with spring or Belleville washers so creep does not loosen the joint. Contact faces are often tin- or nickel-plated, or a copper–aluminium bimetallic insert is used where the bar meets a copper terminal. In battery packs, aluminium busbars are frequently laser or ultrasonically welded to cell tabs instead of bolted.

Manufacturing route

From conductor bar to insulated part

  1. Conductor stockExtruded or rolled flat bar, or strip slit from coil, in the specified alloy and temper with conductivity certified.
  2. Cut and pierceBlanks are cut, punched or CNC machined for holes and slots, and deburred.
  3. FormBars are bent on CNC benders to the 3D routing; edge radii are kept generous so insulation covers evenly.
  4. Prepare surfacesContact faces are masked or plated; the rest of the bar is cleaned and pretreated for coating adhesion.
  5. InsulatePowder coating, fluidised-bed PA, heat-shrink, lamination or over-moulding, then curing.
  6. TestCoating thickness, adhesion, dielectric withstand and insulation resistance on each part or batch, with dimensional checks against the drawing.

  1. Specify3D model or drawing with conductor section and routing, alloy and temper, minimum conductivity, exposed contact areas and their finish, insulation type and thickness, system voltage, test voltage, temperature class, flame rating and quantity.

FAQ

Insulated aluminium (aluminum) busbars: FAQ

Why use aluminium instead of copper for EV busbars?

Weight and cost. For the same resistance an aluminium bar needs about 1.6 times the cross-section of copper but weighs about half as much and costs less. Where space allows the larger section, aluminium reduces pack mass and material cost.

Which aluminium alloy is best for busbars?

1350 or 1060 for maximum conductivity (about 61 – 62 % IACS) and easy forming. 6101 in T6 or T61 is used where the bar must be stiffer or stronger, accepting conductivity of about 55 – 57 % IACS.

Is epoxy or PA coating better for busbar insulation?

Epoxy powder gives high dielectric strength and a hard finish and is the common choice for rigid bars. Polyamide (PA11/PA12) is tougher and more flexible, so it resists cracking from vibration, thermal cycling or later bending. The right choice depends on voltage, temperature and mechanical duty.

How are aluminium busbars connected to copper terminals?

With plated contact faces (tin or nickel), a contact compound, and bolted joints that use spring washers to hold preload. For permanent joints, bimetallic copper–aluminium inserts or friction-welded transitions avoid galvanic corrosion at the interface.

What tests are done on insulated busbars?

Typically dimensional inspection, insulation thickness, adhesion, dielectric withstand (hipot) and insulation resistance, with partial discharge testing for 800 V-class systems where specified. Conductor conductivity is reported on the material certificate.

Can Metline supply both conductor bar and finished insulated busbars?

Yes. We supply certified conductor bar and strip, and formed and insulated busbars made to your drawing, to customers worldwide.
