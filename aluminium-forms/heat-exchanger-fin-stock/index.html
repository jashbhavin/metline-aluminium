Aluminium Heat Exchanger Fin Stock

Aluminium fin stock is thin strip, usually 0.05 – 0.4 mm, that heat-exchanger makers stamp or roll into fins to carry heat from tubes into air. Mechanically expanded HVAC coils use 1100, 1200, 8011 or 3102 fin, often with a hydrophilic or anti-corrosion coating; brazed automotive and micro-channel exchangers use zinc-bearing 3003-type fin, bare or clad. Metline supplies slit fin stock to EN 683 and ASTM B209 for coil makers worldwide.

Overview

What is aluminium fin stock?

In an air-conditioner or radiator, most of the heat-transfer area is in the fins, not the tubes. Fins are thin, so a small change in gauge, temper or coating changes the coil's weight, cost and efficiency. Fin stock is therefore made to tight gauge, flatness and formability limits.

There are two main construction types. In mechanically expanded (tube-and-fin) coils, used in room air-conditioners, chillers and refrigeration, fins are stamped with collars on a high-speed press, stacked, and copper or aluminium tubes are pushed through and expanded to grip them. The fin must stretch into tall collars without cracking, so formability and a consistent temper matter most. In brazed exchangers, used in car radiators, condensers, charge-air coolers and micro-channel coils, corrugated fins are brazed to flat tubes in a furnace at around 600 °C. The fin must keep its shape at brazing temperature (sag resistance), have good strength after brazing, and be slightly anodic to the tube so it corrodes first and protects it.

Choosing fin stock is a balance between thermal conductivity, strength, formability, corrosion behaviour and cost. Pure 1xxx alloys conduct best; 3xxx and 8xxx alloys are stronger; zinc additions make fins sacrificial.

What fin stock buyers specify

  • Alloy, temper and gauge, with gauge tolerance and flatness
  • Width of slit coils, core ID and coil OD to suit the fin press or fin mill
  • Elongation or collar-height requirement for stamped fins
  • Coating type and weight for hydrophilic or anti-corrosion fins
  • Post-braze strength, sag and corrosion potential for brazed fins

Tempers matter more than usual. H22, H24 and H26 fins are strain hardened and partly annealed, giving a combination of strength and elongation that supports high collar drawing. Full-soft O temper is used for very deep collars or slit-and-louvre designs. For brazed fins, H14 or H24 starting tempers are chosen to give the right recrystallised grain structure and sag resistance after the braze cycle.

Hydrophilic coating

A thin water-loving layer (often blue or clear) that spreads condensate into a film so it drains instead of bridging between fins. Improves airflow, reduces noise and odour on evaporators.

Anti-corrosion (gold epoxy)

Epoxy or polyester coating for coastal, industrial or chemical atmospheres, often combined with a hydrophilic top layer.

Bare (mill finish)

Used for condensers and dry coils, and for all brazed fins where coatings would burn off.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) fin stock: frequently asked questions

Which aluminium alloy is used for air-conditioner fins?

8011 and 1100 (or 1200) in H22, H24 or H26 temper are the most common for stamped AC fins, typically 0.09 – 0.15 mm thick, often with hydrophilic coating on evaporators.

What does hydrophilic fin coating do?

It makes condensed water spread into a thin film and drain away instead of forming droplets that bridge the fin gaps. That keeps airflow and heat transfer high and reduces noise, odour and water carry-over.

Why do automotive fins contain zinc?

Zinc makes the fin electrochemically more active than the tube, so the fin corrodes preferentially and protects the thin tube wall from pitting leaks.

What is sag resistance?

During brazing at around 600 °C, thin fins can soften and droop. Sag-resistant fin alloys and tempers keep their shape so fins stay bonded to the tubes at the right pitch.

What thickness range does EN 683 cover?

EN 683 covers wrought aluminium finstock from 60 µm to 400 µm thick. Coated strip is outside its scope and is specified separately.
