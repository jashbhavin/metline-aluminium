Aluminium Coil

Aluminium coil is rolled sheet or strip wound on itself, the form in which most flat aluminium leaves the mill and the starting stock for roll forming, stamping, cladding, cans, fins and slit strip. Common grades are 1050, 1100, 3003, 3105, 5052, 5754, 6061 and 8011, in O, H1x and H2x tempers. Metline supplies mill-finish, coated and slit coil to ASTM B209 and EN 485 for customers worldwide.

Overview

What is aluminium coil?

A rolling mill produces aluminium as a long continuous strip. Winding that strip into a coil is the cheapest way to store, ship and feed it, which is why manufacturers who process large volumes of sheet buy coil and cut, slit or form it on their own lines.

Coil starts either as a direct-chill (DC) cast slab that is hot rolled and then cold rolled, or as continuously cast (CC) strip that goes straight to cold rolling. DC material has a finer, more uniform structure and is preferred for deep drawing, anodising and critical surfaces; CC material is economical for general-purpose sheet, foil stock and fin stock. Cold rolling sets the final gauge and the strain-hardened temper; an anneal produces soft O temper or a partially annealed H2x temper.

The word coil is used for any wound flat product from a few hundred millimetres to around two metres wide. Narrow coils produced by slitting a wide master coil are often called strip. Very thin gauges below about 0.2 mm overlap with foil.

What to state when ordering coil

  • Alloy, temper and governing standard (for example 3003-H14 to ASTM B209)
  • Thickness, width and tolerances; slit edge or mill edge
  • Inner diameter of the core, maximum outer diameter or coil weight, and whether a core is required
  • Surface: mill finish, brushed, embossed, pre-painted (PE, PVDF) or laminated
  • Winding direction, eye to wall or eye to sky packing, and interleaving

In H temper codes the first digit tells you how the strength was reached (1 = strain-hardened only, 2 = strain-hardened and partly annealed, 3 = stabilised) and the second digit how hard it is (2 = quarter hard, 4 = half hard, 6 = three-quarter hard, 8 = full hard). An H24 coil has roughly the strength of H14 but better elongation, so it is chosen when parts are formed after cutting.

Cold-rolled coil under EN 485-4 has thickness tolerances that tighten with gauge; for example, coil around 1 mm thick and up to 1250 mm wide is typically held to a few hundredths of a millimetre. Where strip feeds a progressive die or roll former, also specify flatness, edge camber and crossbow, since these cause more line stoppages than thickness variation.

Processing

Slitting, cut-to-length and coating

Slitting

Master coils are slit into narrow strips with clean, burr-controlled edges and wound onto the core size your line uses.

Cut to length

Coil is levelled and sheared into flat sheets; levelling improves flatness and relieves coil set.

Pre-painting

Polyester (PE) and PVDF coatings are applied on continuous coil-coating lines for cladding, roofing and ceilings.

Embossing

Stucco and diamond patterns add stiffness and hide scratches on insulation jacketing and panels.

Film protection

PE or PVC film protects the surface during forming and handling.

Annealing

Coil can be supplied fully soft (O) or partially annealed (H2x) to match the forming operation.

Applications

Where aluminium coil is used

  • Building: roofing, cladding, ceilings, composite panel skins, gutters and rainwater goods
  • Transport: truck and trailer bodies, automotive heat shields and inner panels
  • HVAC and thermal: fin stock, insulation jacketing, duct and heat-exchanger parts
  • Packaging: can body and end stock, closures, foil stock, container material
  • Electrical: transformer winding strip, cable wrap and shielding
  • Consumer goods: cookware, lighting reflectors, appliance panels

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) coil: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between aluminium coil and strip?

Both are wound flat products. "Coil" usually means a full-width master coil from the mill, while "strip" means a narrower coil slit from it. Standards such as EN 485 treat them together.

How do I calculate the weight of an aluminium coil?

Weight = π/4 × (OD² − ID²) × width × 2,710 kg/m³, with all dimensions in metres. A 1000 mm wide coil wound from 508 mm to 1200 mm OD weighs about 2,515 kg.

Which coil grade is best for roofing and cladding?

3003 and 3105 in H14, H16, H24 or H26 temper are the usual choices, often pre-painted with PE or PVDF. In coastal areas 5052 or 5754 offers better corrosion resistance.

What is the standard inner diameter of aluminium coil?

505 mm or 508 mm is most common for wide coil. Slit strip is also wound on 150, 200, 300 or 400 mm cores, and 610 mm is used on some lines.

What is the difference between H14 and H24 temper?

H14 is cold rolled to half-hard. H24 is rolled harder and then partially annealed back to half-hard, giving similar strength with better ductility for bending.

Can you supply slit coil to exact widths?

Yes. Coil can be slit to the widths you need, wound on your core size and packed to your coil weight limit, with a mill certificate for every shipment.
