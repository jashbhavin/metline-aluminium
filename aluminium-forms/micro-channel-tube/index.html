Micro-Channel Aluminium Tube

Micro-channel aluminium tube, also called multi-port extruded (MPE) or flat multi-port tube, is a thin, flat extrusion with a row of small parallel channels for refrigerant or coolant. Brazed to louvred fins and headers, it forms compact all-aluminium condensers, evaporators, heat pumps and battery cooling plates. Metline supplies micro-channel tube to drawing, bare, zinc-coated or flux-coated, in coils or cut lengths, worldwide.

Overview

What is micro-channel aluminium tube?

A micro-channel tube looks like a thin flat strip, but inside it carries a row of small ports separated by thin webs. The ports give a large internal surface for the fluid, the webs hold the tube together under pressure, and the flat outer faces give a broad, flat contact for brazed fins. Hydraulic diameters are typically around one millimetre or less, which is where the name comes from.

Micro-channel heat exchangers (MCHX) were first adopted for automotive air-conditioning condensers and are now common in residential and commercial HVAC, heat pumps, chillers, refrigerated transport and electric-vehicle thermal systems. Compared with round-tube plate-fin coils, they hold much less refrigerant, weigh less, take less depth and are made from a single metal, which simplifies recycling and removes galvanic corrosion between tube and fin.

Most micro-channel tube is extruded from rod or billet, then coated and coiled in one continuous line. A folded alternative is made from clad sheet; it uses a different route and is compared in our folded vs extruded micro-channel article. This page covers extruded multi-port tube.

What makes micro-channel tube different

  • Small ports give high refrigerant-side heat transfer and low internal volume
  • Thin webs carry pressure, allowing high burst strength with a thin wall
  • Flat faces give full-length brazed contact with louvred fins
  • All-aluminium coil: lower weight and charge, no copper–aluminium couple
Tube geometry

Geometry parameters to specify

Micro-channel tube is always made to a drawing, because every heat exchanger design has its own port layout. The outer envelope sets fin and header fit; the port geometry sets heat transfer, pressure drop and burst strength. Treat them as two separate sets of requirements.

Alloys & coatings

Alloys and surface coatings

The tube alloy must extrude into very thin webs at high speed, survive brazing at about 600 °C and resist corrosion in service. Lean manganese 3102 and commercially pure 1100 or 1050 extrude most easily; long-life alloys add controlled amounts of manganese, copper or zinc to make the tube more noble than the fins and slow pitting.

Zinc arc spray

A thin layer of zinc is sprayed onto the flat faces in-line, commonly a few g/m² up to about 10 g/m². During brazing it diffuses into the surface, making the outer skin sacrificial, so corrosion spreads sideways rather than through the wall.

Flux pre-coat

A non-corrosive potassium fluoroaluminate flux, sometimes mixed with silicon or filler powder, is applied to the tube. It removes the need to flux the core before brazing and can supply filler metal for tube-to-fin joints.

Bare tube

Uncoated tube for designs where fins and headers carry the filler and the coil is fluxed after assembly, or where the environment is benign.

Heat exchanger assembly

How micro-channel tube is used in a brazed coil

  1. Cut and stackTubes are cut to length and stacked with louvred fins made from clad fin stock (an Al-Si filler skin on a 3xxx core).
  2. Insert into headersTube ends enter slots in clad header tubes; side plates and fittings complete the core.
  3. FluxThe core is fluxed, unless pre-coated tube or fins already carry flux.
  4. BrazeControlled atmosphere brazing in nitrogen at about 590 – 610 °C melts the Al-Si cladding, which flows by capillary action to form every tube-fin and tube-header joint.
  5. TestFinished coils are leak tested (helium or pressure decay) and sampled for burst pressure.

Because brazing anneals the tube, design strength is the post-braze (O-temper equivalent) strength, not the as-extruded value. Burst pressure, fatigue under pressure cycling and resistance to port collapse should all be checked on brazed samples.

Inspection

Tolerances, testing and acceptance

Typical inspection plan

Outer dimensions
Width, height, flatness, camber and twist, measured continuously or by frequent sampling
Port geometry
Sectioning and optical measurement of ports, webs and walls
Coating weight
Zinc or flux load in g/m², measured by stripping or gravimetric method
Burst and pressure tests
Burst pressure on samples, before and after a simulated braze cycle
Corrosion tests
Acidified salt spray (SWAAT, ASTM G85 Annex A3) or customer-specific tests on brazed samples
Cleanliness
Internal residue and particle limits, with tubes capped or coiled to keep ports clean

Agree which dimensions are production acceptance limits and which are design targets still being developed, especially for a new die.

When a substitute tube is offered, approve samples on your real brazing line before accepting it. Two tubes with the same envelope can behave very differently in brazing, burst and corrosion if the port layout, alloy or coating differ.

Applications

Where micro-channel tube is used

  • Automotive air-conditioning: condensers, evaporators and gas coolers for R1234yf and R744 systems
  • Residential and commercial HVAC: outdoor condenser coils and heat pump coils
  • Electric vehicles: battery cooling plates, chiller and heat pump modules
  • Refrigeration: display cabinets, transport refrigeration and chillers
  • Data centres and electronics: liquid-to-air coolers and rear-door heat exchangers
  • Industrial: oil coolers, charge-air coolers and compact process heat exchangers

FAQ

Micro-channel aluminium (aluminum) tube: FAQ

What alloy is micro-channel tube made from?

Most commonly 3102, 1100 or 1050, which extrude easily into thin webs, plus long-life 3xxx alloys designed to resist pitting. The alloy is chosen together with the fin and header alloys so that the tube stays more noble than the fins.

Why is micro-channel tube zinc-coated?

Zinc sprayed on the flat faces diffuses into the surface during brazing and makes it sacrificial. Corrosion then spreads sideways along the surface instead of pitting through the thin wall, which extends coil life in coastal and polluted air.

What is the difference between extruded and folded micro-channel tube?

Extruded tube is pushed through a die from a single alloy, with ports formed by the die. Folded tube is formed and brazed from clad sheet. Extruded tube dominates HVAC; folded tube is used in some automotive and high-performance designs.

How is micro-channel tube joined?

By controlled atmosphere brazing in nitrogen at about 590 – 610 °C with a non-corrosive flux. The Al-Si cladding on fins and headers melts and forms all joints in a single furnace pass.

Can micro-channel tube handle CO2 (R744) pressures?

Yes, with designs made for it: smaller, rounder ports, thicker webs and validated burst performance. Standard HVAC tube is not automatically suitable; ask for a design and burst test on brazed samples.

Do you supply micro-channel tube in coils?

Yes. Metline supplies micro-channel tube in coils for in-line cutting or as cut lengths, bare or coated, with test records, to customers worldwide.
