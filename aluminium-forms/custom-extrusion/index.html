Custom Aluminium Extrusions

A custom aluminium extrusion is a profile made through a die cut specifically for your cross-section, so the part arrives with its ribs, slots, screw ports and hollows already formed. Most custom profiles are extruded in 6063 or 6060 for fine detail and finish, or 6061, 6082 and 6005A for structural strength. Metline manages the whole route from drawing review and die to finishing, machining and certified delivery for customers worldwide.

Overview

Why use a custom aluminium extrusion?

Extrusion pushes a heated aluminium billet through a shaped steel die, much like toothpaste from a tube. Because the die is relatively inexpensive compared with casting or forging tools, it is economical to design a cross-section that does exactly what your product needs instead of building it from standard bars, angles and tubes.

A well-designed profile can combine several parts into one: a heat sink with integral mounting slots, a window frame with thermal-break grooves, a battery tray side member with cooling channels, or a machine frame with T-slots and cable ducts. Fewer parts means fewer fasteners, less machining and fewer assembly errors. Material is placed only where it carries load or heat, so custom profiles are often lighter than the standard sections they replace.

The trade-off is the up-front die and sampling effort. Custom extrusion pays back when the profile is used repeatedly, when it removes machining or assembly steps, or when no standard section gives the required function.

Typical benefits of a custom profile

  • Part consolidation: snap fits, hinges, screw bosses and channels in one piece
  • Lower weight through material placed only where needed
  • Less machining, because features are extruded in, not cut out
  • Consistent cross-section over any length, ready for cutting, punching and finishing
Alloy selection

Choosing the alloy and temper

The alloy controls how fast and how thin a profile can be extruded, how it looks after finishing and how strong it is. Softer alloys extrude faster and fill fine detail; stronger alloys need more press pressure, slower speeds and thicker walls.

Design for extrusion

Design rules that keep cost and risk down

Keep walls uniform

Large differences in wall thickness make metal flow unevenly through the die, causing distortion and surface streaks. Try to keep the thinnest wall at least half the thickness of the thickest.

Mind the circumscribing circle

The smallest circle that encloses the profile (CCD) decides which press can make it. Larger CCDs need bigger presses and allow fewer die openings, so cost rises with size.

Respect minimum walls

For 6063 a wall around 1 mm is possible on small profiles; walls of 1.5 – 3 mm are more practical. Stronger alloys and larger profiles need thicker walls.

Avoid deep narrow slots

A slot much deeper than it is wide leaves a weak tongue of die steel that can deflect or break. Keep the depth-to-gap ratio moderate, or make the feature semi-hollow by agreement.

Radius every corner

Sharp inside corners concentrate stress in the die and the part. Inside radii of 0.5 – 1 mm help metal flow and improve anodised appearance.

Choose solid before hollow

Hollow profiles need porthole or bridge dies with welding seams, which cost more and may need checking for seam quality. Use a hollow only where torsional stiffness or a closed channel is really needed.

Mark decorative faces

Show which surfaces are visible so the die can be oriented to keep die lines and handling marks on hidden faces.

Add identification features

Small grooves or ribs help assemblers orient the part and can mark which side is up after anodising.

Specify only critical tolerances

Standard tolerances suit most features; tighten only the dimensions that mate with other parts.

From drawing to delivery

How a custom extrusion project runs

  1. Drawing reviewWe check the section for extrudability, alloy choice, wall ratios, tolerances and finish, and suggest changes that reduce die risk or cost.
  2. Die design and manufactureA solid, semi-hollow or porthole die is designed for the agreed press and made from hot-work tool steel such as H13.
  3. Trial and first-articleSample lengths are extruded, aged and measured against the drawing; a first-article inspection report is issued for approval.
  4. ProductionApproved profiles are extruded, quenched, stretched to straighten, cut and aged to the specified temper.
  5. Finishing and fabricationAnodising, powder coating, thermal-break assembly, cutting, CNC machining, punching or bending as required.
  6. Inspection and dispatchDimensional, mechanical and finish checks, with a 3.1 certificate, then export packing and shipment.

Tolerances in EN 755-9 and ANSI H35.2 depend on the alloy group, the size of the dimension, whether it crosses a hollow, and the CCD. Precision profiles under EN 12020-2 are held closer and are the normal choice for window and door systems. Straightness, twist, flatness and angularity are specified separately from cross-section dimensions; tell us if the profile must slide in a mating part or seal against glass, so the critical features can be measured at every die change.

Finishing and fabrication

Finishing and secondary operations

Anodising

Clear, bronze, black and colour anodising; hard anodising for wear. 6060 and 6063 give the most uniform appearance.

Powder coating

Any RAL colour, architectural or industrial grades, with masking where needed.

Mechanical finishes

Brushing, polishing and bead blasting before anodising for a satin or bright surface.

Machining

Drilling, milling, tapping and end machining on CNC centres to drawing.

Cutting and punching

Precision saw cutting to length, notching and punching of holes and slots.

Bending and assembly

Stretch or roll bending, thermal-break crimping, and assembly with fittings.

Applications

Where custom extrusions are used

  • Building: curtain walls, windows, doors, louvres, handrails and partitions
  • Electrical and electronics: heat sinks, LED housings, enclosures, busbar and cable trunking
  • Transport and EV: battery tray members, crash rails, roof rails, rail car and bus body sections
  • Renewables: solar mounting rails, clamps and frame profiles
  • Industrial: machine frames, conveyor profiles, pneumatic cylinder barrels, T-slot systems
  • Consumer goods: furniture, display systems, appliance trims, sports equipment

FAQ

Custom aluminium (aluminum) extrusions: FAQ

Who owns the extrusion die?

The die is normally paid for by the customer and dedicated to their profile, though it remains at the extrusion plant. Ownership and replacement terms should be stated in the quotation.

What is the thinnest wall that can be extruded?

In 6063 on small profiles, walls around 1 mm are possible, but 1.5 – 3 mm is more economical and more consistent. The minimum rises with profile size and with stronger alloys such as 6061 and 6082.

6063 or 6061 for a custom extrusion?

Choose 6063 for complex shapes, thin walls and the best anodised appearance. Choose 6061 or 6082 when the profile carries significant load and needs roughly 40 – 50 % more yield strength.

What file should I send for a custom extrusion quote?

A dimensioned 2D drawing (DXF, DWG or PDF) of the cross-section, plus a 3D model if the part is machined. Include alloy, temper, finish, length, critical tolerances and expected annual volume.

Can custom profiles be machined and anodised before delivery?

Yes. Profiles can be cut, CNC machined, punched, bent and anodised or powder coated, then packed ready for assembly.

Which tolerance standard applies to custom extrusions?

EN 755-9 or ANSI H35.2 for general profiles, and EN 12020-2 for precision 6060/6063 profiles, unless tighter limits are agreed on the drawing.
