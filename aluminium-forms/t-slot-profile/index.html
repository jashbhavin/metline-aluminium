T-Slot Aluminium Framing Profiles

T-slot aluminium profiles are extruded framing sections with undercut grooves on each face, so frames can be bolted together with T-nuts and brackets instead of welding. They are a system: profile series, slot size, fasteners and connectors must all match. Metline supplies T-slot profiles in metric and imperial series, cut to length and machined for connectors, with accessories, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What are T-slot aluminium profiles?

A T-slot profile is a hollow or semi-hollow extrusion, usually square or rectangular in outline, with a T-shaped groove running the full length of one or more faces. Fasteners slide or drop into the groove and clamp brackets, panels or other profiles anywhere along it. The result is a structure that can be designed in hours, assembled with hand tools, and later moved, extended or rebuilt.

Because joints are bolted, there is no welding distortion, no painting after assembly and no heat damage to the anodised finish. That makes T-slot framing the default choice for machine guards, workbenches, automation cells, test rigs, cleanroom equipment, displays and many custom structures. For long production runs of a single frame, a welded or dedicated extrusion may cost less; for anything that changes, T-slot usually wins.

The key point for buyers is compatibility. Profiles from different makers can share an outside size but differ in slot opening, groove depth, wall layout and core hole, so their nuts and connectors may not fit each other. Always name the system and profile reference, or send a cross-section drawing.

What to specify

  • Series and profile: outside size (e.g. 40 × 40, 40 × 80) and light, standard or heavy section
  • Slot size: 6, 8 or 10 mm metric, or 10 / 15 series imperial
  • Length, cut tolerance and any end tapping, counterboring or access holes
  • Finish: clear anodised, black anodised or mill
  • Fasteners, brackets and accessories that match the system

Material

Alloy, temper and mechanical properties

T-slot profiles are almost always extruded from 6063 or the similar European grade 6060, aged to T5 or T6. These alloys flow easily through complex dies, give sharp slot detail and produce a smooth surface that anodises evenly. Where higher strength is needed, 6061-T6 or 6005A-T6 is used for heavy profiles.

Design

Designing a T-slot frame: stiffness, deflection and joints

T-slot frames are usually limited by deflection, not by strength. Aluminium has about one third of the stiffness of steel, so a beam that is strong enough may still sag or vibrate too much. Deflection of a simply supported beam under a central load is F·L³ / (48·E·I): doubling the span increases deflection eightfold, while switching from a 40 × 40 to a 40 × 80 profile on edge raises the moment of inertia roughly five- to sixfold.

Pick the profile for stiffness

Size beams from the maker's Ix and Iy values and a deflection limit, commonly L/500 to L/1000 for machine frames.

Choose joints by load

Angle brackets are quick and adjustable; internal anchor or end connectors give clean, stiff joints; gusset plates carry the highest moments.

Add bracing

Diagonal braces or panels turn a flexible rectangle into a rigid frame, often more effectively than a heavier profile.

Mind the fastener

Slot nuts carry a rated pull-out load; heavy or vibrating loads need more nuts, longer brackets or through-bolting.

Allow for vibration

Dynamic machines may need heavier profiles, filled cores or damping feet to avoid resonance.

Plan cable and air routing

Core holes and slot covers can carry cables and compressed air neatly inside the frame.

Quality

Tolerances, machining and finish

Precision T-slot profiles are typically extruded to tighter-than-standard tolerances for outside size, twist and straightness, because linear guides and square frames depend on them. EN 12020-2 (precision 6060/6063 profiles) and EN 755-9 (general profiles) give the reference limits; system makers often hold tighter internal values for straightness and twist per metre.

  • Cutting: saw cut to length, commonly within ±0.2 – 0.5 mm, with square ends for butt joints
  • End machining: tapping of the core hole, counterbores for anchor fasteners, access holes for connectors
  • Anodising: clear (natural) or black anodising gives corrosion and wear resistance and a consistent appearance
  • Inspection: dimensional check of profile, length and squareness; EN 10204 3.1 material certificate on request
Applications

Where T-slot aluminium framing is used

  • Machine building: machine frames, guarding, safety fencing and enclosures
  • Automation: robot cells, pick-and-place gantries, linear-motion systems and conveyor frames
  • Workplace: lean workstations, assembly benches, carts and material racks
  • Laboratories and cleanrooms: test rigs, optical tables, laminar-flow hood frames
  • 3D printing and CNC: printer frames, router gantries and small machine bases
  • Retail and events: displays, exhibition stands and signage frames
  • Renewables and electronics: test fixtures, server racks and solar test structures

FAQ

T-slot aluminium (aluminum) profiles: frequently asked questions

Are T-slot profiles from different brands compatible?

Not reliably. Two profiles with the same outside size can have different slot openings, groove depths and core holes, so nuts and connectors may not fit. Stay within one system, or confirm the cross-section before mixing parts.

What alloy are T-slot profiles made from?

Usually 6063 or 6060 in T5 or T6 temper, which extrudes sharp slot detail and anodises well. Heavy profiles may use 6061-T6 or 6005A-T6 for extra strength.

What slot size goes with a 40 × 40 profile?

Most 40 mm systems use an 8 mm slot; some use 10 mm. Check the profile drawing before ordering nuts, bolts and brackets.

How do I choose the right profile size for my frame?

Start from the load and the allowable deflection, then use the maker's moment of inertia to calculate sag. In most frames, stiffness rather than strength decides the size. Bracing and deeper profiles (40 × 80 instead of 40 × 40) are the most efficient ways to stiffen a frame.

Can T-slot profiles be cut and machined to length?

Yes. Profiles are supplied in full lengths or cut to a cut list, with end tapping, counterbores and access holes for hidden connectors.

Is anodising necessary on T-slot extrusions?

It is standard because it protects the surface, resists wear from sliding nuts and gives a uniform look. Mill-finish profiles are cheaper but mark and oxidise more easily.
