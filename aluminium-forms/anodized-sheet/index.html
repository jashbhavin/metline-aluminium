Anodised Aluminium Sheet

Anodised aluminium sheet carries a hard, transparent layer of aluminium oxide grown electrochemically from the metal itself, which protects it against corrosion and wear and can be coloured. It is not a paint: the film is part of the surface and will not peel. Metline supplies pre-anodised sheet and coil and anodising-quality sheet in 1050, 1100, 5005, 5052 and 6061, with coating thickness and colour to order, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is anodised aluminium sheet?

In anodising, the sheet is made the anode in an acid electrolyte, usually sulphuric acid. Current converts the surface aluminium into a layer of aluminium oxide that grows partly into and partly out from the original surface. The film is made of millions of fine hexagonal pores, which can be filled with dye or metal salts for colour and are then closed by sealing.

The result is a surface far harder than the base metal, with excellent resistance to weathering and handling marks, a metallic look that paint cannot match, and no flaking because the coating is not a separate layer. Anodised sheet is used for building facades, signage, lighting reflectors, appliance and electronics panels, nameplates, interior trim and anywhere a durable metallic finish is wanted.

Sheet can be anodised in two ways. Coil (continuous) anodising runs the coil through the anodising line at speed, which gives very consistent colour from sheet to sheet and is economical for large areas; the sheet is then cut and formed after anodising. Batch anodising treats cut sheets or finished parts on racks, so it is used when parts are fabricated first or when thicker or hard coatings are needed.

Before you order, decide

  • Pre-anodised sheet (anodise first, then form) or anodising-quality sheet that you anodise after fabrication
  • Coating thickness class, matched to indoor, outdoor or marine exposure
  • Colour and gloss, approved on a physical sample under agreed lighting
  • Which face is decorative, and whether both faces need the same finish

Which alloys anodise best?

The alloy controls how clear, uniform and bright the anodic film looks. Elements such as silicon, copper and iron form particles that do not anodise, so they cloud or darken the film. For decorative work, order "anodising quality" (AQ) sheet, which is produced with tighter control of chemistry and grain size.

Coating thickness

Coating thickness classes and where to use them

Coating thickness is the main driver of durability. EN ISO 7599 designates classes by minimum average thickness in micrometres (AA 5, AA 10, AA 15, AA 20, AA 25). In North America, Aluminum Association architectural Class II is 10 – 18 µm (0.4 – 0.7 mil) and Class I is 18 µm (0.7 mil) and above. The table gives common guidance.

Class (ISO 7599)Minimum average thicknessTypical exposure
AA 55 µmIndoor decorative parts in dry conditions; often on coil-anodised sheet
AA 1010 µmIndoor use and parts that will be handled
AA 1515 µmOutdoor use in mild, rural environments
AA 2020 µmOutdoor use in urban and industrial environments

Hard anodic (Type III)Typically 25 – 75 µmWear surfaces, sliding parts, abrasion resistance

Military and aerospace orders often specify MIL-A-8625: Type II (sulphuric acid) and Type III (hard coat), with Class 1 undyed and Class 2 dyed. Thicker films are harder wearing but crack more easily when the sheet is bent.

Colour and sealing

Colouring and sealing

Clear (natural)

Undyed film that keeps the metallic look of the base sheet. The most durable choice outdoors.

Electrolytic colour

Tin or other metal salts deposited at the bottom of the pores give light-fast bronze, champagne and black shades suitable for facades.

Organic dyes

Bright colours such as red, blue and gold. Many dyes fade in sunlight, so they are mainly for interior use.

Brushed or bright

Mechanical brushing or chemical brightening before anodising gives a satin or mirror finish under the film.

Hot-water or nickel seal

Sealing closes the pores, locking in colour and maximising corrosion resistance. Seal quality is tested by acid dissolution (ISO 3210) or admittance (ISO 2931).

Batch matching

Colour varies slightly between batches and alloys. For large facades, order from one production run and approve limit samples.

Fabrication

Bending, cutting and joining anodised sheet

The anodic film is a ceramic: it is hard but cannot stretch. When pre-anodised sheet is bent, the film on the outside of the bend develops fine cracks ("crazing"). Thin films (5 – 10 µm) on soft tempers tolerate tight bends with little visible effect; thick architectural films need larger radii, and bends should run across the rolling direction where possible. Always trial-bend a sample in the actual coating before production.

  • Cutting: shear or saw with sharp tools; cut edges are bare aluminium and will dull over time outdoors
  • Drilling and punching: support the decorative face and keep protective film on to avoid scratches
  • Joining: rivets, screws, adhesive tapes and structural adhesives; welding destroys the finish locally
  • Electrical contact: the film is an insulator, so earth connections need the coating removed or a penetrating fastener
  • Cleaning: neutral detergent and water; avoid alkaline cleaners and abrasive pads, which attack the film

FAQ

Anodised (anodized) aluminium sheet: frequently asked questions

Can anodised aluminium sheet be bent?

Yes, but the oxide film cracks on the outside of the bend. Thin coatings on soft tempers bend well with little visible crazing; thick coatings need generous radii. Trial a sample, or fabricate first and anodise afterwards.

What coating thickness do I need outdoors?

At least 15 µm for mild exposure, 20 µm for urban and industrial sites, and 25 µm for coastal or marine locations, following EN ISO 7599 classes.

Which alloy is best for anodising?

1050 and 1100 give the brightest, clearest finish; 5005 is the architectural standard and matches 6063 extrusions; 5052 and 6061 are used where more strength is needed.

Does anodised aluminium fade?

Clear and electrolytically coloured finishes are very light-fast. Many organic dyes fade in sunlight, so dyed colours are best for interior use unless a light-fast dye is specified.

Is anodised sheet electrically conductive?

No. The anodic film is an electrical insulator. Remove the coating locally or use a penetrating fastener where an electrical or earth connection is needed.

What is the difference between anodising and powder coating?

Anodising converts the surface into oxide and keeps a metallic look; it cannot peel. Powder coating is a paint layer on top, available in any colour and gloss, but it can chip.
