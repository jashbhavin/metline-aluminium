Aluminium Round Bar

Aluminium round bar (round rod, or aluminum rod in the US) is a solid cylindrical section made by extrusion or by extrusion followed by cold drawing. It is the main stock for turned parts, shafts, pins, fittings and machined components. Metline supplies round bar in every common grade, from free-machining 2011 to high-strength 7075, cut to length and certified to ASTM, AMS and EN standards, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is aluminium round bar?

Round bar is the simplest long product in aluminium: a solid circle of constant diameter supplied in straight lengths, typically 3 to 6 metres. It is the default feedstock for CNC lathes and bar-fed turning centres, and is also used directly as shafts, rollers, pins, spacers and electrical conductors.

Two production routes dominate. Extruded bar is pushed hot through a round die and then heat-treated and stretched; it covers the widest diameter range and is the economical choice when the part will be machined all over. Cold-finished (drawn) bar is extruded slightly oversize and then drawn through a die at room temperature, which gives a tighter diameter tolerance, better roundness, a brighter surface and, in some tempers, higher strength. Very large diameters, above roughly 300 mm, are often made as forged or cast-and-homogenised bar.

Buyers sometimes treat a diameter as the whole specification. It is not: an extruded 6082-T6 bar and a drawn 6082-T6 bar of the same nominal size differ in tolerance, straightness and surface, and a 2011-T3 free-machining bar behaves completely differently on a lathe from a 6061-T6 bar. The grade, temper, production route and standard all belong on the order.

Specify round bar with five items

  • Alloy and temper, for example 6082-T6, 7075-T651 or 2011-T3
  • Diameter and the tolerance class or standard (extruded or drawn)
  • Length: random mill lengths, fixed lengths or cut pieces
  • Governing standard: ASTM B211, B221, AMS, EN 754 or EN 755
  • Certification, packing and any ultrasonic or special test requirement

Tempers

Round bar tempers explained

T6

Solution heat-treated and artificially aged. Standard temper for 6xxx and 7xxx bar where residual stress is not critical.

T651

As T6 but stretched about 1 – 3 % after quenching to relieve stress. Preferred for parts with heavy or asymmetric machining.

T6511

Stretched like T651 with minor straightening allowed afterwards; common on extruded 6061 and 7075 bar.

T3 / T351

Solution treated, cold worked and naturally aged. Typical for 2011 and 2024 bar; T351 is the stretched version.

T73 / T7351

Over-aged 7075 that trades some strength for much better resistance to stress-corrosion cracking.

O and H112

Annealed (O) for forming; H112 is the as-extruded condition for non-heat-treatable grades such as 5083.

Tolerances & inspection

Diameter tolerance, straightness and inspection

Extruded round bar is supplied to the tolerance tables of EN 755-3 or ASTM B221 (ANSI H35.2), which widen as diameter grows. Cold-drawn bar to EN 754-3 or ASTM B211 is markedly tighter and rounder, and precision-ground bar can be held tighter still. Always check that the machining allowance on your part covers both the diameter tolerance and any bow along the length.

Fabrication

Machining, welding and finishing round bar

Turning

2011-T3 gives the smallest chips and highest speeds; 7075-T6, 2024-T351 and 6061-T6 also machine very well with sharp, polished carbide. 6063 and 5xxx grades are softer and more prone to built-up edge.

Drilling and threading

Use high-helix drills and through-coolant where possible. Roll-formed threads in 6061 and 7075 have better fatigue performance than cut threads.

Welding

6061, 6082 and 5083 bar weld readily by TIG or MIG. 2011, 2024 and 7075 are generally not fusion welded in structural service.

Anodising

6061, 6063 and 6082 give the most even anodic finish. 2011 and 2024 anodise with a darker, less uniform appearance; 7075 hard-anodises well.

Corrosion

5xxx and 6xxx bar suit outdoor and marine use. 2xxx and 7xxx bar need anodising, paint or cladding in corrosive environments.

Stress relief

Choose stretched tempers (T651, T6511, T351) when large amounts of metal are removed from one side or tight post-machining straightness is needed.

Applications

Where aluminium round bar is used

  • Precision turned parts: connectors, valve bodies, nozzles, camera and optical parts, fasteners
  • Machinery: shafts, rollers, spindles, bushings, pneumatic pistons and end caps
  • Automotive and EV: brake and suspension components, sensor housings, battery connectors
  • Aerospace and defence: fittings, pins, actuator parts and hardware to AMS specifications
  • Marine: fittings, shackles and hardware in 5083, 6061 and 6082
  • Electrical: conductor rod and terminal stock in 1xxx and 6101 alloys
  • Consumer goods: bicycle and sports components, furniture and decorative rods

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) round bar: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between extruded and drawn aluminium round bar?

Extruded bar is formed hot through a die and covers a wide diameter range at lower cost. Drawn (cold-finished) bar is extruded and then pulled through a die cold, giving tighter diameter tolerance, better roundness and a brighter surface. Choose drawn bar for bar-fed lathes and parts that keep the bar surface.

Which aluminium round bar is easiest to machine?

2011-T3 is the classic free-machining grade, producing small broken chips at high speed. 7075-T6, 2024-T351 and 6061-T6 also machine very well. Lead-free alternatives such as 6026 are available where lead content is restricted.

How do I calculate the weight of aluminium round bar?

Multiply the diameter in millimetres squared by 0.0021206 to get kg per metre at 2.70 g/cm³. A 50 mm bar weighs about 5.30 kg/m, so a 3 m length weighs about 15.9 kg.

Is 6061 or 6082 better for round bar?

They are close substitutes. 6082 is the standard in Europe and holds strength slightly better in large diameters; 6061 is more common in the Americas and Asia and has a slightly better anodised appearance. Either suits most machined parts.

Can 7075 round bar be welded?

7075 is not considered fusion-weldable for structural use because it is prone to hot cracking and loses strength in the weld zone. Use mechanical fastening, or choose 6061 or 6082 if welding is required.

Do you supply aluminium round bar cut to length?

Yes. Round bar can be supplied in mill lengths or saw-cut to fixed lengths and pieces, with mill test certificates, and shipped worldwide.
