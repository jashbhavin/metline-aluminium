Coated Automotive Aluminium Tube

Coated automotive aluminium tube is precision drawn or extruded tube with a protective layer added for life under the vehicle: a PA12 nylon jacket for fuel, brake, cooling and air-conditioning lines, or a zinc or flux coating for tubes that are brazed into radiators and condensers. The aluminium core is usually 3003, 3102 or 5052. Metline supplies bare and coated tube to drawing, with full traceability, to vehicle and component makers worldwide.

Overview

What is coated automotive aluminium tube?

Steel brake and fuel lines have been the default for decades, but they are heavy and depend on zinc and polymer layers to survive road salt. An aluminium line weighs about one third as much for the same size, and when it is sheathed in nylon it resists stone chipping and corrosion as well as or better than coated steel.

The term covers two quite different products. Polymer-coated fluid tube is a seamless drawn aluminium tube with an extruded layer of polyamide 12 (PA12, also called nylon 12), sometimes with a conversion pretreatment underneath for adhesion. It is bent into routed lines for fuel, vapour, clutch, coolant, heater and air-conditioning circuits. Metallic or flux-coated heat-exchanger tube is extruded round or multi-port (micro-channel) tube with a thin sprayed zinc layer or a pre-applied brazing flux. During furnace brazing the zinc diffuses into the surface and forms a sacrificial layer that protects the core from pitting, and the flux removes oxide so the fins bond to the tube.

In both cases the coating and the metal work as one system. The coating gives impact and corrosion protection; the aluminium core carries the pressure, holds the bend and provides the thermal performance. That is why the core alloy, temper and wall thickness must be specified as carefully as the coating itself.

Why vehicle makers use coated aluminium tube

  • About 65 % lighter than a steel line of the same outside diameter and wall
  • PA12 jackets resist stone impact, road salt, fuel splash and abrasion
  • Zinc diffusion layers stop perforation of thin-wall condenser and radiator tubes
  • Aluminium is fully recyclable, and lines can be bent on standard CNC tube benders
Coating systems

Coating options and what each one does

PA12 / PA11 polyamide

Extruded over the drawn tube as a continuous jacket. Gives stone-chip, abrasion and salt protection for under-body lines. Plastic clips and fittings can be welded or moulded directly to the jacket.

Conversion pretreatment

A chromate-free conversion layer applied before polymer coating improves adhesion and stops corrosion creeping under the jacket from a cut end or damage point.

Zinc arc spray

Zinc is sprayed onto extruded tube straight after the press. In the brazing furnace it diffuses into the surface, creating a zinc-rich layer that corrodes sideways instead of pitting through the wall.

Pre-flux coating

Non-corrosive potassium fluoroaluminate flux, sometimes mixed with silicon powder, is applied with a binder so the assembler can skip fluxing before controlled-atmosphere brazing.

Paint and powder coat

Used on visible tubes such as roof rails or trim tubes; mainly for appearance, with some corrosion benefit.

Anodising

Hard, thin oxide layer for wear surfaces and decorative tubes. Not used where tubes must be brazed.

Agree acceptance on the condition the part will actually see. A coating that passes on a straight sample can crack or thin on the outside of a tight bend, so many programmes test bent and clipped samples after thermal cycling. Ask for tube lot, coating batch and printed line identification to be traceable to the mill certificate.

Working with coated tube

Bending, joining and assembly tips

  • Bending: O-temper 3003 and 5052 bend to radii of about 2 – 3 × OD on CNC benders; use mandrels for thin walls and polished tooling to avoid marking the jacket.
  • Strip zones: mark where the polymer must be removed for flares, crimped fittings or brazed joints, and specify how the cut edge is sealed.
  • Galvanic isolation: keep bare aluminium away from copper and steel fittings in wet areas, or use coated or aluminium fittings.
  • Brazing: zinc-coated tube must be brazed within the furnace profile agreed with the supplier; too long at temperature drives zinc too deep and weakens the protection.
  • Cleanliness: fluid lines are delivered with sealed ends and controlled internal residue.

FAQ

Coated automotive aluminium (aluminum) tube: FAQ

Why coat aluminium tube with PA12?

PA12 nylon is tough, resists fuels, oils and road salt, and absorbs stone impacts. On an under-body line it protects the thin aluminium wall from chipping and corrosion and lets plastic clips be welded directly onto the tube.

Can aluminium replace steel brake lines?

Aluminium lines are used for clutch, hydraulic and some brake circuits when the design, fittings and validation are developed for aluminium. A steel line cannot be swapped for aluminium of the same size without checking burst pressure, fatigue and fitting design.

What does the zinc coating on condenser tube do?

After brazing, the zinc forms a thin diffusion layer that is more electrochemically active than the core. Corrosion then spreads sideways through that layer instead of pitting through the tube wall, which greatly extends the life of thin-wall tubes.

Which alloy is used for coated fuel lines?

3003 in O temper is the most common because it bends easily and resists corrosion. 5052 is chosen when higher pressure or stiffness is needed.

Do you supply tube in coils?

Yes. Small-diameter drawn tube can be supplied in coils or in straight lengths, bare or coated, with capped ends and a mill certificate.
