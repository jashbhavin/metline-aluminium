7020 Aluminium Tube

7020 is a weldable aluminium-zinc-magnesium alloy that gives tube a minimum tensile strength of 350 MPa in T6 and, unlike 6xxx alloys, recovers much of its strength in the weld zone by natural ageing. It is used for welded frames in rail vehicles, military bridges and vehicles, motorcycles and bicycles. Metline supplies 7020 tube extruded or cold-drawn to EN standards, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is 7020 aluminium tube?

EN AW-7020 is the most widely used weldable 7xxx alloy in Europe. About 4.5 % zinc and 1.2 % magnesium give it T6 strength well above 6082, while copper is held below 0.2 % so it can be fusion welded without the cracking that rules out 7075.

The defining feature of 7020 is what happens after welding. In 6082-T6, the heat-affected zone permanently loses roughly half its proof strength unless the whole structure is re-heat-treated. In 7020, the heat of welding dissolves the strengthening precipitates, but they re-form at room temperature over the following weeks to months. The joint therefore regains a large part of its original strength without further treatment, giving welded structures noticeably higher design values than 6xxx alloys.

7020 extrudes reasonably well into round tube, square and rectangular hollows and custom profiles, and can be press-quenched. That combination of weldability, recoverable strength and extrudability made it the standard alloy for welded rail vehicle underframes, mobile military bridges, armoured vehicle structures and high-performance motorcycle and bicycle frames.

Design points to keep in mind

  • 7020 is sensitive to stress corrosion cracking if exposed to sustained tensile stress in corrosive conditions
  • Avoid cold working after final heat treatment and keep residual stresses low
  • Allow time for natural ageing before full loading of welded structures
  • Protect cut edges and welds with paint or coatings in aggressive environments

Welding & fabrication

Welding and fabricating 7020 tube

Filler metal

Use 5xxx filler: ER5183 or ER5556 for higher weld strength, ER5356 for general work. Avoid 4xxx fillers.

Natural ageing

Weld-zone strength rises for weeks after welding. Many specifications require a minimum ageing period before proof testing or service.

Stress corrosion

Grind or dress weld toes where specified, avoid cold straightening after T6, and protect welds with coatings.

Forming

Bend and flare in a freshly solution-treated or annealed state, then age. T6 needs generous radii.

Machining

Good in T6: turning, milling, drilling and threading of connection ends.

Finishing

Anodising, painting and powder coating are all used; painting is common on rail and defence structures.

Applications

Applications of 7020 tube

  • Rail vehicles: welded underframes, bogie and structural members, connectors
  • Defence: mobile and assault bridges, armoured vehicle structures and special vehicle frames
  • Motorcycles and bicycles: welded frames and swing arms
  • Lifting and access: crane booms, lifting frames and heavy-duty ladders
  • Industrial: automation frames and heavy brackets where welded strength matters
  • Mobility and sport: wheelchair frames and sports equipment

FAQ

7020 aluminium (aluminum) tube: frequently asked questions

What is the minimum strength of 7020-T6 tube?

EN 755-2 and EN 754-2 specify a minimum tensile strength of 350 MPa, 0.2 % proof strength of 280 MPa and elongation of 10 % (A) for T6 tube in the usual wall range.

Can 7020 tube be welded?

Yes. It is designed for fusion welding with 5xxx filler such as ER5183, ER5356 or ER5556, and the weld zone regains much of its strength by natural ageing.

Can 7020 replace 7075 tube?

Only where the lower strength is acceptable. 7075-T6 is much stronger but not weldable; 7020 is chosen for welded parts. Re-check loads and safety factors before substituting.

Is 7020 corrosion resistant?

General corrosion resistance is good, but 7020 can suffer stress corrosion cracking under sustained tension in corrosive conditions. Correct heat treatment, low residual stress and protective coatings manage the risk.

How long does natural ageing take after welding?

Most of the recovery happens in the first few weeks, and it continues more slowly for months. Follow the ageing period in your design code or welding specification.
