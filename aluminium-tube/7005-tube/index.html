7005 Aluminium Tube & Pipe

7005 is a weldable aluminium-zinc-magnesium alloy best known for bicycle frames: it is stronger than 6061-T6 and regains much of its strength after welding by natural ageing, so frames need not be fully re-heat-treated. Overview

What is 7005 aluminium tube?

7005 belongs to the weldable branch of the 7xxx family. Its zinc and magnesium form fine MgZn2 precipitates that give high strength, while very low copper keeps it weldable and zirconium helps the weld zone recover.

Unlike 7075, which cracks when fusion welded, 7005 can be TIG or MIG welded with 5xxx filler. After welding, the heat-affected zone softens but then hardens again by natural ageing at room temperature over several weeks, recovering a large share of its original strength. That behaviour is why 7005 became popular for welded bicycle frames: manufacturers can avoid the full solution heat treatment that 6061 frames need after welding (although many still give 7005 frames an artificial ageing cycle to speed up recovery).

The alloy also extrudes into thin-walled hollow sections far more easily than 7075, and its extrusions can be press-quenched. Beyond cycling, 7005 tube is used for sports equipment, wheelchairs, tent and trekking poles, motorcycle and vehicle parts, and structural members in rail and transport where a welded, stronger-than-6061 section is needed.

Key advantages of 7005 tube

  • Typical yield strength about 290 – 345 MPa, above 6061-T6
  • Fusion-weldable, with natural recovery of weld-zone strength
  • Extrudes into thin-walled, butted and shaped tubes
  • Good fatigue performance for cyclically loaded frames

Working with 7005

Welding, heat treatment and finishing

Welding

TIG or MIG with ER5356 or ER5556 filler. Clean thoroughly and control heat input; thin butted walls need careful technique.

Post-weld treatment

The weld zone recovers by natural ageing over weeks. Many builders apply an artificial ageing cycle (or full T6 treatment) to restore strength faster and more uniformly.

Forming

Bend, swage or hydroform in a softer condition and age afterwards. Ovalising and tapering are standard for frame tubes.

Machining

Machines well; used for head tubes, bottom brackets and dropouts machined from thick tube or bar.

Finishing

Anodises and powder coats well. Anodised 7005 has a slightly darker tone than 6061.

Corrosion

Less resistant than 6xxx alloys, particularly to stress corrosion under sustained tension. Protect with paint or anodising and avoid residual stress from cold work.

Applications

Applications of 7005 tube

  • Bicycles: road, mountain, BMX and e-bike frames, handlebars and seat posts
  • Sports and outdoor: trekking and ski poles, tent poles, archery and climbing equipment
  • Mobility: wheelchairs, walking aids and lightweight medical frames
  • Automotive and motorcycle: frame members, swing-arm and bumper structures
  • Rail and transport: welded structural extrusions where strength above 6xxx is required
  • Military and portable structures: lightweight frames and shelters

FAQ

7005 aluminium (aluminum) tube: frequently asked questions

Is 7005 stronger than 6061?

Yes. Typical yield strength of 7005 in T6 or T53 is about 290 – 345 MPa compared with 276 MPa for 6061-T6, and the gap widens after welding because 7005 recovers strength by natural ageing.

Do 7005 bike frames need heat treatment after welding?

Not necessarily. The weld zone recovers much of its strength at room temperature over several weeks. Many manufacturers still apply artificial ageing or full T6 treatment for consistent properties.

What filler wire is used for 7005?

ER5356 or ER5556 are the usual choices. 4xxx fillers are not recommended because the weld metal would be much weaker.

Can 7005 tube be butted and tapered?

Yes. Single-, double- and triple-butted tubes and tapered or shaped tubes are produced to drawing.

How does 7005 compare with 7075 for tube?

7075 is much stronger but cannot be fusion welded reliably and is harder to extrude into thin tubes. 7005 is the practical choice for welded frames.
