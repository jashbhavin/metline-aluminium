5083 Aluminium Pipe

5083 aluminium pipe is the strongest of the common non-heat-treatable pipe alloys, with about 4.5 % magnesium for excellent resistance to seawater and full strength retention after welding. It is specified for shipboard and offshore piping, desalination plants and cryogenic LNG and industrial-gas lines, where it gets tougher as temperature falls. Metline supplies 5083 seamless pipe and tube in O, H111 and H112 to ASTM B241 and EN standards, worldwide.

Overview

What is 5083 aluminium pipe?

5083 is an aluminium-magnesium-manganese alloy that gets its strength from magnesium in solid solution, not from heat treatment. That makes it unusually forgiving to weld: a butt-welded 5083 pipe joint retains nearly the full strength of annealed parent metal.

Pipe in 5083 is extruded seamlessly over a mandrel, then supplied annealed (O), lightly strain-hardened (H111) or as extruded (H112). Its combination of strength, weldability and corrosion resistance makes it the standard alloy for seawater cooling and fire-main lines on aluminium vessels, offshore platform piping and topsides, and the piping, tanks and heat exchangers of air-separation and LNG plants.

Cryogenic service is a particular strength. Unlike carbon steel, 5083 has no ductile-to-brittle transition: its tensile strength and toughness both rise as it is cooled towards −196 °C, which is why it is widely used for liquid-nitrogen, liquid-oxygen and LNG equipment. The one limit is sustained heat. Above roughly 65 °C, alloys with more than about 3 % magnesium can slowly become sensitised to intergranular corrosion, so 5083 is not used for hot-water or steam lines.

Why 5083 for pipework

  • Highest strength of the non-heat-treatable pipe alloys
  • Welds keep close to full O-temper strength
  • Excellent seawater and marine-atmosphere resistance
  • Tough at cryogenic temperatures down to −196 °C and below

Approvals

Marine class approval and cryogenic service

For ship and offshore piping, the pipe usually must be manufactured and tested under the rules of a classification society such as DNV, ABS, Lloyd's Register or Bureau Veritas. Class rules for aluminium alloys set chemistry, mechanical testing frequency, corrosion testing for high-magnesium alloys and the certificate format, often with surveyor witness. State the class society and grade on the enquiry so material can be supplied with the right certification.

For cryogenic equipment, 5083-O is the usual choice because it combines weldability with excellent low-temperature toughness. Design codes such as ASME B31.3 list allowable stresses for 5083 pipe and permit its use at very low temperatures without impact testing in many cases; follow the code edition that governs your project.

Seawater systems

Cooling water, ballast and fire-main lines on aluminium hulls, joined by welding to 5083 or 5086 plate.

Offshore

Topside utility lines, helideck structures and walkways where weight and corrosion matter.

LNG and industrial gas

Cold-box piping, transfer lines and vaporiser connections at −162 °C and below.

Working with 5083

Welding, bending and corrosion care

Welding

MIG and TIG with ER5183 (preferred for matching strength) or ER5356. Clean oxide and oil carefully; preheat is not normally needed.

Bending

O and H111 pipe can be induction or cold bent; allow larger radii than for 3003 or 6063.

Sensitisation

Avoid sustained service above about 65 °C and do not heat for forming beyond recommended times; ASTM G67 testing is used to check susceptibility.

Galvanic contact

Isolate from copper alloys and stainless steel in seawater with insulating flanges or gaskets.

Machining

Gummy in O temper; sharp tools with high rake angles and good lubrication give the best finish.

Fittings

Butt-weld elbows, tees and reducers in 5083 or compatible 5xxx alloys keep the system uniform.

FAQ

5083 aluminium (aluminum) pipe: frequently asked questions

Is 5083 aluminium pipe suitable for seawater?

Yes. 5083 has excellent resistance to seawater and marine atmospheres and is the standard alloy for shipboard and offshore piping, provided it is isolated from copper alloys and stainless steel.

Can 5083 pipe be used for hot water?

Not for sustained service above about 65 °C. High-magnesium 5xxx alloys can become sensitised to intergranular corrosion at elevated temperatures over time.

Why is 5083 used in cryogenic plants?

It has no ductile-to-brittle transition: strength and toughness increase as temperature falls to −196 °C. It also welds well, so complex cold boxes and LNG lines can be fabricated reliably.

Which filler wire should be used to weld 5083 pipe?

ER5183 is preferred because it gives the closest match to 5083 strength. ER5356 is also used. Avoid 4xxx fillers in structural seawater service.

What schedules are available in 5083 pipe?

Aluminium pipe follows standard NPS sizes in schedules such as 5, 10, 40, 80 and 160, plus metric OD × wall sizes. Availability depends on size and temper; send your list for confirmation.

What is the difference between 5083-O and 5083-H111?

O is fully annealed. H111 has slight strain hardening from straightening or stretching, which raises the minimum yield from about 110 MPa to about 165 MPa.
