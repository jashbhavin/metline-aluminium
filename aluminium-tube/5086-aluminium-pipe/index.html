5086 Aluminium Pipe & Tube

5086 aluminium pipe is a 4 % magnesium, non-heat-treatable marine alloy that combines high strength with excellent resistance to seawater and keeps most of its strength when welded. It is widely used for boat and ship piping, rails and fittings, desalination plants, storage tanks and cryogenic lines. Metline supplies 5086 drawn seamless and extruded pipe in Schedule 40, 80 and 160, plus round and square tube, in H32, H111, H112 and O, worldwide.

Overview

What is 5086 aluminium pipe?

5086 is an aluminium-magnesium alloy with about 4 % magnesium, slightly less than 5083. It is strengthened by cold work rather than heat treatment, so welding does not destroy a heat-treated temper and the welded joint keeps close to annealed parent-metal strength.

In pipe form, 5086 is most often supplied in H32: strain-hardened and stabilised, giving a useful jump in yield strength over the annealed condition while staying ductile enough to bend and flare. Drawn seamless pipe is produced in standard nominal pipe sizes and schedules; extruded seamless pipe and tube cover heavier walls and larger sizes, and square tube is available both drawn (H111) and extruded (H32).

Boatbuilders like 5086 because it matches the 5086 and 5083 plate used for hulls and decks, so pipe, rail and structure can be welded with the same filler and will corrode at the same rate. It is also chosen for desalination plant lines, fuel and chemical storage tank fittings, and low-temperature service, where its toughness increases as temperature falls.

Key properties of 5086 pipe

  • High strength: H32 typical tensile about 290 MPa, yield about 207 MPa
  • Excellent resistance to seawater, salt spray and marine atmospheres
  • Very good weldability with ER5356 or ER5183 filler
  • Ductile at cryogenic temperatures

Working with 5086

Welding, finishing and machining

Welding

MIG and TIG weld readily with ER5356 or ER5183. As-welded strength approaches O-temper values, so H32 gains are lost only in the heat-affected zone.

Bending and forming

H32 bends well for rails and T-tops; O and H111 for tighter radii.

Surface finishing

Mill finish, polished, brushed, anodised or painted. Anodised 5086 has a slightly grey tone.

Machining and CNC

Cutting, drilling, threading and CNC machining to drawing; sharp tools and lubricant prevent smearing.

Service temperature

Avoid sustained service above about 65 °C to prevent sensitisation; 5086 is a little less sensitive than 5083.

Galvanic isolation

Separate from copper alloys and stainless steel in wet service.

Applications

Where 5086 pipe is used

  • Shipbuilding and boats: seawater, fuel and fire-main piping, rails, stanchions, T-tops and arches
  • Desalination: seawater intake and process lines
  • Storage tanks: fill, vent and drain lines on fuel and chemical tanks
  • Welded structures: frames and handrails exposed to coastal atmospheres
  • Low-temperature service: cryogenic and refrigerated piping

FAQ

5086 aluminium (aluminum) pipe: frequently asked questions

Which surface finishes are available for 5086 pipe?

Mill finish, brushed, polished, anodised and painted or powder coated. Tell us the finish, any film protection and whether the inside surface matters.

Can 5086 pipe be supplied machined?

Yes. Cutting to length, drilling, threading and CNC machining to drawing can be arranged; send the drawing, tolerances and quantity.

How is 5086 pipe made?

Seamless pipe is extruded from a hollow billet over a mandrel and, for smaller sizes and tighter tolerances, cold drawn. Square tube is either drawn (H111) or extruded (H32).

Is 5086 stronger than 5083?

No. 5083 has slightly more magnesium and is somewhat stronger in the annealed condition. 5086 is commonly supplied in H32, which raises its yield strength, and is slightly less prone to sensitisation.

What does Sch 40 mean for aluminium pipe?

It is the wall-thickness schedule from ASME B36.10M, the same as for steel pipe. A 2 in Sch 40 pipe has a 2.375 in OD and 0.154 in wall; Sch 80 has a 0.218 in wall.

What is 5086 pipe used for?

Ship and boat piping and rails, desalination plants, storage-tank lines, welded coastal structures and low-temperature service.
