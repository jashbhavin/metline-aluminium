Overview

What is 6061 aluminium tube?

6061 tube is round hollow stock made from the magnesium-silicon alloy 6061 and heat-treated, usually to T6. It offers about twice the strength of 3003 or 6063-T5 tube while remaining easy to weld, bend in softer tempers, machine and anodise.

The same alloy is sold under several product names that buyers sometimes confuse. Extruded tube is pushed through a die and suits most structural work. Drawn seamless tube starts as an extruded hollow and is cold-drawn through a die over a mandrel, giving tighter diameter and wall tolerances, a smoother surface and slightly higher guaranteed strength; it is the route for hydraulic lines, precision sleeves and aircraft tubing. Pipe is tube made to nominal pipe sizes (NPS) and schedules so it fits standard fittings and flanges.

Because 6061 combines strength with good corrosion resistance and weldability, it is the default tube alloy in North America and much of Asia for frames, handrails, scaffolding-type structures, vehicle parts, compressed-air lines and countless machined components. In Europe, 6082 fills the same role; both are covered on this site.

Choosing the right 6061 tube product

  • Structural frames and general fabrication: extruded tube to ASTM B221 or EN 755-2
  • Hydraulic, pneumatic and precision parts: drawn seamless tube to ASTM B210 or AMS specifications
  • Piping with standard fittings: seamless pipe to ASTM B241 in Schedule 40 or 80
  • Load-bearing structural pipe in buildings and bridges: ASTM B429

Tempers

Which 6061 tube temper to order

6061-T6

Solution treated and artificially aged. Full strength; the standard condition for structural and machined tube.

6061-T6511 / T6510

Stretched after quenching to relieve stress, so tube stays straight and stable when machined. Common on extruded tube for CNC work.

6061-T4

Naturally aged. Bends more easily; parts can be formed and then aged to T6.

6061-O

Annealed for flaring, swaging, spinning and tight bends, followed by heat treatment if strength is needed.

Tolerances & testing

Tolerances and testing

Extruded tube follows the ANSI H35.2 tables (ASTM orders) or EN 755-7 for seamless and EN 755-8 for porthole tube. Drawn tube is held to the tighter B210 or EN 754-7 tables; typical OD tolerance on small drawn tube is a few hundredths of a millimetre. Straightness, ovality and wall eccentricity matter for precision sleeves and hydraulic fittings, so state limits on the drawing when standard tables are not enough.

Working with 6061

Bending, welding, machining and finishing

Bending

T6 tube bends to a centreline radius of roughly 3 – 5 × OD on a mandrel bender, depending on wall. For tighter radii, bend in O or T4 and age afterwards.

Welding

TIG or MIG with ER4043 (crack-resistant) or ER5356 (stronger fillets, better anodise match). Expect reduced strength in the heat-affected zone.

Machining

Turns and bores well in T6 with sharp carbide tools; T6511 keeps long sleeves straight after boring.

End forming

Flaring, beading and swaging are best done in O or T4 temper.

Finishing

Anodises clear or coloured, hard-anodises for wear, and accepts powder coating.

Joining to steel

Isolate from steel or stainless fittings with washers or sealant to avoid galvanic corrosion.

Applications

Applications of 6061 tube and pipe

  • Structures: handrails, guard rails, scaffold-type frames, canopies and space frames
  • Transport: truck and trailer components, roll cages for light vehicles, bicycle frames and motorcycle parts
  • Fluid systems: compressed-air lines, hydraulic and fuel lines, irrigation pipe
  • Marine: bimini frames, rails, masts for small craft and outriggers
  • Machinery: rollers, shafts, pneumatic cylinder bodies, spacers and sleeves
  • Aerospace ground equipment: stands, tow bars and fixtures
  • Sport and medical: wheelchair frames, crutches, tent poles, telescopic poles and fitness equipment

  1. SpecifyAlloy and temper (for example 6061-T6 drawn seamless, 25 × 2 mm), section size and wall thickness, length, quantity and the governing standard (ASTM B210, B221, B241 or EN 755-2). A drawing helps for cut parts.
  2. QuoteWe confirm availability, tolerances, certification and delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP) for your destination.
  3. ProcessMaterial is cut to length, de-burred and, if you ask, bent, machined or finished before packing.
  4. DocumentEach shipment carries a mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 with chemistry and tensile results, plus the packing list. Third-party inspection can be arranged.
  5. ShipBundled with end caps and interleaving or packed in seaworthy wooden cases, then shipped by sea or air.

FAQ

6061 aluminium (aluminum) tube: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between extruded and drawn 6061 tube?

Extruded tube is pushed through a die and suits structural work. Drawn seamless tube is extruded and then cold-drawn over a mandrel, giving tighter tolerances, a smoother surface and a higher minimum tensile strength (290 MPa against 260 MPa in T6).

How much does 6061 tube weigh per metre?

Use π × (OD − wall) × wall × 2.70 / 1000. A 50 × 3 mm tube weighs about 1.20 kg/m and a 25 × 2 mm tube about 0.39 kg/m.

Can 6061-T6 tube be bent?

Yes, on a mandrel bender with a centreline radius of roughly 3 – 5 times the OD. Tight bends risk cracking in T6; bend in O or T4 temper and age to T6 afterwards if needed.

Is 6061 pipe suitable for pressure service?

Seamless 6061-T6 pipe to ASTM B241 / ASME SB-241 is used for pressure piping, compressed air and process lines. Porthole (structural) tube should not be used for pressure unless the specification allows it.

What is the European equivalent of 6061 tube?

EN AW-6061 to EN 755-2. Many European designs specify 6082-T6 tube instead, which has similar or slightly higher strength.

Do you export 6061 tube?

Yes. Metline supplies 6061 tube and pipe worldwide with mill test certificates and export packing.
