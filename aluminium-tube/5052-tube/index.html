5052 Aluminium Tube

5052 aluminium tube is a magnesium-alloyed, non-heat-treatable tube that is roughly twice as strong as 3003 in the annealed state, with very good resistance to seawater and fuels and excellent fatigue strength for its class. It is the standard choice for aircraft fuel and low-pressure hydraulic lines, marine tubing and bent tube assemblies. Metline supplies 5052 drawn seamless and extruded tube in O, H32, H34 and H38, worldwide.

Overview

What is 5052 aluminium tube?

5052 contains about 2.5 % magnesium and 0.25 % chromium. Magnesium in solid solution makes it the strongest of the common non-heat-treatable tube alloys after 5083 and 5086, and chromium protects it against stress corrosion. Strength comes from cold work, designated by H3x tempers that include a stabilising treatment.

Tube made from 5052 sits in a useful middle ground. It is strong and tough enough for fuel, oil and hydraulic lines that vibrate, yet ductile enough in the annealed O temper to be bent, flared and beaded into complex routings. It does not depend on heat treatment, so welding and brazing do not destroy a carefully aged temper as they do with 6061; the weld zone simply returns to O-temper strength, which is still higher than most other non-heat-treatable alloys.

Its corrosion resistance is excellent in marine atmospheres, salt spray and many fuels and hydraulic fluids, which is why aircraft fuel and vent lines, boat fuel and water lines and offshore instrument tubing are commonly 5052. Annealed drawn seamless 5052 tube is specified for aerospace under AMS 4069 (close tolerance) and AMS 4070.

Why choose 5052 tube

  • About 75 % stronger than 3003 in the O temper
  • Excellent resistance to seawater, salt spray and fuels
  • Good fatigue strength for vibrating fluid lines
  • Weldable, with no heat-treated strength to lose

Tempers

Choosing a 5052 tube temper

O

Annealed for bending, flaring and beading. The temper of aerospace fuel and hydraulic line tube to AMS 4069 and 4070.

H32

Light cold work plus stabilisation: a good compromise for straight runs that still need moderate bends.

H34

Stiffer and stronger, for straight tube, handrails and structural uses.

H38

Full hard for maximum strength in straight lengths, such as antenna and instrument tubing.

H112

Extruded tube as pressed, for larger diameters and heavier walls.

Why "H3x"?

The 3 means the tube was stabilised by low-temperature heating after cold work, which prevents the gradual softening that 5xxx alloys otherwise show at room temperature.

Working with 5052

Bending, flaring, welding and finishing

Bending

O-temper tube bends to tight radii on rotary-draw benders; H32 accepts moderate bends. Use mandrels on thin walls.

Flaring and beading

Accepts 37° flares and hose beads for fittings without cracking in O temper.

Welding

TIG and MIG weld well with ER5356 (higher strength, better colour match) or ER5554 for elevated temperature service. Avoid ER4043 if anodising.

Brazing

Not recommended: the magnesium content interferes with flux-based brazing. Use 3003 if brazing is required.

Machining

Fair; harder tempers machine better than O. Use sharp tools and lubricant.

Finishing

Anodises well with good colour uniformity; takes paint and powder coatings.

Applications

Where 5052 tube is used

  • Aerospace: fuel, oil, vent and low-pressure hydraulic lines in annealed drawn tube
  • Marine: fuel and water lines, rails, T-tops and fittings on boats
  • Automotive and motorsport: fuel and coolant lines, intercooler pipework
  • Industrial: instrument and pneumatic tubing, chemical lines, pressure gauge tube
  • Structures and furniture: handrails, ladders and outdoor furniture near the coast

FAQ

5052 aluminium (aluminum) tube: frequently asked questions

Is 5052 tube good for hydraulic lines?

Yes, for low and medium pressure lines. Annealed drawn seamless 5052 tube to AMS 4069 or 4070 is widely used for aircraft fuel, oil and low-pressure hydraulic lines. High-pressure aircraft hydraulics normally use 6061-T6 tube, titanium or stainless steel.

Can 5052 tube be welded?

Yes, by TIG or MIG with ER5356 filler. Because 5052 is not heat-treated, welds keep O-temper strength, about 193 MPa tensile, with no large loss.

Can 5052 tube be brazed?

Generally no. Its magnesium content makes flux brazing unreliable. Use 3003 tube for brazed heat exchangers.

What is the difference between 5052-O and 5052-H32 tube?

O is fully annealed (about 193 MPa tensile) for maximum bendability. H32 is lightly cold worked and stabilised (about 228 MPa tensile), stiffer and better for straight runs.

Is 5052 tube suitable for seawater?

Yes. It has excellent resistance to seawater and marine atmospheres and is common on boats. For heavy structural marine pipe, 5083 or 5086 offer more strength.

Do you supply 5052 tube internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies 5052 tube worldwide with mill certificates and export packing.
