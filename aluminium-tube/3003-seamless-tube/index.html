3003 Cold Drawn Seamless Aluminium Tube

3003 seamless tube is extruded from a hollow billet with no longitudinal weld seam, then cold drawn (or cold rolled) to final size for close tolerances, thin walls and a smooth bore. Without seam welds it withstands internal pressure, expansion and flaring more reliably than welded or port-hole tube, which is why it is specified for condensers, evaporators, refrigerant lines and pressure tubing. Overview

What makes 3003 seamless tube different?

"Seamless" describes how the hollow is formed. A solid or pierced billet is pushed over a mandrel so the tube wall is continuous all round, with no weld line where metal has rejoined. Drawing then reduces diameter and wall to precise final dimensions.

Aluminium tube can be made three ways: welded from strip, extruded through a port-hole (bridge) die, which leaves internal weld lines where the metal streams rejoin, or extruded seamlessly over a mandrel. Port-hole and welded tube are fine for frames and low-pressure uses. For tubes that are expanded into fins, flared, bent tightly or held under refrigerant pressure for decades, a seamless wall removes the most likely failure path.

Cold drawing adds a second advantage. Each pass through a die over a plug or mandrel improves roundness, concentricity and surface finish, so drawn seamless 3003 tube holds much tighter tolerances than as-extruded tube. The amount of cold work in the last pass, and whether a final anneal is applied, sets the temper from soft O to full-hard H18.

Advantages of seamless drawn 3003 tube

  • No longitudinal weld lines, so uniform burst and expansion behaviour
  • Tight OD, wall and concentricity tolerances for fin-pack and fitting assembly
  • Smooth, clean bore for refrigerant and fluid lines
  • Thin walls achievable that extrusion alone cannot produce
Production route

How cold drawn seamless 3003 tube is produced

  1. Cast and homogeniseDC-cast 3003 billet is homogenised to even out the manganese-bearing particles for consistent drawing.
  2. Seamless extrusionThe billet is pierced or bored and extruded over a mandrel into a thick-walled hollow (the "tube shell" or "mother tube").
  3. Cold drawing or rollingThe shell is drawn through dies over plugs or a floating mandrel, or cold rolled on a pilger mill, in several passes to reduce OD and wall.
  4. Intermediate annealBetween heavy passes the tube is annealed to restore ductility.
  5. Final temperThe last pass and any final anneal set the temper: O (fully annealed), H12 to H18 (increasing cold work).
  6. Finish and testStraightening, cutting, cleaning, eddy-current or leak testing, and inspection of dimensions and surface.

Applications

Where seamless 3003 tube is specified

  • Condensers and evaporators in air-conditioning, heat pumps and commercial refrigeration
  • Refrigerant lines and capillary tube where leak-tightness over the product life is critical
  • Oil coolers and hydraulic coolers with expanded tube-and-fin construction
  • Instrument and gas lines needing clean bores and reliable flare joints
  • Precision mechanical parts such as sleeves, rollers and antenna elements made from tight-tolerance tube

FAQ

3003 seamless aluminium (aluminum) tube: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between seamless and welded aluminium tube?

Seamless tube is extruded over a mandrel so the wall is continuous, then drawn to size. Welded tube is roll-formed from strip and joined along one seam. Seamless tube is preferred for pressure, expansion and flaring; welded tube is economical for high-volume radiator and condenser tube.

Why is port-hole extruded tube not called seamless?

A port-hole die splits the metal into streams that rejoin around a mandrel bridge, leaving longitudinal extrusion welds. These are sound for most uses but are weaker under pressure and expansion than a truly seamless wall.

What temper is best for refrigeration tube?

O (annealed) is common because it bends, flares and expands easily. Brazing will anneal the tube anyway, so design pressure should be based on O-temper strength.

What does "cold drawn (rolled)" mean?

After extrusion the tube shell is reduced cold, either by drawing through dies over a mandrel or by cold rolling on a pilger mill. Both improve tolerances and surface and add strength through cold work.

Which standard covers 3003 heat-exchanger tube?

ASTM B234 covers drawn seamless tube for condensers and heat exchangers, including 3003; ASTM B210 covers general-purpose drawn seamless tube; EN 754 covers cold drawn tube in Europe.
