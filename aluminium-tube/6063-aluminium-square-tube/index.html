Overview

What is 6063 square tube?

6063 square tube is a four-sided hollow extrusion in the 6063 alloy, usually aged to T5 or T6. It is chosen when the tube will be seen: its surface is smoother and more uniform than 6061, and it costs less to extrude.

Square tube resists bending equally about both axes and twisting better than an open channel or angle of the same weight. That makes it the natural choice for legs, posts and grid frames where loads come from several directions. In 6063, the trade-off is modest strength: a T5 tube has roughly half the yield strength of 6061-T6, so it suits furniture, partitions, shelving and light frames rather than heavy machine bases.

Because 6063 flows so easily through a die, square tube can be produced with very small sizes (12 – 15 mm across), thin walls of around 1 mm, sharp outside corners for a crisp architectural look, or fully rounded corners for furniture and handrails. Many systems also add a slot or T-groove to one face so panels and connectors can be fixed without drilling.

Typical buying decisions

  • Sharp or radiused corners: sharp corners look architectural; radiused corners are safer to handle and coat more evenly
  • T5 or T6: T5 for appearance and cost, T6 where posts or rails carry load
  • Finish: anodise for a metallic look and wear resistance, powder coat for colour matching
  • Metric or imperial: both size series are supplied, so replacement parts match existing designs

Tempers

6063 tempers for square tube

6063-T5

Cooled from the press and artificially aged. The most common architectural temper: adequate strength with the best surface and lowest cost.

6063-T6

Fully solution treated and aged, about 30 % stronger than T5. Chosen when the section carries load, such as handrails or wide-span mullions.

6063-T52

Aged to a slightly lower strength than T5 for better bendability and stability; often used for thermal-break and bent sections.

6063-T66

A European higher-strength variant of T6 obtained by tighter process control, listed in EN 755-2.

6063-T4

Naturally aged; easier to bend and stretch-form, then age to T5 or T6.

6063-O

Annealed for maximum formability before final shaping.

Tolerances & inspection

Tolerances and quality control

Square tube to EN orders follows EN 755-9 or the tighter EN 12020-2 for precision architectural sections; ASTM orders follow ANSI H35.2. For furniture and display systems where tubes slot into cast or plastic connectors, the outside size and corner radius matter more than usual, so we recommend stating them as critical dimensions.

Working with 6063

Finishing and fabrication

Anodising

6063 is the reference alloy for decorative anodising: low iron and copper give a clear, uniform film in natural, bronze, black and champagne colours.

Powder coating

Accepts polyester and PVDF coatings to Qualicoat or AAMA 2603/2604/2605 specifications after chromate or chrome-free pre-treatment.

Welding

TIG and MIG with ER4043 or ER5356. Strength near the weld falls towards O-temper values, so weld at low-stress points.

Bending

Bends more readily than 6061. T5 or T52 tube suits stretch bending and roll bending for curved façades and furniture.

Machining

Softer than 6061 and can be gummy in T5; sharp, polished tools and lubricant give the best finish.

Corrosion

Very good resistance in urban and rural atmospheres, and good in coastal locations when anodised or coated.

Applications

Applications of 6063 square tube

  • Furniture: chair and table legs, bed frames, outdoor furniture and modular shelving
  • Architecture: balustrade posts, fence pickets, pergolas, screens and louvres
  • Interiors: partition frames, ceiling suspension, shop and office fit-out
  • Exhibition and retail: display stands, poster frames and modular booths
  • Light engineering: machine guards, enclosures, solar frames and light trolleys
  • Lighting and electronics: LED housings and equipment racks where conductivity and finish both matter

FAQ

6063 square tube: frequently asked questions

What is 6063 square tube used for?

Mostly furniture, partitions, display systems, railings, pergolas and light frames: anywhere the tube is visible and loads are moderate.

How much does a 25 × 25 × 1.5 mm aluminium square tube weigh?

About 0.38 kg per metre with sharp corners (2 × (50 − 3) × 1.5 × 2.70 / 1000).

Is 6063 aluminum square tubing weldable?

Yes, by TIG or MIG with ER4043 or ER5356. The area around the weld softens, so weld at low-stress points or allow for reduced strength.

6063 or 6061 square tube: which should I buy?

Buy 6063 for appearance, finishing and cost; buy 6061-T6 when the tube carries significant structural load.

Can you supply square tube with rounded corners?

Yes. Square tube can be extruded with sharp, small-radius or fully rounded corners; tell us the radius you need.
