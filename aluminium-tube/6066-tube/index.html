6066-T6 Aluminium Tube

6066-T6 is the strongest of the common 6xxx extrusion alloys, with a typical yield strength around 350 MPa, roughly 25 % more than 6061-T6. It is chosen for extruded tube and hollow sections that must carry high loads in thin walls, where 7xxx alloys would be too costly or difficult. Metline supplies 6066 tube in round, square, rectangular and custom hollow sections to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is 6066 aluminium tube?

6066 is an aluminium-magnesium-silicon alloy with deliberate additions of copper and manganese. The extra silicon, copper and manganese raise its T6 strength well above 6061 and 6082, while the alloy can still be extruded into tubes and hollow sections and heat-treated like other 6xxx grades.

The alloy fills a gap between general structural grades and the high-strength aerospace alloys. 6061-T6 tube has a typical yield of about 276 MPa; 7075-T6 reaches about 505 MPa but is hard to extrude into hollows, difficult to weld and sensitive to stress corrosion. 6066-T6 sits at about 350 MPa, so designers can reduce wall thickness by around a fifth compared with 6061 for the same bending stress, without moving to a 7xxx alloy.

The price of that strength is in fabrication and corrosion. 6066 extrudes more slowly than 6061, it is more prone to weld cracking, and its copper content (0.7 – 1.2 %) makes it more susceptible to intergranular corrosion in aggressive environments. Used where those limits are understood, it is an efficient structural tube for frames, booms, vehicle members and forged or extruded load-bearing parts.

When 6066 tube makes sense

  • A 6061 or 6082 section would need a heavier wall than the design allows
  • The part is mechanically fastened or bonded rather than heavily welded
  • The tube will be anodised or painted, or is used in a dry or mildly corrosive environment
  • Extruded hollow sections are needed, which rules out most 7xxx alloys

Working with 6066

Machining, welding, forming and finishing

Machining

Machines well in T6, with better chip breaking than 6061 thanks to higher hardness. Use carbide tools and flood coolant.

Welding

Weldability is rated only fair: the alloy is more susceptible to hot cracking than 6061. Use 4xxx filler (ER4043 or ER4145) and expect a large strength loss near the weld; mechanical fastening or bonding is often preferred.

Forming

Limited ductility in T6. Bend in O or T4 and then age, or use large radii.

Corrosion protection

Copper lowers resistance to intergranular corrosion. Anodise, paint or powder coat tube used outdoors or near the sea.

Heat treatment

Solution treat at about 530 °C, quench, and age at about 175 °C for T6; follow the governing specification for exact cycles.

Temperature

Like other 6xxx alloys, prolonged service above about 150 °C over-ages the material and reduces strength.

Applications

Applications of 6066 tube

  • Transport structures: vehicle frame members, trailer cross-members and suspension parts
  • Lifting and access: booms, ladders, telescopic masts and lightweight beams
  • Sport and leisure: highly stressed frame tubes and components where weight matters
  • Aerospace ground equipment and defence: extruded and forged structural parts to AMS-QQ-A-200/10
  • Industrial equipment: machine frames, conveyor structures and handling equipment
  • Modular structures: staging, temporary structures and prefabricated frames

FAQ

6066 aluminium (aluminum) tube: frequently asked questions

How strong is 6066-T6 compared with 6061-T6?

Typical yield strength is about 350 – 360 MPa for 6066-T6 against 276 MPa for 6061-T6, roughly 25 – 30 % higher. Guaranteed minimums under ASTM B221 are 310 MPa yield for 6066-T6 and 240 MPa for 6061-T6 extrusions.

Can 6066 tube be welded?

It can, but it is more crack-prone than 6061 and loses much of its strength near the weld. Use 4xxx filler, qualify the procedure, and consider bolted or bonded joints for highly stressed parts.

Is 6066 corrosion resistant?

Less so than 6061 or 6063, because of its copper content. It performs well when anodised, painted or used indoors; avoid bare 6066 in marine or industrial atmospheres.

Is there a European equivalent of 6066?

No direct EN equivalent is listed. European designs usually use 6082-T6 for slightly lower strength or 7020-T6 where a weldable higher-strength alloy is needed.

Can 6066 be supplied as drawn tube?

6066 is normally supplied extruded. Drawn tube and special sizes can be discussed on enquiry; send the dimensions and tolerances you need.
