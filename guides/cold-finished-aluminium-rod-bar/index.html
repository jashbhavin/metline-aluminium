Cold Finished Aluminium Rod & Bar

Cold finished aluminium bar is extruded rod or bar that has been drawn cold through a die, then straightened and heat-treated or tempered, to give tighter diameter tolerances, better roundness and straightness and a cleaner surface than extruded bar. It is the standard feedstock for automatic lathes and CNC bar feeders. This guide covers grades, ASTM B211 and AMS-QQ-A-225 properties, tolerances, sizes and how to choose between cold finished and extruded bar.

Overview

What does "cold finished" mean?

"Cold finished" (CF) describes how the bar reaches its final size. Instead of using the bar as it comes off the extrusion press, the mill draws it at room temperature through a precision die, sometimes in more than one pass. The drawing step sizes the bar accurately and gives it a smooth, bright surface.

In ASTM language, cold finished bar is covered by ASTM B211 ("rolled or cold finished bar, rod and wire"), while extruded bar falls under ASTM B221. The aerospace equivalent of B211 is AMS-QQ-A-225, which replaced the old US federal QQ-A-225 specification and is divided into slash sheets by alloy. In Europe, cold drawn bar is ordered to EN 754.

For machine shops the difference is practical. A Swiss-type or multi-spindle automatic lathe feeds bar through a collet or guide bush with very little clearance. Bar that is oversize, out of round or bent will jam, chatter or produce scrap. Cold finished bar is made for exactly this kind of work: it feeds smoothly, holds a consistent diameter from bar to bar and often needs no skimming cut on the outside diameter.

Why machinists choose cold finished bar

  • Tight diameter tolerance and good roundness for collet and guide-bush feeding
  • Better straightness, which reduces whip and vibration at high spindle speeds
  • Smooth surface that can be left as-is on some parts, saving a turning operation
  • Consistent properties from lot to lot for repeatable cycle times
  • Chamfered or deburred ends available to stop bars catching in the feeder

For high-volume screw-machine work, 2011 and 6262 are chosen for machinability; 6061 when corrosion resistance and anodising matter; 2024 and 7075 when strength is critical. Lead-free versions of free-machining grades (for example 2011 variants with bismuth and tin instead of lead, and 6xxx free-machining grades) are increasingly specified to meet RoHS and end-of-life vehicle rules.

The T3, T8 and T9 tempers are worth understanding. T3 means solution treated, cold worked and naturally aged; T8 means solution treated, cold worked and then artificially aged; T9 means solution treated, artificially aged and then cold worked. The cold-work step in these tempers is often the drawing operation itself, which is why they appear mainly on cold finished products. T651 and T351 indicate bar stretched to relieve stress, which reduces distortion during heavy machining.

How it is made

How cold finished bar is produced

  1. Cast and homogenise billetThe alloy is cast to billet and homogenised so that the structure is uniform.
  2. Extrude redraw stockBillet is extruded to a round, hex or square slightly larger than the finished size.
  3. Draw coldThe bar is pulled through a hardened die, in one or more passes, to reduce and size the section and improve the surface.
  4. Heat-treat and ageHeat-treatable alloys are solution treated, quenched and aged to the required temper; for T8 and T9 tempers the order of cold work and ageing is controlled.
  5. StraightenRoll or rotary straightening brings the bar within straightness limits; stretched tempers are stretched by a controlled amount.
  6. Finish and inspectBars are cut to length, ends chamfered if required, and checked for diameter, roundness, straightness, surface and properties; eddy-current or ultrasonic testing on request.

Some precision bar is centreless ground after drawing to achieve even tighter tolerances and a finer surface. Ground bar is a separate product, used where the outside diameter is a finished, functional surface, such as pistons, guide rods and shafts.

Tolerances

Tolerances, straightness and surface

Standard tolerances for cold finished bar come from ANSI H35.2 (ASTM and AMS orders) and EN 754-3 to -6 (round, square, rectangular and hexagonal bar for EN orders). Cold finished tolerances are substantially tighter than extruded tolerances for the same size, and they are normally specified as a plus-or-minus or a minus-only band on diameter.

What to specify for precision feeding

Diameter tolerance
Standard drawn tolerance, or a tighter ISO band such as h9 or h11 by agreement
Roundness
Out-of-round limit, important for collet and guide-bush feeding
Straightness
Per standard, or a tighter limit in mm per metre for high-speed bar feeders
Ends
Square cut, chamfered or deburred
Surface
Bright drawn, free of scratches, die marks and pickup; ground if the OD is a finished surface
Length
Random or exact lengths to suit the bar feeder, often 2.5 – 3.7 m

A useful rule is to order only the tolerance the process needs. If the outside diameter is turned in every part, standard cold finished tolerance is usually enough. If the bar OD becomes a finished surface or the feeder is very tight, ask for a defined ISO tolerance or ground bar.

Above about 100 mm diameter, cold finishing becomes impractical and extruded or forged bar is used, often peeled or turned to size. Below that, cold finished bar usually pays for itself in less scrap, fewer stoppages and fewer machining passes, even though the material costs more per kilogram.

Machining tips

Machining cold finished aluminium bar

Free-machining grades

2011-T3 and 6262-T9 break chips into small pieces, so automatic lathes can run unattended without bird-nesting.

6061-T6

Produces longer chips than free-machining grades; use chip breakers, high pressure coolant and sharp, polished carbide.

2024 and 7075

Machine very well and hold tight tolerances; their strength makes them suitable for thin walls and threads.

Tooling

Use high positive rake, polished flutes and high spindle speeds. Built-up edge is the main enemy in softer grades.

Distortion

Choose stretched tempers (T351, T651) for parts with heavy, unbalanced stock removal.

Finishing

6061 and 6262 anodise well; 2011 and 2024 anodise with a less bright, slightly coloured film.

Applications

Where cold finished bar is used

  • Automotive: brake pistons and valves, fuel-system fittings, ABS and transmission components
  • Aerospace: fasteners, bushings, hydraulic fittings and pins in 2024, 7075 and 6061
  • Fluid power: hydraulic and pneumatic fittings, valve spools and manifold parts
  • Electronics: connector bodies, standoffs, heat-dissipating inserts and housings
  • Medical and optics: instrument components, lens mounts, precision spacers
  • Automation: rollers, shafts, guides and fixture parts
  • Consumer products: camera, bicycle and sporting hardware, knobs and trim

FAQ

Cold finished aluminium (aluminum) bar: FAQ

What is the difference between cold finished and extruded aluminium bar?

Extruded bar is used as it comes from the press. Cold finished bar is drawn cold through a die after extrusion, which gives tighter tolerances, better roundness and straightness, and a smoother surface.

Which standard covers cold finished aluminium bar?

ASTM B211 for commercial orders, AMS-QQ-A-225 and its slash sheets for aerospace, and EN 754 in Europe.

Which grade machines best on automatic lathes?

2011-T3 is the classic free-machining grade. 6262-T9 machines almost as well and has better corrosion resistance and anodising response. Lead-free free-machining grades are available where regulations require them.

Is 6061-T6 cold finished bar stronger than extruded 6061-T6?

The specified minimums are the same. The main benefits of cold finished 6061 are dimensional accuracy and surface quality rather than strength.

What sizes can be cold finished?

Typically from a few millimetres up to about 100 mm in round, with similar ranges for hex and square. Larger sizes are supplied as extruded or forged bar.

Can you supply bar with chamfered ends?

Yes. Chamfered or deburred ends are available on request so that bars feed smoothly into automatic bar feeders.
