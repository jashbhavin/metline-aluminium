Pre-painted aluminium guide

Colour Coated Aluminium Sheet, Coil & Strip

Colour coated (pre-painted) aluminium sheet, coil and strip is rolled aluminium that has been cleaned, pre-treated and painted on a continuous coil-coating line before it is formed. Fabricators receive a finished, durable surface and only need to cut, bend or roll-form it. This guide compares alloys and paint systems (PE, HDP, PVDF), lists typical specifications and tests, and explains how to choose for roofing, cladding, signage, ceilings and transport.

Overview

What is colour coated aluminium?

Painting aluminium after fabrication is slow and inconsistent. Coil coating paints the metal while it is still a flat strip, at high speed and under factory control, so the film thickness, cure and colour are uniform along thousands of metres.

On a coil-coating line the strip is unwound, degreased and given a chemical conversion layer (chromium-free systems are now standard in many markets) to anchor the paint and resist corrosion. A primer is roller-applied and cured, then a finish coat; the reverse side usually gets a thinner back coat. Each layer passes through an oven for a few seconds at a peak metal temperature typically above 200 °C. The coil is then cooled, optionally covered with protective film and rewound.

The result is a pre-finished material that can be slit into strip, cut into sheets, roll-formed into roofing and gutters, folded into cassette panels or bonded into composite panels, with the coating flexing with the metal.

Why fabricators use pre-painted coil

  • Consistent colour and film thickness across a whole project
  • No painting shop, solvent emissions or curing ovens at the fabricator
  • Coatings formulated to bend and roll-form without cracking
  • Lightweight, non-rusting substrate that is fully recyclable at end of life

For most roofing and cladding, 3003 or 3105 in a half-hard H24 or H26 temper is the economical choice. Choose 5005 when appearance flatness matters on facade panels, and 5052 for coastal and transport applications where strength and corrosion resistance justify the extra cost. Pure grades suit interiors and deeply formed parts.

Paint systems

PE, SMP, HDP and PVDF coatings compared

SystemTypical dry film (top side)WeatheringFlexibilityTypical uses
Polyester (PE)About 18 – 25 µmGood for interiors and moderate exteriorsVery goodCeilings, interior panels, signage, roller shutters
Silicone-modified polyester (SMP)About 20 – 25 µmBetter gloss and colour retention than PEGoodRoofing and siding in moderate climates
High-durability polyester (HDP)About 20 – 30 µmMuch better UV resistance than standard PEGoodRoofing and cladding where PVDF is not required
PVDF (fluorocarbon, 70 % resin)About 25 µm (2-coat) to 35 µm (3-coat)Highest colour and gloss retention, chalk resistanceVery goodFacades, curtain wall panels, premium roofing, coastal sites

Film thicknesses are typical market values. The paint manufacturer's data sheet and the project specification set the actual requirement.

PVDF coatings based on 70 % fluoropolymer resin are the usual requirement where a performance standard such as AAMA 2605 (now published by FGIA) is specified for high-performance exterior architectural coatings. AAMA 2604 and 2603 describe progressively lower performance levels, which HDP and PE systems are typically designed to meet. In Europe, coil-coated aluminium sheet and strip is supplied to EN 1396, and coating performance is tested to the EN 13523 series of methods developed with the European Coil Coating Association.

Applications

Where colour coated aluminium is used

Roofing and siding

Roll-formed profiles, standing-seam roofs, flashings, gutters and downpipes; 3003, 3105 and 5052 with SMP, HDP or PVDF.

Facades and cladding

Cassette panels and composite panel skins on public buildings, airports and stations; PVDF on 3003, 5005 or 5052.

Ceilings and interiors

Lay-in tiles, linear ceilings, partitions, kitchen and bathroom panels; PE on pure grades or 3003.

Signage and display

Sign blanks, shopfronts, billboards and exhibition panels; high-gloss PE or PVDF.

Transport

Truck and trailer bodies, caravan and RV walls, rail and bus interiors; 5052 and 3105.

Appliances and industry

Appliance housings, roller shutters, insulation jacketing and cold-room panels.

Metline supplies the coated aluminium sheet, coil and strip for these products. Fabricators and system companies form, assemble and install the finished roofing, cladding, ceilings and signage.

Working with coated coil

Forming and handling tips

  • Respect the minimum bend radius for the alloy, temper and coating; check the coating's T-bend rating against your tightest fold
  • Form at temperatures above about 10 °C; cold paint films are less flexible
  • Keep protective film on during forming and installation, and remove it within the period the film supplier specifies, especially in sunlight
  • Cut with sharp shears or saws to avoid edge burrs that lift the coating
  • Use compatible fasteners (aluminium or stainless steel) and avoid contact with copper or bare steel
  • Install panels from one production lot on each elevation, with the same rolling direction, to avoid visible colour or sheen differences
  • Store coils and sheets dry and covered; trapped moisture between sheets causes staining

FAQ

Colour coated aluminium (aluminum) sheet and coil: FAQ

What is the difference between PE and PVDF coated aluminium?

Polyester (PE) is economical and flexible, suited to interiors and moderate exteriors. PVDF, based on fluoropolymer resin, holds its colour and gloss far longer under sunlight and is the usual choice for facades and premium exterior work.

Which alloy is best for colour coated roofing?

3003 or 3105 in H24 or H26 temper for most roofs; 5052 near the coast or where higher strength is needed.

How thick is the paint on coil coated aluminium?

Typically 18 – 25 µm for polyester top coats and about 25 – 35 µm for 2- and 3-coat PVDF, with a 5 – 10 µm back coat on the reverse side.

Can colour coated sheet be bent without cracking the paint?

Yes, within the T-bend rating of the coating and the minimum bend radius of the alloy and temper. Form above about 10 °C and use the right tooling.

Is colour coated aluminium the same as aluminium composite panel?

No. Composite panel bonds two thin coated aluminium skins to a polymer or mineral core. Colour coated coil is the material used for those skins, and is also used on its own as solid sheet.

Can you match a specific colour?

Yes. Colours can be matched to RAL, Pantone or a physical sample within an agreed ΔE, subject to the paint system and minimum run size for the coating line.
