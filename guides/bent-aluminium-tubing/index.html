Tube bending guide

Bent Aluminium Tubing & Mandrel Bends

Bent aluminium tubing is round, square or rectangular aluminium tube formed to a set angle and centreline radius (CLR), from gentle 30° offsets to tight 180° U-bends. Mandrel bending keeps the tube round and the wall smooth even at radii of 1.5 to 2 times the diameter. This guide explains how to choose the alloy, temper, wall and radius, compares the bending processes and gives size tables for common 6061-T6 bends.

Overview

What is bent aluminium tubing?

Bending turns straight tube into the elbows, offsets, loops and frames that carry air, fluids and loads around real equipment. Aluminium is chosen because it is light, corrosion-resistant and easy to finish, but it is less forgiving than mild steel: the outside of every bend is stretched and thinned, the inside is compressed and wants to wrinkle, and the round section tries to flatten. Good bent tubing comes from matching alloy, temper, wall thickness, radius and tooling so that none of these effects goes too far.

Three numbers describe any bend. The bend angle is how far the tube turns. The centreline radius (CLR) is the radius measured to the middle of the tube. The D of bend is CLR divided by outside diameter (OD): a 2 in OD tube bent on a 3 in CLR is a 1.5D bend. A fourth number, the wall factor (OD divided by wall thickness), tells the bender how thin-walled the tube is. Tight D and high wall factor together make the hardest bends.

Bent tubes are supplied either as ready-made mandrel bends (a short bend with straight legs that the customer cuts and welds into a system, common in intake, intercooler and exhaust work) or as fully formed parts bent to a CAD drawing with several bends in different planes, as used for frames, handrails, furniture and machine guards.

Quick selection rules

  • For tight bends (1.5 – 2D) in a strong tube, choose 6061-T6 or 6063-T6 with mandrel bending
  • For the tightest bends or very thin walls, bend in O or T4 temper and heat-treat afterwards if strength is needed
  • For large sweeps and arcs (10D and above), roll bending is cheaper and needs no mandrel
  • For 7xxx tube, keep radii generous (often 5D or more) or bend before final ageing
Bend geometry

How radius controls stretching and wall thinning

When a tube bends, its outer fibre must lengthen. A simple geometric estimate of the strain at the outside of the bend is OD ÷ (2 × CLR + OD), or 1 ÷ (2D + 1). The table shows why tight bends are demanding: at 1.5D the outer surface is stretched by about a quarter, which is more than the tensile elongation of many T6 tubes. Mandrel bending succeeds anyway because the mandrel and pressure die apply compressive and supporting forces that move the neutral axis outward and spread the deformation, but the margin is small.

D of bend (CLR ÷ OD)Approx. outer-fibre strainWhat it means in practice
1D33 %Very tight; needs soft temper (O or T4), thick wall and full mandrel tooling
1.5D25 %Tight; achievable in 6061-T6 and 6063 with mandrel and wiper die on suitable walls
2D20 %Common production radius for mandrel-bent T6 tube
3D14 %Comfortable for most alloys; plug mandrel or compression bending may suffice
5D9 %Large radius; suits 7xxx tube and roll bending
10D5 %Sweep or arc; roll bending, no mandrel needed

Wall thinning follows the same pattern. On the outside of the bend the wall typically thins by roughly the same proportion as the outer-fibre stretch, less the support given by tooling, while the inside wall thickens. For pressure tubes, structural members and parts that will be polished, the thinned outer wall must still meet the design minimum, so specify a minimum wall after bending where it matters.

Strain values are geometric estimates for guidance only. Achievable radii depend on the actual tube, tooling and machine, and should be confirmed by trial bends.

6061 versus 6063 for bending: 6061-T6 is about 30 % stronger, which is why most performance mandrel bends are 6061. 6063 bends more easily, cracks less on tight radii and gives a smoother, brighter anodised surface, so it is preferred for visible architectural and furniture tubing where loads are modest.

Bend soft, then harden: heat-treatable alloys can be bent in the O or freshly solution-treated condition and then aged to T6. Bending immediately after solution treatment (the "W" condition) gives excellent formability, but parts must be bent within a short time or refrigerated to delay natural ageing. Full re-heat-treatment after bending can distort complex parts, so fixtures may be needed.

Typical strengths are Aluminum Association / ASM values for the stated tempers; tube specified minimums are set by ASTM B210, B221, B241 or B483 and EN 754/755.

Bending processes

Mandrel, compression, roll and stretch bending compared

Rotary-draw mandrel bendingTube clamped to a rotating bend die and drawn around it, with an internal mandrel (plug or ball links) and often a wiper die to stop wrinklesTight radii (1 – 3D), thin walls, round section that must stay round; CNC multi-bend partsDedicated tooling for each OD/CLR; higher cost per bend

For fluid systems such as intakes, intercoolers, coolant and fuel lines, mandrel bending is the standard because flattening and wrinkles disturb flow and make sealing harder. For frames, furniture and handrails, compression or rotary-draw bending without a mandrel is usually adequate and cheaper. Square and rectangular tube needs section-specific tooling and is more prone to inner-face buckling, so it is normally bent at larger radii than round tube of the same size.

Worked example

Calculating cut length and allowing for springback

The developed (cut) length of a bent tube is the sum of its straight sections plus the arc length of each bend, measured along the centreline. Arc length equals CLR × bend angle in radians, or CLR × angle ÷ 57.3 when the angle is in degrees.

Example: a 2 in (50.8 mm) OD 6061-T6 tube is bent 90° on a 3 in (76.2 mm) CLR with two 6 in (152.4 mm) legs. The arc is 76.2 × 1.571 = 119.7 mm, so the centreline length is 152.4 + 119.7 + 152.4 = 424.5 mm. The bender then adds a grip allowance at one or both ends, which is trimmed after bending, so the blank is cut longer than this figure.

Aluminium springs back after the bending force is removed, so the tube must be over-bent slightly. Springback grows with yield strength and with radius: a T6 tube bent on a large radius springs back more than an O-temper tube bent tightly. The radius also opens up a little. CNC benders store a correction for each alloy, size and radius, found by trial bends on the first pieces of each batch, which is why first-article inspection matters even for repeat parts.

Tolerances should be stated on the drawing. CNC rotary-draw benders typically hold bend angle to about ±0.5° and positions between bends to about ±0.5 – 1 mm on well-set-up jobs; ovality and wall-thinning limits depend on the use and are best agreed per part, with tighter limits for fluid fittings that receive hoses or couplings. Check parts with a bending fixture, coordinate measuring arm or a go/no-go gauge for repeat production.

Design

Design rules for bent aluminium parts

  • Use one CLR per part where possible: every different radius needs its own bend die, adding cost and set-up time.
  • Leave straight length between bends: the clamp needs grip length, commonly at least one to two times OD, between consecutive bends.
  • Allow straight legs at the ends: bends cannot start right at the tube end; extra length is added and trimmed after bending.
  • Choose a standard OD and wall: standard sizes have existing tooling and stock tube, which shortens supply and lowers cost.
  • Position seams carefully: on welded tube, orient the seam at the neutral axis (side of the bend), not on the outer or inner radius.
  • Specify the measurement datum: state whether dimensions are to tube centreline or outside surface and give bend data as XYZ or LRA (length, rotation, angle).
  • Plan heat treatment and finishing: bend before anodising or powder coating; if bending in O or T4, plan the ageing step and any distortion correction.
Finishing

Surface finishes and secondary operations

Anodising

Clear, black or coloured anodising after bending protects outdoor and decorative parts. 6063 gives the most uniform appearance; 6061 anodises well; 7xxx tube anodises too, though colour can be slightly darker.

Polishing

Mechanical polishing gives a bright finish for visible intake tubes, medical frames and furniture. Polish after bending, as bending marks the surface.

Powder coating

Durable colour and UV resistance for transport, industrial and outdoor equipment. Clean and pre-treat bends thoroughly.

End forming

Beading for hose retention, flaring, swaging, expanding and reducing ends to suit couplings and seals.

Machining

Cutting to length, drilling, slotting and punching for brackets and fixings, ideally after bending to keep holes aligned.

Welding

TIG and MIG welding of bends into assemblies. With 6061-T6 the heat-affected zone loses strength; use ER4043 or ER5356 filler and design for reduced weld-zone strength or re-heat-treat.

Applications

Where bent aluminium tubing is used

  • Automotive and motorsport: intake, intercooler and charge-air piping, coolant lines, roll cages for some classes, seat and chassis frames
  • HVAC and refrigeration: 3003 and 1xxx return bends, coils and connecting lines
  • Gas and fluid systems: burner tubes, compressed-air and vacuum lines, fuel and oil lines
  • Transport: bus, rail and trailer handrails and grab bars, truck accessories
  • Marine: boat rails, bimini and T-top frames, ladders (5052, 6061 or 6082, anodised)
  • Medical and mobility: wheelchair, walker and bed frames, equipment handles
  • Furniture, lighting and display: chair frames, lamp arms, shop-fitting and exhibition structures in 6063
  • Sports and outdoor: bicycle frames, fitness equipment, tent and trekking poles
  • Industrial equipment: cart and trolley frames, machine guards, conveyor and robot cable guides

FAQ

Bent aluminium (aluminum) tubing: frequently asked questions

Why choose mandrel bending over press bending?

Mandrel bending supports the tube from inside, so it stays round, does not wrinkle and keeps a smooth bore even on 1.5 – 2D radii. Press bending is cheaper but flattens the tube, which restricts flow, makes hose and coupling fits unreliable and looks poor on visible parts.

What does CLR mean and how do I choose it?

CLR is the centreline radius of the bend. Divide it by the OD to get the D of bend: 1.5D is a common compact radius, 1D is very tight and needs special tooling and softer tube, and 2 – 3D is easier, cheaper and thins the wall less.

Does 6061-T6 crack when bent?

It can if the radius is too tight, the wall too thin or the tooling poorly set. With mandrel tooling 6061-T6 is routinely bent at about 1.5 – 2D. For tighter bends, bend in O or T4 temper and age afterwards, or use 6063.

What gauge should I choose for intake and intercooler tubes?

16 gauge (0.065 in / 1.65 mm) suits most naturally aspirated and moderate-boost systems. 14 gauge (0.083 in / 2.11 mm) or 2 mm metric wall is preferred for high boost, heavy vibration or where the bend will be welded and ported.

Can bent aluminium tube be used outdoors or in marine use?

Yes. 5052, 6061 and 6082 tubes perform well outdoors, especially when anodised or powder-coated after bending. For boats, use anodised tube and isolate stainless fittings to avoid galvanic corrosion.

Can U-bends have long, equal legs?

Yes. 180° bends can be made with equal or unequal straight legs to your dimensions, within the limits of the bending machine and the clamp length needed between bend and tube end.

Can you bend square, rectangular or custom profiles?

Yes, with section-specific tooling. Square and rectangular tubes normally need larger radii than round tube, and custom extrusions need a feasibility check of the section before tooling is made.

Need bent aluminium tube or mandrel bends?

Send your drawing or bend data, tube size, alloy and quantity. We will confirm feasibility, tolerances and a delivered price.
