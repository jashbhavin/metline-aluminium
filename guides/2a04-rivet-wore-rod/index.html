2A04 Aluminium Wire for Rivets (H14, T4)

2A04 (formerly LY4) is a high-magnesium Al-Cu-Mg rivet alloy designed for joints that run hot, typically in the 125 – 250 °C range. Supplied as H14 or T4 wire to GB/T 3195, it reaches at least 275 MPa shear strength in smaller diameters, but rivets must be driven within a few hours of quenching. Its Russian counterpart is Д19П.

Overview

What is 2A04 rivet wire?

2A04 is a Chinese national-standard (GB) designation in the 2xxx Al-Cu-Mg family. The "A" in the middle shows that the grade was carried over from the older Chinese system, where it was called LY4. Its nearest Russian counterpart is Д19П (1197).

Its chemistry, 3.2 – 3.7 % copper with 2.1 – 2.6 % magnesium plus manganese and a trace of beryllium, matches the Russian cold-heading wire grade Д19П in GOST 4784. The high magnesium raises both strength and resistance to softening at temperature, which is why the alloy is chosen for rivets in hot zones of airframes.

Performance

Shear strength and riveting performance

GB/T 3195 accepts rivet wire on two tests carried out on heat-treated samples: a shear test and an upsetting (riveting) test in which a sample of set height-to-diameter ratio is compressed into a head without cracking. Values below are the requirements commonly quoted from the standard; confirm them against the edition named on your order (the current edition is GB/T 3195-2023).

The time limits are the key practical difference from 2A01 and 2A10. Because 2A04 contains much more magnesium, it hardens quickly after quenching, and larger rivets reach the limit sooner. Rivet shops plan heat-treatment batches to match installation, or use cold storage to extend the window, following their process specification.

Tempers

H14 or T4: which supply temper?

H14

Half-hard drawn wire. Straight and stable in automatic headers; the rivets are solution treated after heading.

T4

Solution treated and naturally aged wire, used where the rivet maker heads and drives within the alloy's working window.

O (on request)

Annealed for severe head forms; heat treated after forming.

Applications

Where 2A04 rivets are used

  • Aircraft engine areas and hot structure: rivets near engines, ducts and exhausts where 2A01 or 2A10 would lose strength
  • Defence and missile structures: joints exposed to aerodynamic or propulsion heating
  • High-load riveted joints: where the highest shear capacity among GB rivet alloys is needed

2A04 is not the right choice for everyday joints: the driving time limit adds cost and risk. Use 2A01 or 2A10 unless temperature or shear load requires 2A04.

FAQ

2A04 aluminium (aluminum) rivet wire: FAQ

What is 2A04 aluminium used for?

Heat-resistant structural rivets for joints that operate at roughly 125 – 250 °C, such as areas near engines, and for high-shear riveted joints.

How long after quenching can 2A04 rivets be driven?

Per the commonly quoted GB/T 3195 values: within 6 h for up to 5 mm, 4 h for 5 – 6 mm and 2 h for 6 – 8 mm diameters.

What is the equivalent of 2A04?

The Russian rivet wire grade Д19П has the same composition range. There is no direct Aluminum Association equivalent.

What was 2A04 called before?

LY4 in the older Chinese duralumin designations.

Can 2024 rivet wire replace 2A04?

Not directly. 2024 has more copper and less magnesium, different driving limits and different elevated-temperature behaviour, so a substitution needs approval from the designer.
