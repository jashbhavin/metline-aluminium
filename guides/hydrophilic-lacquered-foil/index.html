Hydrophilic Lacquered Aluminium Foil for Heat Exchanger Fins

Hydrophilic lacquered foil is thin aluminium fin stock coated with a water-loving film, so that condensate spreads into a thin sheet and drains instead of forming droplets that bridge the fins. It is the standard fin material for air-conditioner evaporators and heat-pump coils. This guide explains how the coating works, which base alloys and tempers suit which fin dies, and the coating tests a buyer should specify.

The problem it solves

Why evaporator fins need a hydrophilic coating

When warm, humid air passes over a cold evaporator coil, water condenses on the fins. On bare aluminium the water beads up, and with fin spacing of only 1.2 – 2 mm those beads quickly join neighbouring fins into water bridges.

Water bridges block part of the air path, so the fan has to work harder and air-side pressure drop rises. Droplets can be blown off the coil into the room ("spitting"), they make noise, and the standing water encourages corrosion and microbial growth that cause odours. In heat pumps running in heating mode, retained water also speeds up frost build-up and lengthens defrost cycles.

A hydrophilic coating lowers the contact angle of water on the fin surface from roughly 70 – 90° on bare aluminium to below about 10°. Instead of beading, condensate wets the surface as a continuous thin film and runs down to the drain pan. The coil keeps its open airway, airflow stays steady, and the heat exchanger delivers its rated capacity in wet operation.

What the coating changes in practice

  • Lower and steadier air-side pressure drop in dehumidifying conditions
  • Less water carry-over from the coil into the air stream
  • Quieter operation and faster drainage after defrost
  • An extra corrosion barrier under the hydrophilic layer
How it is made

Structure and production of hydrophilic foil

Hydrophilic fin foil is made on a continuous coil-coating line. Bare fin stock is unwound, cleaned and given a conversion pre-treatment (chromium-free systems are now normal), then coated with one or two thin organic layers that are cured in an oven and recoiled for slitting.

LayerTypical materialFunction
SubstrateAnnealed or partly hardened fin foil, 8011, 3102, 1100, 1200 or 8006Conducts heat, gives formability for fin collars
Pre-treatmentChromium-free conversion coatingAdhesion of the organic layers and first corrosion barrier
Corrosion-resistant base coatEpoxy or similar resin, often tinted blue or goldBlocks salt and pollutant attack on the aluminium
Hydrophilic top coatWater-wettable polymer with hydrophilic additivesKeeps the water contact angle low over the coil's life

Total dry film is very thin, typically about 1 – 3 µm per side, equivalent to a coating weight of roughly 1.5 – 4 g/m². That is thin enough not to add measurable thermal resistance, yet it must survive punching, collar drawing and tube expansion without cracking. Some products are coated on both sides; others put the hydrophilic layer on one side and a corrosion layer on the other.

Temper is chosen around the fin press tooling. Draw-type fin dies stretch the collar out of the flat foil in several stages and need soft, high-elongation material, so O or H22 is common. Drawless (ironing) dies form the collar in fewer steps and prefer the slightly stiffer H24 or H26, which also helps thin fins resist handling damage. If the temper is wrong, collars crack or the fin pitch varies, and tube-to-fin contact after expansion suffers.

Tensile strength, elongation and Erichsen cupping value are agreed in the technical specification for each alloy, temper and gauge and are reported on the mill certificate. Ask for the values that suit your die rather than relying on a generic table.

Options

Coating types and colours

Clear (silver) hydrophilic

Transparent film that keeps the metallic look of aluminium. Provides the basic hydrophilic function at the lowest cost, suited to standard indoor evaporators.

Blue hydrophilic

Hydrophilic top coat over a tinted corrosion-resistant base. The most common choice for residential and light commercial air conditioners; the colour also makes coating coverage easy to check.

Gold hydrophilic / anti-corrosion

Heavier corrosion-resistant base layer for coastal, humid or polluted sites and for outdoor coils. Often specified for heat pumps and condensers.

Self-lubricating hydrophilic

Contains a lubricating component so fins can be stamped with little or no volatile stamping oil, reducing cleaning and odour problems.

High-corrosion-resistant grade

Thicker coating system tested to longer salt-spray exposure, for marine and industrial atmospheres.

Hydrophobic (for contrast)

Water-repellent coatings are used on some outdoor coils to shed frost and water; they are a different product and should not be confused with hydrophilic foil.

Surface checks cover even colour, absence of uncoated spots, scratches, wrinkles, streaks and edge burrs. A burr or a crushed edge is a common cause of fin cracks at high stroke rates, so edge quality from slitting is worth inspecting at goods-in. Coils should be sealed in film with desiccant, packed in cases and stored dry; coated foil is usually expected to be used within about 12 months of production.

Selection advice

Common mistakes when buying hydrophilic foil

  • Specifying colour instead of performance: blue or gold describes the base coat tint, not the corrosion rating. Write the salt-spray hours and contact-angle limits into the order.
  • Ignoring the fin die: a temper that runs well on a draw-type die may crack on a drawless die, and vice versa. Send the die type and collar height.
  • Testing only fresh foil: initial contact angle is easy to meet; persistent hydrophilicity after wet/dry cycles is what matters in service.
  • Overlooking stamping oil: some coatings lose hydrophilicity after contact with certain fin oils. Test with the oil you use, or choose a self-lubricating grade.
  • Using standard coating at the coast: outdoor and coastal coils need an anti-corrosion grade or a different coil design.

FAQ

Hydrophilic aluminium (aluminum) fin foil: FAQ

What does hydrophilic mean for aluminium fin foil?

It means the surface attracts water. Condensate spreads as a thin film with a contact angle below about 10°, instead of forming beads that bridge the gaps between fins.

Is blue hydrophilic foil better than gold?

Not inherently. The colour comes from the base coat. Gold is often used for higher corrosion-resistant systems and blue for standard evaporators, but compare the tested salt-spray hours and contact angles, not the colour.

Which temper should I order for fin stamping?

Draw-type fin dies generally need soft O or H22 foil; drawless dies usually run H24 or H26. Your die maker's recommendation and a trial coil are the safest guide.

Does the coating reduce heat transfer?

The dry film is only about 1 – 3 µm thick, so its thermal resistance is negligible. In wet operation the coating usually improves real capacity because airflow is not blocked by water bridges.

Can hydrophilic foil be used on outdoor condensers?

Yes, but choose an anti-corrosion grade, particularly near the coast or in polluted air, and confirm the salt-spray rating suits the site.

How should coated fin foil be stored?

Keep coils sealed in their film with desiccant, in a dry store away from condensation and temperature swings, and use them within the shelf life stated by the supplier, typically about a year.
