Wall panel materials guide

Aluminium Wall Panels: Choosing the Right Panel Material

Aluminium wall panels line interior walls, clad facades and form partitions because they are light, flat, durable and available in almost any colour. The four main panel types are solid aluminium sheet, aluminium composite material (ACM), aluminium honeycomb panel and insulated sandwich panel. This guide compares them for fabricators, system companies and project buyers, and explains the alloys, thicknesses, coatings and fire classes to specify.

Overview

What an aluminium wall panel has to do

A wall panel is judged on flatness, appearance over decades, fire performance, weight and how easily it can be fabricated and fixed. Aluminium scores well on all of them: a 3 mm solid panel weighs about 8 kg/m², it does not rust, it takes durable factory-applied coatings and it can be folded, routed and perforated with simple tools.

Metline supplies the aluminium that panel makers and installers work with: coil and sheet in architectural alloys, pre-painted and anodised sheet, skin coil for composite panels, honeycomb core and panels, and extrusions for sub-frames and trims. We do not fabricate or install wall systems; the guidance below explains what the panel must achieve so that you can order the right material.

Exterior facade cladding has additional structural and regulatory demands; for that angle see our pages on aluminium cladding panels and facade systems.

Panel types

Four types of aluminium wall panel compared

Solid aluminium panel1.5 – 4 mm sheet, folded into trays, often with welded or bonded stiffenersBest fire performance (aluminium is non-combustible), very durable, fully recyclable, can be curved and perforatedHeavier and less flat than composites at large sizes; needs stiffenersFacades, soffits, column covers, high-traffic interiors

Aluminium honeycomb panelAluminium skins bonded to aluminium honeycomb core, typically 10 – 25 mmHighest stiffness-to-weight, exceptional flatness over large spansHigher cost, edges must be sealedLarge-format feature walls, lobbies, transport hubs, stone-look panels
Insulated sandwich panelAluminium faces bonded to PIR/PU, mineral wool or EPS coreThermal insulation and wall in one elementFoam cores need fire assessment; mineral wool is heavierCold rooms, industrial buildings, clean rooms, modular units

A useful rule: if fire performance or a very long service life comes first, use solid aluminium; if flatness at low weight and cost comes first indoors, use ACM with a suitable core; if a large panel must stay perfectly flat, use honeycomb; if the wall must insulate, use a sandwich panel.

Finishes

Coatings and surface finishes

PVDF (fluoropolymer)

Two- or three-coat coil coating with a high fluoropolymer resin content; the standard for exterior facades because of its colour and gloss retention. In North America, high-performance coatings are tested to AAMA 2605.

Polyester (PE) and SMP

Lower-cost coil coatings for interiors and less exposed exteriors; AAMA 2603/2604 classes cover lower performance tiers.

Powder coating

Applied to fabricated panels after folding; tested in Europe under Qualicoat or GSB schemes; good for complex shapes and small batches.

Anodising

A metallic, non-peeling oxide surface; 5005 gives the most consistent colour. Exterior grades need adequate coating thickness and sealing.

Wood, stone and metallic effects

Printed or sublimated finishes on pre-coated sheet, plus brushed, mirror and embossed textures for interiors.

Perforation

Round, square or slotted patterns for acoustic absorption (with backing fleece and cavity) or ventilated facades.

Coil-coated sheet to EN 1396 is painted before fabrication, which gives the most consistent colour; specify the coating system, film thickness, colour reference and gloss together, and order all panels for one elevation from the same batch.

Fire safety

Fire classification of aluminium wall panels

Fire regulations now drive panel choice on many buildings. Aluminium metal itself is non-combustible, so solid aluminium panels and honeycomb panels with an aluminium core achieve the highest reaction-to-fire classes. With composites and sandwich panels the core and adhesive determine performance.

Panel typeTypical reaction-to-fire resultComment
Solid aluminium with inorganic coatingBest classes (non-combustible or A1/A2)Organic coatings are thin; confirm the class with the coating supplier
ACM with polyethylene (PE) coreCombustibleRestricted or banned on facades of tall and many other buildings in several countries
ACM with fire-retardant (FR) coreTypically B-s1,d0Check national rules for building height and use
ACM with A2 mineral coreA2-s1,d0Used where combustible cladding is not permitted
Honeycomb with aluminium coreTypically A2-s1,d0Depends on adhesive and skins
Sandwich with mineral wool coreA1/A2 achievablePIR/PU cores need system testing

European classes refer to EN 13501-1. In the US, exterior wall assemblies are commonly tested to NFPA 285 and surface burning to ASTM E84. Always use the classification document for the exact product and system.

Design and fixing

How wall panels are fixed and what that means for the material

  • Hook-on or pin-and-hook trays: folded solid or ACM cassettes hang on a sub-frame; panels can be removed individually. Requires accurate folding and corner notching.
  • Concealed clip and Z-clip: clips on the panel back engage rails; common for interior linings and partitions.
  • Exposed fixing: screws or rivets through the face; simple and cheap for industrial walls, trailers and garages.
  • Structural bonding: adhesive tapes and silicones bond panels to sub-frames; needs clean, primed surfaces and compatible coatings.
  • Sub-frames: extruded aluminium rails, brackets and helping-hand brackets carry the panels and allow thermal movement.

Design for thermal movement: aluminium expands about 2.4 mm per metre for a 100 °C change, so a 4 m panel moving between −10 °C and 70 °C changes length by roughly 7.5 mm. Slotted holes, floating fixings and open or sealed joints of suitable width prevent buckling. Check wind-load deflection for large panels and add stiffeners where needed.

Applications

Where aluminium wall panels are used

  • Commercial interiors: lobbies, feature walls, lift lobbies, retail and hospitality interiors
  • Public and transport buildings: airports, stations and hospitals, where cleanability and fire class matter
  • Facades and soffits: rainscreen cladding, column covers, canopies and fascias
  • Industrial and cold-chain buildings: insulated walls, cold rooms and clean rooms
  • Partitions and modular rooms: offices, control rooms and laboratories
  • Vehicles and modular units: trailer and container walls, site cabins

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) wall panels: FAQ

What thickness of aluminium sheet is used for wall panels?

Solid exterior panels are typically 2.5 – 4 mm, most often 3 mm. Interior linings and partitions commonly use 1.5 – 2 mm. Composite panels are usually 3 – 6 mm overall with skins of about 0.3 – 0.5 mm.

Which aluminium alloy is best for wall panels?

3003-H14/H24 is the general choice for painted solid panels; 5005 is preferred when panels are anodised; 5052 or 5754 suit larger or coastal panels that need more strength; 3105 is common for pre-painted coil and composite skins.

Are aluminium composite panels safe on facades?

It depends on the core. Polyethylene-core ACM is combustible and is restricted on many buildings. Fire-retardant (FR) and A2 mineral-core panels, solid aluminium and aluminium honeycomb panels are used where higher fire classes are required. Always check local regulations.

How long do coated aluminium wall panels last?

Properly specified PVDF-coated or anodised panels routinely give decades of service outdoors; coating warranties vary by system and exposure. Interior panels last essentially indefinitely with simple cleaning.

Are aluminium wall panels recyclable?

Yes. Solid aluminium panels and honeycomb panels are fully recyclable, and aluminium can be remelted repeatedly without losing its properties. Composite panels are recycled by separating the skins from the core.

Does Metline install wall panel systems?

No. Metline supplies the aluminium sheet, coil, composite skins, honeycomb and extrusions used to make wall panels, cut and finished as required, to fabricators and project buyers worldwide.
