Building profiles guide

Aluminium Railing Profiles for Buildings

Aluminium railings are assembled from a small set of extruded profiles: a top handrail, posts, a bottom rail and infill or glass channels, usually in 6063-T5/T6 or 6061-T6 with a powder-coated or anodised finish. This guide explains what each profile does, typical wall thicknesses and weights, the guard height and load rules designers must meet, and how to choose alloy and finish. Metline supplies the railing profiles and stock; installation and the finished railing are the responsibility of the fabricator.

Overview

Why aluminium is used for railings

Railings and balustrades on stairs, balconies, terraces, walkways and ramps are a natural fit for aluminium extrusions: they do not rust, need no repainting, weigh about a third as much as steel, and the profile can integrate screw ports, glass channels and cable routes in one section.

A railing system is normally designed by a system company or fabricator and built from repeated standard profiles, which keeps die costs low and makes site assembly quick. Posts are fixed to the slab or stringer with base plates or side fixings; the handrail and bottom rail span between posts; infill of pickets, cables, perforated sheet or glass fills the space between them.

For residential and commercial buildings the main choices are style (picket, glass, cable or panel infill), the post spacing that the profiles can support, and a finish that suits the exposure. Coastal balconies, pool surrounds and public walkways call for a heavier finish class and careful detailing of fixings.

Components

The profiles in a typical railing system

Top handrail

The graspable rail and the main structural member resisting the horizontal line load. Often a rounded or oval hollow section with an underside channel that clips over the posts or infill.

Posts

Square, rectangular or round hollow sections that carry the handrail load down to the fixing. Corner and end posts carry more load and often use thicker walls or internal reinforcement.

Bottom rail

Holds the lower end of pickets or panels and keeps the infill aligned. Lightly loaded, so thinner walls of about 1 mm are common.

Handrail brackets and wall rails

Separate profiles or castings that mount a secondary grab rail to a wall or to posts, as required on stairs.

Glass base shoe and channels

Heavy-wall U-channels that clamp toughened laminated glass along the floor edge, used for frameless glass balustrades.

Pickets and infill

Small square or round tubes, flat bars or perforated sheet panels between rails.

Design rules

Guard height, openings and design loads

Railings that protect a drop are guards (barriers), and building codes set their minimum height, maximum openings and the loads they must resist. The profiles must be sized so the assembled railing meets these rules with an appropriate safety factor.

RequirementInternational Building Code (US)Typical European / UK practice
Guard height, commercial1067 mm (42 in) minimum1100 mm common for balconies and external edges; check national annex
Guard height, dwellings914 mm (36 in) under the residential code900 – 1100 mm depending on location
Handrail height on stairs864 – 965 mm (34 – 38 in)900 – 1000 mm typical
Maximum opening in infillMust not pass a 102 mm (4 in) sphereUsually 100 mm sphere
Horizontal load on top rail0.73 kN/m (50 lb/ft) line load or 0.89 kN (200 lb) point loadLine loads from EN 1991-1-1 national annex or BS 6180, from about 0.36 to 3.0 kN/m by occupancy

Summary only. Always design to the current code and national annex for the project location; local authorities may impose stricter rules.

Structural design of the aluminium members follows the Aluminum Design Manual in North America or EN 1999-1-1 (Eurocode 9) in Europe. The usual governing checks are post bending at the base, deflection of the handrail between posts, and the strength of the base fixing and anchors into concrete or timber.

6063 and 6061 minimums from ASTM B221 for extruded profiles; EN 755-2 values apply to EN orders.

6063 gives the smoothest surface for anodising and complex shapes; 6061 and 6005A are chosen when post spacing or loads push 6063 beyond its limits. A useful rule: if the calculation needs a thicker 6063 post wall than about 3 mm, a 6061-T6 or 6005A-T6 post with a thinner wall is usually lighter and cheaper.

Surface finishes

Finishes for indoor, outdoor and coastal railings

  • Powder coating: the most common finish, in any RAL colour. Specify AAMA 2604 or Qualicoat class 2 for exterior railings and AAMA 2605 or PVDF for severe coastal exposure.
  • Anodising: natural, bronze or black, at least 20 µm thickness for outdoor use. Hard-wearing on handrails because there is no paint film to chip.
  • Woodgrain: sublimated wood-effect over powder coat, for warmth to the touch and appearance.
  • Brushed or polished: for interior stair rails, protected by clear anodising or lacquer.

In marine environments, the fixings matter as much as the finish. Use stainless-steel fasteners with nylon or EPDM isolating washers, avoid direct contact with copper-bearing timber treatments, and seal cut ends and drilled holes before coating where possible.

Fabrication

Cutting, joining and installation notes

  1. Cut and machineProfiles are cut to length and punched or drilled for pickets and fixings, ideally before coating so that cut edges are protected.
  2. JoinMost systems are screwed or clipped together with concealed connectors; welding is possible but reduces 6063-T6 and 6061-T6 strength near the weld and spoils the finish.
  3. FixBase plates bolted to concrete with approved anchors, or side-fixed to the slab edge; edge distances and anchor capacity must be checked.
  4. InspectCheck post plumb, handrail alignment and opening sizes; load-test sample sections where the specification requires it.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) railing profiles: frequently asked questions

Which aluminum alloy is best for railings?

6063-T5 or T6 for handrails, bottom rails and pickets, because it extrudes complex shapes with a smooth finish. Highly loaded posts and brackets often use 6061-T6 or 6005A-T6 for extra strength.

How thick should aluminium railing profiles be?

Load-bearing handrails and posts typically have walls of about 2 – 3 mm, while bottom rails and infill channels are often about 1 mm. The final thickness depends on post spacing, the code line load and the fixing method.

How high must a balcony railing be?

Under the International Building Code a commercial guard must be at least 42 in (1067 mm) high; many European projects use 1100 mm for balconies. Local codes vary, so confirm the rule for your site.

Are aluminium railings suitable near the sea?

Yes, with the right finish and fixings: anodising of 20 µm or more, or AAMA 2605 / PVDF coating, stainless fasteners isolated from the aluminium, and sealed cut ends.

Can you supply railing profiles to our own design?

Yes. Send the profile drawings and we can supply the extrusions in the required alloy, temper and finish, cut to length. Fabrication and installation of the railing remain with your team.

Need profiles for a railing project?

Send your profile drawings, finish and quantities. We will confirm alloy, temper, finish and delivery for your site.
