Aluminium Filler Rod for TIG Welding

The right aluminium TIG filler rod depends on the base alloy, the service conditions and whether the part will be anodised or heat-treated after welding. For most jobs the choice is ER4043 for 6xxx alloys and castings, and ER5356 or ER5183 for 5xxx alloys and marine work. This guide explains the classifications, gives a selection chart and covers sizes, packing and storage, so you can order the correct rod first time.

Overview

What an aluminium TIG filler rod does

In gas tungsten arc welding (GTAW, or TIG) the arc melts the base metal and the welder feeds a straight, bare filler rod into the pool by hand. The rod supplies the metal that fills the joint, and its chemistry largely decides the strength, crack resistance, ductility, corrosion behaviour and colour of the finished weld.

Aluminium weld metal is a mixture of melted filler and melted base metal, typically somewhere between 20 % and 50 % base metal depending on joint design. That dilution is why filler choice matters so much: a filler that is fine on one alloy can produce a crack-sensitive or brittle weld on another. Hot cracking, the commonest defect in aluminium welding, happens when the weld metal passes through a composition range with a wide freezing range. Filler alloys are designed to push the weld chemistry out of that range.

TIG filler rods are made from the same wire as MIG welding wire, but straightened and cut to length rather than wound on spools. Rods are normally 914 mm (36 in) or 1000 mm long and come in diameters from 1.6 mm to 4.8 mm. They are classified under AWS A5.10 in North America with the prefix ER (usable as electrode or rod) or R (rod only), and under ISO 18273 internationally with the prefix S Al.

Quick answers

  • 6061, 6063 and 6082 structures: ER4043 for general work, ER5356 for higher fillet shear strength or anodised colour match
  • 5083 and 5086 marine plate: ER5183 for full joint strength, ER5356 for general fabrication
  • Cast aluminium (A356, 319, 355): ER4043 or high-silicon ER4047
  • Parts running hot (above about 65 °C): avoid 5xxx fillers with more than 3 % magnesium; use ER4043 or ER5554
  • Anodised parts that must match in colour: 5xxx fillers, never 4043 or 4047

Silicon lowers the melting point and makes the pool more fluid, which reduces solidification cracking and helps the rod wet the joint. Magnesium strengthens the weld by solid solution and matches the electrochemical behaviour of 5xxx base metals. Manganese and chromium refine grain structure and add strength, which is why ER5183 and ER5556 produce stronger welds than ER5356.

Grades explained

The main TIG filler rods and when to use each

ER4043

The most widely used aluminium rod. Fluid pool, bright smooth bead and very good resistance to hot cracking. Suits 6xxx extrusions and plate, 3xxx alloys and most castings. Not heat-treatable on its own, but picks up magnesium from 6xxx base metal by dilution. Turns dark grey after anodising.

ER5356

General-purpose aluminium-magnesium rod. Higher as-welded strength and better ductility than 4043, good seawater resistance and a close colour match after anodising. Used on 5xxx alloys and on 6xxx where fillet shear strength or appearance matters.

ER5183

Higher-strength 5xxx rod with added manganese. Specified for 5083 and 5456 so that the weld meets the base-metal strength required by marine class rules and cryogenic tank codes.

ER5556

The strongest common 5xxx filler, for high-strength 5xxx plate and structures under dynamic load. Behaves much like ER5183 in the pool.

ER4047

High-silicon rod with the narrowest freezing range and lowest melting point. Used to repair castings, reduce shrinkage and distortion in thin sections, and as a filler for some brazing and leak-tight joints.

ER4943

A 4043 type with a magnesium addition, giving higher as-welded strength while keeping the easy handling and crack resistance of 4043. Increasingly used on 6xxx structures in place of 4043.

ER4643

Silicon-magnesium rod designed for 6xxx parts that are solution heat-treated and aged after welding, so the weld responds to heat treatment and approaches T6 strength.

ER5554

Lower-magnesium rod for 5454 and similar alloys in service above 65 °C, such as truck tanks carrying hot cargo and wheels.

ER1100 / ER2319

ER1100 joins pure aluminium where conductivity or chemical resistance matters. ER2319 is for heat-treatable aluminium-copper alloys such as 2219 in aerospace tankage.

Performance

What strength to expect from a TIG weld

The strength of a welded aluminium joint is usually controlled by the heat-affected zone of the base metal, not by the filler. Heat-treated (T6) and strain-hardened (H) tempers soften next to the weld, so the joint is weaker than the parent plate whatever rod is used. Design codes such as AWS D1.2 and the Aluminum Design Manual, and qualification codes such as ASME Section IX, therefore specify minimum as-welded tensile strengths for each base alloy.

Values are the qualification minimums used in AWS D1.2 and ASME IX for groove welds; your procedure qualification record governs actual design values.

Where filler choice does matter is in fillet welds, which fail through the weld metal. Here a 5xxx filler gives noticeably higher shear strength than 4043: for the same fillet size, a 5356 weld carries roughly half as much load again. Undiluted 4043 weld metal is also less ductile, which matters in joints that must bend or absorb impact. Filler manufacturers typically quote all-weld-metal tensile strength around 200 MPa (29 ksi) for 4043 and around 260 MPa (38 ksi) for 5356; these are typical, not guaranteed, values.

A useful rule of thumb is to choose a rod diameter close to the base-metal thickness for thin material, and no more than about half the thickness on plate where several passes are needed. A rod that is too thin melts back before it reaches the pool; one that is too thick chills the pool and causes lack of fusion.

Good practice

Storage, cleaning and TIG technique

  1. Store dryKeep rods in their sealed tubes in a heated, dry store. Moisture forms hydrated oxide on the rod surface, which releases hydrogen in the arc and causes porosity, the second most common aluminium weld defect.
  2. Clean the rod and the jointWipe rods with a lint-free cloth and solvent if they look dull or dirty. Degrease the joint, then remove the oxide with a dedicated stainless-steel brush used only on aluminium.
  3. Set the machineUse AC for aluminium so the cleaning half-cycle breaks up the oxide film. Pure argon suits most work; argon-helium mixtures add heat for thick sections. Zirconiated or pure tungsten is traditional; lanthanated and ceriated tungsten work well on modern inverters.
  4. Feed correctlyKeep the hot end of the rod inside the gas shield, and dab it into the leading edge of the pool rather than into the arc. Withdrawing the rod into air oxidises the tip and drags oxide into the next dab.
  5. Finish cleanlyFill the crater before breaking the arc, or use current down-slope, to avoid crater cracks. Cut off any oxidised rod end before restarting.

Common mistakes

  • Using ER4043 on 5083 or other high-magnesium alloys, which gives a brittle, crack-prone weld
  • Using ER5356 on parts that run above 65 °C, where the weld can become sensitised to stress corrosion
  • Welding with 4043 on parts to be anodised, which leaves a dark grey weld line
  • Leaving opened rod tubes in a humid workshop and then chasing porosity in the welds

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) TIG filler rod: frequently asked questions

Should I use 4043 or 5356 filler rod?

Use 4043 for 6xxx alloys, 3xxx alloys and castings when crack resistance and a smooth bead matter most, and for parts that run hot. Use 5356 on 5xxx alloys, on parts that will be anodised, and where fillet welds need higher shear strength or the weld must be more ductile.

Which filler rod is used for 6061 aluminium?

ER4043 is the standard choice for 6061 and gives crack-free welds. ER5356 is equally common where the weld must match the colour after anodising or carry higher fillet loads. ER4943 offers higher as-welded strength than 4043, and ER4643 is used when the welded part is heat-treated again.

Can aluminium TIG filler rods be used on cast aluminium?

Yes. ER4043 suits most silicon-bearing castings such as A356, 319 and 355. ER4047 has more silicon and a lower melting point, which helps on porous or crack-prone castings. Clean and preheat castings to drive out absorbed oil and moisture before welding.

Which filler rod should I use for anodised parts?

Use a 5xxx filler such as ER5356. Silicon-bearing fillers like 4043 and 4047 turn dark grey or black when anodised, leaving a visible weld line.

What diameter of TIG rod should I choose?

For sheet up to about 3 mm use 1.6 mm rod, 2.4 mm for general work between about 2 and 5 mm, and 3.2 mm or larger for plate. Match the rod to the welding current and joint size so it melts cleanly at the pool edge.

What documents come with the filler rods?

A certificate of conformity to AWS A5.10 or ISO 18273 with the lot chemistry is standard. An EN 10204 3.1 certificate and safety data sheet can be supplied on request.

Do you ship filler rods internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies aluminium filler rods and welding wire worldwide from Mumbai, in moisture-protected export packing.
