Custom Large Plate-Cut Aluminium Busbar

When a busbar is too wide, too thick or too long for an extrusion die, it is sawn from thick aluminium plate instead. Plate-cut bars in 1070, 1060, 1350 or 6101 carry tens of thousands of amps in rectifiers, electrolysis lines, smelters and generator connections. This guide explains when to choose the plate-cut route, which alloy and temper to specify, the tolerances that matter and how Metline supplies these bars to drawing worldwide.

Overview

Why cut large aluminium busbars from plate?

Most aluminium busbar is extruded or rolled to standard flat-bar sizes: roughly 2 – 40 mm thick and up to about 200 – 300 mm wide. Above that envelope, the press force and container size needed for a solid, high-purity section climb steeply, and a die for a one-off size is rarely economic. Sawing the bar from a thick rolled or hot-rolled plate removes those limits.

A heavy DC installation such as an aluminium potline, a chlor-alkali cell room or a large rectifier station may need a single conductor of 50,000 – 70,000 mm2 or more. Making that from several thinner bars bolted in parallel adds joints, each of which adds contact resistance, heat and a maintenance point. A monolithic bar cut from 100 – 120 mm plate carries the same current with fewer interfaces.

Plate is also the practical answer when the conductor is not a simple straight bar: L-shaped lugs, wide terminal pads, stepped thickness transitions and flag connectors can be profiled directly from the plate by sawing, CNC milling or waterjet cutting, and then joined to straight runs by welding.

Choose the plate-cut route when

  • The cross-section is wider than about 300 mm or thicker than about 40 mm
  • The quantity is too small to justify a dedicated extrusion die
  • The part has wide pads, steps or cut-outs that would need heavy machining from bar anyway
  • You need high-purity 1xxx conductivity in a section an extruder cannot press solid

Material selection

Which aluminium alloy for a large conductive busbar?

Conductivity falls as alloy content rises, so heavy DC conductors are almost always made from commercially pure 1xxx aluminium. A heat-treatable 6xxx grade is chosen when the bar must also carry mechanical load, hold bolt preload over decades or resist short-circuit forces without bending.

AlloyAl contentTypical conductivityStrengthBest suited to
1070 / 1070A≥ 99.70 %About 61 – 62 % IACSLowPrimary DC conductors, rectifier and electrolysis connections where conductivity is the priority
1060≥ 99.60 %About 61 – 62 % IACSLowGeneral conductive bars, cut parts and simple bent pieces; wide availability
1350 (EC grade)≥ 99.50 %, with B, V, Ti, Cr controlled61.0 % IACS minimum (annealed higher)LowConductor applications that must meet electrical-conductor specifications
6101Al-Mg-Si55 – 59.5 % IACS depending on temper (T6 to T64)Medium to highBolted joint areas, self-supporting runs, bars exposed to short-circuit forces

Conductivity of 1xxx plate depends on purity and temper; 6101 values are the temper minimums in ASTM B317. Specify the required minimum % IACS on the order and it will be verified on the certificate.

For heavy bars the usual temper is H112 (as hot-rolled) or O (annealed). H112 plate keeps a modest work-hardened strength and machines more cleanly; annealed plate gives the highest conductivity and bends most easily but is soft enough to dent in handling. Cold-worked H14 or H18 tempers are rarely practical at these thicknesses.

If the design is close to its thermal limit, a 1 % IACS difference matters: over a 10 m run at 60,000 A it changes the I2R loss by tens of watts per metre. If the bar must also act as a structural member, 6101-T6 or T61 gives several times the yield strength of 1070 for a loss of only a few percent in conductivity.

Sizing for current. Large DC conductors are not sized from a simple amps-per-mm2 rule. The designer balances temperature rise, power loss over the plant life, voltage drop, magnetic field effects (critical in electrolysis cells) and short-circuit forces. Published design figures for heavy DC bus are often well under 1 A/mm2, which is why such large sections are needed. Always confirm the section with a thermal calculation for the actual installation, ventilation and ambient temperature.

Dimensional control

Tolerances and shape control for plate-cut busbars

Long, heavy, soft bars move during cutting and handling, so shape tolerances deserve as much attention as nominal size. The values below are typical starting points for agreement; tighter figures are possible with machined faces and should be written on the drawing.

Plate condition

Starting from stress-relieved or flattened plate reduces the bow that appears when a narrow strip is released from a wide plate. For long bars the cut direction is kept parallel to the rolling direction.

Cut quality

Saw-cut faces are deburred and all aluminium chips removed so they cannot be trapped in joints. Contact faces can be fly-cut or milled for flatness and low contact resistance.

Handling

Soft 1xxx bars bend under their own weight if lifted at one point. Lift with spreader beams or multiple slings and support on timber at close intervals.

Further processing

Machining, bending and connections after cutting

Holes and slots

Bolt holes, slotted holes for thermal movement and tapped holes are CNC machined to the drawing. Hole position tolerances refer to a datum edge, so specify which edge.

End and contact machining

Ends can be milled square, stepped or reduced in thickness to mate with equipment terminals. Contact pads are machined flat to the finish your joint design requires.

Bending

Sections up to moderate thickness can be bent in annealed or H112 1xxx material; very thick bars are normally profiled from plate or welded at the corner instead of bent.

Welded joints

MIG or TIG welding with 1100 or 1188 filler joins straight runs to lugs and transitions while keeping conductivity close to the parent metal. 4043 filler is stronger but less conductive.

Flexible connections

Laminated packs of thin aluminium sheet or foil, pressure- or fusion-welded at the ends, absorb thermal expansion, vibration and assembly misalignment. See flexible aluminium busbar.

Bimetallic transitions

Where a bar meets a copper terminal, an aluminium-copper transition plate or clad section avoids galvanic corrosion at the joint. See copper-clad aluminium busbar.

Surface treatment

Surface finish and contact reliability

Aluminium forms a thin, hard oxide film within seconds of being cut. The film is harmless on the body of the bar but raises resistance in bolted joints, so joint preparation and plating decisions are made zone by zone.

TreatmentPurposeNotes
Cleaning and deburringRemoves chips, burrs and oilStandard on every bar
Wire brushing + contact compoundBreaks oxide and seals the jointDone at installation on bare aluminium joints
Tin platingStable, low-resistance contact surfaceMost common plating for aluminium bus joints; see tin plated aluminium busbar
Nickel platingCorrosion and wear resistance at contact zonesUsed in aggressive or high-temperature atmospheres
Silver platingLowest contact resistanceFor high-reliability, high-current contacts; specified area only
Insulating coating or sleevingPhase isolation, touch protection, identificationMasked away from contact faces

In electrolysis plants the bar is often left bare because the atmosphere attacks most coatings and the joints are welded rather than bolted. In switchgear and generator connections, plated contact pads with masked insulation elsewhere are normal. State on the drawing which faces are contact faces so that masking and machining are done in the right places.

Applications

Where large plate-cut aluminium busbars are used

  • Aluminium smelting potlines: cathode collector bars, risers and ring bus between cells, where currents of several hundred kA run through aluminium conductors
  • Chlor-alkali and electrolysis: cell-to-cell connections, rectifier outputs and anode feeders in chlorine, hydrogen and electro-winning plants
  • Rectifier and converter stations: DC output bus from large thyristor or diode rectifiers
  • Generators and transformers: low-voltage, high-current leads and terminal connections
  • Electric furnaces and induction heating: supply bus where large sections and water-cooled joints are common
  • Data centres and heavy industry: main distribution bus where standard catalogue bars would need too many parallel runs

A typical example: an electrolysis plant needs ten straight conductors of 600 × 120 mm, 10 m long, in 1070A-H112, with machined contact pads at each end and slotted bolt holes for thermal movement. Each bar weighs close to two tonnes, so the order also covers lifting points, timber supports and a long-load transport plan.

Quality and delivery

Inspection, documentation and export packing

  1. Dimensional checkThickness, width, length, flatness, camber and cut squareness measured against the drawing, with results recorded per bar.
  2. Surface and edgesVisual inspection for scratches, dents, inclusions, oil and oxide; confirmation that burrs and chips are removed.
  3. Machined featuresHole positions, slot sizes, end faces and contact areas verified against the drawing.
  4. Material documentsMill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 with chemistry and mechanical results; conductivity measured and reported when specified.
  5. Marking and packingLabels with alloy, temper, size, weight and cast or batch number. Bars are wrapped, laid on timber at close spacing, braced against movement and shipped in long crates, flat racks or open-top containers.

FAQ

Large plate-cut aluminium (aluminum) busbar: FAQ

Can a 600 mm wide or 100 mm thick aluminium busbar be extruded?

Rarely. Solid high-purity sections of that size need very large presses and a dedicated die, which is uneconomic for most projects. Sawing the bar from thick plate is the usual route for sections above roughly 300 mm wide or 40 mm thick.

Which alloy gives the best conductivity for a large busbar?

1070A and 1060 give about 61 – 62 % IACS and are the normal choice for heavy DC conductors. 1350 is used where an electrical-conductor specification applies. Choose 6101 when the bar also needs strength, accepting 55 – 59.5 % IACS depending on temper.

How heavy is a 600 × 120 mm aluminium busbar?

The cross-section is 72,000 mm², so it weighs about 194 kg per metre, or roughly 1.94 tonnes for a 10 m bar.

What tolerances should I specify on a 10 m plate-cut busbar?

Specify thickness, width and length tolerances, plus flatness across the width, bow along the length, camber and cut squareness. Typical starting values are ± 1 – 3 mm on thickness, ± 2.5 – 3 mm on width and − 0 / + 3 to + 20 mm on length; machined contact faces can be held much tighter.

Should large aluminium busbars be tin plated?

Plate only the contact zones when joints are bolted and the atmosphere allows it; tin is the usual choice. Welded joints and bars in electrolysis cell rooms are usually left bare.

Can Metline machine the holes and ends?

Yes. Metline supplies the bars cut to drawing with holes, slots, end machining and contact-face finishing on request, with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and export packing for long loads.
