Aluminium Foil for Food Containers and Trays

Food container foil is the aluminium foil that container makers press into takeaway trays, baking pans, airline meal dishes and lids. The right alloy, temper and gauge decide whether a tray draws without cracking, holds its shape when full and survives oven and freezer. Metline supplies container foil in jumbo and slit rolls to container manufacturers worldwide; this guide explains how to choose it.

Overview

What container foil is and who uses it

Aluminium foil containers are formed on presses from flat foil in a single stroke. A die set draws the foil into a cavity, forms the walls and curls the rim, producing anything from a small muffin cup to a deep catering tray. The process is fast and nearly scrap-free, but it is very sensitive to the foil: small changes in strength, elongation, thickness or lubrication show up as torn corners, split rims or trays that collapse when lifted.

Container foil is therefore a specified product, not general-purpose foil. It is supplied to container and tray manufacturers in alloy and temper combinations chosen for the container style, with tight gauge tolerance, a clean degreased or evenly lubricated surface, and coils wound to run continuously on automatic presses.

Metline supplies the foil: jumbo rolls for slitting and slit rolls to the width of your press. We do not make or sell finished containers. The sections below cover the material choices that container makers and their buyers need to agree.

What good container foil delivers

  • Enough elongation to draw deep corners without cracking
  • Enough strength and stiffness for the finished tray to hold its load
  • Consistent gauge across width and length so dies run without adjustment
  • A clean, food-safe surface with controlled lubricant or lacquer
  • Low pinhole count and no oil stains, scratches or roll marks

Tempers

Choosing the temper: O, H22 or H24

O (annealed)

Softest condition with the highest elongation. Used for deep draws and wrinkle-wall containers where the foil gathers into pleats around the corners.

H22 (quarter hard)

Strain-hardened then partially annealed. Slightly stronger than O while keeping good formability; a common all-round choice for standard takeaway trays.

H24 (half hard)

Higher strength and stiffness for rigid and smoothwall containers that must stack and carry weight; less elongation, so draw depth is limited.

H26 / H18

Harder tempers used mainly for lids and flat components that are not deeply drawn.

The temper should match both the container geometry and the die design. A die built for H22 foil may tear O-temper foil less often but produce floppy trays; the same die with H24 foil may give rigid trays but split corners. When changing supplier or alloy, run a die trial and agree mechanical property ranges in the purchase specification.

Compliance

Food contact compliance

Aluminium foil is one of the most widely used food-contact materials, but buyers in regulated markets need documents that tie each lot to the relevant rules. These are the points usually covered:

  • EN 602 sets the composition requirements for wrought aluminium intended for contact with food; container alloys such as 8011, 3003 and 3004 are normally supplied to it
  • EU Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004 is the framework for all food-contact materials in the EU; a declaration of compliance accompanies supply
  • Council of Europe Resolution CM/Res(2013)9 gives guidance on metals and alloys in contact with food, including a specific release limit for aluminium
  • Lacquers and coatings must meet the rules for coating materials in the destination market, for example the US FDA 21 CFR 175.300 for resinous coatings
  • Lubricants used on the foil for forming must be food-grade and applied evenly at a controlled level
  • Pinholes and cleanliness are checked to agreed limits; foil is degreased by annealing and packed to prevent contamination

Aluminium release into food increases with acidic or salty foods and with long contact at high temperature. Container makers often use a protective lacquer on the food-contact side for tomato-based, citrus or heavily salted products.

Processing

Practical tips for forming foil containers

  1. Match temper to dieConfirm the temper the die was designed for. Changing from H22 to O or H24 usually needs die clearance or blank-holder adjustment.
  2. Control lubricationToo little lubricant causes tearing at corners; too much causes slipping and oil residue. Agree the lubricant type and coating weight with the foil supplier.
  3. Store foil properlyKeep coils in a dry area at stable temperature and let them reach press-room temperature before opening to avoid condensation and oxide staining.
  4. Check rim curlSplit rims point to low elongation or excessive work-hardening; consider a softer temper or slightly heavier gauge.
  5. Validate with food testsFor new products, test containers with the actual food, cooking and freezing cycle, especially for acidic or salty contents.

FAQ

Food container aluminium (aluminum) foil: FAQ

Which alloy is best for aluminium foil containers?

8011 is the most common choice for wrinkle-wall trays because it balances formability and strength. 3003 and 3004 are preferred for rigid and smoothwall containers, where stiffness and lid sealing matter.

What thickness is used for takeaway containers?

Most takeaway trays use 0.04 – 0.08 mm foil. Smoothwall and larger catering pans use 0.08 – 0.15 mm or more, depending on size and load.

What is the difference between wrinkle and smoothwall containers?

Wrinkle-wall containers gather the foil into pleats at the corners and use soft, thin foil. Smoothwall containers have unpleated walls and a flat rim that can be heat-sealed; they need stronger alloys and heavier gauge.

Is container foil food-safe?

Container foil supplied to EN 602 composition limits, with clean surfaces and food-grade lubricants, is suitable for food contact. Buyers usually request a declaration of compliance for the destination market with the mill certificate.

Do you sell finished foil containers?

No. Metline supplies container foil in jumbo and slit rolls to container manufacturers. We can match alloy, temper, gauge, width and coil size to your press line.

Can the foil be lacquered or coloured?

Yes. Clear, gold and coloured lacquers and protective food-side coatings are available on request, along with release coatings for baking applications.
