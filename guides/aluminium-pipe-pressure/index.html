Aluminium Pipe and Tube for Pressure Vessels

Pressure vessels and pressure piping use seamless aluminium pipe in a small group of proven alloys: 5083 and 5086 for welded and cryogenic service, 6061 and 6063 for higher strength or lighter duty, and 3003 or 1060 for low-pressure and heat-transfer lines. This guide explains which grade to choose, the specified minimum properties under ASME SB-241, how welding changes the design strength, and what documentation a vessel fabricator or classification surveyor will ask for.

Overview

Why aluminium is used for pressure equipment

Aluminium is not the strongest pressure-vessel material, but it solves problems that steel cannot: it does not become brittle at cryogenic temperatures, it resists many corrosive media without lining, it does not spark or contaminate product, and a given vessel weighs roughly a third of its steel equivalent.

Those properties explain where aluminium pressure pipe actually appears: liquefied gas and cryogenic plant (LNG, liquid oxygen, nitrogen and argon), air-separation units, compressed-air and nitrogen distribution networks, refrigeration circuits, process lines in food, beverage and pharmaceutical plants, gas-insulated electrical switchgear, and lightweight vessels on ships, trucks and aircraft.

The design codes that cover aluminium vessels are well established. In North America, ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code Section VIII, Division 1, Part UNF governs non-ferrous vessels, and the permitted pipe and tube material is ASME SB-241 (seamless extruded) or SB-210 (drawn seamless). In Europe, EN 13445-8 adds the aluminium-specific requirements to the unfired pressure vessel standard, and pipe is supplied to EN 755 with chemistry to EN 573-3. Classification societies such as Lloyd's Register, DNV, ABS and Bureau Veritas apply their own material rules to vessels and piping built for ships and offshore units.

What makes a pipe suitable for pressure service

  • Seamless construction (extruded over a mandrel or drawn), not porthole-die extrusion with longitudinal weld seams, unless the code expressly permits it
  • A listed alloy and temper with code-allowable stresses at the design temperature
  • Certified chemistry and tensile results for the lot, traceable to the heat
  • Hydrostatic or non-destructive testing where the specification or purchaser requires it
Alloy selection

Which aluminium alloys are used for pressure pipe?

Most pressure work is done with four alloys. The choice is driven by whether the pipe will be welded, the temperature range and the medium it carries.

AlloyUsual tempersTypical pressure useWhy it is chosen
5083O, H111, H112Cryogenic and LNG piping, welded vessels, marine systemsHighest strength of the non-heat-treatable alloys; keeps near-full strength across welds; tough down to −196 °C and below
5086O, H111, H112Welded vessels and marine pipingSlightly lower strength than 5083 but easier to form; excellent seawater resistance
6061T4, T6, T6511Compressed-air and gas lines, bolted or mechanically joined systems, aerospace vesselsHigher strength in the unwelded condition; good machinability for threaded and flanged ends
6063T5, T6Low and medium-pressure air, vacuum and process linesSmooth extruded surface, good corrosion resistance, economical
6351T4, T6Structural and medium-pressure tube where 6061 is not stockedStrength similar to 6061; common in some markets
3003O, H112, H14Refrigeration, heat exchangers, low-pressure fluid linesVery formable and brazeable; modest strength
1060 / 1100O, H112Chemical and food lines at low pressureHighest corrosion resistance and purity; low strength

Heat-treatable alloys such as 6061 and 6063 lose a large part of their strength in the weld heat-affected zone, while 5083 and 5086 in the O or H111 condition lose very little. That is why welded cryogenic vessels and ship systems are dominated by 5xxx alloys, and 6xxx pipe is preferred where joints are mechanical (flanged, threaded, grooved or compression fittings).

Welding

How welding changes the design strength

For a welded vessel, the governing strength is not the pipe in its delivered temper but the weld and heat-affected zone. Codes therefore list separate, lower allowables for welded construction in heat-treatable alloys.

MaterialEffect of weldingPractical consequence
5083-O / H111, 5086-O / H111Heat-affected zone returns to roughly the annealed (O) condition, which is close to the delivered strengthWelded design stress is similar to unwelded; the reason 5xxx alloys dominate welded vessels
5083-H112 / H32 strain-hardenedWork-hardening is lost next to the weldDesign welded areas to O-temper values
6061-T6Tensile strength in the weld zone falls to about 165 MPa (24 ksi), well below the 260 MPa parent minimumThicker walls or post-weld solution treatment and ageing
6063-T5 / T6Weld-zone tensile strength falls to about 115 MPa (17 ksi)Suited to low-pressure welded lines only
3003Welds at close to O-temper strengthLow-pressure service

Filler for 5xxx pipe

ER5183 matches the strength of 5083 and is the usual choice for cryogenic vessels; ER5356 is common for 5086 and general marine work. Avoid 4xxx fillers on high-magnesium alloys because Mg2Si can embrittle the joint.

Filler for 6xxx pipe

ER4043 resists hot cracking and is preferred where the part may be heat-treated after welding; ER5356 gives higher shear strength and a better colour match after anodising.

Procedure qualification

ASME Section IX or ISO 15614-2 procedure and welder qualifications are required. Clean the oxide layer immediately before welding and keep filler wire dry to avoid porosity.

Product forms

Seamless extruded, drawn and large-diameter pipe

Seamless extruded pipe

Extruded from a hollow billet over a mandrel so there is no longitudinal weld line. The standard route for SB-241 pipe in nominal sizes from about 1/2 in to 12 in and larger, in Schedule 5, 10, 40, 80 and 160 wall thicknesses.

Drawn seamless tube

Extruded shells cold-drawn to closer diameter and wall tolerances and a smoother bore. Supplied to ASTM B210 / ASME SB-210 for small-bore instrument, hydraulic and refrigeration lines.

Large-diameter and heavy-wall pipe

Very large or thick-walled cylinders for vessel shells are produced by extrusion on large presses, by piercing and forging, or by rolling and welding plate. Rolled-and-welded shells are designed as welded construction.

Porthole (bridge-die) tube

Lower cost, but it contains internal weld seams formed in the die. It is not accepted as seamless pipe for code pressure parts unless the specification allows it.

Fittings and flanges

Elbows, tees, reducers, stub ends and weld-neck flanges in matching alloys, so the system can be welded with one procedure.

Bar and plate for nozzles

5083 plate and bar for heads, reinforcing pads, nozzle bodies and tube sheets.

If your vessel or piping must carry a classification society certificate, state the society and the rule edition at enquiry. The pipe then has to be sourced from a manufacturer holding the relevant approval and tested accordingly; material bought on a standard 3.1 certificate cannot be upgraded to class certification afterwards.

Selection advice

Common mistakes when specifying aluminium pressure pipe

  • Using unwelded allowables for a welded 6061 vessel. The weld zone governs; use the welded allowable or design for post-weld heat treatment.
  • Ordering porthole tube for a code pressure part. Specify "seamless" and the code material specification on the order.
  • Choosing a 5xxx alloy with more than about 3 % magnesium for long service above 65 °C. Alloys like 5083 and 5086 can become sensitised to intergranular and stress corrosion at sustained elevated temperature; 5454 is the usual choice for warm service.
  • Ignoring galvanic contact. Isolate aluminium from copper, brass and carbon steel fittings, or use transition joints.
  • Forgetting the medium. Aluminium resists many gases, refrigerants and neutral solutions, but strong alkalis, mercury and some chlorinated solvents attack it.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) pressure pipe: frequently asked questions

Can aluminum pipe be used for pressure vessels?

Yes. ASME Section VIII Division 1 (Part UNF) and EN 13445-8 both cover aluminium vessels. Seamless pipe to ASME SB-241 or EN 755 in alloys such as 5083, 5086, 6061 and 6063 is used for shells, nozzles and connecting piping.

Which alloy is best for a welded aluminium pressure vessel?

5083 in the O or H111 temper is the usual choice, especially for cryogenic service, because its strength across the weld is close to the parent metal. 5086 is a slightly softer alternative, and 5454 is preferred where the vessel runs warm for long periods.

Why is seamless pipe required instead of extruded porthole tube?

Porthole tube contains longitudinal seams where metal re-joins inside the die. They are generally sound, but codes treat only mandrel-extruded or drawn seamless pipe as seamless material, so porthole tube is not accepted for code pressure parts without specific permission.

What is the minimum yield strength of 6061-T6 pipe?

ASTM B241 specifies 240 MPa (35 ksi) minimum yield and 260 MPa (38 ksi) minimum tensile strength. After welding, the tensile strength in the heat-affected zone drops to about 165 MPa unless the part is heat-treated again.

Does aluminium pipe work at cryogenic temperatures?

Yes. Aluminium has no ductile-to-brittle transition, and alloys such as 5083 actually gain strength and keep their toughness at liquid-nitrogen temperature, which is why they are standard for LNG and air-separation equipment.

Can you supply pipe with Lloyd's Register or other class certification?

Tell us the society and rule edition when you enquire. Class-certified material has to come from an approved manufacturer and be tested as the rules require, so it is arranged per order rather than supplied from general stock.

Specifying pipe for a pressure vessel?

Send the alloy, size, schedule, code and testing requirements. Our technical team will confirm a compliant supply route and certification.
