Wine Capsule Foil Coil for Top Discs and Skirts

Wine capsule foil coil is lacquered, printed or laminated aluminium foil and thin strip that capsule makers convert into the top discs and skirts covering the cork and neck of a wine bottle. Most of it is 8011 or 3105 alloy, coated with a decorative lacquer on the outside and a heat-seal or protective lacquer on the inside. This guide explains capsule types, alloys, coating structures, sizes and the checks that keep a capsule line running.

Overview

What is wine capsule foil coil?

The capsule on a wine bottle does three jobs: it protects the cork and bottle lip from dust, insects and damp in the cellar, it shows that the bottle has not been opened, and it carries much of the brand's shelf appearance. Aluminium is the dominant material because it forms easily around the neck, takes lacquers and print well, gives a premium metallic look and is recyclable.

Capsule foil coil is the semi-finished material behind that capsule. A capsule maker buys wide coils of coated or printed aluminium, slits them, prints or embosses them where needed and then blanks, draws, rolls or laminates the material into discs and skirts. The foil supplier is responsible for the metal, its gauge and flatness, and for coatings that adhere, seal and survive forming without cracking or changing colour.

This page is written for capsule converters, printers and packaging buyers. Metline supplies the aluminium foil and strip in coil form; it does not make or sell finished capsules.

What a good capsule foil must do

  • Form into a disc or skirt without tearing, wrinkling or coating cracks
  • Keep colour and gloss consistent from coil to coil and batch to batch
  • Seal reliably to the skirt or liner with the specified heat-seal system
  • Run on high-speed slitting, printing and forming lines without breaks or telescoping
Capsule types

Types of wine capsule and the aluminium each uses

Aluminium capsule (still wine)

A one-piece capsule drawn and rolled from lacquered aluminium. Light, rigid enough to keep its shape and fully recyclable. The material is thicker than disc foil and is usually 8011 or 3105.

Polylaminate capsule

A skirt of aluminium-polyethylene-aluminium laminate, softer and quieter than a pure aluminium capsule, with an aluminium top disc sealed on. Widely used for mid-price still wines.

Sparkling wine foil

A hood of thin embossed or printed foil, often laminated with paper, that covers the cork and wire cage of sparkling wine and has a tear strip for opening.

Heat-shrink capsule with foil disc

A PVC or PET shrink sleeve topped with an aluminium disc for print and branding. The disc is the aluminium element.

Tin capsule

Rolled pure tin, heavy and pliable, used on premium wines. Tin is not an aluminium product and Metline does not supply it; aluminium-polyethylene laminates are often chosen as a lighter alternative.

Historical lead capsules

Tin-coated lead foil capsules were phased out in the early 1990s and banned in the US by the FDA in 1996 because of lead residues on the bottle lip.

Because the disc is sealed to the skirt, the two specifications must be developed together. A heat-seal lacquer that bonds well to a polyethylene laminate may perform poorly against a vinyl-lacquered aluminium skirt, so state the mating material in the enquiry.

Alloys

8011 and 3105: composition and how to choose

Two alloys cover most capsule work. 8011 (and its European variant 8011A) is an aluminium-iron-silicon alloy that rolls to clean, pinhole-free thin gauges with good formability. 3105 is an aluminium-manganese-magnesium alloy that is stronger and stiffer at the same thickness. Coatings

Coating and laminate structures

Most capsule foil is coated on both sides. The outer side carries appearance; the inner side carries sealing or protection. The table shows the usual layers and what each contributes.

A common structure for still-wine discs is a clear or coloured primer outside and a heat-seal lacquer inside. For skirts that are printed by the converter, a print-receptive primer on the outside and a protective lacquer inside is typical. Always confirm the coating system and coat weight against your sealing equipment with trial runs.

Premium still wineHeavier disc, deeper embossing, higher-gloss finishDisc stiffness, emboss depth, colour match
Sparkling wineThin foil hoods or stiffer 3105 componentsTear strip, wire-cage coverage, emboss pattern
Older or slower forming equipmentThinner, softer specifications that feed easilyTemper, coil flatness, coating slip
Unusual bottle necksFix dimensions before gauge and structureNeck diameter, height, taper, skirt length
  • Start from the bottle: neck diameter, taper and capsule height decide blank size and forming strain.
  • Match the line: share the forming method (drawing, spinning, rolling) so temper and coating flexibility suit it.
  • Fix the colour standard: agree a physical master sample and a measured colour tolerance (ΔE) before mass production.
  • Test the seal: run disc-to-skirt sealing trials at production speed and temperature, not just in the lab.
Compliance

Food-contact and regulatory checks

A capsule does not normally touch the wine, but it does touch the bottle lip, and residues there can reach the glass when the wine is poured. That is why lead capsules were banned and why buyers expect capsule materials to meet food-contact rules.

Decoration

Printing, embossing and special effects

Most of the visual value of a capsule is added on the foil before it is formed, so the coil has to be prepared for the decoration route. Rotogravure gives the deepest, most consistent solid colours and fine metallic effects and suits long runs; flexographic printing is more economical for shorter runs and frequent design changes. Either way, the primer must be compatible with the ink system and fully cured, or ink will lift during forming.

Embossing of the top disc, such as a crest, logo or vintage, stretches the foil locally. Deeper embossing needs a slightly thicker gauge or a softer temper, and a lacquer flexible enough not to craze at the edges of the relief. Hot-foil stamping, metallic inks and matt-gloss contrast varnishes are applied by the converter, but they still depend on the surface energy and cure of the base coating supplied with the coil.

Registration

For printed discs, coil camber and gauge variation translate directly into misregistration. Tight flatness and straight edges matter more than nominal gauge.

Metallic look

A clear or tinted primer over bright foil gives gold, copper and champagne shades without metallic inks, and keeps the aluminium sheen.

Tear strips and perforations

Sparkling foils and easy-open skirts use tear tabs or perforation lines. The foil must tear cleanly along them and nowhere else.

Practical advice

Common mistakes when specifying capsule foil

  • Specifying the disc without the skirt: heat-seal lacquers are chemistry-specific; a disc tested only against one skirt material may fail against another.
  • Quoting closure-stock properties for foil: tensile values for 0.2 mm screw-cap sheet do not apply to 50 µm disc foil. Agree property ranges for the actual gauge.
  • Approving colour on a flat sample only: lacquer colour can shift slightly after forming and heat sealing. Approve colour on a formed and sealed capsule.
  • Ignoring storage conditions: lacquered coils stored in humid or hot warehouses can block (stick between wraps) or pick up condensation. Keep coils sealed until use and let them reach room temperature before unwrapping.
  • Changing only one variable at a time in trials: it is better to change gauge, temper and coating in planned combinations than to discover interactions during mass production.

Capsule foil is also a sustainability choice. Aluminium capsules can be recovered with other aluminium packaging, and remelting aluminium uses only a small fraction of the energy needed to make primary metal. Laminates with polyethylene are harder to recycle in some regions, so brand owners with recycling targets increasingly specify mono-material aluminium capsules.

FAQ

Wine capsule aluminium (aluminum) foil: frequently asked questions

Is capsule foil coil a finished capsule?

No. It is coated or printed aluminium in coil form that a capsule maker converts into discs and skirts. Metline supplies the coil, not finished capsules.

What is the difference between 8011 and 3105 for capsules?

8011 is softer and rolls to cleaner thin gauges, so it suits most still-wine discs and skirts. 3105 is stronger and stiffer at the same thickness and is chosen for rigid parts, sparkling-wine components and screw caps.

What is a polylaminate capsule?

A capsule whose skirt is a laminate of aluminium foil, polyethylene and aluminium foil. It is softer than a pure aluminium capsule and is closed with an aluminium top disc.

Why do top discs need a heat-seal lacquer?

The disc is bonded to the skirt or capsule by heat and pressure. The heat-seal lacquer must match the mating surface, whether that is a vinyl, nitrocellulose, PVC or polyethylene layer.

When did wine capsules stop using lead?

Tin-coated lead capsules were phased out in the early 1990s; the US FDA banned them in 1996. Aluminium, aluminium laminates, tin and plastics replaced them.

Are aluminium wine capsules recyclable?

Yes. Aluminium capsules and discs can be recovered with other aluminium packaging, and lacquers burn off during remelting. Laminates are recyclable where collection systems accept them.
