Perforated Aluminium Sheet

Perforated aluminium sheet is flat sheet or coil punched with a regular pattern of holes, used for facades, ceilings, acoustic panels, machine guards, ventilation grilles and filters. Its performance depends on four choices: the alloy and temper, the sheet thickness, the hole shape and pitch, and the finish. This guide explains each one, shows how to calculate open area and weight, and lists the information a perforator needs to quote.

Overview

What is perforated aluminium sheet?

Perforated aluminium sheet, also called punched aluminium sheet or perforated aluminium panel, starts as ordinary rolled sheet or coil. A press punches thousands of identical holes in a repeating pattern, after which the sheet is levelled, trimmed and, if required, coated. The result keeps most of the stiffness of solid sheet while letting air, light, sound and liquid pass through it.

Aluminium is the default metal for perforated panels in buildings and transport because it is about one third of the weight of steel for the same thickness, does not rust, and accepts anodising, powder coating and PVDF paint. The holes can be round, square, slotted, hexagonal or decorative, and they can cover the whole sheet or leave plain margins for fixing.

Most perforated sheet is produced in two ways. High-volume standard patterns are made on wide multi-tool presses that punch a full row with each stroke, feeding from coil. Custom and decorative layouts are punched on CNC turret presses or, for thicker gauges and complex shapes, cut by laser. Punching is faster and cheaper per hole; laser cutting removes the need for a dedicated tool and suits one-off artwork patterns.

Why specify aluminium for perforated panels

  • Light weight reduces the load on facade sub-frames, ceiling grids and vehicle structures
  • Natural oxide film resists corrosion outdoors; anodising or PVDF extends life further
  • Takes a wide range of colours and textures for architectural work
  • Non-magnetic, non-sparking and fully recyclable at end of life
Material selection

Which aluminium alloy for perforated sheet?

Punching works best in alloys with good ductility. A temper that is too hard tends to crack between holes, while a temper that is too soft leaves burrs and distorts. The grades below cover nearly all perforated work; the base sheet is supplied to ASTM B209 or EN 485 and the chemistry to EN 573-3.

AlloyTypical tempersCharacterTypical perforated uses
1050 / 1100H14, H24Commercially pure, very ductile, lowest strengthAcoustic ceiling tiles, light grilles, filter screens
3003H14, H24Mn-alloyed, a little stronger than 1100, punches cleanlyHVAC grilles, speaker grilles, general-purpose panels
5005H14, H34Low Mg, matches anodised colour wellAnodised facade and ceiling panels
5052H32, H34Higher strength, very good corrosion resistanceMarine grilles, vehicle panels, exterior cladding, step plates
6061T6Heat-treatable, stiff and strong; less formableMachine guards, platforms, structural screens
6063T5, T6 (extruded)Usually an extrusion alloy; perforated profiles and louvre bladesSunshade blades, perforated profiles

For facade cladding that will be anodised, 5005 is generally preferred because it gives a more even colour than 3003 or 5052. For panels that will be bent along the hole field, choose an H24 or H34 temper rather than H14 or H18: the partial annealing in the "2" and "3" tempers leaves more elongation for forming. 6061-T6 is chosen when strength matters, but it needs generous bend radii and is harder on tooling.

Values for each grade are given on the mill test certificate supplied with the base sheet. Perforating removes material but does not change the alloy or temper.

Patterns

Hole shapes and arrangements

Round holes, 60° staggered

The most common pattern. The staggered layout gives the strongest sheet and the highest open area for a given hole size and pitch, and it looks uniform from every angle.

Round holes, straight (90°)

Holes in square rows. Lower open area than staggered for the same pitch, but simpler to set out where fixings or brackets must align with the hole grid.

Square holes

Square holes in straight rows give a very high open area and a clean, technical look. Popular for screens and partitions.

Slotted holes

Long slots, straight or staggered, with round or square ends. Used where directional airflow, drainage or a linear appearance is needed, and in grain-drying and sorting screens.

Hexagonal holes

Hexagons nest closely, so very high open area is possible with narrow bars. Common in speaker grilles, radiator covers and lightweight guards.

Decorative and image patterns

Diamond, cross, fish-scale and custom shapes, or graded hole sizes that build an image across a facade. Usually CNC punched or laser cut.

Two dimensions define any round-hole pattern: the hole diameter (D) and the pitch, also called the centre distance (P), measured from the centre of one hole to the centre of the next. The solid strip left between holes is the bar or bridge. Pattern orientation also matters: on a long sheet, the stagger can run along the length or across it, and the two orientations look different and behave differently when the sheet is bent.

Calculations

How to calculate open area

Open area is the percentage of the sheet surface taken up by holes. It controls airflow, light transmission, acoustic transparency, weight and strength, so it is the single most important number in a perforated sheet specification. Use these formulas:

PatternOpen area formula (%)
Round holes, 60° staggered90.69 × D² ÷ P²
Round holes, straight 90° rows78.54 × D² ÷ P²
Square holes, straight rows100 × W² ÷ P² (W = hole width)
Slots, straight rows100 × (W × L − 0.215 W²) ÷ (Pw × Pl) for round-end slots

There is a practical ceiling. The bar between holes has to be wide enough to punch reliably, which in aluminium normally means a bar no narrower than the sheet thickness. That limits round-hole patterns to about 60 – 65 % open area. Higher figures are possible with hexagonal or square holes, or with expanded metal instead of perforated sheet.

Open area figures are geometric. Measured airflow and acoustic performance also depend on sheet thickness, hole edge condition and any backing material.

Solid aluminium sheet weighs about 2.70 kg per square metre for every millimetre of thickness (2.68 kg for 5052). A perforated sheet weighs the solid weight multiplied by (1 − open area). For example, a 1220 × 2440 × 2 mm sheet in 3003 weighs about 16.1 kg solid; with 40 % open area it weighs about 9.6 kg. Surface

Finishes and colours

Perforated sheet can be finished before or after punching. Coating coil before perforating is economical but leaves raw aluminium on the hole edges; coating after punching covers the edges and is preferred for exterior work. Common options:

Mill finish

The as-rolled surface. Suitable for hidden acoustic layers, filters and industrial guards.

Anodised

Clear, bronze, black and other colours. Hard, integral and lightfast; specify an anodising-quality alloy such as 5005 for an even appearance.

Powder coated

Any RAL colour in matt, satin or gloss; covers hole edges. Choose a facade-grade (e.g. Qualicoat class 2) powder for outdoor exposure.

PVDF coated

Fluoropolymer paint for high-exposure facades, with very good colour and gloss retention. Metallic and mica effects are available.

Brushed or polished

Directional or reflective finishes for interiors, retail and signage.

Wood grain and printed

Transfer-printed patterns that imitate timber or stone on ceilings and soffits.

Applications

Where perforated aluminium sheet is used

  • Facades and cladding: rainscreen panels, screening of plant rooms and car parks, solar shading and image-perforated feature walls
  • Ceilings and acoustic panels: perforated trays and baffles with a non-woven fleece or mineral wool behind them to absorb sound
  • HVAC: supply and return grilles, diffuser faces and equipment enclosures
  • Machine guards and enclosures: visibility and ventilation with protection from moving parts
  • Filtration and screening: support layers for filter media, strainers and grading screens
  • Transport: lightweight interior panels, step treads and radiator covers in buses, rail cars and boats
  • Furniture and retail: shelving, display panels, lockers and seating made from perforated stock
  • Electronics: speaker grilles, server-rack doors and vented covers

Metline supplies the perforated aluminium sheet and coil; the ceilings, facade systems, grilles and furniture are made by fabricators and system companies using that material. For ceiling trays and cladding systems, see the related guides below.

Design advice

Design tips and common mistakes

  1. Start from functionFix the minimum open area needed for airflow, light or sound first, then choose hole size and pitch. Smaller holes at the same open area look finer from a distance and give a smoother surface.
  2. Respect the hole-to-thickness ruleKeep the hole diameter at least equal to the sheet thickness and the bar no narrower than the thickness. Breaking this rule leads to broken punches, cracked bars and higher cost.
  3. Allow for strength lossA perforated sheet is less stiff and less strong than solid sheet. As a rough guide, stiffness falls roughly in line with open area, so panels with 40 – 50 % open area may need thicker gauges, return flanges or closer supports.
  4. Plan margins and foldsLeave unperforated borders where the sheet will be fixed or bent. Folding through a hole field gives an irregular edge; if you must, use a softer temper and a larger bend radius.
  5. Expect some distortionPunching stretches the sheet, so it is levelled afterwards. Very thin sheet with high open area may still show slight waviness; specify a flatness requirement if it matters.
  6. Specify the edge conditionBurrs are on the exit side of the punch. State which face is visible, and ask for deburring if the panel will be handled.

Common specification mistakes

  • Quoting hole size and open area but not the pitch or pattern orientation
  • Choosing a hard temper (H18, T6) for panels that must be folded
  • Coating before punching on exterior panels, leaving bare hole edges
  • Forgetting that the perforated margin pattern must line up across adjacent panels

Ordering

How to order perforated aluminium sheet

  1. Specify the materialAlloy and temper (e.g. 5052-H32), thickness, and the base standard (ASTM B209 or EN 485).
  2. Define the patternHole shape, size and pitch, staggered or straight, orientation relative to the sheet length, and margins. A drawing is best.
  3. Give sizes and finishSheet or panel size, quantity, coating type and colour, and whether coating is before or after perforating.
  4. Confirm termsWe confirm feasibility, tolerances, certification and delivery on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
  5. Packing and shippingSheets are interleaved or film-protected and packed in seaworthy wooden cases or on pallets, with the EN 10204 3.1 certificate and packing list.

FAQ

Perforated aluminium (aluminum) sheet: frequently asked questions

What is the most common perforated aluminium pattern?

Round holes on a 60° staggered pitch. A 1/8 in hole on 3/16 in centres, or a 3 mm hole on 5 mm centres, are typical general-purpose patterns, with about 40 % and 33 % open area respectively.

Which alloy is best for perforated aluminium sheet?

3003-H14 for general indoor use, 5005 for anodised architectural panels, 5052-H32 for outdoor and marine exposure, and 6061-T6 when strength and stiffness matter more than formability.

How do I calculate the weight of perforated aluminium sheet?

Multiply the solid weight (2.70 kg/m² per mm of thickness) by one minus the open area. A 2 mm sheet with 40 % open area weighs 5.4 × 0.6 = 3.24 kg/m².

What is the smallest hole that can be punched?

As a rule, the hole diameter should be at least equal to the sheet thickness. Micro-perforation below 1 mm is possible in thin gauges; smaller holes in thicker sheet need laser cutting or special tooling.

Can perforated aluminium sheet be bent?

Yes. Use an H24 or H34 temper, a bend radius of at least one to two times the thickness, and preferably a plain margin along the bend line. Bending through holes leaves an uneven edge.

Is perforated aluminum suitable for exterior facades?

Yes. Choose 5005 or 5052, and anodise or PVDF/powder coat after perforating so the hole edges are protected. Check panel stiffness and wind load with the facade engineer.
