Small-diameter tube guide

Small Aluminium Tubing

Small aluminium tubing usually means round tube from about 1 mm up to 20 mm outside diameter, with walls from 0.2 to 2 mm. It is made by extrusion followed by cold drawing, or by welding thin strip, and it is supplied in straight lengths or in coils. This guide shows which manufacturing route, alloy and temper suit each use, with sizes, weights, tolerances and the details to put on an order.

Overview

What counts as small aluminium tubing?

There is no single standard definition, but in the trade "small tubing" covers round tube small enough to be drawn through a die in several passes: roughly 1/16 in to 3/4 in (1.6 – 19 mm) in imperial markets and 1 – 20 mm in metric ones. Below about 3 mm it is usually called capillary tube. At these sizes the wall is a large fraction of the diameter, so tolerances, surface quality and cleanliness matter as much as alloy strength.

Small tubes carry refrigerant in air-conditioners and heat-pump coils, coolant and fuel in vehicles, oxygen and gases in medical equipment, and signals in instrument lines. They also work as light structural members: antenna elements, tent and kite frames, model and drone parts, pointers, telescopic rods and spacers. In each case aluminium is chosen for its low weight (about one-third the weight of copper or steel tube of the same size), its corrosion resistance and how easily it bends, flares and brazes.

The challenge with small tube is consistency. A refrigerant capillary must have a precise bore to meter flow; a medical gas line must be clean inside; a sliding telescopic set needs a controlled OD and ovality so sections fit without play. Those requirements decide the production route more than the alloy does.

Product types

Types of small aluminium tube and how they are made

Extruded tube

Pushed through a die from a hot billet. Economical for 6063 and 6061 tube from about 6 mm OD upwards, with walls down to about 0.8 – 1 mm. Tolerances follow extrusion tables, so it suits structural and decorative uses.

Drawn seamless tube

Extruded hollows cold-drawn over a mandrel through successive dies. Gives tight diameter and wall tolerances, a bright smooth surface and strengths raised by cold work. Standard for precision and pressure tube.

Capillary tube

Drawn to very small sizes, from about 1 mm OD and 0.3 mm bore, mainly in 1050, 1060, 1100 and 3003. Used for flow metering, sensor lines and thermostatic bulbs.

Coil tube

Long lengths of soft (O temper) tube in level-wound or pancake coils, typically 3003 or 1100. Lets HVAC and appliance plants feed tube straight into bending and cutting machines.

Welded tube

Roll-formed from strip and seam-welded at high frequency. Very thin walls and long lengths at low cost; common for heat-exchanger and furniture tube.

Micro multi-port tube

Flat extruded tube with several tiny channels, used in microchannel condensers and battery cooling plates rather than round-tube coils.

Precision

Tolerances, straightness and cleanliness

Drawn tube is held to much tighter limits than extruded tube. Under the standard tables a drawn tube a few millimetres in diameter is controlled to a few hundredths of a millimetre on mean diameter, while an extruded tube of the same size may vary by about a tenth. Specify which standard applies and add any tighter limits explicitly.

RequirementTypical referenceNotes
Drawn seamless tubeASTM B210 / B210M; EN 754-7General and pressure use; diameter, wall and ovality limits by size
Drawn general-purpose and HVAC tubeASTM B483 / B483MIncludes coiled tube; coils limited to non-heat-treatable alloys and thin walls

For refrigerant, medical-gas and instrument service, also agree an internal cleanliness level (residual oil or particles per unit area), end caps, and whether the tube must be dry-nitrogen sealed. Straightness for straight lengths and the coil wind pattern for coil tube should be written on the order.

Fabrication

Bending, end forming, joining and finishing

Bending

Soft 1100-O and 3003-O tube bends to tight radii, often by hand or on simple benders. T6 tubes need larger radii and mandrel support to avoid flattening or cracking on the outside of the bend.

End forming

Flaring, beading, swaging, expanding and reducing are routine in O and H1x tempers. Check that flared ends meet the fitting standard (for example SAE 45° flare) before mass production.

Brazing

3003 and 1xxx tubes are furnace or torch brazed with Al-Si filler. Keep tube and fitting clean, and control heat closely because tube walls are thin.

Welding

TIG welding of small tube is possible but needs low currents and good fit-up. 7075 should not be fusion welded; join it mechanically or with adhesives.

Cutting

Use burr-free cutting (rotary or orbital) for refrigerant tube, followed by deburring and blowing out chips.

Finishing

6063 and 6061 anodise to clear or coloured finishes; bright-dip and mirror polishing suit visible parts.

Ordering

How to specify small tubing

  1. SizeOD and wall (or ID and wall), with the dimensions that are critical marked on the drawing.
  2. Alloy, temper and standardFor example 3003-O to ASTM B483, or 6061-T6 to ASTM B210.
  3. Form and lengthCut lengths with tolerance, random straight lengths, or coil weight and wind type.
  4. ExtrasCleanliness, end caps, anodising, end forming or bending to drawing.
  5. DeliveryEXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms, with the EN 10204 3.1 certificate and export packing in cartons or cases.

FAQ

Small aluminium (aluminum) tubing: frequently asked questions

What is the smallest aluminium tube available?

Capillary tube can be drawn down to about 1 mm OD with a bore of a few tenths of a millimetre, usually in 1xxx or 3003 alloy. Below about 3 mm, sizes and tolerances are normally agreed per order.

Which alloy should I choose for refrigerant lines?

3003 in O temper is the usual choice: it bends and flares easily, brazes well and resists corrosion. 1100 is used where maximum ductility matters.

Is drawn tube better than extruded tube?

For precision it is. Drawing gives tighter diameter and wall tolerances, better roundness and a smoother bore. Extruded tube costs less and is fine for structural or decorative parts.

Can small 6061 tubing be supplied in coils?

Generally no. Coiled tube is made in soft non-heat-treatable alloys such as 1100, 3003 and 5052. 6061-T6 small tube comes in straight lengths.

How much does 10 × 1 mm aluminium tube weigh?

About 0.076 kg per metre, so roughly 13 metres per kilogram.

Can you cut and bend small tubes to drawing?

Yes. Tube can be cut to length, deburred and supplied with bends or formed ends to your drawing, with dimensional reports on request.
