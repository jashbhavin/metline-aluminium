5000 Series Aluminium Wire and Rod for Rivets

5000 series rivet wire is aluminium-magnesium alloy wire drawn for cold heading into solid, semi-tubular and blind rivets. Strength rises with magnesium content, from about 1 % in 5005 to over 6 % in 5A06, while corrosion resistance stays excellent, which is why 5xxx rivets are standard in marine, transport, building and magnesium-alloy structures. This guide compares the grades and explains how to specify them.

Overview

Why use aluminium-magnesium wire for rivets?

A rivet is a short piece of wire that must survive being upset cold into a head, then carry shear and tension for the life of the structure without corroding. 5xxx alloys meet those demands well: they gain strength from magnesium in solid solution and from cold work rather than heat treatment, so rivets can be driven as received without the refrigeration or timed driving that 2017 and 2024 rivets need.

Magnesium also gives these alloys some of the best corrosion resistance among aluminium grades, particularly in seawater and industrial atmospheres. That makes 5xxx rivets a natural match for 5xxx and 6xxx sheet in boats, rail cars, truck bodies, containers and cladding, where joining like with like avoids galvanic problems. 5056 rivets are also the traditional choice for fastening magnesium alloy parts, because their electrochemical potential is closer to magnesium than copper-bearing aluminium rivets.

Choosing within the 5xxx family

  • Low magnesium (5005, 5050): soft and easy to head; for snap and decorative rivets
  • Medium magnesium (5052, 5754, 5A02): balanced strength and formability; general solid and blind rivets
  • High magnesium (5056, 5154, 5083, 5A05, 5A06): highest shear strength; structural and marine rivets

Magnesium ranges are registered composition limits (Aluminum Association for 4-digit grades, GB/T 3190 for 5A grades). Temper availability varies by diameter; confirm at enquiry.

5xxx alloys cannot be strengthened by heat treatment, so the temper is set by drawing reduction and, where needed, a final anneal. Annealed (O) wire heads most easily and is used for large or complex heads; strain-hardened and stabilised tempers such as H32 give higher shear strength in the finished rivet.

Quality

What makes rivet wire head well

  • Upset test: a sample is compressed to a set ratio (often to one third or half its length) without cracking; ask for it on the certificate.
  • Surface integrity: seams and die marks open into cracks on the head, so surface inspection matters more than for general wire.
  • Consistent temper: variable hardness along a coil changes head size and rivet length on automatic headers.
  • Grain size: coarse grains give an orange-peel head; annealing practice should keep grains fine.
  • Ovality and tolerance: tight diameter control is essential for feeding and cut-off accuracy.
Applications

Where 5xxx rivets are used

Marine and shipbuilding

5052, 5154, 5056 and 5083 rivets in boats, superstructures and fittings exposed to seawater.

Rail, bus and truck bodies

Solid and blind rivets joining 5xxx and 6xxx panels and extrusions.

Containers and trailers

Blind rivets for side panels and liners, where corrosion resistance and low weight matter.

Aerospace

5056 "B" rivets, identified by a raised-cross head marking, especially where magnesium parts are fastened.

Building and HVAC

Cladding, roofing, ductwork and signage, often with anodised or painted finishes.

Brake linings and automotive

Larger-diameter 5754 rod rivets for brake shoes and assemblies.

Ordering

What to specify

  1. Grade and standardFor example 5056-H32 to ASTM B316, or 5A06 to GB/T 3196.
  2. Diameter and toleranceNominal size and the tolerance your header needs.
  3. Temper and propertiesTemper plus any tensile, hardness or upset requirement.
  4. PackagingCoil weight and inside diameter to suit your payoff, or straight lengths.
  5. DeliveryQuantity, destination and terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP), with EN 10204 3.1 certificates.

Avoid using high-magnesium rivets (over about 3 % Mg) in parts held above 65 °C for long periods: they can become sensitive to intergranular corrosion. 5052 or 5754 are safer in warm service.

FAQ

5000 series aluminium (aluminum) rivet wire: FAQs

Which 5xxx alloy is strongest for rivets?

5A06 and 5056 have the highest magnesium content and the highest shear strength in the family. 5056-H32 is the internationally recognised structural choice; 5A06 is used where GB standards apply.

Do 5xxx rivets need heat treatment before driving?

No. They are non-heat-treatable and are driven in the delivered temper, unlike 2017 or 2024 "ice-box" rivets.

Why are 5056 rivets used with magnesium parts?

Their electrode potential is close to magnesium, so galvanic corrosion is much lower than with copper-containing 2xxx rivets.

What diameter tolerance does rivet wire need?

Typically about ±0.02 to ±0.05 mm depending on size and header. Tell us your feed system and we will confirm what can be held.

Can 5xxx rivets be anodised?

Yes. 5005 and 5052 anodise well and are often chosen for visible rivets on anodised panels.
