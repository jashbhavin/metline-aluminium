Composite panel materials guide

Colour Coated Aluminium Skin Sheet and Coil for Composite Panels

An aluminium composite panel (ACP or ACM) is two thin aluminium skins bonded to a polymer or mineral core, and the skin coil decides how flat, durable and colour-stable the finished panel is. Facade panels normally use 0.5 mm skins of 5005, 3105 or 3003 with a PVDF finish; signage and interior panels use thinner 0.18 – 0.30 mm skins with polyester coatings. This guide covers alloys, gauges, coatings, fire-rated cores, quality checks and how panel makers should specify skin coil.

Overview

What the skin coil does in an ACP

A typical facade ACP is 4 mm thick: a 3 mm core between two 0.5 mm aluminium skins. The skins carry nearly all the bending load, so the panel behaves like a light, stiff I-beam. They also provide the visible finish, the weather barrier and, in fire-rated panels, part of the protection for the core.

Panel makers buy skin as pre-painted coil. The coil is unwound on a continuous laminating line, the core is extruded or fed between the two skins, an adhesive film bonds the layers under heat and pressure, and the panel is cut to length. Any defect in the coil, such as poor flatness, gauge variation, edge wave or weak primer adhesion, shows up as waviness, delamination or colour mismatch on the facade.

The exposed face is usually painted with a high-durability finish; the reverse, which faces the core, carries a primer or wash coat chosen to bond with the adhesive film. On the panel's inside face, the second skin often carries a simple service coat because it is never seen.

What panel makers need from skin coil

  • Consistent gauge and good flatness across the full width for even lamination
  • A back-side primer that bonds reliably to the core adhesive (checked by peel tests)
  • Top coat with the right weather performance for the application (PE indoors, PVDF outdoors)
  • Tight colour control between batches on large projects
Alloy options

Which alloy for which panel

AlloyCharacterTypical ACP use
5005 (H14, H22, H24, H44)Al-Mg 0.5 – 1.1 %; good strength, excellent weathering, uniform appearancePremium facade and curtain-wall cladding, exposed outer skins
3105 (H14, H16, H24)Al-Mn-Mg; stronger than 3003, good paint responseFacade skins, A2 and FR panels, signage exposed to wind
3003 (H14, H16, H24)Al-Mn; moderate strength, very good formability and corrosion resistanceStandard facade and interior ACP, transport and signage panels
3004Al-Mn-Mg; stronger than 3003Larger panels where extra skin stiffness helps
1100 / 1060Commercially pure; soft, very formable, lower strengthInterior decorative panels, signage, inner skins

For exterior work, many national ACP standards and system manufacturers list 3003, 3105 and 5005 as permitted skin alloys, with 5005 often used for the outer face. Tempers are selected so the skin stays flat during lamination yet allows routing and folding of panel cassettes without cracking the paint. Exact mechanical limits for each alloy and temper are in ASTM B209 and EN 485-2 and are reported on the mill certificate.

AAMA 2605 is the usual specification for exterior PVDF. It is based on 70 % PVDF resin systems and requires ten years of South Florida exposure with colour change no more than 5 ΔE units and limited chalking, plus salt-spray, humidity and abrasion tests. AAMA 2604 is the intermediate tier, with five years of exposure. Ask for the coating maker's test certificate for the specific system and colour.

Fire safety

Cores and fire classification

The core, not the skin, decides most of an ACP's fire behaviour. Since several serious facade fires involving polyethylene-cored panels, many countries restrict or ban combustible cladding on taller buildings, so skin suppliers must know which core the panel will use.

Core typeMake-upTypical classificationWhere it is used
PE (LDPE)PolyethyleneCombustible; not permitted on many building facadesSignage, interiors, low-rise uses where regulations allow
FRMineral-filled polymerOften B-s1,d0 to EN 13501-1Facades where codes accept Class B
A2Mostly inorganic mineral filler with little binderA2-s1,d0 to EN 13501-1High-rise and public buildings requiring limited combustibility

Classifications apply to the tested panel system, not to the skin coil alone. In the US, panel systems are tested to ASTM E84 and the full wall assembly to NFPA 285. Skin coil itself is non-combustible, but its primer and adhesive compatibility affect how the panel performs in fire tests, so changes to coil or coating should be checked with the panel maker's certification body.

Applications

Applications for coated skin coil

  • ACP facades and rainscreen cladding on offices, malls, airports and residential towers
  • Aluminium honeycomb panels for curtain walls, ceilings and rail interiors, with skins bonded to honeycomb core
  • Fire-rated FR and A2 composite panels for hospitals, schools and transport hubs
  • Signage and fascia for retail chains, fuel stations and wayfinding
  • Insulated sandwich panels with PU, PIR or mineral wool cores for cold rooms and clean rooms
  • Transport bodies, trailer side panels and rail interiors
  • Interiors such as wall linings, ceilings, partitions and furniture panels

Metline supplies the aluminium skin coil and sheet that panel makers laminate; the finished panel, its fire classification and its installation remain the responsibility of the panel manufacturer and facade contractor.

FAQ

ACP aluminium skin coil: frequently asked questions

What thickness of aluminium skin is used in ACP?

Facade panels normally use 0.5 mm skins on a 3 mm core for a 4 mm panel; some standards allow 0.4 mm. Signage and interior panels often use 0.18 – 0.30 mm skins.

Which alloy is best for exterior ACP skins?

5005 is widely used for the exposed face because of its weathering and appearance; 3105 and 3003 are also common. All three are accepted by most facade specifications.

What is the difference between PE and PVDF coated aluminum skin?

Polyester is economical with a wide colour range but fades and chalks sooner outdoors. PVDF, specified to AAMA 2605, keeps its colour and gloss for many years in sun and pollution and is the normal choice for facades.

Does the skin affect the fire rating of an ACP?

The core governs most of the fire behaviour, but the skin, primer and adhesive form part of the tested system. Any change to the skin coil should be checked against the panel's fire certification.

Can you match project colours?

Yes. Colours can be matched to RAL, Pantone or a physical sample, with an agreed ΔE tolerance and gloss range, and supplied from a single batch for large projects where possible.

How is skin coil packed for export?

Coils are wrapped in protective film and moisture barrier, packed eye-to-sky or eye-to-wall on reinforced wooden pallets or in cases, with the mill test certificate and coating test report.
