Oval Aluminium Tubing: Selection Guide

Oval aluminium tubing is extruded or drawn tube with an elliptical or flat-sided oval section. It is stiffer than a round tube of the same weight in one direction, fits slim spaces and looks lighter, which is why it is used for handrails, furniture, sunshades, boat rails, bicycle frames and heat exchangers. This guide covers alloys, tempers, sizes, section properties, tolerances, finishes and how to order.

Overview

What is oval aluminium tubing?

An oval tube has two different outside dimensions: a major axis (the long dimension) and a minor axis (the short one). In a true elliptical tube the outline is a smooth ellipse; in a flat-sided or flat-oval tube, two straight parallel faces are joined by semicircular ends. Both are made from the same alloys as round tube.

The shape changes how the tube behaves. Material is placed further from the centre along the major axis, so the tube resists bending much better in that plane and less well across it. It also presents a narrow face to wind or water flow, packs closely in rows (as in a radiator core) and gives handrails and furniture a slimmer, more comfortable profile.

Oval tube is made in two main ways. Extrusion pushes a heated billet through a die, either around a mandrel (seamless) or through a porthole die that welds the metal back together around a bridge (structural or porthole tube). Drawing then pulls extruded or round tube through an oval die to reach closer tolerances, thinner walls and a better surface. Round tube can also be formed into an oval by rolling, which suits short runs.

When oval beats round tube

  • Loads act mainly in one plane, such as a handrail, ladder rail or frame member
  • Space is limited in one direction, as in radiator cores or slim furniture
  • Wind or water drag should be reduced, as on sunshades and boat towers
  • Appearance matters: oval sections look lighter and feel better in the hand

For most architectural and furniture uses, 6063-T5 offers the best combination of extrudability, surface and cost. Choose 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 when the tube carries real structural load or will be welded into frames, 5052 when seawater or chemicals are involved, 3003 when the tube will be bent tightly or brazed into heat exchangers, and 7075 only when strength-to-weight is critical and the part can be protected from corrosion.

Product types

Types of oval tube and oval extrusions

Standard elliptical tube

Smooth ellipse, usually extruded 6063 or 6061. The general type for handrails, furniture and frames.

Flat-sided (flat-oval) tube

Two parallel flats joined by round ends. Higher moment resistance and easier to fix to flat surfaces; the standard shape for radiator and condenser tubes. See our flat-oval radiator tube guide.

Seamless drawn oval tube

Drawn from seamless stock for close tolerances, thin walls and pressure duties; used for hydraulic and aerospace lines and precision parts.

Oval extrusions with features

Ovals with screw ports, slots, grooves, threaded channels or internal webs, such as handrail ovals with a glass or infill slot and furniture ovals with fixing channels.

Large and heavy-wall ovals

Structural ovals for columns, towers and sunshade blades, often 6061-T6 or 6082-T6.

Bent, telescopic and tapered ovals

Ovals bent to radius, nested telescopic sizes for poles and ladders, and oval-to-round transitions formed at the ends.

Tolerances and quality

Tolerances, inspection and quality

Oval tubes are toleranced on major axis, minor axis, wall thickness, straightness, twist and length. Extruded ovals follow ANSI H35.2 tables for ASTM orders and EN 755-9 or EN 755-8 style tolerances for EN orders; drawn ovals can achieve tighter limits. The table summarises how the two routes differ; confirm the actual limits on the drawing for each size.

Each delivery includes a mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1. Dimensional checks are carried out against the drawing; eddy-current or pressure testing can be added for seamless tube in fluid service.

Finishing and processing

Finishing, bending and fabrication

Anodising

Clear or coloured films of about 10 – 25 µm improve wear and corrosion resistance; 6063 gives the most uniform result.

Powder coating and PVDF

Powder at about 60 – 120 µm for furniture and railings; PVDF (fluorocarbon) for long-life exterior architecture.

Brushing and polishing

Linear brushed or bright-polished finishes for consumer and marine products.

Bending

Ovals can be bent about either axis, but bending about the minor axis (the easy way) is far simpler. Use mandrels and form in softer tempers (O, T4) where radii are tight, then age if needed.

Cutting and drilling

Saw, laser or CNC cutting to length and profile; CNC drilling and punching of fixing holes.

Welding

TIG and MIG with 4043 or 5356 filler for 6xxx ovals. Expect strength loss in the heat-affected zone of T6 tubes.

Avoiding problems with thin-wall ovals

  • Bend in O or T4 temper and age afterwards when radii are tight
  • Support the inside with a mandrel or filler to stop the flat faces collapsing
  • Orient the oval correctly on the drawing: state which axis carries the load
  • Protect finished faces with film during bending and fabrication
Ordering

How to specify and order oval tubing

  1. Define the useStructural, decorative, fluid-carrying or heat-exchange; indoor, outdoor or marine.
  2. Pick the shape and sizeElliptical or flat-sided; major axis, minor axis, wall and length, or a drawing for custom ovals.
  3. Choose alloy and temper6063-T5 for decorative, 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 for structural, 5052 for marine, 3003 for forming and brazing.
  4. Choose the processExtruded for general use and large sizes; drawn seamless for tight tolerances, thin walls or pressure service.
  5. Name the standard and finishASTM B221, B210, B241 or EN 755 / EN 754; anodising, powder or mill finish.
  6. Agree packing and termsWrapped bundles or wooden cases with support every 1 – 1.5 m for long tubes; EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP.

FAQ

Oval aluminium (aluminum) tubing: frequently asked questions

Is oval tube stronger than round tube?

In one direction. At equal weight, an oval tube is stiffer and stronger when bent in the plane of its major axis and weaker across it. A 40 × 20 × 1.5 mm oval is about 50 % stiffer than a round tube of the same weight in its strong direction.

What is the difference between elliptical and flat-oval tube?

Elliptical tube has a continuously curved outline. Flat-oval tube has two straight parallel sides joined by round ends, which gives more moment resistance, easier fixing to flat surfaces and close packing in heat-exchanger cores.

Which alloy is best for oval handrails?

6063-T5 or T6 for indoor and general outdoor handrails, because it anodises and powder-coats well. Use 6061-T6 for higher loads and 5052 or anodised 6061 near seawater.

Can oval aluminium tube be bent?

Yes. Bending about the minor axis is easiest. Tight bends need mandrel support and a soft temper (O or T4), followed by ageing if full strength is required.

How do I calculate the weight of an oval tube?

Weight per metre equals cross-sectional area (mm²) × 0.0027. For an elliptical tube, area is π × (a·b − (a−t)(b−t)) with a and b the outside half-axes and t the wall. A 40 × 20 × 1.5 mm oval weighs about 0.36 kg/m.

Do you make custom oval extrusions?

Yes. Ovals with slots, screw ports, grooves or internal webs can be extruded from a new die made to your drawing. Send a CAD drawing, alloy, finish and annual quantity.
