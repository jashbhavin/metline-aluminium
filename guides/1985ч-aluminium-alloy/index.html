1985ч (1985ch) Aluminium Alloy: Marine Sheet and Plate

1985ч (often written 1985ch in Latin script) is a Russian heat-treatable aluminium alloy with high magnesium and a zinc addition, made for hull plating and other welded marine structures. It is supplied mainly as sheet and thick plate under GOST R 56371-2015 and GOST R 56370-2015, in the 1985чАТ (clad) and 1985чТ (unclad) quenched and naturally aged conditions. This guide decodes the designation, the tempers and the data you need to order it.

Overview

What is 1985ч aluminium alloy?

1985ч is a wrought alloy in the Russian 19xx family, the group reserved for zinc-and-magnesium-bearing aluminium alloys. Unlike 1980, where zinc is the larger addition, 1985ч carries about 5 – 6 % magnesium with roughly 2.2 – 2.8 % zinc. The result behaves like a strong AMg-type marine alloy that can also be hardened by heat treatment.

The suffix "ч" (Cyrillic "ch") stands for chistyi, meaning "pure". In Russian practice it marks a higher-purity version of a grade with tighter limits on iron, silicon and other impurities. That is why the same alloy appears as 1985ч, 1985ch, 1985Ch or "1985 ч" in different documents; they all refer to the same material.

The alloy was developed for shipbuilding, where hull plating must be weldable, tough and resistant to seawater while saving as much weight as possible. A small beryllium addition protects the high-magnesium melt from oxidation during casting, and manganese, chromium and zirconium keep the grain structure fine. Copper is held very low, because even small amounts reduce corrosion resistance in seawater.

In short

  • High-magnesium Al-Mg-Zn alloy that responds to quenching and natural ageing
  • Supplied as marine sheet, thick plate, strip and ribbed panels
  • Clad (А) and unclad versions exist; the letters describe cladding and heat treatment
  • No direct ISO, EN or ASTM counterpart, so substitutes need design approval

Designations

How to read 1985чА, 1985чАТ and 1985чТ

Russian temper codes are letters added after the grade. They are not the same as ANSI tempers, so translate them carefully on purchase documents.

CodeMeaningTypical product
1985чАNormal cladding (А). Describes the surface layer only, not a heat treatmentClad sheet in the as-produced condition
1985чАТNormal cladding + quenched and naturally aged (Т)Marine sheet and plate
1985чТNo cladding, quenched and naturally agedThick marine plate

Natural ageing continues for some time after quenching, so the measured strength depends on how long after quenching the test is made. Some acceptance values in the 1985ч standards are tied to a stated natural-ageing period. State the temper and, if required, the test timing on your order so the mill certificate matches your acceptance procedure.

Working with 1985ч

Welding, forming and protection

Welding

High-Mg Al-Mg-Zn alloys weld by MIG and TIG with Al-Mg fillers chosen under the approved welding procedure. Low copper and zirconium additions help limit hot cracking.

Forming

Bend and roll plate in the condition and radius set by the yard procedure. Heavy cold work after ageing reduces ductility.

Cladding

Clad (А) material carries a thin, more anodic surface layer that sacrificially protects the core; avoid grinding it away on finished surfaces.

Corrosion

High-magnesium alloys can become sensitised by long exposure to warm temperatures, which raises the risk of intergranular corrosion. Keep service temperatures moderate and follow coating schemes.

Machining

Machines like other medium-strength marine alloys; sharp tools and good chip clearance prevent smearing.

Galvanic contact

Insulate from steel, stainless fasteners and copper alloys in seawater with washers, sleeves and sealants.

Applications

Where 1985ч is used

  • Shipbuilding: hull structural plates, superstructure plating, reinforcement plates, ribbed panels and repair replacement plates
  • Aviation-related structures: structural and reinforcement plates and integral stiffened panels where low weight and stability matter
  • Industrial structures: equipment panels, support plates, machining blanks and cut-to-size plates in marine atmospheres

Where a project does not specifically require 1985ч, Western marine grades such as 5083, 5383 or 5059 are usually easier to source and approve. Metline supplies those grades as well and can help compare them.

FAQ

1985ч (1985ch) aluminium alloy: frequently asked questions

Are 1985ч and 1985ch the same material?

Yes. "ч" is the Cyrillic letter transliterated as "ch". It marks a higher-purity version of the grade. 1985ч, 1985ch and 1985 ч all refer to the same alloy.

What is the difference between 1985чАТ and 1985чТ?

Both are quenched and naturally aged (Т). 1985чАТ also has normal cladding (А) on the surface for added corrosion protection; 1985чТ is unclad and is used mainly for thick plate.

What are common 1985ч thick plate sizes?

Thick plate to GOST R 56370-2015 is typically 12 – 35 mm thick, 1200 or 1500 mm wide and 2000 – 7000 mm long; 20 × 1500 × 4000 mm is a common size.

Can 1985ч be used for marine applications?

Yes, that is its main purpose. It is used for hull, superstructure and reinforcement plating in welded ship structures, with suitable coatings and galvanic isolation.

Does 1985ч have an ASTM or EN equivalent?

No direct equivalent exists. High-magnesium marine grades such as 5083, 5383 and 5059 fill similar roles but are non-heat-treatable, so substitution needs approval from the designer or classification society.

Can I enquire without complete drawings?

Yes. Grade, temper, thickness, approximate size, quantity and the application are enough for a first review; drawings can follow for cut parts.
