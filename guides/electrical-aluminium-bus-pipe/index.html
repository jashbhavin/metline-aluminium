Electrical Aluminium Tubular Bus Pipe

Tubular bus pipe is an aluminium tube used as a rigid conductor, mainly for outdoor substation bus, generator connections and high-current indoor runs. A tube puts the metal where AC current actually flows and where bending stiffness is greatest, so it spans further between insulators and runs cooler than a flat bar of the same weight. This guide covers alloy choice, standard sizes, electrical and structural data, fabrication and ordering.

Overview

Why bus conductors are made as tubes

In an alternating-current conductor, current crowds towards the surface (skin effect). In a thick flat bar or solid rod the centre carries little current, so part of the metal is wasted. A tube with a wall of sensible thickness uses nearly all its cross-section, which keeps AC resistance close to DC resistance.

The same geometry is ideal structurally. For a given weight, a round tube has a far higher moment of inertia than a flat bar or solid rod, and it is equally stiff in every direction. That lets substation designers use longer spans between post insulators, reducing the number of supports, foundations and connectors. The round surface has no sharp edges, which lowers the surface electric field and the risk of corona at high voltage, and the large surface area dissipates heat well.

Tubular bus is therefore the standard rigid conductor for air-insulated substations from medium voltage up to extra-high voltage, and is also used for generator and transformer connections, rectifier and furnace bus, and indoor high-current runs in industrial plants.

Advantages of tubular bus

  • Low AC resistance ratio because the wall carries current evenly
  • High stiffness in all directions, so longer spans and fewer insulators
  • Smooth round surface that limits corona and radio interference
  • Large cooling surface and natural convection inside and outside the tube

Most utility specifications call for 6101-T6 or 6063-T6. 6101 gives roughly 3 – 4 percentage points more conductivity than 6063 for a similar strength level; 6063 is often easier to source in large diameters. 1350 conducts best but is too soft for long outdoor spans and is mainly chosen for indoor bus that is supported at short intervals.

Mechanical minimums for 6101 tubes are set by ASTM B317/B317M; for 6063 and 6061 pipe by ASTM B241/B241M or B429/B429M. The mill certificate states the measured values for the lot.

Engineering

Design checks: current, spans, vibration and expansion

  1. Current ratingAmpacity depends on ambient temperature, solar radiation, wind, emissivity and permitted conductor temperature. IEEE 605 gives a method for outdoor tubular bus; manufacturers' tables based on it are a starting point.
  2. DeflectionGravity sag is checked against a limit, commonly a fraction of the span or of the tube diameter set by the utility. Deflection of a simply supported tube is proportional to the fourth power of span and inversely to the moment of inertia, so a modest diameter increase allows a much longer span.
  3. Short-circuit forceElectromagnetic forces between phases during a fault load the tube and insulators; tube strength (yield of the chosen temper) and insulator cantilever rating must both be checked.
  4. Wind and iceOutdoor bus must carry wind pressure and, in cold regions, radial ice; these often govern span in exposed sites.
  5. Aeolian vibrationLight, steady winds can make long spans vibrate. A common remedy is a length of stranded conductor placed loosely inside the tube as a damper, or purpose-made dampers.
  6. Thermal expansionAluminium grows about 2.3 mm per 10 m for every 10 °C of temperature change. Use sliding clamps and flexible expansion connectors so movement does not load the insulators.
Processing

Cutting, bending, welding and finishing bus pipe

Cutting and machining

Tubes are saw-cut square, deburred and can be drilled or slotted for fittings and drain holes. Ends can be machined for welded couplers.

Bending

Bending success depends on ductility, the ratio of wall to diameter and the bending method. Seamless tube in a ductile temper bends more reliably than extruded structural pipe with seam welds from a porthole die.

Welding

MIG and TIG with argon or argon-helium shielding. 4043 filler is common for 6xxx bus; joints are solvent-cleaned and wire-brushed, and parts may be pre-dried at low temperature to remove moisture. Tack welding controls alignment.

Weld strength

Welding locally reduces the strength of T6 tempers, so couplers or sleeves are often used at joints, and joints are placed away from the point of maximum bending moment.

Contact surfaces

Clamp and terminal areas are cleaned, brushed and coated with oxide-inhibiting compound; tin or nickel plating of ends gives a stable contact to copper equipment terminals.

Protection

Anodising, insulating sleeves or heat-shrink are used where tubes must be touched-safe or protected from aggressive atmospheres.

System components

Fittings used with tubular bus

A tubular bus system needs more than tube. These are the parts buyers usually specify alongside the conductor; Metline supplies the aluminium tube, bar and plate from which they are made and can machine aluminium parts to drawing.

  • Couplers and sleeves: join tube lengths and restore strength at welded or bolted joints
  • Fixed and sliding support clamps: hold the tube on post insulators while allowing thermal movement at one end
  • Expansion connectors: flexible laminated or braided links between tube sections and equipment
  • T-connectors and angle fittings: branch the bus to disconnectors, breakers and transformers
  • Terminal pads: flat palms drilled to NEMA or IEC hole patterns for bolting to equipment terminals
  • End caps and corona balls: close tube ends and reduce electric field at high voltage
Where it is used

Applications of aluminium bus pipe

Air-insulated substations

Main and transfer bus, equipment interconnections and jumpers from 11 kV to extra-high voltage.

Power stations

Generator and transformer connections carrying very high currents at short spans.

Industrial plants

Furnace, rectifier and electrolysis bus, plus indoor high-current distribution.

Renewable plants

Collector substations for wind and solar farms, where weight and corrosion resistance help on remote sites.

Railway and traction

Substation bus feeding traction networks.

Earthing

Ground conductors and risers where a rigid, corrosion-resistant conductor is required.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) bus pipe: frequently asked questions

Which alloy is best for tubular bus pipe?

6101-T6 is the dedicated bus-conductor alloy, with at least 55 % IACS conductivity and good strength. 6063-T6 is a close alternative with slightly lower conductivity. 1350 conducts best but is too soft for long outdoor spans.

Why use a tube instead of a flat busbar outdoors?

A tube carries AC current evenly, is equally stiff in every direction and has no sharp edges. It spans further between insulators, resists wind and fault forces, and limits corona at high voltage.

What is the weight of 4 inch Schedule 40 aluminium bus pipe?

NPS 4 Schedule 40 has a 114.3 mm OD and 6.02 mm wall, a cross-section of about 2,050 mm² and a weight of about 5.5 kg per metre (3.7 lb/ft).

How do you stop long tubular bus spans from vibrating?

Light steady winds can cause aeolian vibration. Designers usually insert a loose length of stranded conductor inside the tube as a damper, or fit purpose-made dampers, following IEEE 605 guidance.

Should I order seamless or extruded structural pipe?

Seamless pipe to ASTM B241 is preferred if tubes will be bent or highly stressed. Extruded structural pipe is acceptable for straight runs where the specification allows it.

Can you supply bus pipe with plated ends and cut lengths?

Yes. Metline supplies bus pipe cut to length, drilled, bent and with tin- or nickel-plated contact ends on request, with mill test certificates and export packing, worldwide.
