Spiral Welded Aluminium Pipe

Spiral welded aluminium pipe is made by winding a strip or narrow plate of 5xxx alloy into a helix and welding the seam, which produces large diameters (about 219 to 1580 mm) from material much narrower than the pipe circumference. Because the weld runs at an angle, it carries less of the hoop stress than a straight seam. This guide covers how the pipe is made, alloys, sizes and weights, testing and where it is used.

Overview

What is spiral welded aluminium pipe?

Seamless aluminium pipe is extruded, and extrusion presses limit how large it can be. Above roughly 500 – 700 mm diameter, almost all aluminium pipe is fabricated from plate or strip and welded. There are two ways to do it: bend a full-width plate into a cylinder and weld one straight (longitudinal) seam, or wind a narrower strip around at an angle and weld a continuous helical seam. The second method is spiral welding.

Spiral welding is familiar from large steel line pipe, but aluminium is harder to do well: the metal conducts heat rapidly, has a tenacious oxide film and distorts easily, so forming and seam alignment must be tightly controlled. Mills that make aluminium spiral pipe use either inert-gas arc welding (MIG/TIG) or friction stir welding, a solid-state process that joins the metal below its melting point and leaves a fine-grained weld with low distortion.

The products are almost always non-heat-treatable Al-Mg alloys such as 5052, 5754 and 5083 in the O or H112 condition. These alloys weld without losing strength relative to the annealed base metal, resist corrosion in water, seawater and industrial atmospheres, and stay tough at cryogenic temperatures.

Why choose spiral welded pipe

  • Diameters beyond the reach of extrusion presses, from about 219 mm up to around 1580 mm
  • One strip width can make many diameters by changing the forming angle
  • Continuous production, so long pipe lengths with fewer girth welds on site
  • Helical seam sees a lower share of hoop stress than a longitudinal seam
Process

How spiral welded aluminium pipe is made

  1. Strip preparationCoil or plate of the chosen alloy is levelled, edge-trimmed and edge-prepared so that the seam meets with consistent fit-up.
  2. Helical formingThe strip is fed into a forming head at a set angle and rolled into a continuous helix. The angle and strip width together decide the diameter.
  3. Seam weldingThe helical seam is welded inside and/or outside by MIG/TIG with Al-Mg filler, or by friction stir welding.
  4. Cut-offA travelling saw cuts the endless pipe to the ordered length, commonly 6 – 12 m and longer where transport allows.
  5. Finishing and testingEnds are faced and bevelled, the weld is inspected (visual, penetrant, radiographic or ultrasonic), and pipes can be hydrostatically tested.

Bends, tees, reducers and end caps for spiral pipe systems are fabricated separately from the same alloys, usually as segmented mitre bends or pressed fittings.

Engineering

Why a helical seam carries less stress

In a thin-walled pipe under internal pressure, the hoop (circumferential) stress is twice the axial stress. A longitudinal seam lies along the pipe axis, so it is loaded by the full hoop stress. A helical seam lies at an angle φ to the axis, and the stress acting across it is σn = σhoop × (1 − ½ sin²φ).

The helix angle follows from the strip width B and pipe diameter D: cos φ = B / (πD). The table shows the result for a few combinations.

In practice the seam of a spiral pipe sees roughly 50 – 75 % of the hoop stress, compared with 100 % for a straight seam. That margin is useful, but design codes still apply the weld joint efficiency set by the inspection level, so the pipe wall is not automatically thinner. The real gain is a more forgiving joint and fewer site welds.

Alloys and mechanical properties

Values below are EN 485-2 minimums for O/H111 sheet and plate, which govern the strip used to make the pipe. H112 material is supplied as rolled and carries similar or slightly higher values; actual results appear on the certificate.

AlloyTensile strength Rm (MPa)0.2 % proof Rp0.2 (MPa, min)Main traits

Because O-temper material is already fully annealed, the heat of welding does not lower its strength, so the welded pipe keeps close to base-metal properties. Alloys with more than about 3 % magnesium, such as 5083, should not be used for long periods above about 65 °C, where they can become sensitive to intergranular corrosion; 5754 or 5454 is chosen for warm service. Filler metal is normally ER5356 or ER5183 for 5083.

Quality

Inspection, testing and codes

  • Dimensional checks: OD at the ends, ovality, straightness, wall thickness and end squareness against the order
  • Weld inspection: visual and dye-penetrant as standard; radiography or ultrasonic testing of the helical seam for pressure duty
  • Hydrostatic test: each pipe or a sample tested to a multiple of design pressure, per the governing code
  • Procedure qualification: welding procedures and welders qualified to ISO 15614-2 / ISO 9606-2 (arc) or ISO 25239 (friction stir)
  • Piping codes: EN 13480-8 for aluminium industrial piping or ASME B31.3 for process piping, as the project requires
  • Certificates: EN 10204 3.1 for strip material and pipe; third-party inspection on request
Applications

Where spiral welded aluminium pipe is used

Cryogenic plants

5083-O pipe for air-separation units, LNG and gas-processing plants, where aluminium stays tough at very low temperatures.

Gas-insulated lines

Large aluminium enclosure tubes for gas-insulated transmission lines and busbar ducts.

Marine and offshore

Seawater intake and discharge, ventilation trunks and structural tubes on vessels and platforms.

Process ducting

Large ducts for corrosive gases, fume extraction and ventilation where steel would corrode.

Water and outfall

Lightweight large-bore pipe for cooling water and outfalls, easy to handle on site.

Tanks and shells

Cylindrical shells for storage vessels and silos, built to the applicable pressure-vessel code.

FAQ

Spiral welded aluminium (aluminum) pipe: frequently asked questions

What is the largest aluminium pipe diameter available?

Spiral welding can produce aluminium pipe up to about 1580 mm OD. Larger shells are possible as rolled-and-welded fabrications from plate.

Is spiral welded pipe weaker than seamless pipe?

Not in the O-temper 5xxx alloys normally used: welding does not reduce their annealed strength, and the helical seam sees less hoop stress. Design pressure still depends on the code and the weld inspection level.

Which alloy is best for cryogenic service?

5083-O is the standard choice. It keeps its toughness at liquid-nitrogen and LNG temperatures and welds well with ER5183 filler.

Can spiral welded pipe be used for hot fluids?

For sustained service above about 65 °C, choose 5754 or 5454 rather than 5083, because high-magnesium alloys can become sensitised to intergranular corrosion.

How long are the pipes?

Typically 6 – 12 m, cut from continuous production. Longer lengths are possible if transport and handling allow.

Can you supply matching fittings?

Yes. Elbows, tees, reducers and heads in the same alloy can be supplied with the pipe, along with mill certificates.
