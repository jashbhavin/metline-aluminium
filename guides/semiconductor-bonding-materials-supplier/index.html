Semiconductor Die-to-Package Bonding Materials: How to Choose Wire or Ribbon

Die-to-package bonding materials are the fine wires and flat ribbons that carry signals and current from the pads on a chip to the leadframe, substrate or module terminals. The right choice depends on four things: the bonding process (ball or wedge), the current each connection carries, the pad metallisation and the reliability test the package must pass. This guide maps those decisions across gold, copper, silver and aluminium. Metline supplies the aluminium wire and ribbon; the other metals are explained for comparison.

Overview

What die-to-package bonding materials do

Inside almost every packaged semiconductor, the chip is glued or soldered to a pad and then connected electrically by metal links welded to its bond pads. Wire bonding remains the most widely used way of making those links, even as flip-chip and copper-clip interconnects grow, because it is flexible, mature and cheap per connection. The material in the link has to be soft enough to weld without damaging the chip, strong enough to survive moulding and handling, and stable enough to last decades of temperature swings.

Two formats cover nearly all wire-bonded packages. Round bonding wire ranges from fine wire around 15 – 50 µm in diameter, used for signal connections in ICs, LEDs and discrete devices, up to heavy wire of 100 – 500 µm that carries tens of amps in power modules. Bonding ribbon is a flat strip, typically a few tenths of a millimetre thick and up to about 2 mm wide, which replaces several heavy wires in high-current or high-frequency connections.

Four metals are used for these links. Gold was the original standard and is still chosen where process stability and corrosion resistance matter most. Copper, usually palladium coated, has replaced gold in most high-volume IC packages on cost. Silver alloy wire serves LED and some memory packages. Aluminium, either as fine aluminium-silicon wire or as heavy high-purity wire and ribbon, dominates wedge bonding and power electronics.

ThroughputVery high for fine wireFewer bonds needed per high-current path

In power modules the choice is often between several heavy aluminium wires in parallel and one or two aluminium ribbons. Ribbon reduces the number of bonds and the stack height and spreads current more evenly across the die metallisation; heavy wire is more forgiving of layout changes and works on standard heavy-wire bonders. Our wire bonding vs ribbon bonding article goes deeper into that trade-off.

A strong reason to use aluminium is metallurgical: most silicon and silicon-carbide chips have aluminium or aluminium-alloy pad metallisation. An aluminium wedge bond to an aluminium pad is a monometallic joint with no intermetallic growth, so it does not suffer the Kirkendall voiding that can affect gold-aluminium bonds at high temperature. Gold and copper ball bonds on aluminium pads rely on well-controlled intermetallic layers instead, which is why their reliability testing focuses on high-temperature storage and humidity.

What Metline supplies

Aluminium bonding wire and ribbon options

Al-1 % Si fine wire

Aluminium with about 1 % silicon, drawn to fine diameters (typically from about 18 µm to 75 µm) for ultrasonic wedge bonding in hybrids, sensors, COB and discrete devices. The silicon raises strength so the wire can be drawn fine and handled reliably.

High-purity heavy wire

4N to 5N aluminium in diameters of roughly 100 – 500 µm for power semiconductors. High purity keeps the wire soft, which protects the die during bonding, and gives low resistance.

Alloyed and corrosion-resistant heavy wire

Doped aluminium wires (for example with small additions of nickel or magnesium) offer higher strength, better power-cycling life or corrosion resistance for demanding automotive and traction modules.

Aluminium ribbon

Flat 4N/5N aluminium strip, typically 0.5 – 2.0 mm wide and 0.1 – 0.3 mm thick, with smooth, burr-free edges for ultrasonic ribbon bonding in power modules and battery packs.

Spooling and packing

Wire and ribbon wound on standard spools for automatic bonders, sealed and packed to limit contamination and oxidation in transit.

Production

How bonding wire and ribbon are made

  1. Raw material and alloy designHigh-purity metal (for aluminium, typically 4N to 5N) is selected and alloying or doping elements are added in tightly controlled amounts, often in parts per million.
  2. Melting and continuous castingThe metal is melted and cast into rod under controlled atmosphere or vacuum, so that inclusions and gas porosity do not later cause wire breaks.
  3. Drawing or rollingRod is drawn through a long series of diamond dies to the final diameter; ribbon is rolled flat from wire or slit from thin strip.
  4. AnnealingIntermediate and final anneals set grain size, breaking load and elongation. These properties decide how the wire loops and bonds, so they are controlled to narrow ranges.
  5. Surface treatment and cleaningCoatings (such as palladium on copper wire) are applied where used, and the surface is cleaned of drawing lubricant and particles.
  6. Spooling and inspectionThe wire is precision wound so it pays off smoothly on the bonder, then tested for diameter, breaking load, elongation and surface quality before sealed packing.

When changing material or supplier, compare like for like: same diameter, breaking load and elongation window, the same pad metallisation, and the same bonder settings as a starting point. A small difference in elongation or hardness can change loop shape and the ultrasonic energy needed, so a short design-of-experiments on the bonder is normal before qualification.

Ordering

Information to send with a bonding material enquiry

  • Package and device: IC, LED, discrete, IGBT, SiC module, battery pack or hybrid, with the end application
  • Material and format: wire or ribbon; purity, alloy or dopant; any current part number or datasheet
  • Dimensions: wire diameter in µm or mil (1 mil = 25.4 µm), or ribbon width and thickness
  • Mechanical properties: breaking load and elongation range
  • Pad information: pad metallisation (Al, AlSi, AlCu, Ni/Au, Cu) and pad size
  • Process: ball, wedge or ribbon bonding; bonder model and tool type
  • Reliability targets: pull and shear values, temperature cycling, power cycling or humidity tests
  • Supply: spool type, length per spool, sample quantity and expected volume

FAQ

Semiconductor bonding materials: frequently asked questions

What are die-to-package bonding materials?

They are the fine wires and ribbons, usually of gold, copper, silver or aluminium, that are welded to the bond pads of a chip and to the leadframe, substrate or terminals to make its electrical connections inside the package.

When should I use ribbon instead of round wire?

Use ribbon when one connection must carry high current with low resistance and inductance, or when package height is tight, as in IGBT and SiC power modules, battery interconnects and RF circuits. Round wire is better for fine-pitch pads and complex loop shapes.

Why is aluminium used for power module bonding?

Aluminium bonds ultrasonically at room temperature, forms a monometallic joint with the aluminium pads on most power chips, is soft enough to protect the die and costs far less than gold. Heavy aluminium wire and ribbon are the established solution for power cycling performance.

Which materials work for ball bonding and which for wedge bonding?

Gold, copper and silver alloy wires are used mainly in ball bonding, where a free air ball is formed at the wire tip. Aluminium and Al-1 % Si wire, and all ribbons, are used in wedge bonding.

Can aluminium bonding wire replace gold wire?

Only if the process changes from ball to wedge bonding and the package layout allows it. Aluminium is widely used for wedge-bonded hybrids, sensors and power devices, but high-volume fine-pitch IC packages normally use gold or copper ball bonding.

Does Metline supply gold, copper or silver bonding wire?

No. Metline supplies aluminium bonding wire and ribbon only. Gold, copper and silver are covered on this page as comparisons to help you choose where aluminium fits your package.
