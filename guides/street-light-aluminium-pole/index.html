Aluminium Street Light Poles: Alloys, Tube Sizes & Design

Most aluminium street light poles are made from seamless extruded 6063 or 6061 tube that is welded to a base and then heat-treated to the T6 temper. This guide explains which alloy suits which pole, how wind load and weld zones drive the design, and what to specify when you buy the tube, plate and extrusions. Metline supplies these aluminium materials to pole manufacturers and fabricators worldwide; we do not make or install finished poles.

Overview

Why aluminium is used for lighting poles

Aluminium has about one third the density of steel, so an aluminium lighting pole is much lighter than a steel pole for the same duty; it also needs no galvanising and does not rust. Those three facts explain why municipalities, highway authorities and private developers specify aluminium for street, car-park, park and coastal lighting.

The weight saving matters twice: once in freight and handling, because a crew can often lift a 6 m aluminium shaft by hand, and again on site, where a lighter pole needs a smaller foundation and a lighter crane. Aluminium forms a thin, self-renewing oxide film, so an uncoated pole keeps its structural section for decades, even near the sea or where de-icing salt is used. Painted or anodised poles hold their finish well because there is no rust creeping under the coating.

Aluminium does have trade-offs. Its modulus of elasticity (about 69 GPa) is one third of steel, so an aluminium shaft must have a larger diameter or thicker wall to limit deflection and vibration. Welding reduces the strength of heat-treated alloys locally, which is why quality pole makers heat-treat the whole shaft after the base is welded on. Good pole design is mostly about managing these two points.

What aluminium brings to a lighting column

  • High strength-to-weight ratio, so poles are easier to ship, lift and install
  • No red rust and no need for hot-dip galvanising
  • Good passive safety: aluminium columns can be designed to deform and absorb energy in a vehicle impact
  • Fully recyclable at end of life, with high scrap value
Alloy selection

Which aluminium alloy for which pole?

Pole shafts use heat-treatable 6xxx alloys because they extrude well into seamless tube and reach useful strength in the T6 temper. The choice between them is a balance of strength, surface finish and cost.

Engineering

Design points: wind, deflection, fatigue and welds

Wind load

The governing load on most poles is wind on the luminaire, bracket and shaft. Designers sum the effective projected area (EPA) of the fittings and check bending at the base. Europe uses EN 40-3-1 for loads and EN 40-6 for aluminium columns; North America uses the AASHTO LRFD specification for structural supports for signs, luminaires and traffic signals.

Deflection and stiffness

Because aluminium is more flexible than steel, deflection at the top often controls the section rather than strength. A larger diameter is a far more efficient fix than a thicker wall, since stiffness rises with the cube of diameter.

Fatigue and vibration

Wind can excite vortex-shedding vibration in tall, slender poles. Weld details at the base and at handhole openings are the usual fatigue hot spots; smooth transitions, reinforced handholes and vibration dampers are standard remedies.

Welds and heat treatment

The best practice is to weld the shaft to its base in the T4 or as-extruded condition and then solution heat-treat and age the whole assembly to T6. Welding to AWS D1.2 (North America) or EN 1090-3 (Europe) is normally specified.

Passive safety

Roadside poles in Europe may need classification under EN 12767, which rates how a support behaves in a vehicle impact. Aluminium columns are often chosen because they can be designed to absorb energy or shear away predictably.

Galvanic contact

Steel anchor bolts, stainless fixings and copper earthing parts must be isolated from the aluminium with washers, sleeves or coatings, and concrete contact below ground should be protected with a bituminous coating.

Surface finishing

Finishes for aluminium poles

Mill-finish aluminium weathers to a uniform matt grey and needs no coating for protection. Most specifiers still choose a finish for appearance and to keep the surface clean.

  • Powder coating: the most common finish, in black, grey, green or any RAL colour; use a pretreatment and powder qualified to a recognised architectural standard for outdoor use
  • Anodising: clear or bronze anodic films, at least 20 – 25 µm for exterior exposure; 6063 gives the most even appearance
  • Brushed or satin finish: mechanical finish, usually followed by clear anodising or lacquer
  • Root protection: bitumen or epoxy on the buried section of direct-embedded poles

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) street light poles: FAQ

Which is better for a street light pole, 6063 or 6061?

6063-T6 is the usual choice for standard street and car-park poles because it extrudes with a better surface and is easier to finish. 6061-T6 is about 30 % stronger and is chosen for taller poles, high wind zones, mast arms and sign supports where a thinner wall saves weight.

Do aluminium poles lose strength when welded?

Yes, locally. The heat-affected zone of a T6 alloy drops to roughly half its strength. Good practice is to weld the base on first and then heat-treat the whole pole back to T6, or to design the base using the reduced welded strength.

How long does an aluminium street light pole last?

Aluminium does not rust, so the structural section is preserved for decades with little maintenance. Service life depends mostly on design against fatigue, protection of the buried section, isolation from steel and copper, and the condition of the finish.

What standard covers aluminium lighting columns?

In Europe, EN 40-6 covers aluminium lighting columns, with loads and verification in EN 40-3. In North America, the AASHTO LRFD specification for structural supports for signs, luminaires and traffic signals applies, with tube to ASTM B221 or B429 and welding to AWS D1.2.

Can Metline supply finished poles?

No. Metline supplies the aluminium materials, such as seamless tube, extrusions, plate, sheet and bar, cut to length or machined to drawing. Pole manufacturers carry out tapering, welding, heat treatment, finishing and assembly.

How heavy is an aluminium light pole tube?

A 114.3 mm OD × 3.2 mm wall tube weighs about 3.0 kg per metre, so a 6 m shaft is roughly 18 kg before the base and bracket. A 152.4 × 4.8 mm tube is about 6.0 kg/m.
