Structural Aluminium Tubing: How to Select Alloy, Shape and Size

Structural aluminium tubing is hollow round, square or rectangular extrusion used to carry load in frames, trusses, railings, racks and vehicle structures rather than to carry fluid under pressure. The usual grades are 6061-T6, 6063-T5/T6, 6082-T6 and 6005A-T6, made to ASTM B221 or B429 in North America and EN 755 in Europe. This guide covers the alloys, section data, design codes and buying details; Metline supplies all of these tubes worldwide.

Overview

What makes tubing "structural"?

"Structural" describes the job, not a special alloy. A structural tube is selected for bending, buckling and torsional stiffness, connection details and weight, whereas a pressure pipe is selected for burst strength and leak-tightness. The same 6061-T6 can be either; what changes is the standard, the tolerances and the inspection.

ASTM B429 makes this distinction explicit: it covers 6061 and 6063 extruded pipe and tube for structural uses such as highway and bridge rails, fence posts, handrails, sign structures, awning supports and lighting brackets, and it permits tube produced through bridge or porthole dies. It excludes pressure service, for which the buyer should use seamless tube to ASTM B210 or B241.

Closed sections are the most efficient shapes for members loaded in compression or torsion. A square tube has far higher torsional stiffness than an open channel or angle of the same weight, and a round tube buckles evenly in every direction. That is why tubes dominate machine frames, space frames, stage trusses, solar racks, boat T-tops and vehicle subframes.

Why engineers specify aluminium tube for structures

  • About one third the weight of steel tube of the same dimensions
  • Natural corrosion resistance, so no galvanising and lower maintenance
  • Extruded in almost any size or wall, including custom shapes with internal webs
  • Machines, welds, bends and anodises readily

Shapes

Tube shapes and when to use each

Round tube

Equal stiffness in every direction and the best shape for torsion and columns. Used for handrails, truss chords, masts and space frames. Supplied by OD and wall, from small drawn tube up to large extruded sizes.

Square tube

Flat faces make joints, drilling and bolting simple. Strong in both axes and very good in torsion. The default for machine bases, frames and racks.

Rectangular tube

More material away from the neutral axis in one direction, so it is efficient for beams loaded mostly in one plane, such as floor joists and trailer cross-members.

Hex and oval tube

Used where appearance, grip or anti-rotation matter: handles, tool frames and decorative structures.

Multi-cell and custom hollows

Extruded with internal webs, slots, screw bosses or flanges to cut assembly steps. Custom dies make sense when volumes justify tooling.

Open sections for comparison

Angles, channels, I-beams and T-bars are cheaper per kg and easier to connect, but much weaker in torsion than a closed tube of equal weight.

Two rules of thumb follow from the numbers. First, doubling the outside size of a square tube at the same wall raises its stiffness about seven to eight times while only doubling weight, so go bigger before going thicker. Second, because aluminium is about one third as stiff as steel, an aluminium member replacing steel usually needs a deeper section rather than the same section in a stronger alloy; no aluminium alloy is stiffer than another by any useful margin.

For ordinary structures porthole tube is fully acceptable: properly made extrusion welds have strength close to the parent metal, which is why ASTM B429 allows it. Specify seamless when a design code, a pressure duty or your customer requires it.

Local buckling

Thin walls buckle before the material yields. Codes limit width-to-thickness ratios (b/t) for each element; a 100 × 100 × 2 mm tube may be governed by buckling, not strength.

Welded strength

Heat-affected zones of 6xxx T6 tube fall to roughly half strength. Place welds at low-stress points or use bolted or riveted joints in highly stressed members.

Deflection

With a modulus near 69 GPa, check deflection limits early. Many aluminium frames are stiffness-governed.

Galvanic contact

Isolate steel bolts and brackets with washers, sleeves or coatings, especially outdoors and in marine service.

A typical light-industrial machine base uses 50 × 50 × 3 mm or 80 × 80 × 4 mm 6061-T6 square tube, bolted with corner brackets so that the frame keeps full T6 strength. A typical pedestrian handrail uses 42.4 mm or 48.3 mm OD 6063-T6 round tube, chosen for its surface and anodising response rather than strength.

Fabrication

Cutting, bending, joining and finishing

  • Cutting: carbide circular saws with lubricant; precision cut-to-length and mitre cuts are routine
  • Bending: mandrel bending in T4 or O temper, followed by ageing to T6 if strength is needed; T6 tube bends only to generous radii
  • Machining: 6061-T6 and 6082-T6 machine cleanly; 6063 is softer and can be gummy
  • Joining: MIG/TIG welding, structural adhesives, rivets, bolts and extruded corner connectors
  • Finishing: anodising (clear, colour, hard), powder coating, electrophoretic coating, brushing and polishing

FAQ

Structural aluminium (aluminum) tubing: FAQ

What is the strongest aluminium alloy for structural tubing?

7075-T6 is the strongest common grade, but it is difficult to weld and costs more. For welded structures 6061-T6 and 6082-T6 are the practical high-strength choices.

Is 6063 strong enough for structural use?

For light structures, railings, frames and racks, yes: 6063-T6 has a typical yield strength of about 214 MPa. For heavily loaded or welded members, 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 gives roughly 30 % more strength.

What is the difference between ASTM B221 and B429?

B221 is the general specification for extruded bar, rod, profiles and tube in many alloys. B429 is specific to 6061 and 6063 structural pipe and tube, allows porthole-die tube, and is not for pressure service.

Is porthole extruded tube safe for structures?

Yes. Porthole tube has solid-state extrusion welds that are close to parent-metal strength, and structural standards accept it. Seamless tube is preferred for pressure, severe fatigue or where a specification demands it.

Can aluminium tube replace steel tube in a frame?

Often, with a lighter result. Because aluminium is about one third as stiff, the replacement usually needs a deeper or wider section to match deflection. Check buckling and welded strength as well.

What sizes of structural tube are available?

Round tube from small drawn sizes to large extruded diameters, and square and rectangular tube from about 20 mm up to 150 mm or more, in walls from about 1.2 mm to over 6 mm. Non-standard sizes can be extruded to drawing.
