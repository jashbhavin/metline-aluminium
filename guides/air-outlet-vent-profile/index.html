Aluminium Profiles for Air Outlet Vents and Grilles

Air outlet vents, return grilles, louvres and linear slot diffusers are built from extruded aluminium frames and blades, usually in 6063-T5 or 6060-T5. This guide covers the profile types, alloys and tempers, tolerances, finishes and what a grille fabricator should put on a drawing. Metline supplies the extruded sections and blade strip; the finished vents are made by HVAC manufacturers and fabricators.

Overview

Why air vents are made from extruded aluminium

An air terminal device has a simple job: deliver or return air at the right velocity and direction without noise, drafts or visible streaking. Aluminium extrusion suits it because one die can produce a frame with a decorative face, a screw boss, a clip groove for a damper and a gasket channel in a single section, at low weight and with a surface that anodises or powder coats cleanly.

Compared with pressed steel grilles, aluminium vents do not rust in damp ceiling voids, bathrooms, kitchens or swimming-pool halls, and they weigh roughly a third as much for the same cross-section. Compared with plastic, they are stiffer, do not yellow, tolerate higher supply-air temperatures and meet fire-safety requirements without flame retardants. That is why the great majority of commercial supply diffusers, return grilles and external weather louvres use aluminium.

The extrusion itself is only part of the product. A grille maker cuts frames to length, mitres or notches the corners, punches blade pivot holes, inserts blades and springs, adds opposed-blade dampers and finishes the assembly. Profiles that arrive straight, with consistent wall thickness and a clean, uniform surface, make every one of these steps faster.

Profile types

Profile types used in vents, grilles and diffusers

Frame and border profiles

The visible flange around a grille. Usually 6063-T5 with a 20 – 40 mm face, a return leg for mounting and grooves for corner keys. Wall thickness typically 1.0 – 1.6 mm.

Fixed and adjustable blades

Aerofoil or flat blades set at 0° to 45° deflection. Extruded blades in 6063 give a stiff, rounded leading edge; roll-formed blades use 3003 or 1100 strip 0.5 – 1.5 mm thick.

Linear slot diffuser sections

Long multi-slot extrusions with internal channels for air-pattern controllers. They demand good straightness over 2 – 6 m lengths and accurate slot widths.

Weather louvre blades

Deep, drainable blade profiles with rain-catching hooks for external walls and plant screens. Often 6063-T6 or 6061-T6 for longer spans and wind load.

Damper and mullion sections

Opposed-blade damper blades, linkage bars and intermediate mullions that keep wide grilles flat.

Corner keys and accessories

Small solid profiles cut into corner cleats, spring clips and fixing brackets.

Typical finished products that use these sections include single- and double-deflection supply grilles, egg-crate and perforated return grilles, square and round ceiling diffusers, swirl diffusers, jet nozzles for large halls, gravity (back-draught) louvres, continuous soffit vents and gable vents. Each product type pushes the profile design in a different direction, from slim decorative frames to deep structural louvre blades.

Materials

Alloys and tempers for HVAC vent profiles

Almost all vent profiles use the 6xxx alloys that extrude easily into thin, intricate shapes. Strength demands are modest for indoor grilles, so the choice is usually driven by surface quality and finishing.

Alloy / temperMin. tensile strengthMin. 0.2 % proofWhy it is chosen
6063-T5175 MPa130 MPaStandard for frames and blades; excellent surface for anodising
6063-T6215 MPa170 MPaStiffer blades, longer slot diffusers, external louvres

6060-T6190 MPa150 MPaWhere a little more strength is needed in 6060
6061-T6260 MPa240 MPaStructural louvre mullions and heavy-duty blades
3003-H14 / 1100-H14 strip≈ 140 MPa / 110 MPa—Roll-formed blades and perforated faces

Extrusion minimums summarised from EN 755-2 for walls up to about 3 – 5 mm; ASTM B221 values are similar. Thicker sections and other tempers have different limits, shown on the mill certificate.

6063 gives the brightest, most uniform anodised appearance and the best ability to fill fine features such as narrow slots and thin decorative ribs. It is the right default. Move to 6063-T6 when long blades sag between pivots or when a linear diffuser must stay straight across a wide ceiling, and to 6061-T6 or 6005A-T6 only for external louvres that carry wind load.

Dimensions

Dimensional tolerances that matter for grilles

Vent profiles look simple but they are assembled from many parts, so small dimensional errors add up into visible gaps and loose blades. Standard tolerances for general extrusions are set by EN 755-9 or ANSI H35.2; for 6060 and 6063 precision profiles, EN 12020-2 gives tighter limits and is the better reference for decorative grille sections.

Tolerance checklist

Wall thickness
Typically ±0.10 – 0.15 mm on thin walls under EN 12020-2; state critical walls on the drawing
Slot and groove widths
Blade pivot grooves and clip channels often need ±0.10 mm or better
Straightness
Important on slot diffusers; precision profiles allow about 1 mm per metre or tighter by agreement
Twist
Long blades and slot sections must not twist, or blades bind in the frame
Angularity and flatness of face
Affects mitre fit and the visual line between frame and ceiling
Cut length
Mill lengths of 3 – 6 m, or cut-to-length with ±0.5 mm saw tolerance

Mark decorative faces on the drawing as "significant surfaces". The extruder then orients the die and handling to protect those faces from die lines, scratches and handling marks, and the finisher knows where appearance is inspected.

Matt black inner parts, such as air-pattern controllers inside a slot diffuser, hide the slot visually so that only the air gap is seen. For external weather louvres in coastal sites, specify a seaside-class pretreatment and coating system rather than a standard interior powder.

Design guidance

Designing an extrusion for a vent or diffuser

  • Keep walls uniform: sudden changes in thickness cause flow lines and uneven anodising; aim for a ratio below about 2:1 between thick and thin walls
  • Avoid knife edges: blade tips thinner than about 0.8 mm are hard to extrude straight; round them with a small radius
  • Design snap fits with clearance: allow for coating thickness (60 – 80 µm per side for powder) in clip and blade grooves
  • Add corner key channels: a consistent internal channel makes mitred frames square and strong without welding
  • Use screw bosses, not tapped walls: a split boss in the profile accepts self-tapping screws for dampers and fixings
  • Think about the circumscribing circle: keeping the section within a smaller circle lowers die cost and allows a wider choice of presses
  • Consider hollow versus semi-hollow: hollow blades are stiffer but need porthole dies, which cost more than solid dies

FAQ

Air vent aluminium (aluminum) profiles: frequently asked questions

Which aluminium alloy is used for air vent grilles?

6063-T5 is the standard alloy for frames and extruded blades because it extrudes into thin, detailed shapes and anodises or powder coats very evenly. 6060-T5 is a common European alternative, and roll-formed blades use 3003 or 1100 strip.

What wall thickness do grille profiles have?

Frames are usually 1.0 – 1.6 mm thick and extruded blades about 0.8 – 1.5 mm. External weather louvre blades are heavier, often 1.5 – 2.5 mm, because they carry wind load and span further.

Should vent profiles be anodised or powder coated?

Powder coating is the most common choice for white ceiling grilles because it matches RAL colours. Anodising is preferred for exposed architectural diffusers and matt black inner parts. Both work well on 6063.

Can you extrude a custom air vent profile from my drawing?

Yes. Send the section drawing with critical dimensions and finish. We confirm whether an existing die fits or a new die is needed, then supply samples for approval before production.

Do you supply finished grilles and diffusers?

No. Metline supplies the aluminium extrusions, strip and sheet that HVAC manufacturers and fabricators use to build vents, grilles and diffusers.

What tolerances apply to aluminum vent profiles?

General extrusions follow EN 755-9 or ANSI H35.2. Decorative 6060 and 6063 sections are better ordered to EN 12020-2 precision tolerances, with critical slot and groove widths marked on the drawing.
