Decorative Aluminium Honeycomb Panels

A decorative aluminium honeycomb panel bonds a painted, anodised or stone-faced aluminium skin to a lightweight aluminium honeycomb core and a back sheet, giving a large, very flat panel that weighs a fraction of solid sheet or stone. Architects use them for feature walls, ventilated façades, large-format ceilings and raised floors. This guide covers how the panels are built, which skins and cores to specify, typical sizes and weights, and how to choose between honeycomb, composite and solid aluminium panels. How they are built

What makes up a decorative honeycomb panel

A honeycomb panel works like an I-beam spread over an area. The two aluminium skins carry bending loads in tension and compression, and the honeycomb core holds them apart and carries shear. Doubling the core thickness roughly quadruples bending stiffness while adding very little weight.

Face skin

The visible sheet, usually 3003, 5005 or 5052 aluminium 0.7 – 1.5 mm thick, coil-coated with PVDF or FEVE paint, anodised, printed with wood or stone patterns, or faced with a thin natural stone veneer.

Honeycomb core

Expanded aluminium foil in 3003 or 5052, formed into hexagonal cells typically 6 – 19 mm across. Cell size and foil gauge set the core density, compressive strength and shear stiffness. See the aluminium honeycomb core guide.

Back skin

Usually 0.5 – 1.0 mm aluminium, thicker or reinforced for floors. Ceilings sometimes use FRP or lighter backs to save weight.

Bond

Most decorative panels are laminated with a structural adhesive film under heat and pressure. Brazed panels join skin and core metallurgically with no adhesive, for maximum heat resistance; see brazed honeycomb panels.

Edges

Panel edges are folded, fitted with extruded aluminium edge profiles or sealed, depending on the fixing system. Back-bolt inserts or fixing cleats are set into the panel for concealed fixing.

Optional layers

Perforated face skins with an acoustic fleece behind them turn the panel into a sound absorber for ceilings and walls.

Benefits

Why designers choose honeycomb panels

  • Flatness over large formats: the stiff core keeps panels of several square metres flat without visible "oil-canning", which solid sheet cannot achieve at the same weight
  • Low weight: a 20 mm panel weighs roughly 5 – 7 kg/m², compared with about 54 kg/m² for 20 mm granite, which simplifies sub-frames and fixings
  • Stone look without stone weight: 3 – 5 mm stone veneers bonded to honeycomb reduce weight and breakage risk compared with solid stone cladding
  • Fire performance: with an all-aluminium core and a low-combustible adhesive, panels can achieve high reaction-to-fire classes, subject to testing of the actual build-up
  • Weather and corrosion resistance: aluminium skins and core do not rot, swell or rust, so the panels suit exterior and humid interior use
  • Recyclability: panels are largely aluminium, which retains its value at end of life

Ordering

How to specify decorative honeycomb panels

  1. Application and code requirementsLocation (façade, wall, ceiling, floor), design wind or floor loads, and the reaction-to-fire class required (for example under EN 13501-1 or local codes).
  2. Build-upTotal thickness, face and back skin alloy and thickness, core alloy, cell size and foil gauge, bonding method.
  3. FinishCoating type and colour reference or stone type, gloss, protective film.
  4. GeometryPanel schedule with sizes, shapes, edge details, folds, perforations and fixing inserts, or shop drawings.
  5. Documents and termsMaterial certificates for skins and core, coating and fire test reports, samples for approval, packing and delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP).

FAQ

Decorative aluminium (aluminum) honeycomb panels: FAQ

How thick are decorative aluminium honeycomb panels?

Wall and ceiling panels are usually 10 – 30 mm thick, with 15 – 25 mm common for façades. Floors and other load-bearing uses are thicker and are sized to the load and support spacing.

How much does an aluminium honeycomb panel weigh?

A typical 20 mm façade panel with a 1.0 mm face and 0.7 mm back weighs roughly 5 – 7 kg/m², most of it in the skins. That is about one tenth of the weight of solid stone of the same thickness.

Are honeycomb panels fireproof?

No building panel is simply "fireproof". Aluminium honeycomb panels have an all-metal core, and with a suitable adhesive they can reach high reaction-to-fire classes such as A2-s1,d0 under EN 13501-1, but the class must be proven by tests on the specific build-up.

Can honeycomb panels be curved or folded?

Yes. Panels can be rolled to curves and folded into trays or corners with the core relieved at the bend, although minimum radii depend on thickness and skin alloy. Curved panels should be confirmed at the design stage.

What is the difference between honeycomb panels and aluminum composite panels?

Composite panels (ACP) are thin, typically 3 – 6 mm, with a solid polymer or mineral core, and are routed and folded into cassettes. Honeycomb panels are thicker and stiffer for their weight, stay flat in larger sizes and can carry stone facings.

Can I order only the honeycomb core or skin sheet?

Yes. Metline supplies honeycomb core in 3003 and 5052 and coated or anodising-quality skin sheet and coil separately for panel manufacturers.
