Coiled tube guide

Aluminium Tube Coil (Coiled Aluminium Tubing)

Aluminium tube coil is small-diameter round tube, usually 4 – 20 mm OD, supplied wound into a coil instead of cut into straight lengths. Made from soft, formable alloys such as 1100, 1050, 3003 and 3103 in O or H111 temper, it is uncoiled, straightened, cut and bent on the customer’s own equipment for refrigerant lines, heat exchangers, gas and oil lines and instrument tubing. This guide covers alloys, standards, sizes, weights and how to specify it.

Overview

What is aluminium tube coil?

Tube coil is a supply format, not a separate alloy. Long lengths of small round tube, hundreds or thousands of metres in one piece, are wound onto a coil so that a manufacturer can feed them continuously into straightening, cutting and bending machines with very little scrap and no joints.

There are two main routes to make it. Extruded coiled tube is pressed through a die and coiled directly at the press; ASTM B491 covers this product for general-purpose uses such as refrigeration service, gas lines, oil lines and instrument lines, in outside diameters from 6.00 to 20.00 mm (0.250 – 0.750 in). Drawn coiled tube starts as an extruded hollow that is cold-drawn through dies over a mandrel to reach thinner walls and tighter tolerances, then annealed; seamless drawn tube is covered by ASTM B210 and EN 754, and general-purpose drawn tube by ASTM B483.

Aluminium tube coil competes directly with copper coil in air conditioning and refrigeration. It is far lighter (aluminium is about 2.7 g/cm³ against about 8.9 g/cm³ for copper), its price is less volatile, and it removes galvanic corrosion problems where an aluminium fin coil meets a copper tube. The trade-offs are lower strength, which calls for slightly thicker walls, and the need for proper brazing or mechanical joints in place of simple soft soldering.

Why manufacturers buy tube in coils

  • Continuous feed into automatic straighten-cut-bend lines, so less handling and scrap
  • Long lengths with no joints for refrigerant lines and serpentine heat-exchanger circuits
  • Soft O or H111 temper bends tightly without cracking or kinking
  • Compact, stable packaging for export compared with long straight lengths
Alloy selection

Which alloy for tube coil?

Coiled tube is almost always made from non-heat-treatable alloys, because the tube has to be soft enough to coil, uncoil and bend. Strength therefore comes from wall thickness and alloy choice rather than heat treatment.

For most refrigeration and HVAC work the choice is between 1100 and 3003. 3003 gives a useful margin of strength for higher-pressure refrigerants and is common in brazed heat exchangers; 1100 is chosen where maximum bendability or slightly better conductivity matters. Design

Estimating pressure capability

Pressure rating is the question buyers ask most, and it cannot be answered by the alloy alone. A first estimate uses Barlow’s formula: burst pressure P ≈ 2 × S × t / OD, where S is the tensile strength, t the wall thickness and OD the outside diameter. Working pressure is the burst pressure divided by a safety factor, and refrigeration codes set those factors.

As an illustration, a 9.52 × 1.0 mm tube in 3003-O, taking a conservative tensile strength of 95 MPa, gives an estimated burst pressure of about 2 × 95 × 1.0 / 9.52 ≈ 20 MPa (200 bar). With a safety factor of 3 to 5 the allowable working pressure would sit between about 40 and 65 bar. Treat this as a screening calculation only; brazed joints, bends that thin the outer wall, temperature and the code you design to (for example ISO 5149 or EN 378 for refrigerating systems) all reduce the real figure, and qualified systems must be pressure-tested.

Practical design points

  • Bending thins the wall on the outside of the bend; allow for it in thin-wall tube
  • Brazing anneals the tube locally, so design to O-temper strength even if you buy H111
  • Higher-pressure refrigerants such as R410A and R32 typically need thicker walls than older refrigerants
  • Specify eddy-current testing when leak-tightness is critical
Fabrication

Bending, joining and surface treatment

Straightening and cutting

Coils feed roller straighteners and flying cut-off saws or rotary cutters. Soft O temper straightens easily; consistent temper along the coil keeps cut lengths straight.

Bending

O-temper 1100 and 3003 bend to tight radii; a centreline bend radius of about 2 × OD is achievable with a mandrel or proper tooling. Hairpins for fin-and-tube coils are formed directly from coil.

Brazing

Aluminium-to-aluminium joints are brazed with Al-Si filler (4047 or 4045 clad) and flux, or in controlled-atmosphere furnaces. Aluminium-to-copper transitions need dedicated filler and process control.

Mechanical joints

Flare, compression and press-fit fittings designed for aluminium are widely used on line sets to avoid brazing on site.

Cleanliness

Refrigeration tube is supplied internally clean and dry with sealed ends; specify residue limits if your system needs them.

Surface treatment

Tube can be anodised, coated or given corrosion-resistant treatments for outdoor and coastal service; pure and 3xxx alloys anodise to a clear finish.

Applications

Where aluminium tube coil is used

  • HVAC and refrigeration: refrigerant line sets, evaporator and condenser circuits, heat-pump coils and dehumidifiers
  • Domestic appliances: refrigerator evaporators and freezer coils, often in 1100 or 3003
  • Automotive thermal management: A/C lines, oil-cooler and battery-cooling circuits (alongside microchannel tube)
  • Gas, oil and instrument lines: compressed air, pneumatic control and lubrication lines where light weight and easy routing matter
  • Solar thermal: absorber circuits and storage-tank heat exchangers
  • Medical and laboratory: oxygen-concentrator and analytical-instrument tubing
  • Industrial heat exchangers: serpentine coils for process cooling and heat recovery

In practice the tube’s thermal resistance is a small part of a heat exchanger’s total, so the lower conductivity of aluminium has little effect on coil performance when the design accounts for it. The bigger engineering tasks are jointing and wall thickness. All-aluminium coils also simplify end-of-life recycling.

Ordering

How to order aluminium tube coil

  1. Specify the tubeAlloy and temper (for example 3003-O), OD, wall thickness and the governing standard (ASTM B491, B210 or EN 754).
  2. Specify the coilLevel-wound or pancake, coil ID and OD, coil weight or length, winding direction and end sealing.
  3. TestingState any eddy-current, pressure, cleanliness or flaring and expansion tests you need.
  4. DocumentsMill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 with chemical and mechanical results, plus packing list.
  5. Packing and shippingCoils packed on pallets or in wooden cases with moisture protection, shipped on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms worldwide.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) tube coil: FAQ

Which alloy is best for aluminium refrigerant tube coil?

3003-O and 1100-O are the most common. 3003 is about 20 % stronger and suits higher-pressure refrigerants and brazed heat exchangers; 1100 is slightly softer and more conductive. Both bend easily and resist corrosion well.

What is the difference between O and H111 temper tube?

O is fully annealed and softest. H111 is annealed and then lightly strained by straightening or coiling, so it is marginally stronger than O. For bending and design purposes they behave very similarly.

What sizes of coiled aluminium tube are available?

Outside diameters from about 4 to 25 mm, with 1/4, 5/16, 3/8, 1/2, 5/8 and 3/4 in being the most common for HVAC, and wall thicknesses from about 0.4 to 2.5 mm. Coil size and weight are set to suit your decoiler.

Can aluminium tube coil replace copper in air conditioners?

Yes, and it is widely done. Aluminium is about one-third of the weight of copper, avoids galvanic corrosion against aluminium fins and is usually cheaper. Walls are often slightly thicker and joints must be brazed or made with fittings designed for aluminium.

How do I calculate the weight of aluminium coil tube?

Weight per metre in kg = 3.1416 × (OD − wall) × wall × 2.71 / 1000, with dimensions in mm. A 9.52 × 0.8 mm tube weighs about 0.059 kg per metre.

Which standard covers coiled aluminium tube?

ASTM B491 covers extruded round tube in coils or straight lengths for refrigeration, gas, oil and instrument lines. Drawn seamless tube is covered by ASTM B210 and EN 754, and general-purpose drawn tube by ASTM B483.
