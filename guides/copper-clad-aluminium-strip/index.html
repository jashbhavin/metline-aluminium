Copper Clad Aluminium Strip

Copper clad aluminium strip is thin roll-bonded Cu/Al laminate supplied in coils or slit widths for high-speed stamping and welding. It gives stamped parts a copper face, stripe or edge exactly where they make electrical contact, and aluminium everywhere else for low weight and cost. Metline supplies overlay, inlay and edge-lay strip, typically 10 – 30 % copper, to battery, connector and electronics manufacturers worldwide.

Overview

Why use clad strip instead of plate or solid copper

Strip is the form of copper clad aluminium designed for volume manufacturing. Coils feed straight into progressive dies, laser-welding lines and automated assembly, so a manufacturer can produce millions of bimetal tabs, terminals and connectors without handling individual blanks. Because the bond is made in the rolling mill, every part arrives with a permanent copper-aluminium joint already built in.

The economic case rests on putting copper only where it is needed. A battery connector may need copper only at the weld zone that meets a copper terminal; a lead frame may need copper only on its contact fingers. Inlay and edge-lay strip place a narrow copper stripe exactly there, so the copper content of the part can drop to a small fraction of a solid copper design.

What buyers usually need from strip

  • Consistent layer ratio along the full coil length so every stamped part performs the same
  • Flatness, camber and burr control suited to high-speed progressive dies
  • A bond strong enough to survive bending, stamping and laser or ultrasonic welding
  • Clean, oxide-free copper for welding and plating
Types

Overlay, inlay and edge-lay strip

Overlay, one side (Cu/Al)

Copper covers one full face. Used for transition tabs and connectors that join copper on one side and aluminium on the other.

Overlay, two sides (Cu/Al/Cu)

Copper on both faces with an aluminium core, for conductors that must solder or contact on both sides and stay flat.

Inlay

One or more copper stripes rolled into the aluminium surface so the strip stays flat and uniform in thickness. Copper appears only where the drawing calls for it.

Edge-lay

Copper and aluminium joined edge to edge across the width, so one end of a stamped tab is copper and the other aluminium.

Full clad (wrapped)

Copper on both faces and edges, used where no aluminium may be exposed on the formed part.

Reverse clad (Al/Cu/Al)

Copper core with aluminium faces, for parts welded to aluminium on both sides that need copper conductivity inside.

Processing

Slitting, stamping and welding clad strip

  • Slitting: sharp knives and correct clearance give clean edges; edge burr should be specified for stamping.
  • Stamping and forming: annealed strip forms well; keep bend radii generous on the copper side so the thin copper layer does not thin or crack.
  • Laser and ultrasonic welding: weld copper to copper and aluminium to aluminium; keep heat input low so the interface stays below the temperature where brittle intermetallics grow quickly.
  • Plating: tin or nickel plating on the copper face improves solderability and weld consistency.
  • Storage: keep coils dry and wrapped; copper oxidises and stains in humid air, which affects welding.

Avoid annealing or brazing finished parts. Long exposure above roughly 200 – 250 °C thickens the Al2Cu-type intermetallic layer at the interface and weakens the bond.

Quality

Inspection and certificates

Strip is checked for layer ratio on cross-sections taken from the coil ends, peel and bend tests for bond, thickness and width tolerance, camber and flatness, and surface condition. Conductivity is measured on request. Each delivery comes with an EN 10204 3.1 certificate covering these results, plus coil identification so every part can be traced back to its lot.

FAQ

Copper clad aluminium (aluminum) strip: FAQ

What is inlay copper clad aluminum strip?

Inlay strip has one or more copper stripes rolled into the surface of an aluminium strip, flush with it. Copper is present only where parts need electrical contact or welding, which saves weight and cost.

How thick is the copper layer?

Typically 10 – 30 % of total thickness for overlay strip. On a 0.5 mm strip with 20 % copper, the copper layer is about 0.1 mm thick.

Can clad strip be laser welded?

Yes. Weld the copper face to copper parts and the aluminium face to aluminium parts, with heat input controlled so the bond interface is not overheated.

What is the difference between strip and plate?

Strip is thin laminate (up to about 3 mm) supplied in coils or slit widths for automatic stamping. Plate is thicker and supplied as sheets, blanks or machined parts.

Why is clad strip used in battery packs?

It allows copper and aluminium cell terminals and busbars to be joined by welding same metal to same metal, avoiding brittle direct copper-aluminium welds and galvanic corrosion at the joint.
