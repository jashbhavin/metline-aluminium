Aluminium Foil for Pet Food Containers

Wet pet food trays are pressed from heavy-gauge container foil, usually 3003, 3004, 8011 or 8006, and sealed with a lacquered lidding foil. The foil must deep-draw without cracking, survive retort sterilisation at about 121 – 130 °C and keep a full barrier for a long shelf life. This guide covers alloy and temper choice, gauges, coatings and the food-contact standards tray makers should specify.

Overview

Why aluminium foil for pet food packaging?

Single-serve wet cat and dog food is commonly packed in small aluminium trays with a peelable foil lid. The tray maker presses container foil into shape, fills it, heat-seals the lid and sterilises the sealed pack in a retort. Aluminium is used because it is a complete barrier to light, oxygen, moisture and aroma, survives retorting, and is light and recyclable.

Compared with the alternatives, aluminium has clear advantages for this product. Plastic trays need multilayer barrier films to reach a similar shelf life, and they are harder to recycle. Glass and tinplate cans are heavier and need a can opener or ring-pull end. Paper-based packs need a barrier layer and are not usually suited to retorting. An aluminium tray opens easily, can be spooned from directly and goes into the aluminium recycling stream.

Metline supplies the aluminium container foil and lidding foil in coil and slit widths to tray and lid makers; the trays, lids and filled packs are produced by those manufacturers.

Selection

Which alloy and temper to choose

AlloyStrengthsTypical use in pet food packs
8011Fine grain, very good drawability, widely availableStandard tray foil and lidding foil
8006Higher strength than 8011 with high elongationDeeper or stiffer trays, thinner gauges for the same rigidity
3003Good strength, formability and corrosion resistanceRigid trays and smooth-wall containers
3004 / 3104Higher strength from Mn and MgStiff trays and lids that must resist deformation
5052High strength, excellent corrosion resistanceLids and easy-open components

Soft O temper gives the deepest draw and the most uniform wall; it suits deep trays and complex shapes. H22 and H24 give a stiffer finished tray and better flange flatness, at the cost of some drawability. H26 is used where rigidity is the priority. The tray maker's tooling, draw depth and wall design decide the best combination, so trials on the actual tool are normal before a new gauge or temper is approved.

Coatings

Lacquers, heat-seal layers and retort performance

Bare foil can be used for some dry products, but wet pet food trays are almost always lacquered. The internal lacquer protects the metal from salt, fats and acids in the food and provides the surface the lid seals to. Typical lacquer systems are epoxy-free or BPA-NI formulations, PP-compatible heat-seal lacquers for lids that seal to polypropylene-coated trays, and coloured or gold external lacquers for branding.

  • Retort resistance: lacquers and seals must withstand sterilisation, commonly around 121 °C for 30 minutes and up to about 130 °C for some processes, without blushing, blistering or delamination
  • Peel performance: the seal must stay intact through retorting, transport and storage yet open cleanly by hand
  • Flexibility: lacquer must not crack during deep drawing of the tray wall and flange
  • Food contact: coatings must comply with the migration limits of the market of sale
  • Print: external lacquer must take multi-colour printing and resist scuffing on filling lines

Retort times and temperatures depend on the product and pack size and are set by the filler's thermal process validation, not by the foil supplier.

Pet food is regulated as food-contact packaging in most markets, so the same rules that apply to human food packaging normally apply. Ask for a declaration of compliance covering the lacquer system, and confirm migration testing with the coating supplier for your specific food simulants and retort conditions.

FAQ

Pet food container foil: frequently asked questions

Which aluminium alloy is used for pet food trays?

8011 and 3003 are the most common, with 8006 used where a stiffer tray or thinner gauge is wanted and 3004 for rigid trays. Lids are usually 8011 or 8079 foil, sometimes 3104 or 5052.

What thickness is pet food container foil?

Tray foil is typically about 0.045 – 0.12 mm, with deeper smooth-wall retort trays at the heavier end. Lidding foil starts from about 0.035 mm and is usually laminated or lacquered.

Can aluminium pet food trays be retorted?

Yes. Lacquered aluminium trays and lids are designed for retort sterilisation, typically around 121 °C and up to about 130 °C depending on the process and coating system.

Is aluminium foil safe for pet food contact?

Yes, when the alloy meets EN 602 or equivalent and the lacquer complies with food-contact rules such as EU Regulation 1935/2004 or FDA 21 CFR 175.300. Ask for a declaration of compliance.

What temper should I order for deep trays?

O temper gives the best drawability for deep trays. H22 or H24 gives a stiffer tray and flatter flange for shallower designs. Run trials on your tooling before approving a change.

Are aluminum pet food containers recyclable?

Yes. Aluminium can be recycled repeatedly without losing its properties, and empty, rinsed trays are accepted in many aluminium packaging collection schemes.
