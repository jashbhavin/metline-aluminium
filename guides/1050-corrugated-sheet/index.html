1050, 1060 and 1100 Aluminium Corrugated Sheet

Corrugated sheet in 1050, 1060 or 1100 pure aluminium is the lightest, most formable and most weather-resistant option for low-load roofing, wall cladding, interiors and vehicle linings. It weighs about a third as much as steel sheet of the same thickness and does not rust. The trade-off against 3003 or 3105 sheet is lower strength, which affects purlin spacing and foot traffic. This guide covers alloy choice, profiles, coatings and ordering.

Overview

What is pure aluminium corrugated sheet?

Corrugated sheet is flat aluminium coil roll-formed into a repeating wave or trapezoidal rib. The profile multiplies the bending stiffness of thin sheet many times over, so a 0.5 – 0.9 mm sheet can span between purlins or rails and shed water.

The 1xxx grades used here are at least 99 % aluminium. They are softer than the 3xxx alloys more commonly used for structural roofing, which makes them very easy to roll-form, bend on site and crimp, and gives them the best natural corrosion resistance of any aluminium. They are a good fit for coastal and industrial atmospheres, for interiors where appearance matters and for applications such as reflective insulation cladding, vehicle bodies and agricultural buildings.

Their lower strength means you should use them with closer support spacing, or choose a deeper profile or thicker gauge, than you would for 3003 or 3105 sheet. For roofs that will carry foot traffic or high snow and wind loads, compare both options with the roof designer.

Surface

Surface treatments and colours

Mill finish

Bare, bright aluminium that weathers to a soft grey. The lowest cost and fully recyclable.

Polyester (PE) coating

Coil-coated paint for interiors and moderate exteriors, in a wide colour range.

PVDF (fluorocarbon) coating

The longest-lasting colour and gloss retention for exposed roofs and façades, including coastal sites.

Stucco embossing

An orange-peel texture that hides marks and reduces glare; popular for insulation jacketing.

Anodising

Builds a thicker oxide layer for extra corrosion resistance and a metallic look; normally applied to flat sheet before forming.

Sandblasted or brushed

Matt or directional textures for interiors and design features.

Typical coil-coating colours include regal white, almond, charcoal grey, dark bronze and matt black, alongside mill finish. Standard colours are the quickest to supply; custom colours are matched to a RAL code or sample.

Installation

Design and installation tips

  • Support spacing: pure aluminium is softer than 3003, so follow the profile maker's load-span tables and reduce spacing for 1xxx sheet.
  • Fasteners: use aluminium or stainless-steel fasteners with EPDM washers; never bare steel or copper, which cause galvanic corrosion.
  • Contact with other materials: isolate the sheet from wet concrete, treated timber and copper run-off with tape or paint.
  • Thermal movement: aluminium expands about 2.4 mm per metre per 100 °C, so use oversized holes or sliding clips on long sheets.
  • Overlaps and pitch: follow the minimum roof pitch and side and end laps for the profile to keep water out.
Applications

Where pure aluminium corrugated sheet is used

  • Roofing and cladding: houses, warehouses, farm buildings and canopies, especially near the coast
  • Interiors: feature walls, ceilings, counters and furniture panels
  • Insulation jacketing: corrugated or embossed sheet protecting insulated pipes and tanks
  • Marine: bulkhead linings and deck-house interiors, with a marine-grade coating where exposed
  • Transport: vehicle body panels, trailer linings, cargo floors and steps
Packing

Packing, transport and storage

Sheets are supplied dry, stacked flat with nesting corrugations, and wrapped against moisture. Stacks are usually kept to about 500 mm high and around 2 tonnes gross so they can be handled safely by forklift without crushing the profile. Store under cover, raised off the ground and slightly sloped so any condensation drains; trapped water between nested sheets causes white staining.

FAQ

Corrugated aluminium (aluminum) sheet: FAQ

Is 1050 or 3003 better for corrugated roofing?

3003 is about 20 – 30 % stronger, so it allows wider purlin spacing and resists foot traffic better. 1050 is cheaper, brighter and easier to form, and suits interiors, cladding and well-supported roofs.

What thickness of corrugated aluminium sheet should I use for a roof?

Most roofs use 0.5 – 0.9 mm. Choose the gauge from the profile maker's load-span tables for your wind, snow and support spacing; thinner sheet suits walls and interiors.

Does corrugated aluminium rust?

No. Aluminium forms a protective oxide film instead of rust. Pure grades have the best natural corrosion resistance of all aluminium alloys, though they can stain if stored wet or touched by copper or bare steel.

Can corrugated aluminium sheet be curved?

Yes. Sinusoidal profiles in pure aluminium can be crimp-curved or bent to a radius, which is one reason they are popular for curved canopies and feature walls.

Which colours are available?

Mill finish plus coil-coated colours such as white, almond, grey, bronze and black in polyester or PVDF paint. Custom colours can be matched to a RAL code.
