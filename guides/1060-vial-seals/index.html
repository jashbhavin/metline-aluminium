1060 O and H22 Aluminium Strip and Foil for Vial Seals

Vial seal makers use 1060 aluminium strip, around 0.15 – 0.5 mm thick, in soft O or lightly hardened H22 temper, to draw the crimp caps that hold rubber stoppers on injection vials. The alloy must draw without splitting, crimp tightly, show low earing and carry lacquer or colour cleanly. This guide covers the specification points that decide whether strip runs well on a seal line.

Overview

Why 1060 is used for vial seals

An aluminium vial seal (crimp cap) is a shallow drawn cup that is pressed over the rubber stopper of an injection vial and rolled under the vial lip. It holds the stopper in place and provides tamper evidence; flip-off versions add a plastic button that is snapped away before use.

Making that cup and crimping it puts specific demands on the strip. It must draw in one or two strokes without tearing, so ductility and a fine, even grain matter. The cup rim must be level, so the strip must have low earing (the wavy rim caused by directional properties). It must crimp tightly without cracking on the vial lip, and tear-off scores must open with a predictable force. 1060, at 99.6 % aluminium, offers very high ductility and clean surfaces for lacquering, which is why it is widely used for these caps alongside 8011 and 3xxx alloys.

Dimensional requirements for aluminium caps for injection vials are set by ISO 8362-3 and, for aluminium-plastic combination caps, ISO 8362-6; the cap maker works from those, and the strip specification follows from the cap design.

The thinnest O-temper strip, about 0.15 – 0.22 mm, is used for standard 13 mm and 20 mm crimp seals. H22 at 0.4 – 0.5 mm suits larger or stiffer caps where the extra strength helps the cap keep its shape and resist handling damage.

Thickness consistency matters most. A cap press is set for one gauge; strip that varies changes the drawn height, the crimp and the force needed to remove the tear-off. Tight thickness tolerance, flat strip and straight edges let the press run fast with fewer stoppages.

Protects the metal and gives a bright appearance while allowing printing.

Lacquers on closures that touch the stopper, or that are used in medicine packaging, must meet the regulatory requirements of the target market. Ask for a declaration of compliance for the lacquer system along with the material certificate.

Applications

Seal and cap designs made from 1060 strip

  • Plain crimp seals with a centre hole exposing the stopper for needle access
  • Flip-off seals with a plastic button over the centre
  • Tear-off seals: centre tear-off, double tear-off, scoreline tear-off and universal tear-off designs
  • Bridge seals, where a scored bridge is lifted to reveal the stopper
  • Pull-off and full tear-off caps for infusion bottles and diagnostic vials

Scored designs need consistent temper and thickness, because the opening force depends on the residual metal under the score. If opening forces vary between lots, check thickness and tensile strength first.

FAQ

Vial seal strip: frequently asked questions

Why is low earing important for vial seals?

Earing makes the rim of a drawn cup wavy. Uneven rims crimp unevenly under the vial lip, which can leave gaps or cause rejects on inspection, so seal makers set a maximum, commonly 5 – 6 %.

What thickness of aluminium is used for vial seals?

Most standard 13 mm and 20 mm crimp seals use about 0.18 – 0.22 mm strip. Heavier caps use up to about 0.5 mm.

Should I choose O or H22 temper?

O temper is softest and draws most easily, so it suits thin strip and deep caps. H22 is slightly stronger and stiffer, which helps larger caps keep their shape.

Is 1060 or 8011 aluminum better for vial caps?

Both are used. 1060 is more ductile and lacquers very cleanly; 8011 is a little stronger. The choice depends on the cap design, press tooling and customer approvals.

Can the strip be supplied lacquered and coloured?

Yes. Metline supplies vial seal strip mill finish, lubricated, pretreated, clear-lacquered or colour-lacquered to your colour code, with material certificates.
