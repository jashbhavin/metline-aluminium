Aluminium Tubing for Oxygen Concentrators

Oxygen concentrators separate oxygen from air in columns packed with molecular sieve, and most of those columns, product tanks and coolers are made from aluminium tube. This guide explains which parts use aluminium, the alloys and tempers that suit each one, and how to specify tube for medical and industrial PSA equipment. Metline supplies the tube, cut and finished to drawing, to equipment makers worldwide; it does not make concentrators.

Background

How an oxygen concentrator uses aluminium tube

Most concentrators use pressure swing adsorption (PSA). A compressor pushes filtered air into one of two columns, or sieve beds, filled with zeolite that adsorbs nitrogen under pressure. Oxygen-rich gas passes through to a product tank. Meanwhile the second bed is vented, releasing its nitrogen, and the two beds swap roles every few seconds.

Each sieve bed is a pressurised cylinder that must be light, leak-tight, dimensionally accurate so the end caps seal, and clean inside so that no oil or particles contaminate the sieve or the oxygen stream. Extruded aluminium tube meets all of these needs. It is a third of the weight of steel, does not rust inside the bed, machines easily for O-ring grooves and end-cap fits, and can be anodised.

Aluminium also appears in the product (oxygen) tank, the aftercooler or heat exchanger that cools compressed air before it reaches the sieve, internal manifolds, and the cabinet frame of portable units. Industrial PSA generators use the same principle at larger scale with welded or flanged aluminium vessels.

Oxygen service

Cleanliness, anodising and oxygen compatibility

Concentrated oxygen makes contaminants such as oil, grease and fine particles much easier to ignite, and residues can also poison the zeolite. Tubes for oxygen equipment are therefore cleaned and protected before assembly.

  • Cleaning: degreasing and cleaning to the maker's oxygen-cleaning procedure, often based on ASTM G93 or ISO 15001 practice, with ends capped after cleaning.
  • Anodising: clear or coloured anodising protects the outer surface and gives a clean, durable appearance on visible columns.
  • Inside surface: smooth, free of loose particles and extrusion residue; internal deburring at cut ends.
  • Packing: sealed, capped and bagged tubes so that clean surfaces stay clean in transit.

Concentrators themselves are medical devices that must meet ISO 80601-2-69 and the regulations of each market. The tube supplier provides material certificates and traceability that support the device maker's quality system.

FAQ

Aluminium tube for oxygen concentrators: frequently asked questions

Why are oxygen concentrator sieve beds made of aluminium?

Aluminium tube is light, does not rust inside the bed, machines easily for end-cap seals, can be anodised and is compatible with oxygen when clean. Extrusion also allows twin-bed and multi-cavity sections.

Which aluminium alloy is used for sieve bed tubes?

6063-T5 or T6 is the most common, with 6061-T6 used where higher strength is needed. Industrial PSA vessels often use 5052, 5083 or 6061 plate and tube.

Do concentrator tubes need special cleaning?

Yes. Oil, grease and particles must be removed because they can ignite in concentrated oxygen and contaminate the sieve. Tubes are cleaned to the maker's oxygen-cleaning procedure, capped and bagged.

Can you supply tubes cut to length and anodised?

Yes. Tubes can be cut to column length, deburred, end-machined and anodised, then packed ready for assembly.

Do you supply complete oxygen concentrators?

No. Metline supplies aluminium tube, profiles, bar and sheet to concentrator manufacturers; the device is designed, assembled and certified by the manufacturer.
