Quenched & Pre-Stretched Aluminium Plate

Quenched and pre-stretched plate is heat-treatable aluminium plate that is stretched by 1.5 – 3 % straight after quenching, so the residual stresses locked in by the quench are almost entirely removed. It is what the "51" in tempers such as T351, T651, T7451 and T7751 means, and it is the reason aerospace and precision machinists can remove most of a plate without it warping. This guide explains the tempers, alloys, properties and standards, and Metline supplies pre-stretched plate worldwide.

Overview

Why heat-treated plate is stretched

Heat-treatable alloys get their strength from a three-step cycle: solution heat treatment near 500 °C, rapid quenching in water, then natural or artificial ageing. The quench is essential for strength, but it cools the surface far faster than the centre. The surface ends up in compression and the core in tension, and in thick plate those stresses are large.

If an unstretched plate is machined, removing material upsets that balance and the part bends, twists or moves after it leaves the fixture. Stretching the plate permanently by a small, controlled amount yields the whole cross-section in tension, which evens out the stress pattern. ANSI H35.1 specifies a permanent set of 1.5 – 3 % for stretched plate; residual stresses typically fall by around an order of magnitude.

The cost is a small extra process step and some loss of length at the grip ends. The benefit is predictable machining: thin-walled aircraft ribs, spars and frames are hogged out of solid plate, often removing 80 – 90 % of the metal, and that is only practical with stretched plate.

What pre-stretching gives you

  • Dimensional stability during heavy, one-sided machining
  • Better flatness as supplied, so less skimming before machining starts
  • More uniform properties through the thickness after ageing
  • In some tempers (T8 types), extra strength because the stretch adds cold work before ageing
Process route

How quenched and pre-stretched plate is made

  1. Melting and castingAlloy is melted, degassed and filtered, then direct-chill cast into rolling slabs. Low hydrogen and few inclusions are critical for plate that will be ultrasonically inspected.
  2. HomogenisingSlabs are soaked at high temperature to even out segregation and form the dispersoids that control grain structure.
  3. Hot rollingSlabs are rolled to plate thickness. Good plate shape here makes stretching more effective later.
  4. Solution heat treatmentPlates are heated in an air or salt-bath furnace to dissolve the alloying elements.
  5. QuenchingPlates are quenched, usually by high-pressure water sprays, to trap the elements in solution.
  6. StretchingWithin a short time of quenching, a stretcher grips the plate ends and applies 1.5 – 3 % permanent set.
  7. Sawing and ageingGrip ends are sawn off; plates are naturally aged (T351, T451) or furnace aged (T651, T851, T7x51).
  8. Inspection and finishingUltrasonic testing, conductivity and hardness checks, tensile tests and dimensional inspection before cutting to size.

Practical points make the difference between good and poor stretched plate. Over-stretching beyond the specified set reduces properties and can leave slip lines on the surface. Under-stretching leaves residual stress behind. A plate with poor hot-rolled shape stretches unevenly, and grip marks must be fully cut away. These are why plate is bought to a recognised standard with documented process control.

Alloy selection

Alloys supplied as quenched and pre-stretched plate

2024 / 2124

Al-Cu-Mg. 2024-T351 is the classic damage-tolerant alloy for fuselage and lower wing structure. 2124 is a high-purity version with better through-thickness toughness for thick plate.

2014 / 2017

Al-Cu. High strength and good machinability; used for heavy-duty fittings, tooling and propeller components.

2219

Al-Cu with good weldability and useful strength from cryogenic temperatures to about 300 °C. Used for propellant tanks and elevated-temperature parts.

2618

Al-Cu-Mg-Ni-Fe developed for elevated temperature; engine and wheel parts.

6061 / 6082

Al-Mg-Si. The general-engineering choice for jigs, fixtures, machine frames and vacuum chambers in T651.

7075

Al-Zn-Mg-Cu. Very high strength in T651; over-aged T7351 or T7651 when stress corrosion or exfoliation matters.

7050

Al-Zn-Mg-Cu-Zr with low quench sensitivity, so thick plate keeps its properties at the centre. Standard for bulkheads, spars and frames.

7475

High-purity 7075 variant with superior fracture toughness for fuselage and wing skins.

7055 / 7150

The highest-strength plate alloys in general aerospace use, in T7751, for compression-dominated upper wing structure.

For aerospace orders, inspection plans normally include ultrasonic testing of the full plate, conductivity checks to confirm the over-aged tempers, and traceability from cast to finished piece. Commercial T651 plate for tooling is usually supplied with a mill certificate and dimensional check only. If your part has a critical flatness requirement, state it on the order rather than relying on the standard tolerance tables, which allow more deviation as width and thickness increase.

Applications

Where quenched and pre-stretched plate is used

  • Aircraft structure: wing spars, ribs, bulkheads, fuselage frames and landing-gear support fittings machined from thick 7050, 7075 and 2024 plate
  • Space and launch vehicles: propellant tank domes and barrel sections in 2219, which welds well and stays tough at cryogenic temperatures
  • Moulds and tooling: blow moulds, rubber and foam moulds and press tools in 7075-T651 and 2014-T651
  • Semiconductor and vacuum: chamber bodies, gate valves and wafer-handling plates in 6061-T651
  • Automation and machinery: base plates, gantries, robot arms and inspection fixtures where flatness after machining is critical
  • Defence and motorsport: high-strength brackets, uprights and armour-backing components

The common thread is heavy machining. Wherever a large proportion of a plate is cut away, or a part must hold tight flatness and position tolerances after it leaves the machine, a stretched temper is the default. Plate that will only be cut into simple blanks, bent or welded does not need it, and thin sheet is rarely supplied stretched because quench stresses are already low.

Practical advice

Choosing the right pre-stretched plate

  • Machined fixtures and frames: 6061-T651 or 6082-T651 is usually enough, and much cheaper than aerospace alloys.
  • High strength, no corrosion exposure: 7075-T651 gives the most strength per cost.
  • Aerospace or stress-corrosion risk: specify 7075-T7351, or 7050-T7451 for thick plate.
  • Fatigue and damage tolerance: 2024-T351 or 2124-T851.
  • Welding or high temperature: 2219-T851.
  • Thick sections: choose a low quench-sensitivity alloy (7050, 2124) so the centre of the plate meets specification.
  • Common mistake: ordering T6 instead of T651 for thick plate; the unstretched plate will move during machining.
  • Common mistake: designing to typical values. Use the specified minimum for the actual thickness and direction.

FAQ

Quenched and pre-stretched aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQs

What does "51" mean in T351 or T651?

It means the plate was stress-relieved by stretching after quenching, with a permanent set of 1.5 – 3 % for plate, and receives no further straightening. The digit before it (3, 4, 6, 7, 8) describes the ageing treatment.

Does stretching change the strength of the plate?

Only slightly in most tempers. In T8 tempers such as 2024-T851 the stretch acts as cold work before ageing and raises yield strength noticeably compared with T351.

When should I use T7451 instead of T651?

When stress-corrosion cracking is a risk, especially in thick parts loaded in the short-transverse direction. Over-aged tempers trade some strength for much better corrosion resistance.

Why is 7050 preferred over 7075 for thick plate?

7050 is less quench-sensitive, so the centre of a thick plate cools fast enough to reach full properties. 7075 loses more strength at the core as thickness increases.

Is 6061-T651 a quenched and pre-stretched plate?

Yes. It is solution treated, quenched, stretched and artificially aged. It is the most common stretched plate for general machining.

Can you supply cut blocks instead of full plates?

Yes. Pre-stretched plate can be saw-cut into blocks or blanks to your dimensions, which reduces freight and machining stock.
