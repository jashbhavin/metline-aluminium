Aluminium Honeycomb Panels: Construction, Specification & Sizes

An aluminium honeycomb panel is a sandwich of two thin aluminium skins bonded or brazed to a hexagonal aluminium foil core, giving a flat, very stiff plate at a fraction of the weight of solid sheet. This guide explains how to choose the core, skins, bonding method, thickness and finish, and what to put on an enquiry. Metline supplies honeycomb panels, loose core and skin sheet to fabricators and project buyers worldwide.

Overview

What is an aluminium honeycomb panel?

A honeycomb panel works like an I-beam spread out in two directions. The skins carry tension and compression, and the core holds them apart and carries shear. Doubling the core height roughly quadruples the bending stiffness, while adding only a few hundred grams per square metre, so a 20 mm honeycomb panel can be as stiff as solid aluminium plate several times its weight.

The core is made from aluminium foil, typically 0.04 – 0.10 mm thick, printed with adhesive node lines, stacked, cured and then expanded into hexagonal cells. Cell sizes range from about 3 mm to 19 mm across the flats (1/8 in to 3/4 in). Smaller cells and thicker foil raise core density and strength; larger cells and thinner foil save weight. The skins are usually 0.5 – 2 mm aluminium sheet, coil-coated or anodised when the panel is visible.

Because the panel is mostly air, it does not sag, ripple or oil-can the way large sheets of solid aluminium or aluminium composite panel (ACP) can. That is why honeycomb panels are chosen for large flat facade cassettes, raised floors, cleanroom walls, vehicle floors and bulkheads, lift interiors, signage and lightweight machine covers.

Why engineers specify honeycomb panels

  • Very high stiffness-to-weight ratio: large spans stay flat without stiffeners
  • All-aluminium build is recyclable and can reach non-combustible or limited-combustibility fire classes
  • Good impact and dent resistance compared with thin solid sheet
  • Closed cells give some acoustic and thermal damping
  • Panels can be cut, routed, folded, curved and edge-closed for installation

The table shows the alloys most often used. Core alloy affects strength and corrosion behaviour; skin alloy affects flatness, finish quality and fabrication. Mill test certificates for the skin coil and the core foil can be supplied with the panel.

Mixing alloys between core and skin is normal, but avoid combining aluminium with carbon fibre or stainless skins without isolation, because galvanic corrosion can attack the aluminium core at the bond line. See our guides to 3003 core, 5052 core and 5056 core for core-only data.

Bonding method

Adhesive-bonded versus brazed honeycomb panels

Most panels are laminated with a structural adhesive in a heated press or a cold-press line. Brazed panels instead join the core to the skins metallurgically using an aluminium-silicon filler in a vacuum or controlled-atmosphere furnace at around 600 °C. The choice decides temperature limits, strength and price.

FactorAdhesive-bonded panelBrazed panel
JointEpoxy, polyurethane or modified epoxy film or liquid adhesiveAl-Si braze metal; no organic material
Service temperatureLimited by the adhesive, typically about 80 – 150 °CFar higher; limited by softening of the aluminium itself rather than the joint
Fire behaviourAdhesive adds a small combustible load; A2 builds use low-organic adhesives and thin layersEssentially all-metal, so naturally suited to non-combustible classes
StrengthGood for building, interior and most transport usesHigher flatwise tensile and peel strength
FormatsVery large sheets, curved and folded panelsSmaller formats; furnace size limits panel dimensions
Relative costLowerConsiderably higher

Published strength values vary widely with core density, adhesive and test method. For design, ask for test results to ASTM C297 (flatwise tension), C365 (flatwise compression), C273 (core shear) and D1781 (climbing drum peel) on the actual build.

For high-temperature service, outgassing-sensitive interiors or panels that must be welded at the edges, read our dedicated page on brazed aluminium honeycomb panels.

Fabrication

Cutting, folding, edge closing and finishing

Cutting

Panels cut cleanly with carbide circular saws, CNC routers and waterjet. Support the panel fully to avoid crushing the core near the cut.

Folding

A V-groove routed through the inner skin and core lets the outer skin fold to 90° or other angles, producing cassette trays with a sharp corner.

Curving

Single-curved panels are made by pre-rolling skins or by kerfing the core. Tight radii are best ordered pre-formed.

Edge closing

Open edges are closed with extruded aluminium edge profiles, folded skins or potting compound to keep out water and dirt.

Fixing

Potted inserts, threaded blind inserts, through bolts with spacers and hook-on extrusions spread loads into the core without crushing it.

Finishing

PVDF and polyester coil coatings, powder coating, anodising, wood or stone-grain film and perforation for acoustic panels.

  1. Define the useIndoor or outdoor, span and loads, fire class required, and any sector approval (rail, marine, cleanroom).
  2. Specify the buildTotal thickness, skin alloy and gauge for each face, core alloy, cell size and foil gauge (or required core density), and bonding method.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) honeycomb panels: frequently asked questions

What is the standard size of an aluminium honeycomb panel?

The most common sheet is 1220 × 2440 mm (4 × 8 ft), followed by 1250 × 2500 mm and 1500 × 3000 mm. Larger formats and cut-to-size panels are produced to project requirements.

How much does a 20 mm aluminium honeycomb panel weigh?

With two 1 mm skins and a core of about 50 kg/m³, a 20 mm panel weighs roughly 6.3 kg/m² plus adhesive, compared with 54 kg/m² for 20 mm solid aluminium plate. Heavier cores or thicker skins raise the figure.

Are aluminium honeycomb panels fire resistant?

The aluminium itself does not burn, and panels with low-organic adhesive and suitable coatings can achieve EN 13501-1 A2-s1,d0 or even A1. The class applies only to the tested build, so ask for the report covering your exact construction.

What is the difference between a honeycomb panel and ACP?

ACP is a thin panel (usually 4 mm) with a solid plastic or mineral core. A honeycomb panel has a much thicker, mostly hollow aluminium core, so it is lighter for the same stiffness, stays flatter in large sizes and is easier to make non-combustible.

Should I choose a 3003 or 5052 honeycomb core?

3003 core is economical and fine for building and interior panels. 5052 core is stronger and more corrosion resistant, so it is preferred for vehicles, marine interiors and outdoor panels exposed to salt or humidity.

When is a brazed honeycomb panel worth the extra cost?

When the panel must work above the temperature limit of adhesives, must contain no organic material, or needs the highest peel and tensile strength. For facades and interiors, a good adhesive-bonded panel is normally sufficient.

Can you supply only the core or only the skins?

Yes. Metline supplies expanded or unexpanded aluminium honeycomb core and coil-coated or mill-finish skin sheet for customers who laminate their own panels.
