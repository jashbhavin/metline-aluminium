Laminated Aluminium Foil: Structures, Alloys and How to Specify Them

Laminated aluminium foil bonds a thin aluminium layer to plastic film, paper, foam or fabric so that each layer does the job it is best at: the foil blocks light, oxygen and moisture or reflects heat, while the other layers add strength, sealability, printability or insulation. This guide explains the main laminate families, which foil alloy and gauge suit each one, how laminates are made and tested, and what to put in a specification.

Overview

Why aluminium foil is laminated

Aluminium foil on its own is an excellent barrier and reflector, but it is thin, tears easily, cannot be heat-sealed and develops pinholes at the gauges used in flexible packaging. Laminating it to other materials keeps those strengths and removes the weaknesses.

In packaging, a few microns of foil inside a plastic laminate give a barrier to oxygen, water vapour, light and aroma that clear films struggle to match. The outer film carries the print and protects the foil; the inner film provides the heat seal and the food-contact surface; the adhesive layers tie it all together and cover the occasional pinhole. In building and industrial uses, foil laminated to kraft paper, glass-fibre scrim, foam or woven fabric becomes a vapour retarder, a reflective insulation layer, a duct-wrap facing or a heat shield.

Because so many combinations are possible, laminated foil is usually specified layer by layer, with the foil chosen for its function in the structure. Metline supplies aluminium foil for converters' own laminating lines as well as finished laminated foil built to the customer's structure.

Foil layer

Choosing foil alloy and gauge for a laminate

The foil gauge is set by what the foil has to do in the laminate. Below about 20 µm, foil contains some pinholes; once it is laminated, the films and adhesive cover them and the structure as a whole remains a very high barrier. Thicker foil is used where the foil must give the laminate stiffness, dead-fold, formability or push-through behaviour.

8021 and 8079 have higher iron content than 1235 and give the foil more strength and elongation in the soft temper, which is why they dominate cold-forming and battery film, where the foil is stretched into a cavity without cracking. 1235 is very pure and rolls to the thinnest gauges with few pinholes. 8011 is the general-purpose choice for most laminating work. Push-through blister lidding is a different product: a hard-temper foil with heat-seal lacquer, covered in our PTP foil guide.

Process

Lamination methods

Solvent-based dry lamination

A polyurethane adhesive dissolved in solvent is coated on one web, dried in an oven and nipped to the second web. Versatile and strong, and still common for retort and demanding structures.

Solvent-free lamination

Two-component, 100 % solids polyurethane adhesive applied at low coat weight. No drying oven and no solvent emissions; needs curing time before slitting and pouch making.

Extrusion lamination

A molten curtain of polyethylene is extruded between foil and film or paper, acting as both adhesive and a sealant layer. Widely used for foil-paper-PE structures.

Wet (water-based) lamination

A water-based adhesive bonds foil to paper; the water escapes through the porous paper. A standard route for foil-paper wraps and labels.

Thermal lamination

Heat and pressure bond pre-coated films, foams or fabrics to the foil. Used for foil-foam and foil-cloth insulation products.

Coating instead of laminating

Heat-seal lacquers, primers and colour coats are applied directly to foil. Lighter than a laminate where the full film structure is not needed.

Whatever the method, the foil surface must be clean and wettable so that adhesive or extrudate wets it evenly. Fully annealed foil has had its rolling oil burned off; converters typically check wettability or dyne level at goods-in. Poor wetting shows up later as low bond strength, tunnelling or delamination, especially after retort or exposure to aggressive contents.

Packaging

Foil laminates in packaging

In food, pharmaceutical and personal-care packaging, the laminate is designed around the product, the filling line and the shelf life.

  • Snack, coffee and powder packs: PET / Al / PE or BOPP / Al / PE, with 6 – 9 µm foil for barrier and a PE sealant layer suited to the packing machine.
  • Retort pouches: PET / Al / (OPA) / CPP structures that survive sterilisation at about 121 – 135 °C; adhesives and sealants must be rated for retort.
  • Sachets and stick packs: paper or film / Al / PE for single doses of food, cosmetics and pharmaceuticals.
  • Cold-form blisters: OPA / Al / PVC formed into cavities for moisture-sensitive tablets where a clear PVC or PVDC blister is not a sufficient barrier.
  • Paper wraps: foil-paper laminates for chocolate, confectionery, butter and cigarette inner liners, combining barrier, dead-fold and a printable paper face.
  • Lidding: foil laminated or coated with a peelable heat-seal layer for cups, trays and capsules.

Food-contact laminates must comply with the rules of the destination market: in the EU, the framework Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004 and, for the plastic layers, Regulation (EU) No 10/2011 with migration testing of the multilayer; in the US, the relevant FDA regulations for each layer, including adhesives under 21 CFR 175.105. Ask your laminate supplier for a declaration of compliance covering the whole structure.

Building & industry

Foil laminates for insulation and thermal protection

Aluminium foil has very low thermal emittance, roughly 0.03 – 0.05 for a clean bright surface. Facing an air space, it cuts radiant heat transfer sharply, which is the basis of reflective insulation and radiant barriers. Laminated to other materials, it also acts as a vapour retarder that keeps moisture out of fibrous insulation.

Fire behaviour is tested on the complete laminate, not the foil alone: surface-burning characteristics under ASTM E84 / UL 723 in North America, reaction-to-fire classes under EN 13501-1 in Europe, and older national tests such as DIN 4102 still appear in specifications. A flammability rating for one plastic layer does not classify the finished product. Reflective insulation performance is defined by standards such as ASTM C1224 and EN 16012, and depends heavily on the air spaces in the installed system.

Selection advice

Common mistakes when specifying laminated foil

  • Specifying total thickness only: two laminates of the same thickness can perform very differently. Specify each layer, its gauge and the adhesive system.
  • Using thin foil where it is stretched: cold-forming, deep embossing or folding sharp corners can crack 6 – 9 µm foil. Use a thicker 8021 or 8079 foil where the laminate is formed.
  • Ignoring the contents: acidic, oily, alcoholic or spicy products can attack adhesives and cause delamination; test with the real product, including after retort if applicable.
  • Testing the foil instead of the product: fire, barrier and migration results apply to the finished laminate, not to its individual layers.
  • Poor storage: laminates and foils pick up moisture and dust; store sealed in a dry area and let cold rolls reach room temperature before opening.
Ordering

How to order laminated foil

  1. Describe the applicationProduct packed or insulated, filling or installation process, temperature and environment, required shelf life or service life.
  2. Define the structureEach layer and gauge (e.g. PET 12 µm / Al 9 µm / PE 70 µm), or the performance targets if you want a structure proposed.
  3. Set dimensions and formWidth, roll OD and core ID, or sheet and die-cut sizes; print, coating or adhesive backing.
  4. Agree tests and documentsBond and seal strength, barrier, fire or migration tests; mill certificate for the foil and declarations of compliance.
  5. Packing and deliveryRolls packed in film with desiccant on pallets or in cases, shipped on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.

FAQ

Laminated aluminium (aluminum) foil: FAQ

How do I choose the right laminated foil structure?

Start from what the pack or product must do: barrier level, sealing method, stiffness, print, temperature and fire requirements. Then choose the outer layer for print and protection, the foil gauge for barrier or forming, and the inner layer for sealing or bonding.

What foil thickness is used in flexible packaging laminates?

Usually 6 – 9 µm for pouches and sachets, 7 – 12 µm for retort pouches, and about 45 – 60 µm for cold-form blister base, where the foil is stretched into cavities.

Does thin foil with pinholes still give a good barrier?

Yes, once laminated. The films and adhesive bridge the pinholes, and the laminate as a whole remains a very high barrier to oxygen, moisture and light.

What causes laminated foil to delaminate?

Common causes are poor foil wettability, incorrect adhesive or cure, aggressive contents attacking the adhesive, and retort or heat exposure beyond the adhesive's rating. Bond-strength tests after ageing with the real product reveal most problems.

Is foil-faced insulation fire rated?

It depends on the complete laminate. Facings are tested as finished products, for example to ASTM E84 / UL 723 or EN 13501-1; check the classification for the exact product.

Can you supply plain foil for our own laminating line?

Yes. Metline supplies annealed foil in 8011, 8079, 1235, 8021 and other alloys, slit to width on suitable cores, as well as finished laminated foil to your structure.

What forms can laminated foil be delivered in?

Rolls slit to width, cut sheets and die-cut parts, with options such as adhesive backing, perforation, printing and coating, packed for export.
