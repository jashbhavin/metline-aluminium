Aluminium Foam Panels for Furniture & Custom Cabinetry

Aluminium foam furniture panels are sandwich boards with a porous aluminium core and metal, laminate or veneer faces, made to replace MDF, plywood and particleboard in cabinets, wardrobes and interior doors. They do not swell in wet kitchens and bathrooms, have a non-combustible core, contain no formaldehyde-based resin in the core and are lighter than wood boards of the same thickness. This guide compares builds, weights and costs and explains how to machine and fit hardware to them.

Overview

What is an aluminium foam furniture panel?

An aluminium foam furniture panel is a three- or five-layer sandwich: a slab of closed-cell aluminium foam in the middle, bonded on each side to a facing such as thin aluminium sheet, a thin marine-grade plywood layer, PVC or PET film, high-pressure laminate or a wood veneer. The faces carry bending loads and provide the decorative surface; the foam core keeps them apart, resists crushing and damps vibration.

It is worth being precise about what this product is not. It is not an aluminium composite panel (ACP) for facades, which has a thin polymer or mineral core. It is not a polymer-core panel with an XPS, PET or PU foam centre. And it is not an unfaced decorative foam plate where the pores are the finish. The furniture panel described here uses a metal foam core with a conventional furniture face, so it can be handled like a board in a cabinet workshop.

Metline supplies foam core panels and finished foam sandwich boards, together with aluminium facing sheet and coil and extruded edge and handle profiles, to furniture makers, kitchen and wardrobe manufacturers and fit-out contractors worldwide.

Builds with a thin plywood layer under the decorative face behave more like conventional board in the workshop, accepting staples, edge banding and veneers easily, at the cost of some moisture and fire performance. With a medium-density core, an aluminium foam board is roughly a third lighter than MDF; with a low-density core it approaches half the weight. Aluminium honeycomb panels are lighter still, which is why they suit very large doors and wall panels. Foam is chosen over honeycomb when acoustic damping, edge machinability and core continuity (no hollow cells at cut edges) matter more than minimum mass.

Why use it

Why furniture makers use aluminium foam panels

Moisture stability

The core and metal faces do not absorb water, so sink cabinets, vanity units and laundry furniture do not swell at the edges or around hinge holes.

Non-combustible core

The foam itself does not burn. Hotels, hospitals, ships and public buildings often require low-combustibility furniture; the panel class depends on the full build and is confirmed by test.

No formaldehyde resin in the core

Wood-based boards are bonded with resins regulated under schemes such as CARB Phase 2, EPA TSCA Title VI and E1/E0 classes. A metal-core panel has no such resin; check the adhesive and decorative layers separately.

Lower weight

Lighter carcasses and doors cut freight cost, ease installation of tall wardrobes and reduce load on hinges and runners.

Acoustic damping

The porous metal core damps knocks and rattles, so doors close with a solid sound rather than the hollow note of honeycomb panels.

Recyclability and durability

Metal layers are recyclable, panels do not rot or attract insects, and they hold their flatness over years of use.

Fabrication

Cutting, edge finishing and fitting hardware

  • Cutting: panel saws and CNC routers with carbide or diamond tooling, at speeds suited to aluminium; use extraction for fine metal chips and keep them away from wood dust.
  • Edges: cover cut edges with PUR edge banding, aluminium edge profiles or a routed and filled edge. Exposed cut foam can be left visible as a design feature on decorative pieces.
  • Hinges: concealed cup hinges need a solid insert, an expanding hinge anchor designed for sandwich panels, or a local solid block. Plain wood screws in foam pull out under load.
  • Connectors and runners: use through-bolts, rivet nuts, barrel nuts or bonded inserts; plan insert positions before pressing so they sit in the core during lamination where possible.
  • Bonding: PU or epoxy adhesives for metal faces; standard furniture adhesives for veneers and laminates over a ply sub-layer.
  • Surface finishing: aluminium faces can be brushed, sandblasted, embossed, anodised, powder coated or supplied as PE or PVDF pre-painted coil in RAL colours.

For handles, grip rails and edge trims, extruded profiles are usually the neatest solution; see cabinet handle aluminium profiles and aluminium cabinet materials.

FAQ

Aluminium foam furniture panels: frequently asked questions

How heavy is an 18 mm aluminium foam panel compared with 18 mm MDF?

MDF weighs about 13 kg/m². An 18 mm foam panel with 0.5 mm aluminium faces weighs roughly 7 – 10 kg/m² depending on core density, so it is about a third to a half lighter.

Are aluminium foam panels the same as foam core aluminium panels?

No. "Foam core aluminium panels" usually means aluminium faces on a polymer foam core. Aluminium foam panels have a metal foam core, which does not burn and is more rigid.

How do hinges attach to aluminium foam panels?

Through solid inserts, anchors designed for sandwich panels, or aluminium edge profiles. Standard wood screws driven only into foam do not hold reliably.

Are aluminium foam panels fireproof?

The metal foam core is non-combustible. The fire class of the complete panel depends on adhesives and decorative faces and must be confirmed by test for the chosen build.

What are the disadvantages of aluminium foam panels?

Higher cost than wood boards and honeycomb, the need for inserts for screws, and tooling suited to metal. They are best used where moisture, fire or acoustic performance justify the premium.

Do aluminium foam panels release formaldehyde?

The metal core contains no formaldehyde-based resin. Emissions depend only on the adhesives and decorative layers, which can be specified as low- or no-formaldehyde products.
