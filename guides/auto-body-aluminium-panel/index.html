Auto Body Aluminium Panel Sheet

Auto body aluminium sheet is rolled 5xxx and 6xxx alloy sheet made for car body panels: bonnets, doors, roofs, wings, boot lids and the inner panels and structure behind them. Outer panels use bake-hardenable 6xxx alloys that form well in T4 and gain strength in the paint oven; inner and structural parts often use non-heat-treatable 5xxx alloys. This guide explains which alloy goes where, the tempers and surfaces involved, and how to specify sheet for a body programme.

Overview

Why aluminium is used for car body panels

An aluminium body panel weighs roughly half as much as a steel panel of equivalent stiffness and dent resistance, because the thinner-but-lighter steel sheet is replaced by a somewhat thicker aluminium sheet with one third of the density. On closures alone (bonnet, doors, boot lid, wings) the saving is typically tens of kilograms per vehicle, which improves range, handling and emissions.

Aluminium was first used widely on bonnets and boot lids and is now common on doors, roofs and complete bodies in white. Electric vehicles have accelerated the trend: every kilogram saved in the body offsets battery mass and extends range.

Body sheet is not ordinary commercial sheet. It must form into deep, sharp-featured shapes without splitting, hem tightly around inner panels, show no surface defects under glossy paint, bond and weld consistently, and respond predictably to the paint-bake cycle. That is why body sheet is produced to OEM specifications with tight control of chemistry, texture, surface and ageing.

2xxx alloys such as 2036 were used on outer panels in the 1970s and 1980s but were displaced by 6xxx alloys, which have better corrosion resistance, recyclability and paint-bake response. Some older component lists still mention them.

Alloy choices vary between OEMs and regions. Always follow the vehicle maker's material specification.

Tempers & paint bake

T4, T4P and the paint-bake response

6xxx body sheet is delivered in a solution heat-treated and naturally aged temper (T4) in which it is relatively soft and formable. After stamping and assembly the body passes through the paint-curing oven, typically around 170 – 185 °C for about 20 – 30 minutes, which artificially ages the alloy and raises its yield strength. This "bake hardening" gives dent resistance without sacrificing formability at the press.

T4

Solution treated, quenched and naturally aged. Formable, but continues to age slowly at room temperature, so press performance changes with storage time.

T4P (pre-aged)

Given a short pre-ageing treatment straight after quenching. This stabilises the sheet and strengthens the paint-bake response, so a short, cool oven still delivers good final strength.

O / H111 (5xxx)

Annealed 5182 and 5754 for maximum formability in inner and structural parts. Strength comes from magnesium in solid solution and work hardening during forming; there is no paint-bake gain.

Alloy and temperTypical behaviour
6016-T4 / T4PLow yield as delivered for good hemming and formability; yield roughly doubles after pre-strain and paint bake, typically reaching around 200 MPa
6111-T4Higher strength as delivered and after bake than 6016, for thinner, dent-resistant outer panels; slightly lower formability and hemming performance

Manufacturing

Forming, hemming and joining body sheet

Stamping

Aluminium has lower formability than mild steel and springs back more. Generous radii, optimised draw beads, good lubrication and simulation with correct material data are essential.

Hemming

Outer panels are folded 180° around inner panels. Alloys and tempers for outer panels are chosen to hem flat without cracking; 6016 in T4 and T4P hems well.

Stretcher strains

5xxx alloys can show Lüders lines (stretcher-strain marks) after forming, which show through paint. That is why 5xxx is kept to hidden inner and structural parts.

Joining

Self-piercing rivets, clinching and structural adhesive are the main methods; resistance spot welding, laser welding and flow-drill screws are also used. Pretreated surfaces give durable bonds.

Mixed materials

Where aluminium meets steel or carbon fibre, adhesive, sealer or coatings isolate the materials to prevent galvanic corrosion.

Storage

T4 sheet ages at room temperature, so press shops use stock in order of production and within the shelf life given by the specification.

Related automotive sheet

Brazing sheet for automotive heat exchangers

Besides body panels, cars use large amounts of clad aluminium brazing sheet in radiators, condensers, evaporators, heater cores, oil coolers, intercoolers and battery cooling plates. A 3xxx core is clad with a 4xxx brazing alloy that melts in the furnace, and often with a zinc-bearing 7072 layer that protects the core sacrificially.

FAQ

Auto body aluminium (aluminum) sheet: FAQs

Which aluminium alloy is used for car body outer panels?

6xxx bake-hardenable alloys: mainly 6016 in Europe and 6111 or 6022 in North America, with 6014 and 6451 also used. They form well in T4 and gain strength in the paint oven.

Why is 5182 not used for outer panels?

5xxx alloys can develop stretcher-strain marks during forming that show through paint, and they do not bake harden. They are excellent for inner and structural panels where the surface is hidden.

What is the difference between T4 and T4P?

Both are solution treated and naturally aged. T4P adds a pre-ageing step after quenching that stabilises the sheet during storage and gives a stronger response in the paint-bake oven.

How much weight does an aluminium bonnet save?

Typically around 40 – 50 % compared with an equivalent steel bonnet, because the aluminium sheet is slightly thicker but has one third of the density.

How are aluminium body panels joined?

Mainly with self-piercing rivets, clinching and structural adhesives, plus resistance spot and laser welding. Outer panels are hemmed around inner panels.

Does Metline make body panels?

No. Metline supplies the aluminium sheet, coil and blanks that stamping plants and body manufacturers form into panels, together with mill certificates.

Sourcing aluminium for a body programme?

Send the alloy, temper, gauge, width, surface and specification. We will reply with availability, documents and a delivered price.
