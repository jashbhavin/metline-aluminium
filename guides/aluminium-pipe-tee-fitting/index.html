Pipe fittings guide

Aluminium Pipe Tee Fittings

An aluminium pipe tee is a T-shaped fitting that takes a branch off a run of pipe, either at the same size (equal tee) or a smaller one (reducing tee). Tees are made in 6061, 6063, 5083 and other alloys with butt-weld, socket-weld, threaded, compression or flanged ends. This guide explains each type, the standards that fix their dimensions and pressure ratings, and how to choose the right alloy and size for your system.

Overview

What an aluminium tee does in a piping system

A tee has three openings: two on the run (the straight-through line) and one on the branch at 90°. It can split one flow into two, combine two flows into one, or simply provide an outlet for an instrument, drain or future connection.

Aluminium tees are used wherever the rest of the piping is aluminium: compressed-air and nitrogen networks, vacuum lines, cryogenic and LNG piping, marine fuel and water systems, irrigation, food and beverage process lines, chemical plant handling media that aluminium resists, and lightweight systems in aircraft ground equipment and vehicles. Matching the fitting alloy to the pipe avoids galvanic corrosion at the joint and lets the whole system be welded with one procedure.

Tees are produced in several ways. Small threaded fittings are usually forged or machined from bar. Butt-weld tees are formed from seamless pipe by hydraulic bulging or extrusion of the branch (so-called "drawn-outlet" tees), or fabricated by welding a branch pipe into a header. Very large or special-angle tees are fabricated from pipe or rolled plate. The manufacturing route affects strength and code acceptance, so it should be stated on the order for pressure work.

Three questions that define any tee

  • End connection: butt-weld, socket-weld, threaded, compression or flanged
  • Size: run and branch nominal size, and wall schedule for weld-end tees
  • Material and standard: alloy, temper and the specification it must be certified to
Types

Types of aluminium pipe tee

Equal (straight) tee

All three ends are the same nominal size. The most common tee, used to branch an air or water main into a same-size line or to add a takeoff.

Reducing tee

The branch is smaller than the run (for example 4 × 4 × 2 in). Used to feed a smaller line from a main without a separate reducer, which saves a weld and space.

Butt-weld tee

Bevelled ends welded directly to pipe of the same schedule. Full-strength, smooth bore and leak-tight; the standard choice for process, cryogenic and pressure piping. Dimensions to ASME B16.9.

Socket-weld tee

Pipe slides into a socket and is fillet-welded. Easier alignment on small bores (typically 2 in and under) and lower-pressure lines.

Threaded tee

Female NPT threads to ASME B1.20.1. Quick to install and dismantle; common for 150 lb class utility air, gas and water lines in small sizes. Military threaded aluminium fittings follow MIL-DTL-52618.

Compression and push-fit tees

Mechanical tees used in modular compressed-air systems on drawn aluminium tube. No welding or threading; joints can be reconfigured.

Flanged tee

Flanges on all ends so the tee can be bolted in and removed for maintenance. Used on larger lines and where equipment must be isolated.

Lateral (Y or slant) tee

The branch joins at an angle, usually 45°, which cuts turbulence and pressure loss. Common in drainage, bulk-solids conveying and exhaust lines.

Cross

A four-way variant with two branches. Less common because it concentrates stress and flow disturbance at one point.

Materials

Alloys and tempers for aluminium tees

ASTM B361 covers factory-made wrought aluminium welding fittings and lists the alloys that can be supplied, including 1060, 1100, 3003, 5083, 5086, 5154, 6061 and 6063. The table summarises how they are used.

AlloyTypical conditionBest suited toNotes
6061T6General industrial, compressed air, threaded and flanged teesHighest strength in the group and machines well; loses strength next to welds
6063T6, T52Low-pressure air, water, architectural and handrail teesSmooth surface, anodises well; lower strength than 6061
5083O, H112Cryogenic, LNG, marine and welded pressure systemsRetains strength after welding and stays tough at very low temperature
5086 / 5154O, H112Marine piping and welded vesselsSeawater resistant, weldable
3003O, H112Refrigeration, HVAC, chemical lines at low pressureVery formable, good corrosion resistance
1060 / 1100O, H112Chemical and food serviceHigh purity; low strength

Always match the tee alloy to the pipe alloy, or at least to the same family, so that the welding filler and corrosion behaviour are compatible. A 6061 tee welded into 5083 pipe is possible with ER5356 filler, but the 6061 heat-affected zone becomes the weak point of the joint.

Pressure

How tee pressure ratings are determined

A butt-weld tee made to ASME B16.9 is designed so that it is at least as strong as straight seamless pipe of the same material and schedule. In practice, that means the pressure rating of the system is calculated from the pipe (for example under ASME B31.3 for process piping) and the tee does not need a separate rating, provided it is ordered in the same alloy, temper and wall.

  • Welded systems in 6061 or 6063: base the calculation on the welded strength, because the heat-affected zone around each tee is weaker than the delivered T6 pipe.
  • Threaded tees: the thread root reduces the effective wall, and aluminium threads gall and crack if over-tightened. Keep threaded aluminium fittings to low-pressure utility service and small sizes.
  • Flanged tees: the flange class and gasket set the limit. Aluminium flanges follow the dimensions of the corresponding steel standard but carry lower pressure-temperature ratings.
  • Fabricated tees: a branch welded into a header needs reinforcement calculations (area replacement) and is less efficient than a formed tee.
  • Temperature: aluminium allowables fall above about 100 °C; most aluminium piping runs from cryogenic to around 150 °C.
Selection

How to choose the right tee

  1. Match the pipeConfirm the pipe OD and wall (NPS and schedule, or metric OD × wall). The tee ends must match exactly for butt welding; a mismatch of wall creates a step that disturbs flow and concentrates stress.
  2. Size the branch for flowSelect the branch size from the flow the branch must carry and the acceptable velocity and pressure drop, then decide between an equal tee and a reducing tee.
  3. Choose the end connectionButt-weld for permanent, high-integrity lines; socket-weld for small bores; threaded or compression for small utility lines that may be modified; flanged where removal is needed.
  4. Check pressure and temperatureConfirm the design pressure, temperature and code (B31.3, B31.1, EN 13480 or class rules) and whether welded allowables apply.
  5. Specify standard and documentsState ASTM B361 for wrought welding fittings, ASME B16.9 for dimensions, and the certificate required, normally EN 10204 3.1.

In compressed-air networks, tees are best installed with the branch taken off the top of the main, so condensed water stays in the main and drains away rather than running into tools. See the aluminium air piping guide for layout advice.

Aluminium wins on weight, clean bore and low-temperature service. Steel remains the choice for high-pressure, high-temperature and fire-exposed lines, where aluminium's lower melting range and strength are limiting.

Custom fittings

Custom and non-standard aluminium tees

When the standard range does not fit, tees can be made to drawing. Common requests include tees in metric OD × wall sizes that do not match ANSI schedules, heavy-wall tees for cryogenic headers, lateral tees at 30° or 60°, tees with integral flanges or threaded bosses, and tees machined from solid 6061 bar or forgings for hydraulic or pneumatic manifolds.

What to send

A drawing or sketch with run and branch OD and wall, centre-to-end dimensions, end preparation, alloy and temper, quantity and testing requirements.

Manufacturing route

Machined from bar or forgings for small and complex tees; formed from seamless pipe for weld tees; fabricated from pipe or plate for large diameters.

Inspection

Dimensional check, visual inspection of the crotch area (where wall thinning occurs in formed tees) and, on request, dye-penetrant or radiography of welds.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) pipe tees: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an equal tee and a reducing tee?

In an equal tee all three ends are the same size. In a reducing tee the branch is smaller than the run, for example 3 × 3 × 1-1/2 in, which feeds a smaller line directly without a separate reducer.

Which aluminum alloy is used for pipe tees?

6061-T6 and 6063 are most common for air, water and general industrial lines. 5083 is used for cryogenic, marine and welded pressure systems because it keeps its strength after welding. 3003 and 1100 suit low-pressure chemical and refrigeration lines.

What standard covers aluminium butt-weld tees?

ASTM B361 covers the material and manufacture of wrought aluminium welding fittings, and ASME B16.9 gives the dimensions and tolerances. Pipe wall thickness follows the schedules in ASME B36.10M.

What pressure can an aluminium tee handle?

A B16.9 butt-weld tee is rated the same as matching seamless pipe, so the system rating comes from the pipe calculation. Small threaded aluminium tees are low-pressure fittings, typically catalogued at about 150 psi at room temperature.

Can aluminium tees be connected to steel or copper pipe?

Avoid direct contact, because aluminium corrodes galvanically when joined to copper or steel in the presence of moisture. Use insulating flange kits, dielectric unions or aluminium-to-steel transition joints.

Can you make tees in metric sizes?

Yes. Tees can be produced to metric OD × wall dimensions, or machined or fabricated to your drawing, in the same alloy as the pipe.
