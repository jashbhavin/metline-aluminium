Oversize aluminium guide

Large Aluminium Products: Plate, Bar, Tube, Extrusions and Forgings Beyond Standard Sizes

Large aluminium products are mill products made beyond the size limits of ordinary stock: plate wider than 2.5 m or thicker than about 150 mm, bar and billet several hundred millimetres in diameter, seamless tube approaching a metre across, very wide extrusions and heavy forged rings. They allow one-piece machined structures, fewer welds and shorter fabrication routes on major projects. This guide explains each product family, its realistic size envelope, the alloys used and how to specify and ship oversize aluminium.

Overview

What are large aluminium products?

"Large" is relative to what ordinary rolling mills, extrusion presses and forges produce. Standard sheet and plate stops at about 2,000 – 2,500 mm wide; most extrusions fit inside a 300 mm circle; most round bar stops at about 300 – 500 mm diameter.

Beyond those limits, only a limited number of plants worldwide have the hot-rolling width, stretcher capacity, press force or forging equipment to make the product, and every order needs engineering attention: the casting must be sound through its full thickness, heat treatment must reach the centre, residual stress must be controlled, and the finished piece must be handled and shipped safely.

Buyers turn to oversize aluminium for three reasons. First, integration: a part machined from one plate or forging has no welds, no bolted joints and no assembly tolerance stack-up. Second, weight: aluminium is about one third the density of steel, so even a massive aluminium structure is far easier to lift, ship and install. Third, process savings: material delivered close to the final shape cuts welding, inspection and rough-machining time.

Typical buyers of oversize aluminium

  • Aerospace and space structures machined from thick plate and forgings
  • Semiconductor and flat-panel equipment makers needing large vacuum chamber blanks
  • Rail, marine and offshore builders using wide plate and large extrusions
  • Power and electrolysis plants using heavy busbar and large conductors
  • Mould, die and machine-building shops cutting large blocks and base plates

Plate

Ultra-wide and ultra-thick aluminium plate

Ultra-wide plate comes from hot-rolling mills with wide work rolls and stretchers long and strong enough to stretch the whole plate after quenching. Plates of 3 – 4 m width are used for aircraft wing skins, ship and LNG tank panels, rail car floors and semiconductor equipment frames, where a single wide plate removes a longitudinal weld. Hot-rolled coil and plate are also produced in widths of about 2,600 mm for large panels and tanks.

Ultra-thick plate raises a different challenge: the centre of a thick plate cools more slowly when quenched, so heat-treatable alloys lose some strength at mid-thickness. This is why alloys with low quench sensitivity are chosen for thick sections. 7050-T7451 and 7085 are the aerospace choices for very thick plate, while 7075-T651 is usually limited to more moderate thicknesses. For general engineering, 6061-T651 and 6082-T651 thick plate are standard, and cast plate or 5083 is preferred when flatness and stability matter more than strength.

Stretching to relieve quench stress is possible only up to the stretcher limit, typically around 200 – 250 mm. Thicker blocks are supplied as-rolled, forged or cast, and may be stress relieved by compression or by careful rough machining and ageing.

Long products

Large-diameter bar, tube and square tube

Large-diameter bar and billet

Above about 300 mm, bar is usually forged from cast ingot or supplied as homogenised cast billet. Forging closes porosity and refines grain, so forged bar is preferred for highly stressed parts. Diameters of 600 – 700 mm are common in heavy machining; larger sizes are possible. See large-diameter aluminium bar.

Large seamless tube

Made by piercing and extruding a large billet on a heavy press (including vertical presses), by forging and boring, or by machining from solid. Used for pressure vessel shells, nuclear and petrochemical piping and large cylinders. See large-diameter tube and pipe.

Spiral and seam-welded pipe

For very large diameters with thin walls, aluminium strip or plate is roll-formed and welded. Suitable for ducting, low-pressure pipelines and structural columns. See spiral-welded aluminium pipe.

Extra-large square and rectangular tube

Square sections up to about 1,200 × 1,200 mm are made by extruding a heavy round tube and forming it to square, with walls of several centimetres. See extra-large square tube.

High-precision large tube

Large tubes can be turned and bored to close tolerances and fine surface finishes for cylinders and rollers. See high-precision large tube.

Machined blanks

Any of these can be supplied rough-turned, bored or cut to length so that you receive a blank ready for finish machining.

Shaped products

Large extrusions, forgings and rings

Large extrusions extend the extrusion process to wide hollow panels, heavy beams and long profiles. Rail car body panels, interlocking trailer floors, EV battery tray members and bridge decks are typical. The design rules and size limits are covered in our large aluminium extrusions guide.

Large forgings are made by open-die forging, ring rolling or closed-die forging on heavy presses. Forging aligns grain flow with the shape of the part and closes internal porosity, giving the best fatigue and fracture properties of any product form. Large forged rings are used for rocket and launch-vehicle structures, bearing housings, slewing rings, flanges and pressure vessel connections; open-die discs and blocks serve as blanks for heavy machined parts. See large size forged products and rolled ring forgings.

Handling

How heavy are large aluminium products?

Weight drives lifting, packing and freight decisions, so check it early. Aluminium alloys have a density of about 2.70 – 2.80 g/cm³; the examples below use 2.70 g/cm³ (multiply by 1.03 for 7xxx alloys, which are slightly denser).

Quality

Inspection and testing of large products

Large sections are more likely to hide internal defects than thin products, so inspection matters more. A sensible specification covers:

  • Ultrasonic testing to ASTM B594 or AMS-STD-2154 for aerospace and critical parts, with the acceptance class stated on the order
  • Mechanical testing at specified locations, including through-thickness (short-transverse) tests for thick plate and forgings
  • Conductivity and hardness checks to confirm heat treatment, especially for 7xxx alloys
  • Dimensional inspection of flatness, straightness, ovality and wall thickness, using the tolerances in ASTM B209, EN 485, EN 755 or the drawing
  • Residual stress control by stretching, compression or stress-relief ageing, which reduces distortion during heavy machining
  • Documentation: EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate with heat number traceability; third-party inspection on request
Ordering

How to specify and ship oversize aluminium

  1. Start from the finished partSend the part drawing, not only the blank size. We can then suggest whether plate, forging, bar or tube gives the lowest total cost after machining.
  2. State alloy, temper and standardFor example 7050-T7451 to AMS 4050, or 6061-T651 to ASTM B209, and any ultrasonic class or property direction requirements.
  3. Agree machining allowanceLarge blanks are often supplied with a few millimetres of machining stock per face, or rough machined to save freight weight.
  4. Plan handlingConfirm crane capacity at your site, maximum piece weight and length, and preferred lifting method.
  5. Packing and shipmentHeavy pieces are strapped to timber skids or steel frames, wrapped against moisture and shipped in containers, on flat racks or break-bulk on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.

FAQ

Large aluminium (aluminum) products: FAQ

What is the widest aluminium plate available?

A few mills produce stretched heat-treated plate around 3,500 – 4,000 mm wide, mainly for aerospace and shipbuilding. Most general-purpose plate is 1,250 – 2,500 mm wide. Tell us the width you need and we confirm feasibility for the alloy and temper.

How thick can aluminium plate be?

Stretched plate is generally available up to about 200 – 250 mm. Thicker blocks of 300 – 600 mm are supplied as-rolled, forged or cast. For very thick heat-treated sections, choose alloys with low quench sensitivity such as 7050.

Is a forged block better than thick plate?

Forged blocks have more uniform properties in all directions and fewer internal defects, which matters for highly stressed parts. Thick plate is cheaper and is fine for frames, bases and moderately loaded components.

Can large aluminum products be supplied machined?

Yes. Oversize pieces can be sawn, rough-turned, bored or milled to drawing so that they arrive close to final shape, which also reduces freight weight.

How are oversize aluminium pieces shipped?

Heavy or long pieces are fixed to skids or frames, wrapped against moisture and shipped in containers, on flat racks or as break-bulk cargo, with lifting points agreed in advance.

Which certificates come with large aluminium products?

Every shipment carries an EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate with chemistry and mechanical results. Ultrasonic test reports and third-party inspection can be arranged when specified.

Have a project that needs oversize aluminium?

Send the part drawing or blank size, alloy, quantity and destination. We will recommend the starting form and quote delivered.
