Aluminium Materials for EV Power Batteries

A modern EV battery pack uses aluminium at every level, from 10–20 µm cathode foil inside each cell to 6xxx extrusions in the pack frame. Each part needs a different alloy, temper and gauge. This guide maps the main battery components to the aluminium grades used for them, explains why, and lists what to specify when you buy the material from Metline.

Overview

Why aluminium is everywhere in a battery pack

Aluminium does four jobs in a traction battery: it carries current, moves heat, contains the cell chemistry and holds the pack together. It does all four at about one third of the density of steel or copper, which matters in a component that can weigh several hundred kilograms.

At the cathode, aluminium foil is the only practical current collector, because it is stable at the high positive electrode potential where copper would corrode. In prismatic and cylindrical cells, deep-drawn aluminium cans are lighter than nickel-plated steel and easy to laser weld. Across the module, aluminium busbars and brazed cold plates carry current and heat. At pack level, extruded 6xxx frames and 5xxx or 6xxx sheet form the tray, crash structure and lid.

The catch is that each of these parts has a different priority. Foil needs purity and pinhole-free rolling, cans need deep drawability and weld quality, cold plates need a brazing clad layer, and tray members need strength and crash energy absorption. Choosing the wrong grade for a part is the most common cause of forming cracks, weld porosity or failed corrosion tests.

Inside the cell

Cell-level aluminium: foil, cans and covers

Cathode foil

Rolled from 1xxx alloys to H18 for tensile strength on high-speed coating lines. Battery foil is cleaner and tighter in thickness than packaging foil, with strict limits on pinholes, oil residue and surface wettability. Carbon-coated foil improves adhesion and lowers contact resistance.

Pouch film foil

8021 and 8079 in the O temper are laminated between nylon and polypropylene layers. The iron-rich 8xxx alloys give fine grains and the deep-draw ductility needed to form the cell pocket without cracking.

Prismatic can

3003 is the most common shell material: manganese gives more strength than 1xxx while keeping excellent drawability. 3005 adds magnesium for a little more strength; 1050 is used where conductivity or very deep draws matter more.

Cover plate

Flat 3003 or 1xxx sheet that carries the terminals and safety vent, laser welded to the can. Flatness and clean, oxide-controlled surfaces are critical for a sound weld.

Tabs and flexible links

Soft 1060 or 1070 strip connects pouch-cell electrodes and flexes to absorb movement between cells. Surface cleanliness affects ultrasonic and laser weld strength.

Cylindrical cell cans

Most cylindrical cells still use nickel-plated steel cans, but some designs use aluminium cans and housings, typically from 3xxx alloys.

For prismatic cans, see our dedicated 1050, 3003 and 3005 battery shell guide, which covers chemistry, tolerances and laser-weld requirements in detail.

Module and thermal management

Busbars and cooling plates

Inside a module, busbars link cell terminals in series and parallel. Aluminium busbars are attractive because the cell terminal itself is usually aluminium on the positive side, so an aluminium busbar avoids a dissimilar-metal joint there. 1060 and 1070 give the highest conductivity (about 61–62 % IACS); 6101-T6 adds strength for longer spans. Laminated and insulated busbars combine several conductor layers with insulation films.

Thermal management is the second big aluminium user. Liquid cold plates are usually made from brazing sheet: a 3003 or modified 3003 core clad on one or both sides with a 4343 or 4045 Al-Si filler layer that melts in the brazing furnace and joins stamped channels into a leak-tight plate. Extruded multiport tubes and harp-type plates do the same job in other designs. The choice of core alloy affects post-braze strength and corrosion resistance in water-glycol coolant.

Pack level

Pack trays, frames and lids

The battery tray (or enclosure) protects the cells from crash loads, road debris, water and fire, and in many vehicles also stiffens the floor. The common construction is a frame of 6xxx hollow extrusions, cross members and a sheet or extruded floor, joined by friction stir welding, MIG welding, adhesive bonding and self-piercing rivets.

Specification

What to specify when buying battery aluminium

  • Alloy and temper for each part, plus the governing standard (ASTM B209, EN 485, EN 573-3, or your customer specification)
  • Gauge and tolerance: battery parts often need tighter thickness tolerances than general-purpose sheet and foil
  • Width and coil inner diameter matched to your slitting, stamping or deep-drawing line
  • Surface requirements: cleanliness, oil level, wettability (dyne value) for foil, and surface defects for cans
  • Mechanical properties: tensile range, elongation and earing limits for deep drawing
  • Clad ratio and filler alloy for brazing sheet
  • Documents: mill test certificate (EN 10204 3.1), RoHS / REACH declarations, and PPAP support where your customer requires it

Because battery suppliers qualify materials carefully, plan for sample coils and a trial run on your own line. Lot-to-lot consistency matters as much as meeting the specification once.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) for EV power batteries: FAQ

Why is aluminium foil used for the cathode and copper for the anode?

Aluminium forms a stable passive layer at the high potential of the positive electrode, so it does not corrode there. At the low potential of the negative electrode, aluminium would alloy with lithium, so copper foil is used on the anode side.

Which aluminium alloy is used for prismatic battery cases?

3003 in the O, H12 or H14 temper is the most common, because it combines good deep drawability with more strength than pure aluminium. 3005 and 1050 are also used depending on the design.

What aluminium is used in battery cooling plates?

Usually brazing sheet: a 3003 or modified 3003 core clad with a 4343 or 4045 aluminium-silicon filler, stamped and furnace brazed into a leak-tight plate. Extruded multiport tubes are an alternative.

Which alloys are used for EV battery trays?

Frames and cross members are typically 6061, 6082, 6005A or 6063 extrusions in T6, with 5xxx or 6xxx sheet for floors and lids.
