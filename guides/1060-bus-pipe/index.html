1060 and 1070 Conductive Aluminium Tubular Bus Pipe

Tubular bus pipe in 1060 or 1070 aluminium is chosen when conductivity matters more than span strength: about 61 – 62 % IACS, compared with roughly 52 – 57 % for the 6063 and 6101 alloys used on long outdoor spans. Pure-grade pipe suits high-current indoor runs, short substation connections, jumpers and DC equipment. This guide covers properties, sizes, resistance, tolerances and how to choose.

Overview

What is a tubular aluminium bus pipe?

A bus pipe (tubular busbar) is a rigid, hollow aluminium conductor used to carry large currents between equipment, mainly in substations and heavy-current installations. It replaces flat bars, channels or flexible strung conductors where a stiff, compact and low-loss conductor is wanted.

The round hollow section has three electrical and mechanical advantages. For alternating current, the current crowds towards the outer surface of a conductor (skin effect), so metal at the centre of a solid bar does little work; a tube puts the metal where the current flows, giving a lower AC resistance for a given weight. The smooth, large-diameter surface spreads the electric field, raising the corona inception voltage at high voltage. And a tube has a high stiffness-to-weight ratio, so it can span between support insulators with limited sag.

Most outdoor substation tubes are made from heat-treatable 6063-T6 or 6101-T6, which are much stronger. Pure 1060 and 1070 are used when the conductor is short or well supported and the extra 5 – 10 points of conductivity reduce losses and temperature rise, or when the tube must be bent and welded extensively.

Benefits of tubular bus conductors

  • Lower AC resistance per kilogram than a solid bar, because of skin effect
  • Higher corona inception voltage and lower radio interference at high voltage
  • Good natural cooling by convection from the large surface
  • Rigid: fewer supports than flexible conductors, and a compact layout

Between the two pure grades, the practical difference is small: 1070 is slightly purer and marginally more conductive, 1060 slightly stronger and often cheaper. Choose 1060 as the economical default, 1070 where your specification sets a minimum conductivity close to 62 % IACS. For long spans, calculate sag and short-circuit forces first; if a pure-grade tube would need a larger diameter or more supports than the alloy tube, 6101 or 6063 is usually the better overall choice.

Tolerances

Dimensional tolerances and straightness

Extruded and drawn bus tube is normally supplied to the tolerance tables of EN 755-8 (extruded tube) or ANSI H35.2 for ASTM B241 pipe, at standard or precision level. As an example of scale, standard wall tolerances on extruded tube are a few tenths of a millimetre for walls around 5 – 8 mm, rising to about ±1 mm or more on 12 – 15 mm walls, and diameter tolerances grow from about ±0.3 mm at 60 – 80 mm OD to over ±1 mm above 250 mm OD.

  • State whether tolerances apply to the average or to any point on the section, and whether precision tolerances are required
  • Specify straightness (for example a maximum deviation per metre and over the total length) for long outdoor runs
  • Agree length tolerance; bus pipes are usually cut with a small plus tolerance to allow trimming at site
  • Ask for eddy-current or ultrasonic testing if the tube will be welded and must be free of internal defects
Processing

Bending, welding and surface treatment

Bending

Pure grades bend to tight radii without cracking. Use O or H112 tube for complex bends, with a mandrel or sand fill on thin walls.

Welding

TIG or MIG with 1100 or 4043 filler. Because pure grades are not heat-treated, welds do not suffer the large strength drop seen in 6063-T6.

Joints and fittings

Welded or bolted couplers, expansion fittings and support clamps should be aluminium or bimetallic to avoid galvanic corrosion.

Contact surfaces

Tin, silver or nickel plating, or copper cladding, improves contact resistance at bolted joints and connections to copper equipment.

Cutting and drilling

Saw cutting, drilling and punching of terminal flats to drawing.

Damping

Long outdoor tubes may need internal damping conductors to control aeolian vibration.

Applications

Where 1060 and 1070 bus pipe is used

  • Main and connecting bus in substations where spans are short or well supported
  • Connections between transmission line terminations and substation equipment
  • Jumpers on transmission lines
  • Internal conductors in transformers, switchgear and generator connections
  • High-current DC equipment such as de-icing systems, rectifier and electrolysis plants
  • Indoor high-current distribution where low losses and natural cooling are important
Ordering

How to specify and order

  1. Grade and temper1060 or 1070 and the temper (O, H112, H18), with a minimum conductivity if required.
  2. DimensionsOD, wall, length and tolerances (standard or precision), straightness limit.
  3. ProcessingBending, welding, drilling, plating or cladding to drawing.
  4. Testing and documentsTensile, conductivity and dimensional reports on an EN 10204 3.1 certificate; third-party inspection on request.
  5. Packing and termsLong tubes protected in crates or on reinforced bundles with end caps; EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) bus pipe: FAQ

Why use a tube instead of a solid busbar?

Alternating current concentrates near the conductor surface, so a tube uses its metal more effectively than a solid bar of the same weight. Tubes are also stiff, cool well and raise the corona inception voltage.

Is 1060 or 6063 better for substation bus pipe?

6063-T6 is about three times stronger and is the usual choice for long outdoor spans. 1060 conducts about 15 % better, so it suits short or well-supported runs where lower losses and easier bending and welding matter.

What is the conductivity of 1060 aluminium?

About 61 – 62 % IACS, a resistivity of roughly 0.028 Ω·mm²/m at 20 °C. Hard tempers are slightly lower than annealed.

Can bus pipe be bent and welded?

Yes. Pure grades bend and weld easily, and because they are not heat-treated, welds keep most of the parent strength. Plating or cladding can be added at contact surfaces.

Does Metline supply bus pipe outside India?

Yes. Metline supplies bus pipe worldwide with mill certificates and export crating, on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
