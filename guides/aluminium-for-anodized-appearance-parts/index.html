Alloy selection guide

How to Choose Aluminium for Anodised Appearance Parts

For a visible anodised part, the alloy and the way it was made decide the final look more than the anodising line does. Silicon, iron, copper and manganese all leave their mark in the oxide, and grain structure shows through as streaks or sparkle. This guide matches alloys to finishes (bright, mirror, natural, bead-blasted, brushed and black) and lists the material checks that prevent rejected batches.

The principle

Why the alloy decides how an anodised part looks

Anodising does not add a coating on top of the metal; it converts the surface of the aluminium itself into aluminium oxide. Pure aluminium oxide is colourless and transparent, so anything else in the metal ends up inside the film or under it. Alloying elements and impurity particles either dissolve, stay in the film as inclusions, or leave tiny voids, and each of these changes how light passes through the oxide. That is why two parts anodised side by side in the same tank can look completely different.

For structural parts, designers choose an alloy for strength, then ask whether it can be anodised. For appearance parts the order reverses: start from the finish the customer will see, then find the strongest, most formable alloy that can deliver it consistently. Treating these as separate decisions is the most common source of rework.

Alloy routes

Four alloy routes for appearance parts

Bright and mirror route: 1050, 1070, 1085, 5657, 6463

High-purity base metal with very low iron and silicon. Polishes and chemically brightens to a near-mirror and keeps that brightness through anodising. Strength is modest, so this route suits trim, cosmetic packaging, reflectors and decorative parts that carry little load.

Decorative sheet route: 5005, 5252

Aluminium-magnesium alloys with controlled impurities. 5005 is the architectural "anodising quality" sheet that matches 6063 extrusions; 5252 is a high-purity bright-trim alloy with 5052-level strength and a clearer film.

Housing route: 5052, 5252, 6063, 6061

Balanced strength, formability and machinability for enclosures, consumer electronics, appliance fronts and handles. Usually finished bead-blasted or brushed with natural or black anodising.

Structural appearance route: 6061, 6063, 6013, 7xxx

Used when a visible part must also carry load, such as frames, mid-frames and sports equipment. Requires the tightest control of heat treatment and machining, and the finish is chosen to hide what cannot be controlled.

Chemistry ranges summarised from the Aluminum Association registrations. 5657, 5252 and 6463 are standard designations whose low iron and silicon limits exist specifically for bright anodised finishes.

Alloys to avoid for cosmetic anodising include 3003 and other manganese-bearing grades (grey, blotchy), 2xxx copper alloys (yellow, soft film), 4xxx silicon alloys (dark grey) and most die-casting alloys such as A380 and ADC12, whose high silicon gives a dark, uneven film. If a cast part must match wrought parts, consider painting instead.

Black anodising deserves special mention because it is unforgiving. A black part that looks perfect under the line lights can show bluish, reddish or greenish casts in daylight, and adjacent parts from different coils or extrusion billets may not match. Specify one alloy, one temper and, where possible, one material lot for parts that will sit next to each other in the assembly.

Applications

Typical choices by application

Consumer electronics housings

Laptop lids, tablet backs, speaker grilles, smart-home devices and SIM trays. Sheet housings are commonly 5052 or 5252; machined unibody parts use 6061, 6063 or 6013, and some premium phones use 7xxx alloys. Bead-blast plus natural, grey or black anodising is the usual finish.

Decorative panels and trim

Appliance fronts, elevator panels, furniture and automotive interior trim. 5005 and 5252 sheet for flat panels; 6063 and 6463 for profiles; brushed or natural finishes.

Bright cosmetic packaging

Lipstick cases, perfume caps, cosmetic tubes and bottle collars, often deep-drawn or impact-extruded. 1050, 1070 and high-purity 5xxx; polished or bright-dipped before coloured anodising.

Architectural

Facade cladding, column covers and window trim where panels and extrusions must match. 5005 sheet paired with 6063 extrusions, natural or bronze anodised to AAMA 611 or Qualanod requirements.

Structural visible parts

Bicycle components, camera rigs, drone frames and tool housings. 6061-T6 and 7075-T6 machined parts, usually bead-blasted and dark anodised to hide machining variation.

Lighting and reflectors

High-purity 1085 to 1199 grades, or 5657, brightened and anodised for high reflectance and protection.

Material control

Material requirements to put on the purchase order

  • Specify "anodising quality" (AQ): mills produce AQ sheet and extrusions with tighter impurity limits, controlled grain and inspected surfaces; commercial-quality material of the same alloy may not anodise evenly
  • Fix alloy and temper together: 5052-H32 and 5052-H34 can look slightly different; so can 6061-T6 and T651
  • Single lot for matched parts: order parts that sit side by side from one coil or one extrusion run where possible
  • Surface protection: PVC film or interleaving paper to prevent rub marks, which become very visible after etching
  • Grain direction: state it on the drawing for brushed parts so the brush lines follow the rolling direction
  • Sample approval: anodise samples from the actual material lot and approve them against boundary samples (lightest and darkest acceptable) before production

For extrusions, ask about billet quality as well as alloy. Homogenised billet with fine, uniform grain, extruded at controlled temperature and quenched evenly, is what makes 6063 anodise uniformly. Streaks along a profile usually trace back to die lines, coarse grain at the surface, or uneven quench, not to the anodising line.

Anodising type

Type II or Type III for appearance parts?

Most appearance parts are finished with conventional sulphuric acid anodising (MIL-A-8625 Type II; ISO 7599 classes from about 5 to 25 µm). It accepts dyes in a full range of colours, keeps a bright or satin look, and is thick enough for indoor durability at 10 – 15 µm and for outdoor architectural use at 20 – 25 µm.

Hard anodising (Type III) builds a much thicker, harder film, but it is naturally grey to bronze or dark grey depending on alloy, takes dye less evenly and dulls a bright surface. Use it where wear resistance is the main requirement, and expect a functional rather than decorative look. Assuming that thicker or harder means "better looking" is a common mistake.

Ordering

What to include in an RFQ for appearance parts

  1. Part type and functionHousing, panel, trim, frame or packaging component; what load or wear it must take.
  2. Target finishBright, natural, bead-blasted, brushed or dyed; colour and gloss; reference sample if available.
  3. Alloy directionPreferred alloy and temper, or the requirements from which we can suggest one.
  4. Process routeForming, deep drawing, CNC machining, extrusion and any welding before anodising.
  5. Drawing and quantityDimensions, cosmetic surfaces marked A/B/C, quantity, and the standard for the finish (ISO 7599, MIL-A-8625, AAMA 611 or customer specification).

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) for anodised appearance parts: FAQ

Which aluminium alloy gives the best anodised appearance?

For bright and mirror finishes, high-purity grades such as 1050, 1070, 5657 and 6463 give the clearest film. For housings that also need strength, 5252 and 5052 sheet or 6063 and 6061 extrusions and machined parts are the usual choices. There is no single best alloy; match it to the finish and the load.

Which alloys are best for black anodised parts?

6063, 6061, 5052 and 5252 all dye black well. The bigger challenge is consistency: keep one alloy, temper and material lot for parts that sit side by side, because black shows small differences in film and substrate.

What is the difference between 5052 and 5252 for appearance parts?

Both contain about 2.2 – 2.8 % magnesium and have similar strength. 5252 has much lower iron and silicon and no chromium addition, so it anodises clearer and brighter. Choose 5252 for premium bright or light-coloured finishes and 5052 for bead-blasted or dark finishes where cost matters.

Should I use 6063 or 6061 for an anodised extrusion?

6063 anodises more uniformly and is the standard for visible profiles, trim and frames. 6061 is stronger and machines better but anodises slightly greyer. Use 6061 when the part carries significant load, and finish it bead-blasted or dark to hide variation.

Can die-cast aluminium be anodised for appearance?

Most die-casting alloys contain 7 – 12 % silicon, which produces a dark grey, blotchy film. They can be anodised for protection, but for cosmetic parts painting or powder coating is usually a better choice, or the part can be redesigned as a wrought extrusion or machined part.

Is hard anodising suitable for decorative parts?

Usually not. Type III hard anodising is thick and naturally grey to bronze, and it dulls bright surfaces. It is the right choice for wear resistance; for appearance parts, Type II sulphuric anodising is the normal starting point.
