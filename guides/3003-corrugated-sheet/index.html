3003 Aluminium Corrugated Sheet

3003 corrugated sheet is aluminium-manganese sheet roll-formed into a wave or trapezoidal profile for roofs, walls, vehicle bodies and decorative cladding. It is about 20 % stronger than 1xxx sheet, keeps excellent corrosion resistance, and weighs about one third as much as steel sheet of the same thickness. Here is how to choose the profile, gauge, temper and finish.

Overview

Why 3003 is the standard alloy for corrugated aluminium

Flat aluminium sheet is flexible; corrugating it moves material away from the neutral axis and multiplies its bending stiffness, so a thin sheet can span between purlins and carry wind, snow and foot loads.

3003 adds about 1.2 % manganese to aluminium. That raises strength over 1050 or 1100 by roughly 20 % while keeping the same corrosion resistance and ductility, so the sheet roll-forms cleanly with tight radii and does not crack at the crests. For heavier structural decking or very exposed marine sites, 5052 or 3004 is sometimes preferred; for the lowest cost on light wall cladding, 1xxx or 3105 is used. For most roofing and cladding, 3003 is the balanced choice.

Gauge selection

Choosing thickness and estimating weight

Flat-sheet weight is 2.73 kg/m² per millimetre of thickness; a corrugated sheet uses roughly 10 – 20 % more flat material per square metre of cover, depending on profile depth.

ThicknessFlat weight (kg/m²)Typical use
0.40 – 0.50 mm1.1 – 1.4Interior linings, light wall cladding on close supports
0.60 – 0.70 mm1.6 – 1.9Wall cladding, small roofs, sheds
0.80 – 0.90 mm2.2 – 2.5General roofing, coastal buildings, vehicle bodies
1.0 – 1.2 mm2.7 – 3.3Wider purlin spacing, foot traffic, industrial roofs
1.5 mm4.1Deep deck, heavy-duty and container use

Purlin spacing must come from the profile load tables for the chosen gauge and the local wind and snow loads. As a rule, aluminium needs either closer supports or a slightly thicker gauge than steel for the same span, because its elastic modulus is about one third of steel's.

Surface options

Finishes and coatings

Mill finish

Bare metal that weathers to a soft grey. Cheapest and fully recyclable; normal for agricultural and industrial buildings.

Stucco embossed

A textured pattern that hides handling marks and reduces glare.

Polyester (PE) coated

Coil-coated before forming in a wide range of colours; suits most inland buildings.

PVDF coated

Fluoropolymer paint with better colour and gloss retention for facades and coastal projects.

Anodised

Used for decorative and interior corrugated panels; usually done after forming on cut sheets.

Brushed or printed

Decorative finishes for interiors, retail and furniture.

Coil coating is applied to flat coil before roll-forming so the paint flexes with the profile; standard colours include whites, greys, bronzes and black. Coated coil generally follows EN 1396 or the coater's specification for film thickness and durability.

Aluminium wins where weight, corrosion or long life without repainting matter: coastal homes, livestock buildings with ammonia, chemical plants, transport bodies and remote sites where freight is expensive. Steel remains cheaper up front for inland buildings with long spans.

Good practice

Installation and handling tips

  • Use aluminium or stainless steel fasteners with EPDM washers; never bare steel or copper
  • Separate aluminium from steel purlins with tape or paint, and from wet timber treated with copper preservatives
  • Drill oversized or slotted holes on long sheets to allow for thermal movement of about 2.4 mm per metre per 100 °C
  • Store bundles dry and slightly inclined; trapped condensation between sheets causes white staining
  • Packed stacks are normally limited in height and weight (often about 500 mm and 2 t) for safe lifting
Applications

Where 3003 corrugated sheet is used

  • Roofing and walls: homes, warehouses, farm and poultry buildings, coastal structures
  • Architecture and interiors: feature walls, ceilings, shopfronts, furniture panels
  • Transport: truck and trailer sides, caravans, container panels where payload matters
  • Industrial: equipment enclosures, insulation jacketing and cladding in chemical plants

FAQ

3003 corrugated aluminium sheet: frequently asked questions

What thickness of aluminium corrugated sheet should I use for a roof?

Usually 0.7 – 1.0 mm for 3003 on typical purlin spacing; check the profile load table against your wind and snow loads.

Is 3003 better than 1100 for corrugated roofing?

Yes for most roofs: it is about 20 % stronger with the same corrosion resistance, so it resists dents and foot traffic better.

Will aluminium corrugated sheet rust?

No. Aluminium forms a protective oxide instead of rust. It can stain white if stored wet, and it corrodes if in contact with steel or copper in damp conditions.

Which temper is used for pre-painted corrugated sheet?

Usually H24 or H26, because the paint curing partially anneals the metal; the result behaves much like H14 or H16.

How does aluminium compare with steel roofing?

It is about a third of the weight and lasts longer in coastal or chemical environments, but costs more per square metre and needs a thicker gauge or closer supports.

Can you supply sheets cut to length?

Yes. Corrugated sheet is normally cut to the lengths on your cutting list, with export packing.
