High-Formability Aluminium Sheet for 3C Internal Components

Internal parts of computers, communication devices and consumer electronics (the "3C" sector) are stamped, bent and drawn from thin aluminium sheet at high speed, and they must stay flat afterwards. This guide explains which alloys and tempers form best, how to control springback, flatness and residual stress, and what to specify so that LED back plates, keyboard plates, mid-plates and brackets come off the press consistently.

Overview

Why internal electronic parts need formable aluminium

Housings get the attention, but most of the aluminium inside a laptop, monitor, television, router or phone is hidden: back plates that carry LED strips and spread their heat, keyboard support plates, mid-plates, shields and brackets. These parts are stamped in progressive or transfer dies at tens of strokes per minute, and they must fit a tightly packed assembly with almost no tolerance for warp.

That combination makes formability the first selection criterion, ahead of maximum strength. A sheet that cracks at a tight bend, wrinkles in a shallow draw or springs open after forming will scrap parts and damage dies. A sheet that is formable but carries locked-in stress will look fine off the press and then bow after piercing or trimming releases that stress. The right material balances elongation, a predictable yield strength, flatness and a clean surface for bonding or anodising.

Exterior shells are a different problem, where cosmetic anodising and stiffness lead the choice; see our 3C housing sheet guide for that route. This page is about the internal structure.

What "high formability" means in practice

  • High uniform elongation and a good strain-hardening exponent (n-value), so strain spreads instead of necking
  • Low and consistent yield strength within the coil, so springback is predictable from part to part
  • Fine, uniform grain to avoid orange peel on bends and draws
  • Low residual stress from levelling and annealing, so parts stay flat after piercing and trimming

Other alloys earn a place for specific reasons. 3104 (Al-Mn-Mg, the can-body alloy) offers more strength than 3003 with good drawability and controlled earing. 5050 sits between 3003 and 5052 and is often used for thin keyboard plates. 6063 and 6101 are chosen when thermal or electrical conductivity matters together with heat-treatable strength. 7075 is used where a thin plate must be very stiff and strong, usually machined rather than formed. For these, ask for the certificate values for your gauge rather than relying on generic figures.

Values are standard limits for comparison, not design data for a particular coil. ASTM B209 gives equivalent limits in ksi for US orders.

Tempers explained

O, H1x, H2x and H3x tempers: which forms best?

O and H111

Fully annealed. Maximum elongation and lowest springback. Ideal for deep-drawn shields and brackets, but soft parts dent easily and very flat panels may lack stiffness.

H1x (strain hardened only)

Cold rolled to a set hardness. H14 is half hard. Higher yield for stiff plates, but elongation falls and bends crack sooner, especially along the rolling direction.

H2x (hardened, partially annealed)

Rolled harder, then back-annealed to the same strength level as H1x. H24 has noticeably better elongation and bendability than H14 at similar strength, and is often the smarter choice for bent back plates.

H3x (hardened and stabilised)

Used for 5xxx alloys, which slowly soften at room temperature if not stabilised. H32 is quarter hard with good bendability and is the workhorse temper for 5052 stamped parts.

T4 then T6

For 6xxx sheet: form in the soft, naturally aged T4 condition, then artificially age to T6. Gives formed parts with machined-plate strength.

Lüders lines warning

Annealed 5xxx sheet can show stretcher-strain marks (Lüders bands) on stretched areas. For visible surfaces use H111, H2x or a lightly temper-rolled condition, which suppresses the effect.

Process compatibility

How each forming process affects material choice

Blanking and stamping

A consistent yield strength keeps burr height and dimensions stable. Harder tempers give cleaner shear edges; very soft O temper can drag and form burrs.

Bending

Bend across the rolling direction where possible. Annealed and H2x tempers accept tighter inside radii than H1x. Check minimum bend radii against the EN 485-2 bend tables for your thickness.

Deep and stretch drawing

Choose O or H111 in 5052, 5754 or 3003. A higher n-value and r-value resist thinning and wrinkling. Lubrication and blank-holder force matter as much as the alloy.

Fine piercing

Keyboard plates may have hundreds of holes, each releasing a little stress. Low residual stress (tension-levelled or stretched material) is essential to keep the plate flat.

CNC and secondary machining

Soft 1xxx and O tempers clog cutters. For machined mid-plates use 5052-H32, 6061-T6 or 7075-T6, which chip cleanly.

Finishing and assembly

A clean, oil-free surface helps adhesive bonding, film lamination, painting and anodising. If the part will be anodised, specify anodising quality so grain and inclusions do not show.

Dimensional control

Controlling springback, flatness and residual stress

Most problems with internal 3C parts are not cracks but shape errors. Three material factors drive them:

  • Springback rises with yield strength. Published studies show 5052 springs back more than 1050 under the same bending conditions. Moving from 5052-H34 to H32, or from H14 to H24, reduces springback; so does designing a small embossed rib at the bend.
  • Coil set and crossbow. Strip unwound from a coil remembers its curvature. Tension levelling or stretch levelling at the slitting or cut-to-length stage removes it; ask for levelled material when parts are large and flat.
  • Residual stress. Cold rolling leaves stress gradients through the thickness. When piercing or trimming removes material, the balance changes and the part bows. Stretched, tension-levelled or stabilised material keeps this effect small.
  • Property scatter within the coil. A wide range of yield strength from head to tail means springback varies part to part. Specify a narrower yield window on the order for high-volume tools.

Thickness and flatness tolerances for cold-rolled sheet and strip follow EN 485-4 or ASTM B209 (ANSI H35.2) unless the drawing sets tighter limits. Many 3C buyers ask for tighter thickness tolerance than standard because thickness variation translates directly into springback variation; agree that figure at quotation stage.

Selection advice

Common mistakes when specifying formable sheet

  • Ordering the strongest temper "to be safe": higher strength raises springback and bend cracking. Specify the lowest temper that meets stiffness.
  • Ignoring bend orientation: bending parallel to the rolling direction cracks first. Nest blanks so critical bends run across the grain.
  • Using annealed 5xxx on visible stretched areas, where Lüders lines appear.
  • Leaving flatness to the default standard on large plates that will be heavily pierced.
  • Forgetting conductivity: back plates that spread LED heat work better in 1xxx or 3xxx alloys than in higher-alloyed sheet.
  • Changing supplier or alloy source without a trial run: the same designation from a different process route can behave differently in a tuned die.

FAQ

High-formability aluminium for electronics: FAQ

Which aluminium alloys are best for 3C internal components?

5052 in O or H32 is the most common all-round choice for stamped and drawn parts. 1050 and 3003 suit back plates where heat spreading and easy forming matter more than strength, 5754 handles complex forming, and 6061 or 7075 is used for thin parts that are mainly machined.

Is 5052-H32 good for stamping?

Yes. It combines good stiffness with enough elongation for moderate bends and shallow draws, and it is stabilised so its properties do not drift in storage. For deep draws or very tight bends, use 5052-O or H111.

Which aluminium is used for LED back plates?

Typically 1050, 3003 or 3104 in H14, H24 or similar tempers, because they form easily and conduct heat well. 5052 is chosen when the plate must be stiffer or thinner.

How can I reduce warping after stamping and piercing?

Order tension-levelled or stretch-levelled material with low residual stress, keep thickness and yield within a narrow window, choose the lowest temper that meets stiffness, and balance features such as ribs and holes symmetrically across the part.

What is the difference between H14 and H24?

Both have similar tensile strength. H14 is reached by cold rolling alone; H24 is rolled harder and then partially annealed back, which gives more elongation and better bendability. For bent parts, H24 is usually the better choice.

Can sheet and strip be cut to my drawing?

Yes. Metline supplies cut-to-size sheet, slit strip and coil to your dimensions, with protective film and export packing, to customers worldwide.
