Mesh specification guide

Aluminium Expanded Metal Mesh

Aluminium expanded metal is a one-piece mesh made by slitting and stretching aluminium sheet or coil into a pattern of diamond-shaped openings, with no material removed and no welds. It is lighter than the sheet it came from, rigid, slip-resistant in its raised form and does not rust. This guide explains how it is made and specified, which alloys to choose, how to calculate weight, and where it is used.

Overview

What is aluminium expanded metal?

Expanded metal starts as solid sheet or coil. An expanding press cuts a row of short, staggered slits and, in the same stroke, pulls the metal forward so the slits open into diamonds. The strands and the bonds (the nodes where strands meet) remain one continuous piece of metal. Because nothing is punched out, almost all of the feedstock ends up in the finished mesh.

Compared with perforated sheet, expanded metal gives a larger open area for the weight, more stiffness from the angled strands, and natural grip when left in the raised condition. Compared with woven or welded wire mesh, it has no joints to loosen or break, and the strands cannot unravel when the mesh is cut. These qualities explain why the same basic product turns up in facade screens, walkways, machine guards, grease filters, plaster lath and electronic shielding.

Aluminium versions add corrosion resistance without galvanising, low weight (about one third that of steel mesh with the same pattern), high thermal and electrical conductivity and an excellent base for anodising and powder coating. Metline supplies aluminium coil and sheet for expanded metal production, and expanded aluminium mesh in sheets, coils and cut panels to buyers worldwide.

What to state on an enquiry

  • Type: raised (standard) or flattened; micro mesh for filtration or shielding
  • Pattern: SWD × LWD, strand width and thickness, or a sample or drawing
  • Alloy and temper (e.g. 3003-H14, 5052-H32) and the base thickness
  • Sheet size or coil width and length, and the direction of the LWD relative to the sheet
  • Finish: mill, anodised, powder coated or PVDF; edge condition and any framing
How mesh is described

Expanded metal terminology: SWD, LWD, strand and bond

Expanded metal has its own vocabulary, standardised in North America by the Expanded Metal Manufacturers Association (EMMA, part of NAAMM) in its EMMA 557 standard. Understanding these terms avoids most ordering errors.

TermMeaningWhy it matters
SWD – short way of the designDistance from the centre of one bond to the centre of the next across the short diagonal of the diamondWith strand size, controls weight and strength
LWD – long way of the designSame measurement across the long diagonalUsually about 2 to 2.5 times the SWD for standard patterns
SWO / LWO – short and long way of openingThe clear opening inside the diamond, measured inside the strandsDetermines what passes through: air, light, particles, fingers
Strand width (SW)Width of metal fed forward per stroke, which becomes the strand widthWider strands give more weight, strength and less open area
Strand thickness (T)Thickness of the strand, usually close to the base sheet thicknessMain driver of load capacity
BondThe double-thickness node where two strands meetGives stiffness; in raised mesh the bonds tilt and create grip
Open areaPercentage of the surface that is openVentilation, light transmission, drainage and weight

Patterns are often written as SWO × LWO × T × SW in millimetres, for example 3.2 × 5.0 × 1.5 × 0.7, or in North America by SWD and base gauge, for example "3/4 in, 0.050 in". Because suppliers use different conventions, always state which dimensions you are quoting, and whether they are nominal design dimensions or measured openings. EMMA 557 allows a tolerance of about ±10 % on weights and design dimensions for standard mesh, tighter for load-bearing grating.

Product types

Types of aluminium expanded metal

Raised (standard) expanded metal

Supplied as it leaves the press, with strands and bonds set at an angle to the sheet plane. Strongest for its weight and naturally slip-resistant. Used for walkways, guards, fencing and partitions.

Flattened expanded metal

Raised mesh passed through a cold-rolling mill to flatten the strands into one plane. Smooth to touch, easier to paint and frame, slightly longer in the LWD direction and thinner overall. Used for facades, ceilings, shelving and decorative panels.

Expanded metal grating

Heavy-gauge mesh from thick plate, typically 3 – 6 mm, for platforms, catwalks and stair treads where loads must be carried between supports. Usually 5052 or 6061 alloy.

Micro expanded metal

Very fine mesh from foil and thin strip (about 0.05 – 0.3 mm) with openings below 1 – 2 mm. Used for filtration, battery current collectors, EMI shielding and lightning-strike protection layers in composite parts.

Architectural mesh

Large or decorative patterns, including hexagonal, scale and custom shapes, made for facades, sunscreens, ceilings and interior features. Often anodised or PVDF coated.

Expanded metal lath

Light diamond mesh used as a substrate for plaster, render and fireproofing; the open diamonds key the coating mechanically.

Material selection

Which aluminium alloy for expanded metal?

The alloy must stretch without tearing at the bonds, yet give the finished mesh enough strength and the right surface for its finish. Non-heat-treatable alloys in quarter-hard to half-hard tempers are the usual choice.

1050 / 1100 (O, H14)90 – 125 MPa (H14)Highest ductility, conductivity and corrosion resistance; low strengthMicro mesh, filters, lath, electrical shielding
3003-H14About 150 MPaThe general-purpose expanded metal alloy: expands cleanly, moderate strength, good corrosion resistanceGuards, screens, grease filters, decorative panels
5005-H34About 160 MPaSimilar strength to 3003 with a better, more uniform anodised appearanceAnodised facade and interior mesh
5052-H32About 230 MPaHigher strength and fatigue resistance; excellent in marine atmospheresWalkways, grating, marine and coastal installations

Typical values for the base sheet (Aluminum Association / ASM). Expanding cold-works the bonds, so the mesh behaves as a structure rather than a sheet; load tables from the mesh supplier, not sheet properties, should be used for design.

Base sheet and coil for expanded metal is usually ordered to ASTM B209 or EN 485 / EN 573-3. If the mesh will be anodised, specify an anodising-quality alloy such as 5005 and keep the whole project on material from one source, because the anodised tone varies with composition.

Dimensions

Patterns, sizes and how to calculate weight

Because the press feeds forward one strand width per stroke and each stroke produces half a diamond, the weight of expanded metal per square metre (measured flat on plan) can be estimated from the pattern alone:

Weight formula for expanded metal

  • Weight (kg/m²) ≈ 2 × SW × T × 2.70 ÷ SWD, with SW, T and SWD in millimetres
  • Example: SWD 12.7 mm, strand 1.5 × 1.5 mm gives 2 × 1.5 × 1.5 × 2.70 ÷ 12.7 ≈ 0.96 kg/m²
  • The same pattern in steel would weigh about 2.9 times as much (density 7.85 g/cm³)

Manufacturing

How aluminium expanded metal is made

  1. FeedstockCoil or sheet of the chosen alloy and temper is inspected for thickness, flatness and surface.
  2. Slit and expandThe expanding press feeds the sheet forward by one strand width per stroke while a stepped knife slits and stretches it, offsetting the knife each stroke to stagger the diamonds.
  3. Flatten (optional)Raised mesh is passed through a cold-rolling mill to produce flattened expanded metal with a smooth surface.
  4. Level and cutMesh is levelled and sheared to sheet or panel size, or recoiled.
  5. FinishPretreatment and anodising, powder coating or PVDF coating as specified.
  6. Inspect and packPattern dimensions, strand size, weight per area, flatness and finish checked; sheets interleaved and packed on pallets or in cases.

Pattern choice affects strength in each direction. Expanded metal is stiffer along the LWD than across it, so in walkways and spans the LWD normally runs between the supports. State the LWD direction on the drawing for any load-bearing or framed panel.

Expanding also changes the dimensions of the sheet. A given width of feedstock produces mesh of roughly the same width in the LWD direction, but the length grows several times in the SWD direction, depending on the ratio of SWD to strand width. This is why expanded mesh covers a much larger area than the sheet it was made from, and why ordering by finished area rather than by feedstock weight is normal practice. Flattening then adds a few per cent to the LWD length and reduces the overall thickness.

Fabrication

Fabrication and finishing of expanded aluminium

Cutting

Shearing, laser, waterjet and plasma all work. Cuts through the bonds leave a stronger, neater edge than cuts through the strands.

Framing and edging

Edges are often captured in aluminium angle, U-channel or tube frames, or folded edging strips, which stiffen the panel and remove sharp ends.

Bending and forming

Flattened mesh in soft tempers can be folded and curved; raised mesh can be rolled into curves for facades and guards.

Welding

TIG or MIG with 4043 or 5356 filler joins mesh to frames; low heat input avoids burning through thin strands.

Anodising

Gives a hard, durable metallic finish. Use 5005 or anodising-quality 3003 for consistent colour on visible facades.

Powder coating and PVDF

Powder is economical in any RAL colour; PVDF gives the best colour retention for coastal and high-UV facades. Mesh is best coated after cutting to protect edges.

Aluminium is not always the right choice. For heavy vehicular loads or high-security barriers, steel expanded metal or bar grating carries more load at lower cost; for very fine filtration, woven wire mesh gives more precise openings. Aluminium wins where weight, corrosion resistance and appearance matter.

Selection advice

Selection tips and common mistakes

  • Confirm whether quoted sizes are design dimensions (SWD/LWD) or clear openings (SWO/LWO); mixing them changes the product completely
  • Run the LWD between supports for walkway and spanning panels, and check deflection with the supplier’s load tables
  • Choose raised mesh for grip and strength, flattened mesh for smooth surfaces, painting and framing
  • Use 5052 for coastal or marine sites and 5005 where anodised appearance must be consistent
  • Isolate aluminium mesh from steel frames and fixings with stainless steel fasteners or isolating washers to avoid galvanic corrosion
  • For facades, check fire and wind-load requirements of the local building code for the whole assembly, not just the mesh

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) expanded metal: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between raised and flattened expanded metal?

Raised expanded metal is supplied as expanded, with the strands angled out of plane; it is stronger for its weight and slip-resistant. Flattened expanded metal is cold-rolled after expanding so the surface is smooth and flat, which suits painting, framing and decorative use.

Which aluminium alloy is best for expanded metal?

3003-H14 is the general-purpose choice. Use 5052-H32 for higher strength and marine environments, 5005 for consistent anodised facades, 1100 or 1050 for micro mesh and filters, and 6061-T6 for heavy grating.

How do I calculate the weight of aluminium expanded metal?

Multiply 2 × strand width × strand thickness × 2.70 and divide by the SWD, all in millimetres, to get kg/m². For example, a 12.7 mm SWD pattern with 1.5 × 1.5 mm strands weighs about 0.96 kg/m².

Is expanded metal stronger than perforated sheet?

For the same weight and open area, expanded metal is usually stiffer, because the angled strands and bonds act like a truss and no material is removed. Perforated sheet gives a flatter, more uniform surface and more precise hole sizes.

Can expanded aluminum mesh be anodised or powder coated?

Yes. Both finishes are common. Anodising suits 5005 and anodising-quality 3003; powder coating and PVDF work on all common alloys and are best applied after cutting so the edges are protected.

Does expanded metal waste material?

Very little. The sheet is slit and stretched, not punched, so nearly all the feedstock becomes mesh, and the finished mesh covers a larger area than the original sheet.
