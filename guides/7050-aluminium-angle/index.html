7050 Aluminium Angle: Grades, Tempers, Sizes and Selection

7050 aluminium angle is an extruded L-section in the high-strength Al-Zn-Cu-Mg-Zr alloy developed for thick, stress-critical aircraft parts. Supplied in over-aged, stretched tempers such as T73511, T74511 and T76511, it combines strength close to 7075-T6 with much better resistance to stress-corrosion cracking and exfoliation. This guide covers its chemistry, properties, standards, sizes and how it compares with 7075 angle.

Overview

What is 7050 aluminium angle?

An angle is the simplest structural extrusion: two legs at 90°, either equal or unequal, with a defined wall thickness and root radius. In aircraft structures, 7050 angle is used for stringers, frame chords, clips, longeron details and fittings that are machined from the extrusion.

7050 was introduced in the 1970s to solve problems with 7075. In thick sections, 7075-T6 is vulnerable to stress-corrosion cracking in the short-transverse direction, and over-ageing 7075 to T73 to cure this costs a lot of strength. 7050 raises copper and zinc, keeps iron and silicon very low and uses zirconium instead of chromium for grain control. Zirconium makes the alloy far less quench sensitive, so a thick extrusion develops nearly the same strength at its centre as at its surface.

The practical outcome is an angle that can be ordered in over-aged tempers and still deliver strength close to 7075-T6, while offering much better stress-corrosion, exfoliation and fatigue performance. That is why 7050 is the default 7xxx extrusion alloy on many modern airframes.

In short

  • Strength close to 7075-T6, with stress-corrosion resistance close to 7075-T73
  • Low quench sensitivity, so heavy angles keep their properties through the thickness
  • Supplied stretched (T7x511) for straightness and low residual stress during machining
  • Not weldable by fusion processes; joined by fasteners or adhesive bonding

Mechanical and physical properties

Over-aged 7050 tempers form a ladder: T73 has the best stress-corrosion resistance and the lowest strength, T76 has the highest strength and moderate SCC resistance, and T74 sits between. The "511" suffix means the extrusion was stretched to relieve stress and may be straightened afterwards.

Commercial and aerospace angle can look identical but are not interchangeable. Aerospace material is produced, inspected and certified to the AMS sheet, often with ultrasonic inspection, conductivity checks and lot traceability back to the cast. If the drawing calls up AMS 4341, certification to ASTM B221 alone is not acceptable.

7050 angle sizes and weights

7050 angle is most often specified in inch sizes, reflecting its aerospace roots. Equal angles from about 1/2 × 1/2 in to 8 × 8 in and unequal angles up to about 8 × 6 in are common, with walls from 1/16 in to 1 in. Metric angles and special sections with radiused or sharp corners can be extruded to drawing. Standard lengths are typically 3 – 6 m (about 10 – 20 ft), or cut to length.

Choose 7050 for heavy-wall angle, primary structure and any part where stress corrosion or fatigue governs. Choose 7075-T6511 for thinner, lightly loaded parts where cost and availability matter and SCC is not a risk, and 7075-T73511 when an existing design is qualified on 7075 but needs better SCC resistance. Working with 7050

Machining, joining, finishing and applications

Machining

Machines well with carbide tools and high spindle speeds. Stretched tempers limit distortion; still, balance stock removal on both legs to keep the angle straight.

Joining

Not suitable for arc welding: fusion causes hot cracking and severe strength loss. Use rivets, bolts or Hi-Lok type fasteners, or structural adhesive bonding.

Forming

Limited cold formability in T7x tempers. Joggles and bends are usually machined in or formed before final ageing under qualified procedures.

Surface treatment

Chromic, sulphuric or boric-sulphuric acid anodising and conversion coatings are standard before priming, especially for aerospace parts.

Corrosion

Over-aged tempers resist exfoliation and SCC well, but bare 7050 still needs coating and sealant in exposed or wet locations.

Heat exposure

Avoid processes that reheat the part above about 120 – 150 °C for long periods, such as some paint cures, unless approved.

Where 7050 angle is used. Typical applications follow directly from these properties:

  • Airframes: stringers, frame and spar chords, clips, longeron details and floor-beam attachments
  • Helicopters and business jets: machined fittings and stiffeners from heavy angle
  • Defence: light armoured vehicle and missile structures where strength and SCC resistance are needed
  • Motorsport and high-end machinery: lightweight frames that must survive vibration and fatigue
  • Tooling and test rigs: high-stiffness frames where steel would be too heavy
Pitfalls

Common mistakes when buying 7050 angle

  • Ordering by alloy only: "7050 angle" without a temper and specification can be supplied in any over-aged temper. T73511 and T76511 differ by roughly 50 MPa in strength and noticeably in SCC resistance, so always state both.
  • Accepting commercial certification for aerospace parts: a certificate to ASTM B221 does not satisfy an AMS call-out. Check that the certificate names the exact AMS sheet and revision.
  • Ignoring corner geometry: sharp-corner and radiused-corner angles of the same nominal size are different extrusions. Specify inside and outside radii, or attach the section drawing.
  • Substituting 7075-T6511 to save cost: the strength looks similar on paper, but stress-corrosion behaviour in thick legs is not. Substitution needs engineering approval.
  • Re-heating after delivery: long paint cures or hot forming can over-age the alloy further and change both strength and conductivity. Agree any thermal exposure in advance.
  • Forgetting straightness and twist: long, thin-legged angles can twist. State straightness and twist limits if the part will be machined in long lengths.

FAQ

7050 aluminium (aluminum) angle: FAQs

Is 7050 stronger than 7075?

Not in peak temper: 7075-T6 is slightly stronger than any 7050 over-aged temper. But 7050-T76511 comes close and 7050 keeps its strength far better in thick sections, while offering much better stress-corrosion and fatigue performance.

What does T73511 mean?

Solution heat-treated, stress-relieved by controlled stretching (with minor straightening allowed), and over-aged to the T73 condition for maximum stress-corrosion resistance. It is the temper covered by AMS 4341.

Can 7050 angle be welded?

No, not by conventional MIG or TIG welding. It hot-cracks and loses most of its strength in the weld zone. Assemblies use mechanical fasteners or adhesive bonding.

How do I calculate the weight of 7050 angle?

Multiply the cross-section area t × (A + B − t) by the length and by the density of 2.83 g/cm³. A 2 × 2 × 1/4 in angle weighs about 1.71 kg per metre (1.15 lb per foot), before allowing for corner radii.

Can 7050 angle be anodised?

Yes. Chromic, sulphuric and boric-sulphuric acid anodising are routine for 7050 aerospace parts, usually followed by primer. Expect a slightly darker, less bright film than on 6xxx alloys.

Do you cut angle to length and supply certificates?

Yes. Metline cuts angle to length on request and ships with an EN 10204 3.1 mill certificate tied to the production lot, to customers worldwide.
