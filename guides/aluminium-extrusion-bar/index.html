Extruded bar and rod guide

Extruded Aluminium Bar & Rod: Shapes, Alloys and Specifications

Extruded aluminium bar is solid stock made by pushing a heated billet through a die, which gives round, square, hexagonal, flat and shaped sections in long lengths at low tooling cost. This guide explains which alloys and tempers to choose, the standards that govern extruded bar, how it compares with drawn and rolled bar, and how to specify sizes, tolerances and certificates when you buy. Overview

What is extruded aluminium bar?

An extruded bar is a solid aluminium section produced in one pass through an extrusion die. A cylindrical billet is heated to roughly 400 – 500 °C, loaded into a press container and pushed by a ram through a steel die whose opening has the shape of the finished bar. The metal emerges as a continuous length with that cross-section, is cooled, stretched straight, cut and, for heat-treatable alloys, aged to its final temper.

Because the shape comes from the die opening rather than from rolls or machining, extrusion can make almost any solid cross-section: plain rounds, squares and hexagons, flat bars with sharp or rounded corners, and shaped bars such as angles, tees, channels, half-rounds and triangles. Die cost for a simple solid section is low, so even non-standard sizes are economical once quantities reach a few hundred kilograms.

The term "rod" is normally used for round solid sections and "bar" for square, rectangular, hexagonal or octagonal ones, although buyers and catalogues use the words loosely. ASTM B221 covers extruded bar, rod, wire, profiles and tube under one specification, while the European EN 755 series splits the same products into separate parts for round bar, square bar, rectangular bar, hexagonal bar, tube and profiles.

Extruded bar is the starting stock for a huge range of parts: turned shafts and fittings, CNC-machined brackets, fasteners, busbars, frames, rails and trim. Choosing well means getting four things right: the alloy, the temper, the production route (extruded, drawn or rolled) and the tolerance standard. The sections below cover each one.

When extruded bar is the right choice

  • You need a solid section in long lengths (typically 3 – 6 m, longer on request) at the lowest cost per kilogram
  • The size is too large for drawn bar (above about 75 – 100 mm diameter) or the shape is not a plain round, square or hex
  • Normal commercial tolerances are acceptable, or the bar will be machined all over anyway
  • You want a near-net shape that removes machining, such as an angle, tee or custom profile instead of cutting from plate
Cross-sections

Standard and shaped extruded bar sections

Extruded bar falls into two groups: standard geometric bars that are used as machining stock, and shaped (structural or decorative) bars that are used close to their extruded form.

Round bar / rod

The most common form. Small diameters are often drawn after extrusion for tighter tolerance; large diameters (above about 100 mm) are usually supplied as extruded. Used for shafts, pins, turned fittings, hydraulic parts and fastener blanks.

Square bar

Sharp-cornered or radiused squares, typically 6 – 150 mm across flats. Used for machined blocks, frames, spacers and fixtures.

Hexagonal bar

Hex bar reduces machining for nuts, couplings, fittings and fastener heads because the flats are already formed. Sizes are specified across flats (A/F).

Flat / rectangular bar

Rectangular sections from thin strips to heavy bars, with square or rounded edges. Used for busbars, wear strips, framing, brackets and base plates. See our flat bar range.

Half-round and oval

Semi-circular and half-oval bars used for edging, handrails, trims and decorative details.

Triangle and octagon

Less common geometric bars for special supports, tooling and decorative work, produced from simple solid dies.

Angle (L) and tee (T)

Equal and unequal angles and tee bars for frames, edge protection, supports and stiffeners.

Channel (U, C, J, F)

Open channel sections for tracks, glazing, edging and pipe supports. Channel guide.

Z, H and Y sections

Special connection, framing and support sections, often produced to the customer's drawing.

Beyond plain geometry, extrusion also makes functional bars: T-slot framing bars for machine guarding and automation, conductive bars in 1350, 6101 or 6060 for electrical busbars, and precision ground rods for bushings and guides. Each is described in the related pages at the end of this guide.

Alloy selection

Which alloys are used for extruded bar?

Almost every wrought alloy can be extruded, but they differ greatly in extrusion speed, achievable shape complexity and cost. Soft 6xxx alloys flow easily and can be pushed fast through complex dies; hard 2xxx and 7xxx alloys need slower speeds, higher pressures and simpler shapes, which is why they cost more per kilogram.

GroupTypical alloysCharacterTypical uses
General purpose6060, 6063, 3003, 1100Very easy to extrude, excellent surface and anodising, moderate strengthArchitectural bars, trim, handrails, electrical parts, low-load components
Structural / engineering6061, 6082, 6005A, 6351Good strength after T6 ageing, weldable, machines wellMachined parts, frames, transport, marine fittings, structural members
Marine / non-heat-treatable5083, 5052, 5754, 5086Excellent seawater resistance, weldable without heat treatment; harder to extrudeBoat fittings, offshore parts, welded marine structures
High strength7075, 7050, 7049, 2024, 2014, 2219Highest strength, good fatigue; slower to extrude, limited weldabilityAerospace fittings, defence parts, high-load machine components, sports equipment
Free-machining2011, 6262, 6026Lead-free or low-lead chip-breaking additions for automatic lathesHigh-volume turned parts, screw-machine products
Conductor1350, 6101, 6060High electrical conductivityBusbars, connectors, earthing bars

For most engineering buyers the decision comes down to three candidates. 6061 is the default in the Americas and Asia; 6082 fills the same role in Europe and is slightly stronger in thick sections; and 7075 is chosen when strength matters more than weldability or cost. 6063 is not a machining alloy, but it is the right choice for bars that will be anodised for appearance or bent.

ASTM B221 specified minimums for common grades: 6061-T6 extrusions 260 MPa (38 ksi) tensile and 240 MPa (35 ksi) yield; 6063-T6 205 MPa (30 ksi) tensile and 170 MPa (25 ksi) yield; 6063-T5 up to 12.5 mm 150 MPa (22 ksi) tensile and 110 MPa (16 ksi) yield. Two points are often missed. First, the extrusion minimums in ASTM B221 are lower than the typical values, so a bar can pass the specification while testing well below the handbook figure; if your design relies on higher strength, write it into the order. Second, large-diameter bars cool more slowly at the centre during quenching, so strength falls as diameter rises, especially in 7xxx and 2xxx alloys. For critical parts above about 150 mm diameter, ask for test results taken from the bar centre or consider a forged bar instead.

Tempers

Tempers for extruded bar explained

Extruded bar is supplied in a smaller set of tempers than sheet and plate. The suffix tells you how the bar was cooled, treated and stress-relieved, which affects strength, straightness and how it behaves when machined. Our temper designation guide explains the full system.

TemperWhat it meansWhen to order it

For machined parts, the stretched tempers (T6511, T3511, T73511) are worth the small extra cost. Stretching by 1 – 3 % after quenching removes most of the residual stress that otherwise makes long parts bow when one side is machined away.

Production route

Extruded bar vs cold-drawn and rolled bar

The same alloy and temper can reach you by three routes, and the route affects tolerance, surface, straightness and price. ASTM B221 covers extruded products; ASTM B211 covers cold-finished (drawn) and rolled bar, rod and wire; in Europe, drawn bar falls under EN 754 and extruded bar under EN 755.

The practical rule: if the bar goes into an automatic lathe or collet chuck, or needs a finished surface without machining, buy cold-drawn bar. If it will be machined all over, or the size or shape is beyond the drawing range, extruded bar does the job at lower cost. For very large solid sections where through-thickness properties matter, compare extruded bar with rolled or forged bar.

EN 755-3 covers round bars from 8 mm to 320 mm diameter and sets tolerances on diameter, ovality and straightness; EN 755-5 covers rectangular bars from 2 mm to 240 mm thick and 10 mm to 600 mm wide, including corner radii, squareness and twist. Tolerances widen as size increases and are tighter for "easy" alloys such as 6060 and 6063 than for hard alloys such as 7075, so the alloy you pick changes the tolerance you get.

Quality

Tolerances, straightness and inspection

Commercial extruded bar is supplied to the standard tolerance tables of the order specification. If your drawing needs more, state it explicitly, or allow a machining margin and specify cold-drawn or precision-ground bar.

  • Cross-section: diameter, across-flats or thickness/width to ANSI H35.2 or EN 755-3/-4/-5/-6; tighter "precision" tolerances are possible on small 6xxx bars
  • Straightness: controlled by stretching; stretched tempers (T6511) are straighter and stay straighter when machined
  • Twist and squareness: relevant for square, hex and flat bar; specified per metre of length
  • Ends: saw cut square; cut lengths to drawing tolerance on request
  • Testing: chemistry and tensile tests per lot; hardness and conductivity checks; ultrasonic inspection (for example to ASTM B594 or AMS-STD-2154) for aerospace and large-diameter bar
  • Documents: EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate; third-party inspection on request

A common source of rejection is ordering bar to "drawing tolerance" when the drawing shows finished-part tolerances. Extruded bar is a raw material; specify the stock size and the standard, and leave the finished tolerance to machining.

Fabrication

Machining, welding and finishing extruded bar

Machining

6061-T6, 6082-T6, 7075-T6 and 2024-T3 all machine well with sharp carbide tools, high speeds and flood coolant. Soft tempers (O, F, 6063-T5) are gummy and form built-up edge; for high-volume turning choose 2011, 6262 or 6026.

Welding

6xxx bars weld readily by MIG and TIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler, although the heat-affected zone loses part of its T6 strength. 5083 welds without heat treatment. 7075 and 2024 are generally not fusion welded; use mechanical fastening.

Bending

Bend in T4 or O and age afterwards for tight radii. T6 bars need generous radii and are best bent across the extrusion direction with care.

Anodising

6060 and 6063 give the most uniform decorative anodising; 6061 and 6082 anodise well for protection. 7075 and 2024 accept hard anodising for wear surfaces.

Coating and polishing

Powder coating, wet painting, brushing and mirror polishing are all applied to extruded bar; specify the finish standard on the order.

Cutting and processing

Bars can be supplied cut to length, chamfered, drilled or machined to drawing, which reduces handling and scrap at your plant.

How to specify

How to specify and order extruded bar

  1. Define the dutyLoad, environment (indoor, marine, chemical), operating temperature, and whether the part is welded, machined, bent or anodised.
  2. Choose alloy and temperUse the alloy table above: 6063 for appearance, 6061/6082 for general engineering, 5083 for seawater, 7075/2024 for high strength. Pick a stretched temper for machined parts.
  3. Choose the routeExtruded for general stock and large or shaped sections; cold-drawn for screw-machine work and tight tolerance.
  4. State size and standardShape, diameter or across-flats, length, tolerance standard (ASTM B221 / ANSI H35.2 or EN 755) and any special straightness or finish.
  5. Specify documents and packingEN 10204 3.1 certificate, ultrasonic test if needed, export packing, and delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Ordering 6063 for a heavily loaded machined part because it is cheap: it is roughly 20 – 30 % weaker than 6061-T6
  • Assuming 7075 can be welded like 6061
  • Specifying finished-part tolerances on raw bar instead of a machining allowance
  • Forgetting that thick 7xxx and 2xxx bars have lower minimum properties at the centre

FAQ

Extruded aluminium (aluminum) bar: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between extruded and drawn aluminium rod?

Extruded rod is formed hot through a die and supplied with commercial tolerances and a mill finish. Drawn (cold-finished) rod is extruded first and then pulled cold through a finishing die, which gives tighter diameter tolerances, a brighter surface and sharper corners. Drawn rod costs more and is limited to smaller sizes.

Which aluminium alloy is best for extruded bar that will be machined?

6061-T6511 or 6082-T6 for general engineering parts, 7075-T6511 when high strength is needed, and 2011 or 6262 for high-volume automatic turning. Avoid 6063 and annealed tempers for precision machining, as they are soft and gummy.

How do I calculate the weight of an extruded aluminium bar?

Multiply cross-sectional area by length and density. For a round bar, kg per metre = 0.7854 × d² × 0.0027 with d in mm. A 50 mm round bar weighs about 5.30 kg/m and a 100 mm bar about 21.2 kg/m in 6061.

What is the largest diameter of extruded aluminium bar?

Standard extruded round bar is widely available up to about 300 mm diameter, and large presses can produce bars around 500 mm. Very large diameters are slower to source and have lower centre properties, so forged bar is sometimes preferred for critical parts.

Is extruded aluminium bar weaker than rolled or forged bar?

In the same alloy and temper, extruded bar meets the same type of minimum properties, with some directionality along the extrusion axis. Forging gives better through-thickness properties and grain flow, which matters for highly stressed aerospace or rotating parts.

What does T6511 mean on extruded bar?

T6511 is the T6 condition plus controlled stretching after quenching to relieve residual stress, with minor straightening allowed after stretching. It keeps bars straight and stable during machining.
