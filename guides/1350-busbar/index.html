1350 and 1370 Electrical Aluminium Busbar

1350 is the standard electrical-conductor (EC) aluminium for busbar, with at least 99.50 % aluminium and a conductivity of about 61–62 % IACS; 1370 is a purer variant at 99.70 %. Rectangular EC busbar carries the same current as copper with about 1.64 times the cross-section but only half the weight. This guide covers grades, tempers, standards, sizes with resistance per metre, and the jointing practice that keeps aluminium bus reliable.

Overview

Why 1350 and 1370 are used for busbar

Busbar is the backbone of switchgear, panel boards, bus ducts, transformers and battery systems. For a given resistance, the best conductor is the one that combines high conductivity with low density and cost, and on that measure EC aluminium is hard to beat.

Copper has a conductivity of 100 % IACS and a density of 8.89 g/cm³. 1350 aluminium reaches about 61 % IACS at a density of 2.705 g/cm³. To match the resistance of a copper bar, an aluminium bar needs about 1.64 times the cross-section, yet it weighs only about 50 % as much. Larger bars also have more surface area to shed heat, so in practice aluminium bus is often rated close to its copper counterpart at the same temperature rise.

1370 offers about one percentage point more conductivity because its impurity limits are tighter. It is used where every bit of loss reduction counts, for example in high-current DC bus for electrolysis and in large rectifier installations, while 1350 is the general-purpose choice. Where higher strength or creep resistance is needed, 6101 in T6, T61, T63 or T65 temper trades a few percent of conductivity for much higher strength.

Quick selection

  • 1350: standard EC busbar for switchgear, panels, bus ducts and transformers
  • 1370: maximum conductivity for high-current DC and rectifier bus
  • 6101-T6 / T61 / T63: when the bar must carry mechanical load or resist creep at bolted joints
  • Copper-clad aluminium: when copper contact surfaces are required on an aluminium core

Installation

Bolted joints: how to make aluminium bus reliable

Most problems with aluminium busbar start at joints. Aluminium forms a hard oxide film within seconds, creeps slightly under sustained pressure and expands more than steel bolts. Good practice deals with each of these:

  1. Prepare the surfaceWire-brush or abrade contact faces and apply oxide-inhibiting compound immediately; or use plated (tin or silver) contact areas.
  2. Use enough contact area and pressureSize the overlap to the current, and use several bolts rather than one large bolt to spread pressure.
  3. Control creep and expansionUse Belleville (conical spring) washers and thick flat washers with steel bolts so pressure is maintained through heating cycles.
  4. Avoid galvanic attackWhere aluminium meets copper, use bimetallic plates, tin-plated contacts or copper-clad aluminium, and keep the joint dry.
  5. Torque and checkTighten to the specified torque and include joints in thermographic inspection.
Fabrication

Bending, drilling, plating and insulation

Bending

O and H111 bar bends flatwise around a radius of about 1 × thickness; edgewise bends need larger radii. Harder tempers need more generous radii.

Punching and drilling

EC aluminium is soft; use sharp tools and lubricant to avoid burrs that raise local field stress.

Welding

TIG or MIG with 1100 / 1188-type filler keeps joint conductivity high; 4043 is easier to weld but lowers conductivity in the weld zone.

Plating

Tin is standard for contact faces; silver for high-current or high-temperature joints; nickel where wear or fretting is a concern.

Insulation

Epoxy powder coating, heat-shrink sleeving or PVC/silicone covers for compact and medium-voltage bus.

Cladding

Copper-clad aluminium bar gives copper contact surfaces on a light aluminium core.

Applications

Where EC aluminium busbar is used

  • Low- and medium-voltage switchgear, panel boards and motor control centres
  • Sandwich and air-insulated bus duct (busway) systems
  • Transformer leads, secondary connections and winding strip
  • Electrolysis, smelting and electroplating DC bus (often 1370)
  • Solar inverters, wind turbines and battery energy storage
  • Data centre power distribution and UPS systems

FAQ

1350 / 1370 aluminium (aluminum) busbar: frequently asked questions

What is the conductivity of 1350 busbar?

About 61–62 % IACS at 20 °C, equal to a resistivity of roughly 0.028 Ω·mm²/m. 1370 is slightly higher at about 62 %.

How much bigger must an aluminium busbar be than copper?

About 1.64 times the cross-section for the same DC resistance. It still weighs about half as much as the copper bar.

Which temper should I order for bending?

O or H111 for tight flatwise and edgewise bends. H12 and harder tempers are stiffer and need larger bend radii.

Is 1350 or 6101 better for busbar?

1350 conducts better; 6101 in T6, T61 or T63 is much stronger and resists creep at bolted joints. Choose 6101 when the bar carries mechanical load.

Can aluminium busbar be bolted to copper?

Yes, with tin-plated contacts, bimetallic transition plates or copper-clad aluminium, oxide-inhibiting compound and spring washers, and with the joint kept dry.

Do you cut and plate busbar to drawing?

Yes. We supply 1350 and 1370 busbar cut, drilled, bent, plated or insulated to drawing, with mill certificates, shipped worldwide.
