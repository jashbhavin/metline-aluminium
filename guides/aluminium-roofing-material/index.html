Aluminium Roofing Material: Alloys, Forms and Coatings

Aluminium roofing material is coated or mill-finish aluminium alloy sheet, coil and foil that roof-sheet manufacturers, panel makers and contractors form into roof coverings, insulated panels and radiant barriers. The usual alloys are 3003, 3004, 3105 and 5052 in half-hard tempers, 0.3 to 1.5 mm thick, finished with polyester (PE) or PVDF paint. This guide explains which alloy, temper, thickness and coating to specify for each roof type and climate, and what to check before you order.

Overview

What counts as aluminium roofing material?

"Aluminium roofing material" covers every aluminium product that ends up in a roof build-up, from the visible weather skin to the reflective layer hidden under it. For a buyer the important point is that these are semi-finished materials: coil and sheet that a roll-forming line turns into corrugated, trapezoidal or standing-seam profiles, face sheets that a panel plant bonds to an insulating core, and foil that is laminated into insulation.

Aluminium is chosen for roofs for three practical reasons. It weighs about one third as much as steel for the same thickness (density 2.70 g/cm³ against roughly 7.85 g/cm³), which lowers dead load on purlins and makes long sheets easy to handle. It forms a thin, self-healing oxide film, so a cut edge or scratch does not rust and stain the roof the way bare steel does. And it is fully recyclable at end of life without losing its properties, which matters on projects assessed under green-building schemes.

The trade-offs are equally real. Aluminium has a lower elastic modulus than steel (about 69 GPa against 200 GPa), so profiles need deeper ribs or slightly greater thickness to span the same purlin spacing. It expands about twice as much as steel with temperature, so fixings must allow the sheet to move. And it must be kept away from copper, wet treated timber and certain other metals that cause galvanic or chemical attack. Each of these is manageable when the material is specified correctly, which is what the rest of this guide covers.

The short answer for most roofs

  • Profiled roof sheet: 3105 or 3004 in H24 to H26, 0.5 – 0.9 mm, PVDF or PE coated
  • Corrugated sheet for sheds and agricultural buildings: 3003 or 1050/1100 in H14 to H16, 0.4 – 0.7 mm
  • Coastal and high-humidity sites: 5052 or 3004 base metal with a PVDF coating
  • Standing seam: 3004 or 3105 in a softer H24 temper so the seam can be folded without cracking
  • Radiant barrier and vapour layer: 8011 or 1235 foil laminated to kraft, film or woven fabric

Alloy selection

Which aluminium alloy and temper for roofing?

Roofing alloys come almost entirely from the 1xxx, 3xxx and 5xxx series. They are non-heat-treatable, so strength comes from cold rolling (the H temper), and they keep good corrosion resistance because they contain no copper. Higher manganese and magnesium content gives more strength, which lets a thinner sheet span further.

Typical values from Aluminum Association and ASM data; they are not design minimums. Specified minimums depend on temper and thickness and are reported on the mill test certificate.

H1x tempers

Strain-hardened only. H14 is half-hard, H16 three-quarter hard, H18 full hard. Higher numbers are stronger but tolerate tighter bends less well.

H2x tempers

Strain-hardened then partially annealed. At the same strength level, H24 or H26 bends better than H14 or H16, which is why coil for standing seam and folded flashings is often ordered in H2x.

H3x tempers

Strain-hardened and stabilised, used for magnesium alloys such as 5052-H32 and 3004-H34 so that properties do not drift with time at room temperature.

Paint-line effect

The curing oven of a coil-coating line acts like a light anneal. Coil painted from H14 base can come out near H24 properties, so specify the final temper you need after coating.

Bending check

For roll-formed ribs and seams, ask for the minimum inside bend radius of the chosen temper and thickness. Cracking at the fold is the most common forming complaint on hard tempers.

Matching parts

Order flashings and trims from the same alloy family and coating as the main sheet so colour and weathering match over time.

Surface and coating systems

Mill finish, PE, PVDF and other roof coatings

The coating decides how long a roof keeps its colour and gloss. Aluminium itself does not need paint to resist the weather, but a mill-finish roof dulls and darkens unevenly over time, so most visible roofs are coil-coated. Coil coating is applied to flat strip on a continuous line before forming, which gives a uniform, well-cured film that can be roll-formed without cracking.

Aluminium is often specified for coastal roofs where coated steel would show red rust at cut edges and fixings. The base metal matters less than the detailing, though: a well-drained roof in 3105 with a PVDF coating will outlast a poorly detailed 5052 roof where salt and dirt collect in laps and gutters.

Design and installation

Design rules that protect an aluminium roof

  • Allow for thermal movement. Aluminium expands about 23 µm per metre per °C. A 10 m sheet that swings 60 °C between a winter night and a summer afternoon changes length by roughly 14 mm. Use sliding clips on standing seam, oversized holes with sealing washers on through-fixed sheet, and limit single sheet lengths where fixings are rigid.
  • Separate dissimilar metals. Do not let rainwater run from copper, brass or lead onto aluminium, and isolate steel purlins with a barrier tape or coated purlin. Use aluminium or stainless steel fasteners with EPDM washers, never plain carbon steel or copper.
  • Keep away from treated timber and fresh cement. Copper-based timber preservatives and wet concrete, mortar or lime attack aluminium. Use a membrane or paint barrier between them.
  • Respect minimum pitch. Each profile has a minimum roof slope set by its manufacturer; low-rib corrugated sheet needs more slope than a high standing seam to drain without leaking at laps.
  • Control condensation. Metal roofs over heated or humid spaces need a vapour control layer, insulation or ventilation. Aluminium foil laminates do both radiant and vapour duty.
  • Check structural capacity. Self-supporting profiled sheet in Europe is designed to EN 1999-1-4 (Eurocode 9 for cold-formed aluminium sheeting) and supplied to EN 508-2. Load-span tables come from the profile designer, not from the raw material.
Below the roof

Aluminium foil for radiant barriers and roof insulation

Bright aluminium foil has very low thermal emittance, typically around 0.03 to 0.05, which means it reflects most of the radiant heat that strikes it. Laid with an air gap below a hot roof sheet, it cuts the radiant heat that reaches the ceiling or the insulation below. That is the principle behind radiant barrier sheets and reflective insulation laminates.

Standard radiant barrier

Foil, usually 8011 or 1235 alloy, laminated to kraft paper, PE film or woven polypropylene for tear strength. Needs an air space facing the foil to work.

Perforated foil

Micro-perforations allow water vapour to pass so moisture does not get trapped in the roof. Used where the layer must breathe.

Foil-faced bubble and foam

Foil bonded to air-bubble film or closed-cell foam adds a small amount of resistive insulation to the radiant effect.

Fire-retardant laminates

Composite laminates with fire-retardant adhesives or scrims, tested as a whole product to local reaction-to-fire rules.

Metline supplies the foil and laminating stock; the laminated insulation product and its fire classification belong to the converter. Alloy-specific details are in our 8011 roofing foil and 1100 roofing foil pages.

One point causes frequent confusion: "aluzinc" or "Galvalume" roofing is steel sheet with a 55 % aluminium-zinc metallic coating (ASTM A792 or EN 10346), not aluminium sheet. It is cheaper and stiffer, but the steel core can still corrode where the coating is damaged. Solid aluminium is the better choice where long life near the sea, low weight or freedom from rust staining matters more than first cost.

Ordering

How to specify and order aluminium roofing material

  1. Define the productForm (coil, sheet, foil), alloy and temper after coating, thickness and tolerance, width or sheet size.
  2. Define the finishCoating type and system (PE, SMP, PVDF 2- or 3-coat), colour reference, gloss, backer coat, protective film and embossing.
  3. State the standardsBase metal to EN 485 or ASTM B209, coil-coated strip to EN 1396, coating performance to AAMA 2605 or the project specification.
  4. Give coil detailsInner diameter, maximum coil weight and outer diameter your uncoiler accepts, and winding direction (paint side in or out).
  5. Agree documents and termsMill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1, coating test report, packing in export cases or eye-to-sky coils, and EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP delivery.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) roofing material: frequently asked questions

Which aluminium alloy is best for roofing?

3105 is the most common alloy for painted roof and wall sheet because it balances strength, formability and cost. 3004 is chosen for long-span and standing-seam profiles that need more stiffness, and 5052 for coastal or industrial sites where the best corrosion resistance is wanted. 3003 and 1100 suit corrugated sheet and flashings.

What thickness of aluminium is used for roofing?

Most profiled aluminium roof sheet is 0.5 – 0.9 mm thick. Light corrugated sheet can be 0.4 mm, while long-span and standing-seam roofs in high wind or snow areas often use 0.9 – 1.2 mm. The correct gauge comes from the profile’s load-span table for your purlin spacing and loads.

Is PVDF worth the extra cost over PE coating?

For roofs in strong sunlight, near the sea or on buildings where colour matters, usually yes: PVDF resists UV chalking and fading far better than polyester. For sheds, warehouses and roofs in moderate climates, PE or SMP gives good value.

Can aluminium roofing touch steel purlins?

Direct contact in damp conditions can cause galvanic corrosion of the aluminium. Use galvanised or painted purlins with an isolating tape, and aluminium or stainless steel fasteners with sealing washers.

Is aluzinc roofing the same as aluminium roofing?

No. Aluzinc (Galvalume) is steel sheet with a 55 % aluminium-zinc coating. Aluminium roofing is solid aluminium alloy throughout, so it cannot rust at cut edges and weighs about a third as much.

Does Metline supply finished roofs or install them?

No. Metline supplies the aluminium materials, such as roll-forming coil, flat and coated sheet, embossed coil and roofing foil, cut to size where needed, with mill certificates, to roof-sheet manufacturers, panel makers and contractors worldwide.
