5356 Aluminium Filler Rod for TIG Welding

5356 is the most widely used aluminium-magnesium filler rod for TIG welding 5xxx alloys and many 6xxx alloys. It gives strong, ductile, corrosion-resistant welds that stay close in colour to the parent metal after anodising. This guide covers rod sizes, how to choose a diameter, which base metals it suits, how it compares with 4043, and how to store it. Metline supplies 5356 rod in straight lengths from 1.6 to 6.4 mm and larger, to AWS A5.10, worldwide.

Overview

What is 5356 filler rod?

5356 filler rod is a straight, cut length of aluminium alloy with about 5 % magnesium, fed by hand into the weld pool during gas tungsten arc (TIG) welding. It is classified as ER5356 and R5356 under AWS A5.10 and as S Al 5356 (AlMg5Cr(A)) under EN ISO 18273. The chemistry is identical to 5356 MIG wire; only the form and the process differ.

Magnesium in solid solution gives 5356 weld metal roughly double the shear strength of 4043 weld metal, and a small titanium addition (0.06 – 0.20 %) refines the grain as the weld solidifies, lowering the risk of hot cracking. The alloy is non-heat-treatable, so welds reach their full strength as welded.

Those properties make 5356 the default rod for boats, trailers, truck bodies, tanks, railings and frames in 5xxx and 6xxx alloys, and for any part that will be anodised after welding.

Choose 5356 rod when you need

  • High weld shear and tensile strength in fillet and lap joints
  • A weld that anodises to a colour close to the base metal
  • Good ductility for parts that flex, vibrate or are formed after welding
  • Seawater corrosion resistance matching 5xxx marine alloys

Selection

How to choose the rod diameter

Thin sheet, small bead

1.6 – 2.4 mm (1/16 – 3/32 in). A finer rod melts quickly without chilling the pool, so the welder can keep heat input low and the bead neat. 2.4 mm is the safest starting point for visible welds on 2 – 3 mm sheet.

General fabrication

2.4 – 3.2 mm (3/32 – 1/8 in). Covers most brackets, frames, railings and trailer parts. Start with 3.2 mm for 4 – 6 mm material to keep travel speed up.

Thick plate, multi-pass

3.2 – 4.8 mm (1/8 – 3/16 in). Larger rods add metal faster for big fillets and groove fills; step up to 4.0 mm when the pool needs more filler than a 3.2 mm rod can supply.

A useful rule of thumb: rod diameter is roughly equal to the base thickness up to about 3 mm, and about half to two-thirds of the thickness above that. If the rod tip balls up before it reaches the pool, it is too thin; if it chills the pool and will not melt cleanly, it is too thick.

For 6061 either rod works: choose 5356 for strength, ductility and anodising, and 4043 for crack-sensitive joints, warm service or a smoother bead. Read our 4043 vs 5356 comparison for a deeper look.

Welding practice

TIG welding with 5356 rod

Current

AC (square wave on inverters) to break up the oxide film. As a starting point, about 1 A per 0.025 mm (0.001 in) of thickness.

Tungsten

Ceriated or lanthanated electrodes with a slightly rounded tip; pure tungsten balls readily on older transformer sets.

Gas

Pure argon at about 7 – 10 L/min (15 – 20 cfh); argon-helium for thick plate.

Cleaning

Degrease, then brush with a dedicated stainless brush. Wipe rods with a clean solvent cloth if they have been handled.

Feeding

Dab the rod into the leading edge of the pool rather than into the arc; keep the hot rod tip inside the gas shield.

Crater

Fill craters or taper current at the end of the weld; 5356 craters can crack if left concave.

Handling

Storage and handling

  • Keep rods in their sealed tubes until use and store them in a dry room at steady temperature
  • Handle with clean gloves; skin oil and moisture lead to porosity
  • Keep away from grinding dust, steel and chemicals
  • Reseal part-used tubes at the end of each shift
  • Discard or clean rods that show dull oxide or discoloration

FAQ

5356 aluminium filler rod: FAQ

What is the difference between 5356 filler rod and 5356 welding wire?

The alloy is the same. Rod is supplied in straight lengths for hand-fed TIG welding; wire is wound on spools for machine-fed MIG welding.

Can I use 5356 rod on 6061?

Yes. It is one of the two standard fillers for 6061, giving higher shear strength and a better anodised colour than 4043. Use enough filler to keep dilution low and avoid cracking.

Can 5356 welds be anodised?

Yes. 5356 weld metal anodises white and closely matches 5xxx and 6xxx base metals, whereas 4043 turns dark grey.

Is 5356 suitable for high temperatures?

Not for sustained service above about 65 °C, where the high magnesium content can cause sensitisation. Use 5554 on 5454 or 4043 on 6xxx alloys for warm service.

What size of 5356 aluminum TIG rod should I start with?

2.4 mm (3/32 in) suits most work on 2 – 4 mm material. Use 1.6 mm for thinner sheet and 3.2 mm or larger for thicker plate.

Why do my 5356 welds look sooty?

A grey or black film beside the weld is magnesium oxide smut, normal with 5xxx fillers. Improve gas coverage, clean the joint and brush the smut off after welding.
