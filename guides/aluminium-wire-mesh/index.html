Aluminium Wire Mesh: Types, Mesh Sizes, Alloys & Selection

Aluminium wire mesh is a screen made by weaving or welding aluminium wire (or by slitting and stretching sheet, for expanded mesh) into a regular grid of openings. It weighs about one third as much as steel mesh of the same geometry, does not rust, and is the standard material for insect screens, light filtration, ventilation grilles and decorative facades. This guide explains mesh count and open area, which alloys and weaves to choose, and how to specify it.

Overview

What aluminium wire mesh is and where it fits

Wire mesh (also called wire cloth or screen) is defined by three numbers: how many wires there are per inch, how thick each wire is, and how the wires are joined. From those, every useful property follows: the size of particle or insect it stops, how much air or light passes, how stiff the mesh is and how much it weighs.

Aluminium is chosen over steel and stainless steel when weight, corrosion resistance in ordinary atmospheres and cost matter more than strength and heat resistance. An aluminium insect screen does not stain or rust; a facade mesh can be anodised or powder coated in any colour; and a large ventilation grille is light enough to handle and hang without heavy framing. Where mesh must resist abrasion, high temperature or strong chemicals, stainless steel remains the better choice.

Metline supplies aluminium wire to mesh weavers in the alloys and tempers used for screening, and aluminium mesh in rolls, panels and cut pieces to specification, together with the sheet, coil and extruded frames that are often bought with it. The sections below give you the arithmetic and the selection rules to write a clear mesh specification.

Good reasons to choose aluminium mesh

  • About 2.70 g/cm³, roughly one third of the density of steel or stainless steel
  • Natural oxide film resists atmospheric and fresh-water corrosion without plating
  • Easy to cut, bend, frame and roll-form; takes anodising, powder and liquid coatings
  • Non-magnetic and electrically conductive, useful for EMI shielding screens
  • Fully recyclable at end of life
The arithmetic

Mesh count, opening size and open area explained

Mesh count is the number of openings per linear inch (25.4 mm), counted from the centre of one wire. A 10-mesh screen has ten openings per inch, so the wire pitch is 2.54 mm. When the count differs in the two directions, both are given: an 18 × 16 insect screen has 18 openings per inch one way and 16 the other.

  1. PitchPitch (mm) = 25.4 ÷ mesh count.
  2. OpeningOpening (mm) = pitch − wire diameter. For 10 mesh with 0.50 mm wire: 2.54 − 0.50 = 2.04 mm.
  3. Open areaOpen area (%) = (opening ÷ pitch)² × 100 for square mesh. For the same screen: (2.04 ÷ 2.54)² = 64.5 %.
  4. MassMass per m² ≈ 2 × wires per metre × wire cross-section × density, plus a few per cent for crimp.

Open area is what controls airflow, light transmission and visibility; opening size is what controls what passes through. Thicker wire on the same mesh count gives a stronger, more durable screen but less open area. For 18 × 16 mesh with 0.011 in (0.28 mm) wire, the most common insect screen in North America, the open area works out to about 66 %.

Construction

Woven, welded and expanded aluminium mesh

Plain-weave wire cloth

Each wire passes alternately over and under the next. The most common weave for insect screens, filtration and general screening, from coarse to about 100 mesh in aluminium.

Twill weave

Each wire passes over two and under two, allowing heavier wire on a finer count. Used for fine filtration where plain weave would be too weak.

Dutch weave

Thick warp wires and fine, tightly packed weft wires give a dense cloth with very small, angled openings for liquid and air filtration.

Welded mesh

Straight wires welded at each crossing form a rigid panel that does not unravel when cut. Used for guards, partitions, enclosures and fencing; usually in wire of about 1 mm and above.

Crimped and architectural mesh

Pre-crimped heavy wire or flat strip woven into rigid panels, or cable-and-rod meshes for facades, ceilings, balustrade infill and sun screening.

Expanded mesh

Not woven: aluminium sheet is slit and stretched into diamond openings in one piece. Stiff, cheap and light, for walkways, gutter guards, filters and cladding. See our expanded metal guide.

Hexagonal netting (chicken-wire style) and knitted mesh are also made in aluminium for garden and lightweight enclosure use, but they lack the precision of woven cloth and are not normally specified for screening or filtration.

Which aluminium alloy and temper for mesh wire?

AlloyTypeCharacterTypical mesh use
5154 / 5056Al-Mg (non-heat-treatable)Good strength and very good corrosion resistance; holds shape when woven fineInsect screens, window and door screening, outdoor and coastal mesh
5052Al-MgSimilar to 5154 with slightly lower strength; widely availableGeneral woven mesh, guards, marine screens

6061Al-Mg-Si (heat-treatable)Strongest of the group, used as rod and heavy wireWelded panels and structural mesh

Mesh wire is supplied in a controlled temper. Hard tempers (H18 and similar) give stiff, flat screens that resist denting; softer tempers (O or H14) weave more easily into fine counts and are better for mesh that will be formed, pleated or knitted. For insect screening, a hard-drawn Al-Mg wire is normal because it keeps the screen taut in its frame.

Alloy and temper should be stated on the order and confirmed on the mill certificate for the wire. Wire diameter tolerance is tight on fine mesh: a 0.28 mm screen wire typically holds within a few thousandths of a millimetre, because small changes alter open area noticeably.

Two practical rules help. First, select opening size from what must be stopped, then pick the thickest wire that still gives enough open area for the airflow or visibility you need. Second, match the mesh to its frame: an aluminium screen in an aluminium frame avoids galvanic corrosion, while a steel frame needs isolating or coating.

Finishes, supply forms and specifications

Bright (mill)

Natural aluminium, which dulls to a soft grey as the oxide film thickens. Lowest cost.

Black or charcoal coated

Painted or electrophoretically coated wire. Black screens reduce glare and are easier to see through from inside.

Anodised

Clear or coloured anodising on wire or finished mesh for higher corrosion resistance and a uniform look.

Powder coated

For architectural and welded panels after fabrication, in any RAL colour.

Rolls

Common widths around 0.5 – 1.5 m and lengths of tens of metres, on cores, for screen fabricators and filter makers.

Panels and cut pieces

Flat sheets such as 1 × 2 m or 1220 × 2440 mm, and pieces cut to size with edge finishing or framing.

Buying advice

Common mistakes when buying aluminium mesh

  • Giving only a mesh count. Without wire diameter the opening and open area are undefined; two "18-mesh" screens can differ a lot.
  • Confusing mesh count with opening size. A 10-mesh screen does not have 10 mm openings; it has ten openings per inch.
  • Using soft wire for screens. Annealed wire sags and dents; specify a hard temper for taut screening.
  • Mixing metals. Aluminium mesh fixed with plain steel screws or framed in bare steel will corrode at the contact; use aluminium or stainless fasteners and isolate.
  • Choosing aluminium for hot or abrasive duty. Furnace screens, sand sieves and heavy wear call for stainless steel.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) wire mesh: frequently asked questions

What does mesh count mean?

Mesh count is the number of openings per linear inch. A 20-mesh screen has 20 openings per inch, so the centre-to-centre wire spacing is 25.4 ÷ 20 = 1.27 mm. Subtract the wire diameter to get the opening size.

How do I calculate the open area of aluminium mesh?

For square mesh, open area = (opening ÷ pitch)² × 100. A 10-mesh screen with 0.5 mm wire has a pitch of 2.54 mm, an opening of 2.04 mm and an open area of about 64.5 %.

Is aluminium wire mesh rust-proof?

Aluminium does not rust, because it has no iron to form red rust. It forms a thin protective oxide instead. In salty or polluted air it can pit slowly, so use an Al-Mg alloy such as 5154 or a coated or anodised mesh near the sea.

What is the standard aluminium insect screen size?

The most common is 18 × 16 mesh with 0.011 in (about 0.28 mm) wire, giving roughly 66 % open area. Finer 20 × 20 mesh is used where very small insects are a problem.

Should I choose aluminium or stainless steel mesh?

Choose aluminium for light weight, low cost and good outdoor durability in screens, vents and facades. Choose stainless steel for high strength, abrasion, heat, food processing or aggressive chemicals.

Can Metline supply both the wire and the mesh?

Yes. Metline supplies aluminium wire in mesh alloys and tempers, and aluminium mesh in rolls, panels and cut pieces to specification, with certificates and export packing to customers worldwide.
