Sandwich panel guide

Carbon Fibre Honeycomb Panels: Cores, Skins and Design

A carbon fibre honeycomb panel is a sandwich of two thin carbon fibre laminate skins bonded to a lightweight honeycomb core, most often 5052 or 3003 aluminium honeycomb or aramid paper honeycomb. The core spaces the skins apart, so the panel is many times stiffer than a solid carbon sheet of the same weight. This guide explains how to choose the core and skins, avoid galvanic corrosion between carbon and aluminium, and specify and test the panel; Metline supplies aluminium honeycomb core and aluminium sheet to panel builders worldwide.

Overview

How a honeycomb sandwich works

A sandwich panel behaves like an I-beam spread out into a sheet. The two skins act as the flanges and carry bending loads as tension and compression; the core acts as the web, holding the skins apart and carrying shear between them. Because bending stiffness rises with the square of the distance between the skins, adding a thick, very light core gives a large gain in stiffness for a small gain in weight.

Carbon fibre is an excellent skin material: it is very stiff and strong for its weight and does not corrode. Honeycomb is an excellent core: its hexagonal cells, made from thin foil or resin-impregnated paper, give high compressive and shear strength at densities of only a few tens of kilograms per cubic metre. Together they produce panels used in aircraft floors and interiors, drone frames, racing cars, rail interiors, optical benches, medical tables and premium furniture.

Carbon fibre skins

Skins are usually made from pre-impregnated (prepreg) carbon fabric or unidirectional tape, cured in a press or autoclave, or from dry fabric infused with epoxy. The fibre grade, weave and number of plies set the strength, stiffness and appearance.

Corrosion

Preventing galvanic corrosion between carbon and aluminium

Carbon fibre is electrically conductive and sits far towards the noble end of the galvanic series, while aluminium is anodic. If carbon fibres touch an aluminium core in the presence of moisture, the aluminium corrodes preferentially, and the corrosion can spread along the bond line and weaken the panel. This is the single most important design point when combining carbon skins with aluminium honeycomb.

  • Isolation ply: lay a thin glass-fibre ply (or an adhesive film with a glass or polyester carrier) between each carbon skin and the aluminium core so the two never touch
  • Corrosion-protected core: specify aluminium honeycomb with a corrosion-resistant treatment (conversion coating or polymer coating) rather than bare foil
  • Sealed edges: close the panel edges with potting compound, edge profiles or edge fill so water cannot enter the cells
  • Inserts and fasteners: use insulated inserts and compatible fasteners (titanium or coated stainless) and wet-install with sealant
  • Cut-outs: seal every machined hole or cut-out, as these expose both carbon and core
  • Alternative: where isolation is impractical, use aramid core, which removes the galvanic couple completely

Manufacturing

How the panel is bonded

  1. Prepare the coreCut honeycomb to thickness and size; clean and dry it. Aluminium core is supplied degreased and, where specified, corrosion-treated.
  2. Prepare skinsPre-cured skins are abraded or peel-ply prepared; or prepreg plies are laid directly onto the core for co-curing.
  3. Apply adhesiveA structural film adhesive (usually epoxy) forms fillets at the cell walls, which carry load between core and skin.
  4. CurePress, vacuum bag or autoclave cure under controlled pressure and temperature; pressure must be low enough not to crush the core.
  5. FinishTrim, machine cut-outs, pot inserts and seal edges; apply surface finish if required.

Good adhesive fillets at the cell walls matter more than adhesive quantity. Too little film gives weak bonds; too much adds weight without strength.

Applications

Where carbon fibre honeycomb panels are used

Aerospace and UAV

Floor panels, interior partitions, fairings, equipment shelves and drone bodies. 5052 and 5056 aluminium or aramid cores, depending on load and fire rules.

Motorsport and automotive

Chassis panels, floors, splitters and battery enclosures where stiffness per kilogram is critical.

Rail and transport interiors

Lightweight floors, ceilings and partitions that must meet fire, smoke and toxicity requirements.

Industrial and optical

Robot end-effectors, machine covers, optical breadboards and metrology platforms needing flatness and low weight.

Medical

X-ray and CT table tops, where carbon skins with aramid core give low radiation absorption.

Marine and leisure

Yacht interiors, bulkheads and sports equipment; edge sealing is essential in wet environments.

Ordering

What to specify for a carbon fibre honeycomb panel

  • Panel size, total thickness and tolerances; flatness requirement
  • Skin lay-up: fibre grade, weave, number of plies, orientation and surface finish
  • Core: material and alloy, cell size, foil thickness or density, corrosion treatment
  • Adhesive system and service temperature range
  • Edge treatment, inserts, cut-outs and machining to drawing
  • Fire, smoke and toxicity standard if required by the application
  • Test and inspection requirements and documentation

FAQ

Carbon fibre honeycomb panels: FAQ

Why use a honeycomb panel instead of a solid carbon fibre sheet?

Separating two thin carbon skins with a light honeycomb core multiplies bending stiffness for very little extra weight. A sandwich twice as thick as a solid laminate can be around seven times stiffer at almost the same weight.

Should I choose aluminium or aramid honeycomb core?

Aluminium core gives higher shear and crush strength at a given density and lower cost, and is non-combustible. Aramid core is lighter at low densities, insulating, radar-transparent and has no galvanic issue with carbon skins.

Will carbon fibre skins corrode an aluminium (aluminum) honeycomb core?

They can if the two are in direct contact and moisture gets in. Prevent it with a glass-fibre isolation ply or carrier-supported adhesive film, a corrosion-treated core and fully sealed edges and cut-outs.

Which aluminium alloy is best for honeycomb core?

5052 is the general-purpose choice. 5056 is stronger for high-load parts, and 3003 is an economical commercial grade for less demanding panels.

How thick are carbon fibre skins on honeycomb panels?

A 3K fabric ply is about 0.2 – 0.25 mm thick when cured, so a skin of two to four plies is roughly 0.4 – 1.0 mm. Structural panels may use more plies or unidirectional layers.

Does Metline make finished carbon fibre panels?

Metline supplies the aluminium honeycomb core and aluminium sheet used in these panels. Panel builders bond the carbon skins to the core to their own design.
