5083 Aluminium Plate for Tankers and Cryogenic Tanks

5083 is the strongest common non-heat-treatable aluminium alloy, and it keeps its weld strength, resists salt spray and actually gets tougher as temperature falls. That makes 5083 plate a standard choice for road fuel tanker shells on coastal routes and for cryogenic tanks down to LNG temperature (−162 °C). Metline supplies 5083-O, H111 and H321 tanker plate, 3 – 12 mm and wider, to EN 14286 and ASTM B209, cut to drawing and shipped worldwide.

Overview

Why 5083 is used for tanker shells

A tanker shell is a long welded pressure envelope that carries sloshing liquid, road loads and, for hazardous cargo, crash requirements set by ADR and national rules. The material must be strong after welding, ductile enough to absorb impacts, formable for heads and baffles, and resistant to the cargo and the road environment.

5083 meets those needs better than most aluminium alloys. Its strength comes from magnesium and manganese in solid solution rather than heat treatment, so the heat-affected zone of a weld keeps almost all of the annealed parent strength. A 5083-O shell welded with 5183 filler can be designed close to the O-temper allowable stress, whereas a heat-treated 6xxx structure loses roughly half its strength at the weld.

Compared with the other tanker alloys, 5083 offers the highest strength, which allows thinner walls and lower tare weight, plus outstanding resistance to salt spray. Its limit is temperature: with about 4.5 % magnesium, 5083 can become sensitised if it is held above roughly 65 °C for long periods, so hot cargo such as bitumen calls for 5454 instead.

Strengths of 5083 in tank construction

  • Highest as-welded strength of the common tanker alloys, so thinner, lighter shells
  • Strength and toughness increase at cryogenic temperatures; no ductile-to-brittle transition
  • Excellent resistance to marine atmospheres and de-icing salt
  • Weldable by MIG and TIG with 5183 or 5356 filler

Fabrication

Welding and fabrication of 5083 tanks

Filler

ER5183 is the preferred filler: it gives weld metal that matches the strength of 5083-O. ER5356 is acceptable for lower-stressed welds. Avoid 4xxx fillers, which form brittle Mg2Si with high-magnesium alloys.

Preparation

Remove oil, then oxide by stainless wire brushing just before welding. Keep wire and plate dry to avoid hydrogen porosity.

Heat control

Use moderate heat input and limit interpass temperature; excessive heat widens the softened zone and raises distortion.

Forming

O temper allows spinning and pressing of heads. Form at room temperature; hot forming of 5083 risks sensitisation if done slowly in the 100 – 200 °C range.

Inspection

Penetrant, radiographic or ultrasonic testing of seams as required by the tank code, plus leak and pressure tests.

Corrosion

No internal coating is needed for most fuels. Check compatibility for specific chemicals and avoid copper or mercury contamination.

See our guide to ER5183 and ER5356 tanker welding wire for filler selection in more detail.

FAQ

5083 aluminium (aluminum) tanker plate: FAQ

Is 5083 suitable for LNG and cryogenic tanks?

Yes. 5083-O has no ductile-to-brittle transition; at −196 °C its tensile strength rises to around 400 MPa with higher elongation than at room temperature. It is an accepted material for liquefied gas cargo tanks under the IMO IGC Code.

Can 5083 be used for hot asphalt tankers?

Not for long-term hot service. Above about 65 °C its high magnesium content can lead to sensitisation and intergranular corrosion. Use 5454 for bitumen and heated cargo.

Which filler wire should I use for 5083 tanks?

ER5183 is the usual choice because its weld metal matches 5083-O strength. ER5356 is acceptable for lower-stress joints. Never use 4043 on 5083.

Why use O or H111 instead of H321 for the shell?

Welding softens the heat-affected zone back towards O-temper strength, so the extra strength of H321 is largely lost at the seams. O and H111 also form more easily into shells and heads.

What thickness of 5083 is used for a fuel tanker?

Many road tanker shells use plate around 5 – 6 mm, with heads at or above shell thickness, but the minimum is set by ADR and the tank design standard for each vehicle.

What documents come with the plate?

An EN 10204 3.1 mill certificate with chemistry and tensile results for each lot. NAMLT corrosion test reports and third-party inspection can be arranged.
