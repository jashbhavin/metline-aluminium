ER5183 and ER5356 Welding Wire for Aluminium Tankers

ER5183 and ER5356 are the two aluminium-magnesium fillers used for almost every seam on a 5xxx aluminium tank truck. ER5183 gives the higher weld strength and is the standard match for 5083 shells; ER5356 is the general-purpose choice for 5182, 5754 and lower-stressed joints. Metline supplies both to AWS A5.10 and EN ISO 18273 as MIG spools and TIG rods, with certificates, to tanker builders worldwide.

Overview

Choosing filler for a 5xxx tanker

Tank shells, heads and baffles are built from high-magnesium alloys such as 5083, 5182, 5454 and 5754. These alloys must be welded with a 5xxx filler: silicon-based 4xxx fillers form brittle magnesium silicide in the weld and are not acceptable on base metals with more than about 2.5 % magnesium.

Within the 5xxx fillers, the choice comes down to strength and service temperature. ER5183 contains extra manganese (0.50 – 1.0 %) and gives the highest as-welded strength of the common fillers, enough to match 5083-O. ER5356 has slightly more magnesium but little manganese, with titanium for grain refinement; it is easier to buy, very widely qualified and strong enough for most other tank seams.

Neither filler suits hot cargo. Both contain about 5 % magnesium and, like the base metals, can become sensitised in long service above roughly 65 °C. Bitumen and heated-chemical tankers built in 5454 should use ER5554 filler instead.

Quick rule for tank builders

  • 5083 shell or highest-stressed seam: ER5183
  • 5182, 5754 and general seams: ER5356 (or ER5183 for extra margin)
  • 5454 hot-cargo tanks: ER5554, not 5183 or 5356
  • 6061 / 6063 tubes and fittings to 5xxx: ER5356

Fabrication

Good practice on tanker seams

Storage

Keep wire sealed in a dry store at steady temperature. Return part-used spools to their bags with desiccant; moisture on the wire is a main cause of porosity.

Feeding

5183 and 5356 are stiff enough for push feeders at short lengths; use U-groove rolls, PTFE or nylon liners and push-pull guns for long cables.

Cleaning

Degrease, then stainless-brush the joint just before welding. Do not use brushes that have touched steel.

Shielding

Pure argon is standard; argon-helium mixes increase penetration on thick sections and fast robotic seams.

Heat input

Control heat input and interpass temperature to limit distortion and the softened zone; preheat is not normally needed on 5xxx.

Inspection

Penetrant, radiographic or ultrasonic testing of shell seams as the tank code requires.

FAQ

ER5183 and ER5356 for tankers: FAQ

Is ER5183 or ER5356 better for a 5083 tanker?

ER5183. Its extra manganese gives weld metal strong enough to match 5083-O, which is what welding codes assume when qualifying 5083 joints. ER5356 can be used on lower-stressed attachments.

Can I use ER4043 on a tank shell?

No. On high-magnesium alloys such as 5083, 5182 and 5454, silicon fillers form brittle Mg2Si and the welds can crack. Use a 5xxx filler.

Which filler for a hot bitumen tanker?

For 5454 tanks carrying cargo above about 65 °C, use ER5554. ER5183 and ER5356 contain about 5 % magnesium and can become sensitised in hot service.

What sizes are used for tanker MIG welding?

Most shell seams are welded with 1.2 mm or 1.6 mm wire; 1.0 mm is common for thinner parts and positional welding.

Do ER5183 and ER5356 aluminum welds need post-weld heat treatment?

No. Both fillers and the 5xxx base metals are non-heat-treatable; welds reach full strength as welded.
