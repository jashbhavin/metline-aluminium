B93 (В93) Aluminium Alloy: GOST Composition, Tempers & Supply

B93, written В93 in Cyrillic and V93 or V93pch in Latin transliteration, is a high-strength Al-Zn-Mg-Cu wrought alloy from the Russian GOST system, used mainly for heavily loaded forgings and die forgings. It sits in the same family as 7075 and 7050 but has its own chemistry, including a controlled iron addition, and no exact Western equivalent. This guide explains what the standard requires, how B93 behaves and how to source it or a validated substitute.

Overview

What is B93 aluminium alloy?

B93 is a heat-treatable, high-strength aluminium alloy registered in GOST 4784, the interstate standard that lists the grades and chemical compositions of wrought aluminium and aluminium alloys used in Russia and the CIS. The high-purity version, В93пч (the suffix "пч" means povyshennoy chistoty, "increased purity"), is the grade that appears in the current composition tables and is what most drawings now call up.

Like 7075, B93 is strengthened by zinc and magnesium, which form fine MgZn2-type precipitates during artificial ageing, with copper added to raise strength and improve resistance to stress-corrosion cracking. Unlike most Western 7xxx alloys, it contains no chromium or zirconium as a grain-structure control, and its specification sets a minimum iron content rather than only a maximum. These differences give B93 a character of its own: it was developed as a forging alloy that can be quenched with less severe cooling than 7075 while still reaching high strength, which helps limit distortion and residual stress in thick, complex forged parts.

In practice, B93 is specified on Soviet- and Russian-heritage aircraft, helicopter, rocket and heavy-equipment drawings for frames, fittings, brackets and other parts that carry high static and fatigue loads. Buyers outside the CIS usually meet it when they maintain, repair or re-manufacture such equipment, or when a design originates from a Russian or Ukrainian engineering office.

Key points for buyers

  • B93 / В93пч is an Al-Zn-Mg-Cu alloy, comparable in family to 7075 and 7050, but not interchangeable with them on paper
  • Forgings and die forgings are the main product form; sheet, plate and bar are produced only to order
  • Material must be certified to the GOST or OST document on the drawing, or a substitute must be approved by the design authority

Two things stand out when you compare this analysis with Western alloys. First, the copper level (0.8 – 1.2 %) is lower than in 7075 (1.2 – 2.0 %) and well below 7050 (2.0 – 2.6 %). Second, the absence of chromium and zirconium means grain structure is controlled through processing rather than by dispersoid-forming additions. Both points affect quench sensitivity, toughness and corrosion behaviour, which is why a simple "use 7075 instead" decision is not safe without engineering review.

Properties & tempers

Heat treatment, tempers and mechanical behaviour

B93 is supplied solution heat-treated, quenched and aged. Its strength in the peak-aged condition is in the same high-strength class as other Al-Zn-Mg-Cu forging alloys, and over-aged tempers trade some of that strength for better resistance to stress-corrosion cracking and exfoliation. Guaranteed values depend on the forging standard, section thickness and test direction (longitudinal, long-transverse or short-transverse), so design values must come from the governing GOST/OST document or the customer specification, and acceptance values from the lot certificate.

Practical behaviour

Quench sensitivity
Lower than many 7xxx alloys, so thick forgings can be quenched less severely (for example in warm water) to reduce distortion and residual stress
Forgeability
Good in the hot-working range; suited to open-die and closed-die forgings of complex shape
Machinability
Good in aged tempers, similar to other 7xxx forgings; residual stress from quenching must be considered for thin-walled parts
Weldability
Poor for fusion welding, as with 7075; joints are normally mechanical (bolts, rivets)
Corrosion
Fair; peak-aged material needs anodising, primer or paint in humid or marine air, and over-aged tempers are preferred where stress corrosion is a risk
Temperature
Like other 7xxx alloys, strength falls with long exposure above about 120 °C because the precipitates coarsen
Standards

GOST and OST documents that cover B93

Russian aluminium standards are split between grade standards, which define chemistry, and product standards, which define dimensions, tempers and guaranteed properties for each form. Aviation parts are often governed by OST 1 industry standards or by the design bureau's own technical conditions (TU). The documents most often seen with B93 are:

If your drawing names only "В93" without a product standard, ask the design authority which document applies before ordering. A certificate that shows only the chemistry against GOST 4784 does not prove that the forging meets the mechanical requirements of the part.

B93 compared with В95, 7075 and 7050

No widely recognised one-to-one Western equivalent of B93 exists. The nearest alloys by family are 7075, 7050 and the Russian В95; the table compares their registered composition ranges (wt %).

What this means in practice:

7075 is the usual reference when converting a drawing, because its zinc and magnesium levels are close and it is available everywhere. However, 7075 is more quench-sensitive because of its chromium, and its thick forgings in T6 are prone to stress corrosion, so T73 or T7352 is normally specified for forged fittings. 7050 has much better through-thickness properties in heavy sections and is the standard Western choice for thick forgings in T74; it is a sensible candidate where B93 is used for large parts. В95 is the Russian analogue of 7075 and is more common in plate and extrusions than in heavy forgings.

Any substitution on a flight-critical or pressure-bearing part must be approved by the design authority, usually with a review of static strength, fatigue, fracture toughness and stress-corrosion data for the proposed alloy and temper.

Applications

Where B93 aluminium is used

  • Aircraft structures: frames, fittings, brackets, beams and attachment lugs that carry concentrated loads
  • Helicopters: forged hub, gearbox-support and airframe fittings on Russian-heritage designs
  • Rocket and space hardware: forged rings, frames and support structures
  • Heavy transport: highly stressed forged components where weight saving justifies a 7xxx-type alloy
  • Machinery: load-bearing levers, housings and connecting elements re-manufactured to original drawings
  • Spares and MRO: replacement parts for equipment designed to GOST material standards

For new designs outside the CIS, engineers usually choose 7050, 7075 or 7175 forgings because the qualification data, supply chain and inspection standards are more accessible. B93 remains important wherever an existing drawing, type certificate or customer contract names it.

Processing

Key points for producing and machining B93 forgings

  1. Ingot qualityUse homogenised direct-chill cast ingot or billet with controlled hydrogen and inclusion levels; the high-purity В93пч grade limits silicon for better toughness.
  2. Forging temperatureForge within the producer's qualified hot-working window and avoid overheating, which can cause incipient melting of zinc- and copper-rich phases.
  3. Solution treatment and quenchHeat to the specified solution temperature, then quench promptly. The alloy's lower quench sensitivity allows milder quench media for thick sections, reducing distortion.
  4. AgeingAge to Т1 for maximum strength, or to Т2/Т3 where stress-corrosion resistance matters more than peak strength.
  5. InspectionCheck grain flow, macro-etch, ultrasonic soundness and mechanical properties in the directions the standard requires.
  6. Machining and protectionMachine with sharp carbide tools, allow for stress release in thin walls, and protect finished parts by anodising and priming.

FAQ

B93 aluminium (aluminum) alloy: frequently asked questions

What is B93 aluminium alloy?

B93 (В93, V93pch) is a Russian high-strength Al-Zn-Mg-Cu wrought alloy listed in GOST 4784. It is used mainly for forgings and die forgings that carry high static and fatigue loads in aircraft, helicopter, rocket and heavy-equipment structures.

What is the difference between В93 and В93пч?

В93пч is the increased-purity version, with silicon limited to 0.1 % to improve toughness. It is the grade listed in the current GOST 4784 composition table and the one most modern drawings specify.

Is B93 the same as 7075 aluminum?

No. Both are 7xxx-type alloys, but B93 has more zinc, less copper, a minimum iron content and no chromium. 7075, or more often 7050 for thick forgings, can be proposed as a substitute only after the design authority reviews and approves it.

Can B93 be welded?

Fusion welding is not recommended. Like 7075, B93 is prone to hot cracking and loses strength in the weld zone, so parts are joined mechanically with bolts or rivets.

Which temper should I order?

Use the temper on the drawing. Т1 gives maximum strength; Т2 and Т3 are over-aged tempers that give up some strength for much better resistance to stress-corrosion cracking, which matters for forged fittings under sustained load.

Can Metline supply B93 outside Russia?

Yes. Metline sources GOST-designated alloys and their Western alternatives for customers worldwide, with mill certificates to EN 10204 3.1 and export packing. Send the drawing and governing standard for a technical review.
