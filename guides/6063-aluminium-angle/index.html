6063 Aluminium Angle: Sizes, Weights, Tempers and Finishes

6063 aluminium angle is an extruded L-section in the architectural alloy 6063, usually supplied in T5 or T52 temper for trims, frames and edge protection, or T6 where more strength is needed. It gives a smoother, brighter finish than 6061 angle and takes anodising and powder coating evenly. This guide gives metric and imperial size and weight tables, guaranteed properties, standards, tolerances, finishing and fabrication advice, and a clear rule for choosing between 6063 and 6061.

Overview

What is 6063 aluminium angle?

An angle is two flat legs joined at a right angle. Extruded in 6063, it becomes the workhorse section of architectural and light-fabrication work: the edge trim on a cabinet, the bead that holds a glass pane, the corner guard on a wall, the frame of a display stand, the bracket under a solar panel rail.

6063 is chosen for angle when the part will be seen or when it needs a consistent anodised or coated finish. The alloy's low content of copper, iron and chromium leaves a clean extruded surface with few die lines, so even thin, light sections come off the press straight, sharp-cornered and ready for finishing. It is also one of the lowest-cost extrusion alloys, which matters on projects that use kilometres of trim.

What 6063 angle is not is a heavy structural member. Its guaranteed yield strength in T6 is about 170 MPa, against 240 MPa for 6061-T6. For angle that carries significant load, is bolted under high clamp force, or is machined heavily, 6061 is the better choice, as explained in the comparison section below.

6063 angle at a glance

  • Equal and unequal legs from about 10 × 10 mm to 150 × 150 mm, thickness about 1 – 12 mm
  • T5 and T52 for trims and frames; T6 for railings, brackets and light structures
  • Excellent for clear and colour anodising, powder coating and wood-grain finishes
  • Weight = thickness × (leg A + leg B − thickness) × 0.0027 kg per metre (dimensions in mm)
Profile types

Types of 6063 angle

Equal-leg angle

Both legs the same length, for example 25 × 25 × 2 mm or 1 × 1 × 1/8 in. The most common form, used for frames, edge protection, brackets and general fabrication.

Unequal-leg angle

One leg longer than the other, such as 50 × 25 × 3 mm or 2 × 1 × 1/8 in. Use it where one face provides the fixing and the other acts as a stiffener or a visible return.

Sharp-corner (architectural) angle

Square inside and outside corners for a crisp line on visible trim. Most 6063 stock angle is of this type; it is slightly lighter than radiused angle of the same size.

Radiused or rounded-corner angle

Radiused root and toes remove sharp edges for safety on furniture and handrails, reduce stress concentration and give a softer appearance.

Tapered and lipped angle

Custom profiles with thinner toes, a lip, a bulb or serrations on one leg for gripping gaskets or tiles. These are made on dedicated dies and suit repeat volumes.

Pre-finished angle

Supplied anodised, powder coated or with protective film so it can be installed straight from the pack. Common for interior trim, tile edging and shopfitting.

Magnesium and silicon combine as Mg2Si, the phase that gives strength on ageing. Their levels sit lower than in 6061, which is why 6063 extrudes faster and more easily into thin legs. Copper and iron are capped low to keep the anodised finish bright and colour-consistent along the length of each bar.

In North America, stock angle in 6063 is mainly T52 or T5; in Europe, Asia and the Middle East it is mostly T5 or T6. If your drawing says only "6063 angle", ask your supplier which temper is being offered: the minimum yield strength of T6 is about 55 % higher than that of T5 under ASTM B221.

Tolerances on extruded angle depend on the leg length, the leg thickness and, under EN rules, the circumscribing circle of the section. As a rule of thumb, thin legs are held to a few hundredths of a millimetre on thickness and leg length to a few tenths. Angularity (the 90° between legs) is controlled to roughly ±1° for standard angle and tighter for precision profiles. Straightness and twist are controlled by stretching the extrusion after the press.

For visible architectural work, also agree the surface quality class, the permitted die lines and the colour range for anodised or coated material. These are not covered by the dimensional tables, and most disputes about trims come from surface expectations that were never written down.

Surface finishes

Finishes for 6063 angle

Mill finish

As extruded, with a light natural oxide. Lowest cost; suitable for hidden framing and for parts that will be finished after fabrication.

Clear anodised

Silver-matt or bright finish with a 10 – 25 µm anodic film. Hard, wear resistant and colour stable; 15 – 25 µm is usual for exterior use.

Colour anodised

Champagne, bronze, black, gold and other tones by electrolytic colouring or dyeing. Electrolytic colours are preferred outdoors for UV stability.

Powder coated

Polyester powder in RAL colours, matt or gloss, about 60 – 80 µm. Matches angle to window frames, cladding and furniture.

Wood-grain and special effects

Sublimation wood-grain, textured and metallic powders for interior design and outdoor furniture.

Brushed and polished

Mechanical brushing or polishing before anodising gives a satin or mirror look for shopfitting and decorative trim.

Anodise or coat after cutting and drilling where possible, so cut edges are protected. If angle must be bent, do it before anodising: a tight bend will craze the anodic film. Ask for protective film on finished faces for transport and site handling.

Working with 6063 angle

Cutting, joining, bending and machining

Cutting

Use a carbide-tipped, fine-pitch saw blade with a negative or low positive hook angle and a lubricant mist. Support both legs close to the blade to prevent chatter, then deburr.

Drilling and punching

T5 and T6 drill cleanly with sharp HSS or carbide drills. Thin legs punch well; keep holes at least 1.5 – 2 diameters from edges to avoid tearing.

Mitring and corners

Mitre cuts with corner cleats or brackets give neat frames without welding. Adhesive bonding is common for display and furniture frames.

Welding

TIG and MIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. The heat-affected zone softens considerably, and welding damages anodised finishes, so mechanical joints are usually preferred for visible work.

Bending

Bend T52 or T4 for best results; T5 is acceptable with generous radii; T6 cracks easily. Bending with the leg in compression and using formed tooling reduces distortion.

Fasteners and galvanic corrosion

Use stainless steel or aluminium fasteners with insulating washers in damp environments. Keep angle out of direct contact with copper, bare carbon steel and wet timber treated with copper-based preservatives.

A simple rule: if the angle is mostly there to look good, protect an edge or frame a light panel, use 6063. If a failure of the angle would cause something to fall, collapse or come loose under load, start with 6061 and check the calculation. Our 6061 angle guide covers structural angle in detail.

Buying advice

Checklist before ordering 6063 angle

  • Section: leg A, leg B and thickness, with the corner type (sharp or radiused); attach a drawing for anything non-standard
  • Temper: T5, T52 or T6, and the standard (ASTM B221, EN 755-2, AMS-QQ-A-200/9)
  • Length: mill length or exact cut length, with the length tolerance you need
  • Finish: mill, anodised (film class and colour), powder coated (RAL and gloss) or film-protected
  • Processing: punching, drilling, mitring or machining to drawing
  • Quantity and packing: pieces or kilograms, bundle size, interleaving and export packing
  • Documents: EN 10204 3.1 certificate, coating thickness report, and any third-party inspection

FAQ

6063 aluminium angle: frequently asked questions

How much does 6063 aluminium angle weigh?

Multiply thickness × (leg A + leg B − thickness) in mm by 0.0027 to get kg per metre. A 25 × 25 × 3 mm angle weighs about 0.38 kg/m and a 50 × 50 × 5 mm angle about 1.28 kg/m. A 1 × 1 × 1/8 in angle is about 0.27 lb/ft.

What is the difference between 6063-T5 and T52 angle?

Both are cooled at the press and artificially aged. T52 is aged to a controlled strength window with a maximum as well as a minimum, which gives more predictable bending and forming. T5 has only minimum values.

Is 6063 angle strong enough for structural use?

For light structures, yes, especially in T6 with a guaranteed yield of about 170 MPa. For heavily loaded frames, brackets that carry people or equipment, or bolted connections with high clamp loads, use 6061-T6 angle.

Can 6063 angle be anodised in black or bronze?

Yes. 6063 is the preferred alloy for colour anodising. Electrolytic bronze and black are the most durable outdoors; dyed colours are best kept indoors.

Can 6063 aluminum angle be bent?

Yes, with care. Order T52 or T4 for tight bends, use generous radii in T5, and avoid tight bends in T6. Bend before anodising or powder coating.

What lengths is 6063 angle supplied in?

Usually 5.8 – 6 m metric mill lengths or 12, 20 and 24 ft in imperial sizes. Cut lengths to your drawing are available on request.

Does Metline export 6063 angle?

Yes. Metline supplies 6063 angle from Mumbai to customers worldwide, with mill test certificates, protective packing and shipping by sea or air.
