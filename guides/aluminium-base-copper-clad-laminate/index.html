Aluminium Base Copper Clad Laminate (Al-CCL)

Aluminium base copper clad laminate (Al-CCL), also called insulated metal substrate (IMS), is the raw board used to make metal-core PCBs. It bonds a copper circuit foil to an aluminium plate through a thin, thermally conductive but electrically insulating dielectric, so heat from LEDs and power devices flows straight into the metal. This guide explains the layer build, the grades of dielectric, how to choose the aluminium alloy and thickness, and what to check on a datasheet.

Overview

What is an aluminium base copper clad laminate?

A standard FR-4 circuit board is a poor conductor of heat: its glass-epoxy core conducts only about 0.3 W/m·K. When a board carries high-power LEDs, MOSFETs or IGBTs, that heat has to go somewhere. An aluminium-base laminate replaces most of the FR-4 with an aluminium plate, which conducts heat hundreds of times better and also acts as a built-in heat spreader.

The laminate has three layers. On top is copper foil, later etched into the circuit. In the middle is a dielectric, typically an epoxy or other polymer filled with ceramic particles such as alumina or boron nitride, which insulates the circuit electrically while passing heat. At the bottom is the aluminium base, usually 0.5 – 3.0 mm thick, which spreads heat sideways and passes it into a heat sink, housing or chassis.

The thermal performance of the whole stack is dominated by the dielectric, because it is the layer with the lowest conductivity. Choosing an Al-CCL is therefore mostly a choice of dielectric grade and thickness, with the aluminium alloy and copper weight chosen to suit the mechanical and electrical design.

Typical layer build

Copper foil
18, 35, 70, 105, 140 µm (0.5 – 4 oz); up to about 350 µm (10 oz) for high-current boards
Dielectric layer
About 50 – 200 µm, most often 75 – 150 µm; thermal conductivity ≈ 1.0 – 5.0 W/m·K, higher grades available
Aluminium base
0.5, 0.8, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 and 3.0 mm in 1060, 1100, 3003, 5052 or 6061
Panel sizes
Common panels include 330 × 380, 500 × 600, 500 × 1200 and 1000 × 1200 mm
Thermal design

Why aluminium base boards run cooler

Heat crossing a thin layer meets a resistance per unit area of thickness ÷ conductivity. Comparing a 1.6 mm FR-4 board with a 100 µm Al-CCL dielectric makes the advantage obvious:

LayerThicknessThermal conductivityArea-specific thermal resistance
FR-4 board1.6 mm≈ 0.3 W/m·K≈ 53 K·cm²/W
Al-CCL dielectric, standard grade100 µm1.0 W/m·K1.0 K·cm²/W
Al-CCL dielectric, high grade100 µm2.0 W/m·K0.5 K·cm²/W
Al-CCL dielectric, very high grade100 µm5.0 W/m·K0.2 K·cm²/W
Aluminium base (1060)1.5 mm≈ 230 W/m·K≈ 0.07 K·cm²/W

Calculated from thickness and conductivity only; contact resistance at interfaces and spreading effects are ignored. Use manufacturer datasheet values (often measured to ASTM D5470) for design.

Two lessons follow. First, every step up in dielectric conductivity, or down in dielectric thickness, lowers device junction temperature, which directly extends LED life and power-device reliability. Second, the aluminium base adds very little resistance, so the choice of alloy matters more for flatness, strength and cost than for heat flow. Thinner dielectrics, however, reduce breakdown voltage, so thermal and electrical requirements must be balanced.

Types

Types of aluminium base laminate

General-purpose

Glass-reinforced epoxy prepreg dielectric with modest conductivity (around 1 W/m·K). Low cost; used for automotive and office electronics, power supplies and lower-power LED boards.

High thermal conductivity

Ceramic-filled epoxy or polymer dielectric, typically 1.5 – 5 W/m·K. For high-power LED street and flood lights, power modules, motor drives and EV chargers.

High-frequency

Low-loss dielectric resins for RF and microwave circuits, such as power amplifiers and radar modules, where dielectric constant and loss must be controlled.

Bendable / formable

Thin, flexible dielectrics on soft aluminium so the board can be bent once during assembly into 3D shapes for lamps and compact housings.

Double-sided and multilayer

Two copper layers or a multilayer circuit on an aluminium base, for more complex routing; more costly and harder to make.

Prepreg for lamination

Separate thermally conductive prepregs (often 75 – 125 µm pressed) for board makers who build their own metal-core stacks; match prepreg grade to the laminate grade.

Choosing the aluminium base alloy

The base plate is usually supplied anodised or chemically treated on the back and pretreated on the bonding face for adhesion. Typical properties of the common base alloys are below (handbook values for the stated temper).

Alloy / temperThermal conductivity (W/m·K)Tensile strength (MPa)Yield (MPa)Character
1060-H14≈ 230≈ 100≈ 90Highest conductivity, soft, economical
1100-H14≈ 220125115High conductivity, slightly stronger than 1060

Typical values from Aluminum Association and ASM data; values vary by temper. Order to a specific alloy and temper and check the certificate.

  • Best heat spreading at lowest cost: 1060 or 1100, common for LED boards.
  • Thin, large or punched boards: 5052-H32/H34 stays flat and resists denting during routing and V-scoring.
  • Boards with machined features or structural duty: 6061-T6.
  • Outdoor or automotive use: 5052 or 6061 for corrosion resistance and stiffness.

Processing

From laminate to metal-core PCB

  1. Panel preparationLaminate panels are cut to working size; the aluminium face stays protected by film or anodising.
  2. ImagingPhotoresist is applied and exposed by photolithography or laser direct imaging to define the circuit.
  3. EtchingUnprotected copper is etched away; the aluminium must be shielded from etchants that attack it.
  4. Solder mask and finishWhite or black solder mask is printed, and pads receive HASL lead-free, ENIG, immersion silver or OSP.
  5. ProfilingBoards are routed, punched or V-scored. Harder bases (5052, 6061) give cleaner edges; punching needs sharp, well-maintained tools.
  6. TestingElectrical test, Hi-Pot test between circuit and base, and visual and dimensional checks.

Common mistakes include specifying a very thin dielectric for heat without checking breakdown voltage, choosing soft 1060 for large thin panels that then bow during routing, and comparing thermal resistance figures from different test methods.

Applications

Where aluminium base laminates are used

  • LED lighting: street and tunnel lights, floodlights, high-bay industrial lights, architectural and bridge lighting, automotive headlamps and tail lamps
  • Power conversion: inverters, rectifiers, DC-DC converters, motor drives, solid-state relays and voltage regulators
  • Automotive electronics: power management, ignition, lighting and battery-system control boards
  • EV charging and renewable energy: charger power stages and solar inverters
  • Industrial electronics: power transistors, heaters and high-reliability control circuits
  • Audio and consumer: amplifier output stages and display driver modules

FAQ

Aluminium base copper clad laminate: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an aluminum PCB and Al-CCL?

Al-CCL is the raw laminate: unpatterned copper foil bonded to an aluminium plate through a dielectric. An aluminium PCB (metal-core PCB) is that laminate after imaging, etching, solder mask, finishing and profiling.

Which thermal conductivity grade should I choose?

Match it to power density. Around 1 W/m·K suits low-power and general electronics; 1.5 – 2.2 W/m·K covers most LED lamps and power supplies; 3 W/m·K and above is used for COB LEDs, power modules and automotive lighting.

Is 1060, 5052 or 6061 better as the base?

1060 conducts heat best and costs least, so it is the usual LED board base. 5052 is stiffer and stays flat on large or thin panels. 6061-T6 is strongest and suits boards with machined features or structural duty.

Does a thinner dielectric always mean better cooling?

It lowers thermal resistance but also lowers breakdown voltage. For mains-connected drivers, keep enough dielectric thickness for the required Hi-Pot test and choose a higher-conductivity grade instead.

What copper thickness is standard?

35 µm (1 oz) is most common, with 18 – 140 µm widely available and thicker foils up to about 350 µm (10 oz) for high-current power boards.

What panel sizes are available?

Common panel sizes include 330 × 380, 500 × 600, 500 × 1200 and 1000 × 1200 mm, with base thicknesses from 0.5 to 3.0 mm. Other sizes can be cut on request.
