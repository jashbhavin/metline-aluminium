6061 Aluminium Angle: Sizes, Weights and T6 Properties

6061 aluminium angle is an extruded L-section in the Al-Mg-Si-Cu alloy 6061, almost always supplied in the T6 or T6511 temper for a guaranteed minimum yield of 240 MPa (35 ksi). It is the structural angle of choice for frames, bracing, brackets and supports that must be strong, light, weldable and corrosion resistant. This guide covers profiles, size and weight tables, standards, tolerances and how to fabricate and order it.

Overview

What is 6061 aluminium angle?

An angle is the simplest structural section: two legs joined at 90°. In 6061 it is produced by hot extrusion through a steel die, quenched at the press or in a furnace, stretched straight and then artificially aged to the T6 condition. The result is a section with roughly the yield strength of mild structural steel at about one third of the weight.

6061 is the strongest of the general-purpose extrusion alloys that are still easy to weld and machine. That is why it is specified for load-bearing angle in machine frames, trailers, walkways, solar mounting and marine fittings, while the softer 6063 is preferred for trims, glazing beads and other visible architectural work where finish matters more than strength.

Angle is ordered by leg length A, leg length B and thickness t. Equal-leg angle (A = B) is the most common; unequal-leg angle puts more material in one direction and is used where one face carries the fixing and the other provides stiffness. Two corner forms exist: the Aluminum Association / ASTM B308 structural angle, with a radiused root fillet and rounded toes, and the "sharp corner" or architectural angle, with square inside and outside corners.

Quick facts for designers

  • Specified minimums for 6061-T6 extrusions: 260 MPa tensile, 240 MPa yield (ASTM B221 and EN 755-2)
  • Density 2.70 g/cm³; modulus 68.9 GPa, about one third of steel, so check deflection as well as stress
  • Welds by MIG/TIG, but the heat-affected zone falls to roughly T4 strength
  • Takes anodising, powder coating and paint; mill finish is adequate for most indoor use
Profile types

Types of 6061 angle and when to use each

Equal-leg structural angle

Legs of the same length with a root fillet radius. The default for frames, bracing, gussets and cross-members. Dimensions follow the Aluminum Association standard series used in ASTM B308.

Unequal-leg angle

One leg longer than the other, for example 3 × 2 in or 100 × 50 mm. Useful where a wide face is needed for bolting or welding and the narrow leg acts as a stiffener.

Sharp-corner angle

Square inside and outside corners for edge protection, framing that must sit flush and parts that will be machined. Slightly lighter than radiused angle of the same nominal size.

Radiused or bulb angle

Generous inside radius, sometimes with a bulb on the toe. The radius reduces stress concentration at the root, which helps fatigue life in vibrating structures.

Slotted and punched angle

Angle supplied with pre-punched slots or holes for adjustable bolted racks, cable support and shelving. Punching is done after extrusion, to a drawing.

Custom angle profiles

Non-standard legs, uneven thicknesses, serrations or lips can be extruded on a dedicated die. Worth it for repeat volumes, where a lighter purpose-made section saves material.

Tolerances & inspection

Tolerances, straightness and inspection

Unless the drawing states tighter limits, 6061 angle is delivered to the standard tolerance tables: ANSI H35.2 for ASTM orders, EN 755-9 for EN orders. Tolerances depend on the leg length and thickness band, and are tighter for sharp-corner precision angle than for heavy structural angle.

  • Leg length and thickness: per the tables of the order standard; precision angle can be agreed to closer limits
  • Squareness: the angle between legs is controlled, typically within about ±1° for structural angle
  • Straightness and twist: controlled by stretching; T6511 allows minor straightening after stretching
  • Cut lengths: saw-cut angle is commonly held to about ±1.5 mm (1/16 in); mill lengths have wider limits
  • Certification: EN 10204 3.1 certificate with chemistry, tensile results and temper
Working with 6061 angle

Cutting, welding, bending and finishing

Cutting and drilling

Use carbide-tipped saw blades with a negative or low positive hook and a lubricant. Clamp both legs to stop chatter. T6 drills and taps cleanly; deburr all cut edges before assembly or anodising.

Welding

MIG and TIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. The heat-affected zone drops to roughly 165 MPa tensile, so put welds away from the most highly stressed sections or size members for the welded strength.

Bending

T6 angle cracks if bent tightly. Bend the leg that is in compression where possible, keep generous radii, or order T4 and age to T6 after forming.

Bolted joints

Use stainless or coated steel fasteners with insulating washers in wet environments to limit galvanic corrosion. Avoid direct contact with copper and carbon steel.

Anodising and coating

6061 anodises well, though the finish is slightly less bright than 6063. Powder coating is common for outdoor frames and solar structures.

Corrosion

Good atmospheric resistance; suitable for coastal structures when anodised or painted. For permanent seawater immersion, use 5xxx plate or 6082/6005A with protection.

Applications

Where 6061 angle is used

  • Machinery: frames, guards, mounting rails and base structures
  • Transport: trailer and truck body framing, rail and bus interiors, roof racks
  • Construction: support framing, stair nosings, curtain-wall brackets, handrail posts
  • Marine: superstructure framing, walkways and deck fittings, usually anodised or painted
  • Energy: solar module mounting, battery racks, cable-tray supports
  • Logistics: racking, conveyors and light handling equipment
  • Aerospace ground support: stands, platforms and tooling frames

Pick 6061 when the angle carries load, will be machined, or must keep its section under bolted clamping. Pick 6063 when appearance and cost dominate and loads are light. Where even more strength is needed, 7075 angle offers roughly double the yield of 6061 but cannot be welded structurally.

FAQ

6061 aluminium angle: frequently asked questions

How much does 6061 aluminium angle weigh?

Multiply the cross-section area by the density. For a sharp-corner angle the area is t × (A + B − t). A 2 × 2 × 1/4 in angle is about 1.10 lb/ft, and a 50 × 50 × 5 mm angle about 1.28 kg/m. Radiused structural angle weighs slightly more.

Is 6061 angle as strong as steel angle?

Its minimum yield of 240 MPa is close to that of common mild steel, but its stiffness is only about one third. An aluminium angle of the same size deflects about three times as much, so long spans usually need a deeper or thicker section.

What is the difference between 6061-T6 and T6511 angle?

Both have the same strength. T6511 indicates the extrusion was stress-relieved by stretching and may receive minor straightening afterwards, giving better straightness and less movement when machined.

Can 6061 aluminum angle be bent?

Gentle bends are possible in T6, but tight bends crack. For tight radii, order T4 or O temper, form it, then heat-treat to T6.

Can 6061 angle be welded?

Yes, with MIG or TIG using ER4043 or ER5356 filler. Expect lower strength in the heat-affected zone unless the assembly is re-solution treated and aged.

Is 6061 angle suitable for outdoor and coastal use?

Yes. It resists atmospheric corrosion well; anodising or powder coating adds protection near the sea. Isolate it from copper and carbon steel to prevent galvanic attack.
