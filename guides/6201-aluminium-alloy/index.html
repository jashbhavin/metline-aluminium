6201 Aluminium Alloy for Overhead Conductors

6201 is the heat-treatable aluminium-magnesium-silicon alloy used to make the wire in all-aluminium-alloy conductors (AAAC). Drawn and aged to T81, it is roughly twice as strong as hard-drawn EC aluminium wire while keeping a minimum conductivity of 52.5 % IACS under ASTM B398. That combination lets overhead lines span further with less sag and no steel core to corrode.

Overview

What 6201 is and why it is used

Overhead line designers have three common conductor families: AAC (pure aluminium), ACSR (aluminium on a steel core) and AAAC (aluminium alloy throughout). 6201 is what makes AAAC possible.

An AAC conductor of 1350 wire conducts well but is weak, so it sags heavily and needs short spans. ACSR solves the strength problem with a galvanised steel core, but the steel adds weight, has much higher resistance and can corrode where it meets the aluminium, particularly in coastal and industrial air. AAAC made of 6201 wire is strong enough to support itself across normal distribution and transmission spans, with uniform material throughout, lower weight than ACSR for the same strength class and no bimetallic corrosion.

The metallurgy is the same as other 6xxx conductor alloys: magnesium and silicon dissolve during solution treatment of the rod, then precipitate as Mg2Si during ageing of the drawn wire. 6201 carries more magnesium and silicon than 6101, so it reaches higher strength at the cost of a few points of conductivity.

Why utilities choose 6201 AAAC

  • High strength-to-weight ratio, so less sag and fewer or shorter towers
  • Single material, so no galvanic corrosion between core and outer layers
  • Harder surface than 1350, which resists damage during stringing
  • Lower AC losses than ACSR of the same diameter, as there is no magnetic steel core

IEC 60104 and EN 50183 classify Al-Mg-Si overhead-line wire by type rather than temper, with a stronger type and a more conductive type; 6201-T81 corresponds broadly to the stronger class. Minimum tensile strengths in all these standards depend on wire diameter, so take the exact value from the standard edition named in your contract.

For the same diameter AAAC usually has slightly higher DC resistance than AAC but much more strength; against ACSR it is lighter and avoids core corrosion, while ACSR remains preferred for very long spans and heavy ice.

Applications

Other uses of 6201

  • Substation strung bus and jumpers: stranded 6201 conductor between gantries.
  • Aerial bundled cable: alloy neutral messengers and phase conductors.
  • ACAR conductors: 6201 wires combined with 1350 wires to tune strength and conductivity.
  • Earthing and lightning protection: stranded alloy conductors where strength is needed.

6201 is occasionally extruded into conductive profiles, but for busbar and bus pipe the dedicated extrusion alloy is 6101, whose conductivity is guaranteed by ASTM B317.

FAQ

6201 aluminium (aluminum) alloy: FAQ

What is 6201 aluminium used for?

Mainly as drawn and aged wire for all-aluminium-alloy overhead conductors (AAAC), plus ACAR, aerial bundled cable messengers and substation strung bus.

What is the conductivity of 6201-T81?

At least 52.5 % IACS under ASTM B398, equal to a maximum resistivity of about 32.84 nΩ·m at 20 °C.

What is the difference between 6201-T81 and T83?

Both are solution treated, drawn and aged. T83 is aged to give higher conductivity with somewhat lower strength.

Is 6201 the same as 6101?

No. 6201 has more magnesium and silicon, so it is stronger and slightly less conductive. 6201 is mainly a wire alloy; 6101 is mainly an extrusion alloy for busbar and bus pipe.

Why choose AAAC over ACSR?

AAAC is lighter for similar strength in many sizes, has no steel core to corrode and has lower AC losses. ACSR still suits very long spans and heavy ice loading.
