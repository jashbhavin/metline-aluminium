PCB Aluminium Entry Sheet

An aluminium entry sheet is a thin, hard aluminium sheet laid on top of a stack of printed circuit board panels before mechanical drilling. The drill passes through it first, which centres the bit, prevents burrs on the top copper, protects the board surface from the pressure foot and draws heat away from the drill. Metline supplies entry sheet in 1xxx, 3xxx and 8011 alloys in H18 and H19 tempers, plain or lubricant-coated, cut to panel size.

Overview

What an entry sheet does in PCB drilling

In a typical drilling set-up, one to several PCB panels are stacked on a backup board, an aluminium entry sheet is placed on top, and the stack is pinned to the drilling table. The spindle's pressure foot clamps the stack while the drill plunges through entry sheet, panels and into the backup board.

Drill centring

A smooth, hard skin stops the drill tip from skating as it touches down, so holes land closer to their programmed positions. This matters more as hole sizes shrink.

Burr prevention

The entry sheet supports the top copper foil as the drill breaks in, preventing raised burrs and torn copper around the hole.

Surface protection

It shields the board from scratches, dents and marks from the pressure foot and from chips dragged across the surface.

Heat removal

Aluminium conducts heat quickly away from the drill tip, reducing resin smear on hole walls and helping drill life.

Chip clearance

A clean entry sheet helps the flutes start clearing debris from the first moment of cutting.

Consistent stack height

Uniform thickness keeps the depth-control references stable from stack to stack.

Material

Alloys and tempers used for entry sheet

Entry sheet must be hard enough to centre the drill and resist dents, yet not so hard or abrasive that it wears the drill. That is why it is supplied in full-hard H18 or extra-hard H19 tempers of soft, clean alloys. The choice of alloy mostly trades hardness against drill wear and cost.

AlloyCharacterWhere it fits
1060 / 1050Very pure (≥ 99.6 / 99.5 % Al), softest of the group even in H18Low-cost general drilling where small hole accuracy is less critical
110099.0 % Al with a little copper; a common all-round entry materialStandard double-sided and multilayer drilling
3003Manganese-alloyed, stiffer and harder than 1100 in the same temperSmaller holes and higher hit densities where better centring is needed
3105Mn-Mg alloy with a smooth, hard surface after cold rollingPrecision drilling and as a base for coated entry sheets
8011Fe-Si alloy with good strength at thin gaugesCost-effective alternative at thin gauges

Typical tensile strength rises from roughly 150 – 165 MPa for 1100-H18 to about 200 MPa for 3003-H18, with 3105 and H19 tempers higher again. Very hard sheet is not automatically better: a sheet that is too hard can deflect fine drills and accelerate wear, so many shops settle on 1100 or 3003 H18 for general work and coated sheet for microvias and small holes.

Coated entry sheet

Plain vs lubricant-coated entry sheet

Coated entry sheets carry a thin layer of water-soluble lubricant or resin on an aluminium base, usually 3105 or 1100. The coating lubricates and cools the drill, grips the drill tip to stop it wandering and helps clear chips, which reduces drill breakage and improves hole wall quality.

TypeBenefitTypical use
Plain aluminiumLowest cost; good centring and burr control for standard holesGeneral double-sided and multilayer boards
Water-soluble lubricant coatedReduces heat and friction, extends drill life; residues wash off in waterSmall holes, high-layer-count boards
Resin coatedVery good tip location and hole position accuracyHDI, fine-pitch and IC-substrate drilling with small drills
Laminated (aluminium with paper or film)Cushions the board surface, low costBoards with sensitive surface finishes

As a rule of thumb, coated entry becomes worthwhile when drill diameters drop to a few tenths of a millimetre, when stacks are thick relative to hole size, or when hole position accuracy and drill breakage are the limiting factors.

Quality

Buying checklist and common problems

  • Consistent hardness: variation between lots changes drill behaviour; ask for temper and hardness or tensile results on the certificate.
  • Clean surface: residual rolling oil can contaminate the board and drill; specify low-oil or degreased sheet.
  • Flatness: wavy sheet lets the pressure foot trap air and debris, causing dents and poor centring.
  • Square cutting: burr-free edges and a tight diagonal tolerance make stacking and pinning easier.
  • Packing: sheets should be interleaved or packed tightly and sealed so they do not scratch or absorb moisture.

Typical faults traced to the entry sheet

  • Poor hole position accuracy: sheet too soft, wavy or uncoated for small drills
  • Top-side copper burrs: sheet too thin or not lying flat
  • Excessive drill wear: sheet too hard or contaminated
  • Resin smear: insufficient heat removal; consider coated sheet
Applications

Other uses of thin hard aluminium sheet

The same thin, hard, flat sheet is also used as protective cover sheet in PCB lamination, as separator plates, and as base stock for heat-spreading and shielding parts. For metal-core PCB substrates, thicker 5052 or 6061 plate is used instead; those are different products from entry sheet.

FAQ

PCB aluminium (aluminum) entry sheet: frequently asked questions

What thickness of entry sheet should I use?

0.15 – 0.20 mm suits most drilling. Thinner sheet saves cost on large holes; thicker or coated sheet helps with small drills and thick stacks.

Why is entry sheet supplied in H18 or H19 temper?

Full-hard tempers give a firm surface that centres the drill and resists dents from the pressure foot, while the soft base alloy keeps drill wear low.

Is 3003 better than 1100 for entry sheet?

3003 is stiffer and harder in the same temper, which helps hole position accuracy with smaller drills. 1100 is softer and gentler on drills. Many shops use 1100 for general work and 3003 or coated sheet for finer holes.

When should I use lubricant-coated entry sheet?

When drilling small holes, high-layer-count boards or HDI designs where drill breakage, hole position accuracy or resin smear are problems. The water-soluble coating cools and lubricates the drill.

Can entry sheet be reused?

No. Once drilled, the sheet has holes in the previous pattern that would deflect new drills. It is single-use and is collected as clean aluminium scrap for recycling.

Do you cut entry sheet to our panel size?

Yes. Metline supplies entry sheet cut to your panel dimensions or in coil, plain or coated, with mill certificates and export packing, worldwide.
