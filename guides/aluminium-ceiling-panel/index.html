Ceiling materials guide

Aluminium Ceiling Panels and Ceiling Materials

Aluminium ceiling panels are roll-formed or pressed from thin coated aluminium sheet, usually 0.5 – 1.0 mm 3003, 3105 or 5005, or built as stiff honeycomb and sandwich panels for large formats. They are light, non-combustible, moisture-proof and easy to perforate for acoustics. This guide explains the panel types, suspension systems, alloys, finishes, fire and acoustic performance, and what ceiling manufacturers and contractors should specify when buying the aluminium.

Overview

Why aluminium is used for ceilings

A metal ceiling has to stay flat for decades, survive humidity and cleaning, hide services yet open for maintenance, and meet fire and acoustic rules. Aluminium meets all of these at about one third of the weight of a steel ceiling, which reduces the load on hangers and the structure above.

Unlike gypsum and mineral-fibre tiles, aluminium panels do not sag or stain when exposed to moisture, so they are widely used in bathrooms, kitchens, swimming pools, metro stations, airports and semi-outdoor canopies. The metal is inherently non-combustible, can be perforated for sound absorption, and its coated surface can be wiped or washed. At the end of the building's life the panels are recycled with little loss of value.

Metline supplies the aluminium that ceiling systems are made from: coated and mill-finish coil and sheet, perforated sheet, honeycomb and other sandwich panels, and extruded profiles for baffles, carriers and trims. Ceiling manufacturers roll-form or press the panels, and specialist contractors design and install the complete ceiling.

Main advantages

  • Moisture-proof and dimensionally stable; no sagging in humid spaces
  • Non-combustible base metal, with coated panels commonly classified A1 or A2-s1,d0
  • Perforation plus acoustic fleece turns a reflective ceiling into a sound absorber
  • Demountable panels give quick access to services above the ceiling
  • Light weight reduces hanger loads and makes large spans practical
  • Fully recyclable with no loss of material properties

Linear and baffle ceilings are mainly about appearance and access; lay-in and clip-in tiles are the workhorses of commercial interiors; sandwich panels solve the problem of large panels that would otherwise sag or pillow.

Typical sheet thickness is 0.5 – 0.7 mm for linear strips and small tiles, 0.7 – 1.0 mm for 600 × 1200 mm tiles and clip-in panels, and 1.0 – 1.2 mm or more for long hook-on planks and outdoor panels. Thicker sheet resists denting and pillowing but adds weight and cost; beyond about 1.5 m spans a sandwich panel is usually more efficient.

Acoustic performance

Perforated panels and acoustic performance

Solid aluminium reflects sound. Perforating the panel and backing it with an acoustic fleece or mineral-wool pad turns it into an absorber: sound passes through the holes and is dissipated in the backing. Performance depends on hole size, open area, panel depth and the backing material.

Micro-perforation

Holes of about 0.5 – 1 mm give a nearly plain appearance with useful absorption when combined with fleece.

Round holes

The most common pattern, typically 1.5 – 3 mm holes on a staggered grid with 10 – 25 % open area.

Square and slotted holes

Decorative patterns for feature ceilings; open area and backing still set the acoustic result.

Acoustic backing

Black or white non-woven fleece bonded to the back, or a mineral-wool pad in a sealed bag for hygiene areas.

Absorption is measured in a reverberation room to ISO 354 or ASTM C423 and reported as αw (ISO 11654) or NRC. Well-designed perforated aluminium ceilings with backing commonly achieve NRC values in the 0.5 – 0.8 range, but the figure belongs to a specific panel, perforation and plenum depth, so always ask for the test report of the exact build-up.

Installation systems

Suspension systems and what they demand from the panel

SystemHow it worksWhat the panel must achieve
Exposed T-grid (lay-in)Tiles rest on a visible grid of main and cross teesAccurate tray size and squareness so tiles sit flat
Concealed clip-inPanel edges snap into spring carriersTight edge-form tolerance; stiff edges that hold the clip
Linear carrierStrips clip onto toothed carriers at set spacingConsistent roll-formed profile along long lengths
Baffle carrierBlades hang from carriers by clips or hooksStraight, twist-free blades; extruded or roll-formed
Hook-onHooked panel edges hang on heavy carriers, often with safety catchesStrong edge returns; often sandwich panels for large formats

Suspended ceilings in Europe are covered by EN 13964, which sets requirements for components, deflection, durability classes for humidity and corrosion, and safety. In North America, ASTM C635 and C636 cover grid manufacture and installation, and ASTM E1264 classifies acoustical ceiling products. Outdoor and canopy ceilings must also resist wind suction, which usually means hook-on or screw-fixed panels and tested safety clips.

Surface and fire

Finishes, durability and fire classification

Coil coating (polyester)

The standard for interior ceilings: coated on a continuous line before forming, giving an even colour. Coil-coated aluminium is specified to EN 1396.

PVDF coil coating

For semi-outdoor and outdoor ceilings exposed to UV; better colour and gloss retention than polyester.

Powder coating

Applied after forming, useful for baffles, extrusions and small runs in any RAL colour.

Anodising

A metallic finish grown from the metal; specify 5005 or anodising-quality sheet for colour consistency.

Wood-grain and printed films

Timber or stone patterns by heat transfer or film lamination for warm interiors.

Hygienic coatings

Smooth, washable finishes and sealed backings for hospitals and food areas.

Under EN 13501-1, uncoated aluminium is classified A1 (non-combustible) without testing. Coated panels are typically classified A1 or A2-s1,d0 depending on the organic content of the coating and backing; acoustic fleece and adhesives also count. Ask for the classification report covering the exact panel, coating and backing.

Procurement

What to specify when buying ceiling aluminium

  1. Panel type and systemLay-in, clip-in, linear, baffle, hook-on or sandwich, and the carrier system it must fit.
  2. Alloy, temper, thicknessFor example 3003-H24, 0.7 mm; or 5052-H32, 1.0 mm for outdoor use.
  3. Coil or sheet sizeCoil width and inner diameter for roll-forming lines, or cut sheet size for press lines.
  4. CoatingPolyester or PVDF, colour code, gloss and film thickness; protective film on the visible face.
  5. Perforation and backingHole pattern, open area, unperforated borders and acoustic fleece type.
  6. Documents and deliveryMill certificates (EN 10204 3.1), fire and acoustic reports where needed, and delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP).

Common mistakes

  • Choosing sheet too thin for the panel size, leading to sagging and visible pillowing
  • Mixing coil batches without colour control, giving visible shade differences
  • Using interior polyester coatings on exposed canopies
  • Relying on a generic NRC figure instead of a test report for the actual build-up
  • Ignoring wind suction on outdoor soffits and canopies

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) ceiling panels: frequently asked questions

What thickness is an aluminium ceiling panel?

Most interior tiles and strips are 0.5 – 1.0 mm thick sheet. Long hook-on planks and outdoor panels use about 1.0 – 1.5 mm, and sandwich panels for large formats are typically 6 – 25 mm overall.

Which alloy is used for aluminium ceilings?

3003 and 3105 in H14 or H24 temper are the most common, usually pre-painted. 5005 is chosen for anodised panels and 5052 for outdoor or coastal canopies.

Are aluminium ceilings fireproof?

Aluminium is non-combustible and uncoated aluminium is classified A1 under EN 13501-1. Coated and backed panels are usually A1 or A2-s1,d0; confirm with the classification report for the exact product.

Can aluminium ceiling panels be used outdoors?

Yes, for canopies, soffits and walkways, using a PVDF or outdoor-grade powder coating, a suitable alloy such as 5052 or 3003, and hook-on or screw fixing designed for wind suction.

Do perforated aluminium ceilings absorb sound?

Yes, when backed with acoustic fleece or mineral wool. The absorption depends on perforation, backing and plenum depth and is measured to ISO 354 or ASTM C423.

Does Metline install ceilings?

No. Metline supplies the aluminium coil, sheet, perforated sheet, sandwich panels and profiles used by ceiling manufacturers and contractors, shipped worldwide with mill certificates.

Need aluminium for a ceiling project?

Tell us the panel type, alloy, gauge, coating and quantity. Metline will confirm availability, certification and delivered pricing.
