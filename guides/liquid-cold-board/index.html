Aluminium Sheet for Power Battery Liquid Cold Plates

A battery liquid cold plate is a thin aluminium panel with internal coolant channels that sits under or between battery cells and carries their heat away. Most are made from 3003 sheet or from 3003 core clad with a 4xxx brazing alloy such as 4343 or 4045, stamped and brazed into a sealed unit. Metline supplies the bare and clad aluminium sheet, coil and blanks that cold-plate makers use, worldwide.

Overview

What does a battery cold plate need from its aluminium?

Lithium-ion cells work best in a narrow temperature window, roughly 20 – 40 °C, and lose life quickly if they run hot or unevenly. The cold plate keeps every cell in that window during fast charging and hard driving, so its material has to conduct heat well, stay leak-tight for the life of the vehicle and survive coolant corrosion.

Aluminium meets those needs at a third of the weight of copper. Non-heat-treatable 3003 (Al-Mn) is the base material because it forms well, brazes reliably and resists glycol-water coolant. Its weakness is strength: brazing at around 600 °C anneals it, so a plain 3003 plate ends up soft. As battery packs grow and cold plates double as structural floors, makers turn to clad sheet with stronger, longer-life 3xxx cores.

Metline does not make cold plates. We supply the aluminium they are made from: bare 3003 sheet and coil, clad brazing sheet with one or both sides coated, and blanks cut to size for stamping lines.

Silicon lowers the melting range of the clad layer to about 577 – 615 °C, below the 3003 core (about 640 – 655 °C). In the furnace the clad melts, flows into the joints by capillary action and solidifies as a fillet, while the core stays solid. The magnesium in 4004 disrupts the oxide film in vacuum, which is why it can braze without flux.

Temper choice is about forming, not final strength. Plates with deep channels are stamped from O or H22/H24 (partially annealed) stock that stretches well; flat cover sheets can use harder H14 or H24. Whatever the starting temper, the brazing cycle anneals the core, so post-braze strength depends mainly on the core alloy. Plain 3003 returns to roughly annealed strength (typical yield around 40 MPa), which is why higher-strength modified cores are used when the plate carries load or coolant pressure.

Thermal conductivity of 3003 is about 160 – 190 W/m·K depending on temper, lower than pure aluminium but ample for a plate a millimetre or two thick, where the thermal interface material and coolant film dominate total resistance.

Design

Common cold plate constructions

Stamped and brazed

Two sheets, at least one stamped with channels, brazed face to face. Clad brazing sheet is the natural material. Gives freedom in channel layout and good flatness.

Harmonica / multiport tube

Flat extruded multi-channel tubes joined to headers, used as long thin cooling ribbons between cylindrical or prismatic cells.

Extruded with FSW

A wide extruded profile with internal channels, closed by friction stir welded end caps. Strong and suited to plates that form part of the pack floor.

Inflation (roll-bond) plate

Two sheets roll-bonded except along a printed channel pattern, then inflated by pressure. Thin, light and economical in volume.

Brazed with internal fins

Turbulator fins, pin arrays or offset-strip fins brazed inside the channel raise heat transfer at the cost of pressure drop.

Machined channels

Channels milled into thicker plate and closed by a brazed or welded lid. Used for prototypes and power-electronics cold plates.

Quality

What to check on cold plate brazing sheet

  • Clad ratio and uniformity across the width: too thin starves joints, too thick causes erosion of the core
  • Surface cleanliness: rolling oil residues hinder flux action and wetting; ask for degreased or low-residue surfaces
  • Flatness of blanks, which affects stamping and contact between the two halves
  • Grain structure after brazing: coarse grains resist silicon penetration into the core
  • Corrosion performance: SWAAT or OY-water tests for coolant-side durability where the customer specifies them
  • Leak and burst testing is done on the finished plate by the plate maker, so material traceability by coil number matters

A common mistake is selecting temper only for strength. A hard temper that cracks in a deep channel stamping costs more in scrap than any saving in material. Another is ignoring the coolant: inhibitor packages and flow velocity both affect pitting, so tell your supplier the coolant type so the core and clad combination can be matched.

FAQ

Battery cold plate aluminium (aluminum): FAQ

Why is 3003 used for battery cold plates?

3003 combines good formability, reliable brazing, adequate thermal conductivity and good resistance to glycol-water coolant at low cost. Its limitation is low strength after brazing, which is why stronger 3xxx cores are used for large plates.

What is the difference between 4343 and 4045 cladding?

4343 contains about 7.5 % silicon and 4045 about 10 %. 4045 melts over a narrower range and flows more readily, suiting thicker joints; 4343 is the most common clad for CAB-brazed cold plates.

Can cold plates be brazed without flux?

Yes, in vacuum furnaces using clad alloys containing magnesium such as 4004. CAB brazing in nitrogen uses a non-corrosive fluoride flux.

What temper should I buy for stamped cold plates?

Usually O or H22/H24 for plates with deep channels, and harder H14 or H24 for flat covers. Brazing anneals the core, so the temper mainly affects forming.

Does Metline make finished cold plates?

No. Metline supplies the bare and clad aluminium sheet, coil and cut blanks; stamping, brazing and leak testing are done by the cold-plate manufacturer.
