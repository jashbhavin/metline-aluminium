Tube buying guide

Cold Drawn Aluminium Tube

Cold drawn aluminium tube starts as an extruded hollow and is pulled through hardened dies, usually over a mandrel, until it reaches its final size. The result is tighter diameter and wall tolerances, thinner walls, a smoother bore and higher strength than extruded tube. This guide explains the process, the grades and standards, typical tolerances and sizes, and how to specify drawn tube correctly.

Overview

What is cold drawn aluminium tube?

Drawing is a cold-working process: the tube is pulled, not pushed, through a die at room temperature. Each pass reduces the diameter and wall thickness a little, work-hardens the metal and improves the surface. Several passes, sometimes with anneals in between, bring the tube to its finished size.

Extrusion alone can make aluminium tube, but there are limits. Extruded wall thickness varies more around the circumference, the outside diameter tolerance is wider, very thin walls are hard to extrude, and the surface carries die lines. Drawing corrects all four. That is why hydraulic and fuel lines, instrument tubing, heat-exchanger tubes, antenna and telescopic sections, precision shafts and sleeves, and many aerospace and medical tubes are drawn rather than used as extruded.

In standards terms, "drawn tube" is a distinct product. ASTM B210 and B483, EN 754 and the aerospace AMS-WW-T-700 series cover drawn tube, while ASTM B221 and B241 and EN 755 cover extruded tube. Ordering against the right standard is the first step to getting the tolerances and properties you expect.

What drawing gives you over extruded tube

  • Much tighter outside diameter, inside diameter and wall-thickness tolerances
  • Walls down to a fraction of a millimetre, impossible to extrude reliably
  • Smooth inside and outside surfaces with low roughness
  • Higher strength in non-heat-treatable grades through work hardening (H-tempers)
  • Better concentricity, straightness and roundness for machining and assembly
How it is made

How cold drawn tube is made

  1. Extrude a hollowA billet is extruded into a thick-walled tube shell. For seamless tube the hole is pierced by a mandrel; for lower-cost tube a porthole (bridge) die is used, which leaves longitudinal weld seams.
  2. Point the endOne end is swaged or pointed so it can pass through the die and be gripped by the draw carriage.
  3. DrawThe tube is pulled through a die. A plug or mandrel inside sizes the bore and thins the wall; drawing without a plug (sinking) reduces diameter only.
  4. Anneal between passesHeavy reductions work-harden the metal. Intermediate annealing restores ductility so drawing can continue.
  5. Heat-treat or temperHeat-treatable alloys are solution treated, quenched and aged (for example to T6 or T3); non-heat-treatable alloys are left in a controlled H-temper or annealed to O.
  6. Straighten, cut and inspectTubes are roll-straightened, cut to length, and checked for dimensions, surface, mechanical properties and, where specified, eddy-current or pressure tests.

Fixed plug drawing

A plug held on a rod sits inside the die. Accurate bore and wall, common for straight lengths.

Floating plug drawing

A shaped plug holds its own position in the die. Allows very long lengths and coil drawing on bull blocks.

Mandrel (rod) drawing

A long hard rod travels with the tube. Gives heavy reductions; the rod is then removed by reeling.

Tube sinking

No internal tool. Reduces the outside diameter for final sizing or small tubes; bore finish is less controlled.

In non-heat-treatable alloys (1100, 3003, 5052) the strength comes from the amount of cold work, so the H-temper matters: H12, H14, H16 and H18 are progressively harder and less formable, and O is fully soft for bending and flaring. In heat-treatable alloys the final heat treatment sets the strength, and drawing mainly delivers the dimensions and finish.

Tolerances

Tolerances, surface and straightness

Drawn tube tolerances are set by tables in ANSI H35.2 (for ASTM orders) and EN 754-7/-8 (for EN orders). As a practical guide, the diameter tolerance of drawn tube is a small fraction of the corresponding extruded tolerance, and wall-thickness variation is far smaller because the mandrel controls the bore. Precision tube can be drawn to tighter, agreed tolerances for machined or assembled parts.

Quality characteristics to specify

Diameter
OD or ID tolerance, and which one is critical; mean diameter or any point
Wall
Nominal wall with tolerance, or minimum wall for pressure service
Ovality and concentricity
Important for sealing, bearing fits and machining
Straightness
Per standard, or tighter for shafts and telescopic tube
Surface
Roughness limit (precision tube is often Ra ≤ 0.8 µm), free of scratches and pickup
Testing
Eddy-current, hydrostatic or pneumatic pressure test, flare and flattening tests
Cleanliness
Internal cleanliness for hydraulic, fuel and refrigerant lines

Drawn tube is also more consistent from tube to tube and lot to lot, which matters on automated assembly lines where a press fit, an O-ring groove or a brazed joint depends on the diameter being the same every time.

Order the tolerance you actually need. Tolerances tighter than the standard tables need extra passes, slower drawing or sorting, and raise cost. Conversely, if your part is machined to size, a standard drawn tolerance may be enough.

If the drawing or specification says "seamless", porthole tube is not an acceptable substitute, even though it looks identical. For general structural parts where pressure is not a concern, porthole drawn tube offers the same dimensional benefits at lower cost.

Working with drawn tube

Bending, flaring, joining and finishing

Bending

O and soft H-tempers bend tightly. T6 tube needs larger radii and mandrel bending; bend in T4 or O and heat-treat afterwards for tight bends in strong alloys.

Flaring and beading

Hydraulic tube is flared or beaded for fittings; standards include flare and flattening tests to prove the tube will not split.

Welding and brazing

3003, 5052 and 6061 weld well by TIG or MIG. 3003 is the usual choice for brazed heat exchangers. 2024 and 7075 are generally not fusion welded.

Machining

Drawn 6061-T6, 2024-T3 and 7075-T6 machine cleanly; tight drawn tolerances reduce stock removal on sleeves and bushings.

Anodising

6061 and especially 6063 give attractive clear and coloured anodised finishes on drawn tube.

Corrosion

1100, 3003 and 5052 resist corrosion well. 2024 and 7075 need protection (anodising, primer or paint) in aggressive environments.

Applications

Where cold drawn aluminium tube is used

  • Aerospace: hydraulic, fuel, pneumatic and environmental-control lines; structural tube in 2024, 6061 and 7075
  • Heat exchangers and HVAC: 3003 and 1100 tube for condensers, evaporators and refrigerant lines
  • Automotive: fuel, brake and cooling lines, steering and seat components
  • Instrumentation and medical: small precision tube for sensors, probes and equipment frames
  • Sports and leisure: arrow shafts, tent poles, ski poles, bicycle and telescopic parts in 7075 and 6061
  • Antennas and optics: telescopic masts, lens barrels and housings that need concentric, straight tube
  • Machinery: pneumatic cylinder barrels, rollers, sleeves and bushings
  • Defence and marine: 5052 and 5086 lines for seawater and fuel, structural tube for equipment
Avoid these

Common mistakes when buying drawn tube

Quoting extruded tolerances

Buyers sometimes order "drawn tube" but cite an extrusion standard such as ASTM B221 or EN 755. The supplier may then deliver extruded tube that meets those wider limits. Always cite a drawn-tube standard.

Over-specifying every dimension

Controlling OD, ID and wall at once is over-constrained. Specify two of the three, mark which is critical, and let the third follow.

Ignoring temper for bending

T6 tube that must be bent tightly often cracks. Plan forming around the temper, or buy O or T4 and heat-treat after forming.

Forgetting internal cleanliness

Hydraulic, fuel and refrigerant systems need clean, dry bores. If it is not written on the order, tubes may arrive with drawing lubricant residue.

Mixing seamless and porthole

For pressure duty the order must say "seamless". Porthole tube looks the same but has longitudinal weld seams.

Not stating length tolerance

Random lengths are cheaper than exact cut lengths. If parts are cut from stock, state the usable length and allowable tolerance.

Most problems with drawn tube come from the purchase order, not the mill. A clear specification with alloy, temper, standard, critical dimensions, tests and packing avoids disputes and re-orders.

Ordering

How to specify and order drawn tube

  1. Alloy and temperFor example 6061-T6, 3003-H14 or 2024-T3.
  2. DimensionsOD and wall (or ID and wall), with the critical dimension and tolerance marked; length and quantity.
  3. StandardASTM B210, B483, B234, AMS-WW-T-700, an AMS material spec or EN 754.
  4. Seamless or portholeSeamless for pressure and aerospace; porthole acceptable for general structure.
  5. Tests and documentsPressure, eddy-current, flare and cleanliness tests as required; mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1.
  6. Packing and deliveryBundled or boxed with end caps and interleaving to protect the surface; EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP.

FAQ

Cold drawn aluminium (aluminum) tube: FAQ

What is the difference between drawn and extruded aluminium tube?

Extruded tube is pushed through a die while hot and used as extruded. Drawn tube is extruded first and then pulled cold through dies, which gives tighter tolerances, thinner walls, a smoother surface and, in non-heat-treatable alloys, higher strength.

Is cold drawn tube always seamless?

No. Seamless drawn tube starts from a pierced extrusion with no welds. Drawn tube can also be made from porthole extrusions with weld seams, which is fine for structure but not for high-pressure service.

Which standard should I use for drawn tube?

ASTM B210 for general drawn seamless tube, B234 for condenser and heat-exchanger tube, AMS-WW-T-700 or AMS specs for aerospace, and EN 754 for European orders.

Which alloy is best for hydraulic tube?

6061-T6 drawn seamless tube is the common choice, for example to AMS 4083. 5052-O is used for lower-pressure lines that need to be bent easily, and 3003 for refrigerant lines.

How thin can a drawn aluminium tube wall be?

Precision drawn tube can be produced with walls of about 0.25 mm in small diameters, depending on alloy and OD. Ask for the minimum wall for your exact size.

Can drawn tube be supplied in coils?

Small-diameter soft tube in grades such as 1100, 3003 and 5052 can be supplied in coils; harder tempers and larger sizes are supplied straight.

What certification comes with the tube?

An EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate with chemistry, mechanical properties and the results of any specified tests, such as pressure or eddy-current testing.
