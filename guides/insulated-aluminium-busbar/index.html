Reel-Fed Insulated Aluminium Busbar for EV High-Voltage Systems

Insulated aluminium flat conductor is a solid aluminium alloy bar with continuous insulation, supplied on reels so that busbar makers can straighten, cut, strip, bend and terminate high-voltage connections in-house. It replaces copper busbars and heavy cables in battery packs, junction boxes and the charging path of electric vehicles at roughly half the weight for the same resistance. This guide covers conductor alloys, insulation options, processing and validation.

Overview

What reel-fed insulated aluminium busbar is

A high-voltage battery electric vehicle moves hundreds of amps between the battery pack, the battery junction or disconnect unit (BJB / BDU), the inverter and the charging inlet. Rigid busbars carry that current along fixed routes; flexible cables are used where parts move.

Traditionally the rigid links were copper bars, stamped and then insulated one by one with heat-shrink tube or powder coating. A reel-fed insulated aluminium conductor reverses the sequence: the aluminium flat bar is insulated continuously first and supplied on a reel, and the busbar maker cuts it to length, strips the insulation only where the terminals go, and bends it into the final 3D shape. That removes a separate insulation step for every part and suits automated high-volume lines.

Metline supplies the aluminium conductor material. The customer, or the customer's busbar supplier, carries out forming, termination and assembly, so the material has to be specified around that downstream process as well as around electrical performance.

Weight and resistance

Why aluminium: equal resistance at about half the weight

Aluminium conducts about 61 % as well as copper by volume, so a conductor needs a larger cross-section to reach the same resistance: about 1.64 times larger when comparing electrical-grade aluminium with annealed copper. Because aluminium is less than a third as dense, the larger aluminium bar still weighs about half as much. The penalty is space: the aluminium bar is wider or thicker. In practice designers often use the extra width as a cooling surface, because a flat bar sheds heat well. Where packaging space is tight, copper or copper-clad aluminium may still be the right choice for a short link.

Conductor

Conductor alloy and temper: 6101

Most insulated aluminium busbars use 6101, a heat-treatable Al-Mg-Si alloy developed for electrical conductors. It combines conductivity of roughly 55 – 60 % IACS with much higher strength than pure EC-grade aluminium, which matters when a bar has to hold its bent shape, carry bolt loads and survive vibration. ASTM B317 covers 6101 extruded bus conductor in several tempers.

Insulation

Insulation options: polymer or fire-resistant composite

Polymer insulated

A continuous extruded jacket of PA12, PVC, PE or another polymer, typically from about 0.5 mm thick, chosen for dielectric strength, temperature class, abrasion resistance and how cleanly it strips. Suited to standard high-voltage routing inside the pack and junction box.

Fire-resistant thermal-barrier composite

The aluminium core is wrapped with a fire or thermal barrier layer (for example mica-based or ceramifying materials) under an outer polymer jacket. Intended for bars that run close to cells or modules where flame or high heat from a thermal event must not cause a short circuit.

Alternatives

Heat-shrink tubing, powder epoxy coating or dip coating applied after forming. These suit low volumes or complex 3D parts but add a separate process for each part.

Flame retardancy, fire resistance and thermal insulation are different properties. A material rating for flammability (for example UL 94) does not show that the complete bar keeps its insulation during a flame or thermal-runaway exposure; that has to be tested on a formed sample with the project's temperature, duration and measuring point.

Downstream processing

From reel to finished busbar

A typical line runs: reel feeding, straightening, cutting to length, stripping, bending or forming, and terminal preparation. Each step puts its own demands on the material.

  1. Feeding and straighteningReel size, winding direction, straightness and twist decide whether the bar feeds smoothly. Define reel dimensions, weight per reel and winding pattern to suit your decoiler.
  2. CuttingShear or saw cutting must leave the insulation intact up to the cut and the conductor free of burrs that could damage the jacket.
  3. StrippingInsulation is removed at terminals by laser ablation or mechanical stripping. Specify strip position, length, a clean boundary and the allowed residue on the aluminium.
  4. BendingFlatwise and edgewise bends stress both conductor and jacket. Bend capability depends on temper, width-to-thickness ratio, edge radius, bend radius and insulation type; qualify it on real samples.
  5. Terminal preparationPunching or drilling holes, flattening, plating contact areas, welding or fitting terminals. For the composite type, define how the barrier layers end at the terminal.

Connections

Making reliable aluminium busbar joints

  • Oxide film: aluminium forms a thin oxide immediately. Contact areas are cleaned and often tin- or nickel-plated so that the contact resistance stays low and stable.
  • Creep at bolted joints: aluminium relaxes under sustained bolt load more than copper. Use larger washers or conical spring washers, controlled torque and a joint design validated by thermal cycling.
  • Aluminium to copper: direct contact in a damp environment causes galvanic corrosion. Use plated interfaces, bimetallic transition pieces or welded Al-Cu joints, and seal the joint where moisture can reach it.
  • Welded connections: laser and ultrasonic welding are widely used to join aluminium bars to cell tabs and terminals; weld parameters must be qualified for the specific alloy and temper.
  • Clearance and creepage: terminal areas must keep the insulation distances required by the system voltage and pollution degree, following insulation-coordination rules such as IEC 60664-1.

Selection

When reel-fed insulated aluminium makes sense

Good fit

  • Rigid, flat or bent high-voltage routes that repeat from vehicle to vehicle
  • In-house capability for feeding, stripping, bending and terminal processing
  • Stable volume of similar busbar parts
  • Weight reduction is a project target

Consider another form

  • Connections that flex or move in service: use a high-voltage cable or flexible busbar
  • You buy only finished, terminated 3D busbar assemblies
  • No capability to strip, terminate or join aluminium conductors
  • Small quantities of complex parts, or no space for the larger aluminium section

FAQ

Insulated aluminium (aluminum) busbar: FAQ

How is the minimum bend radius of an insulated aluminium busbar decided?

There is no single value. It depends on the temper, the width-to-thickness ratio, whether the bend is flatwise or edgewise, the angle and the insulation material, and it should be confirmed by bending real samples on your equipment.

Does the fire-resistant composite type keep its protection after bending?

It should, but that must be proved on formed samples: check that the barrier layer stays continuous and undamaged through the bends and then run the thermal or flame test on the formed part.

How is the insulation handled at the terminals?

It is removed locally by laser or mechanical stripping. The strip position, length, boundary quality and allowable residue are defined on the drawing, and contact faces are usually plated.

Why use 6101 rather than pure aluminium?

6101 keeps conductivity close to pure aluminium while giving much higher strength, so bent busbars hold their shape, resist vibration and carry bolt loads better.

Is an aluminium busbar really lighter if it has to be bigger?

Yes. For equal resistance the aluminium section is about 1.6 times larger, but because aluminium is about a third of the density of copper the bar weighs roughly half as much.

What documents come with the material?

Mill test certificates with chemistry and properties, dimensional inspection records, agreed performance test results and reel or batch traceability. Additional automotive project documents are agreed at project start.
