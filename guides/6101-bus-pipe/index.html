Tubular bus conductor guide

6101 Aluminium Tubular Bus Pipe

6101 bus pipe is a round aluminium tube used as a rigid conductor in outdoor substations, switchyards and generator connections. In T6 temper it combines a guaranteed minimum conductivity of 55 % IACS with the strength to span between insulators under wind, ice and short-circuit loads. This guide covers tempers, standard pipe sizes, resistance per metre and the design points that matter to substation engineers.

Overview

Why substations use tubular aluminium bus

In an air-insulated substation the bus must carry thousands of amperes, span several metres between post insulators and keep its shape in storms and short circuits. A round aluminium tube meets all three needs better than flat bar or stranded cable.

A tube places its metal away from the centre, which gives a high section modulus and moment of inertia for its weight, so it deflects less between supports. For alternating current, skin effect pushes current towards the outer surface of a conductor; a tube has no wasted metal at the centre, so its AC resistance is close to its DC resistance. The smooth, large-diameter surface also lowers the electric-field gradient, which raises the voltage at which corona discharge starts and reduces radio noise and losses at high voltage.

6101 is the alloy designed for this job. ASTM B317 guarantees its conductivity for each temper, which a general structural alloy such as 6063 or 6061 does not. In many markets 6063-T6 pipe is also used for substation bus where a lower, unguaranteed conductivity is acceptable; 6101 is chosen when the current rating must be guaranteed.

Advantages of 6101 tubular bus

  • Guaranteed conductivity (55 % IACS minimum in T6) for reliable ampacity
  • High stiffness-to-weight ratio, allowing longer spans and fewer insulators
  • Low AC resistance and high corona inception voltage
  • Corrosion resistance suitable for outdoor service without coating

Engineering

Design considerations for rigid tubular bus

Ampacity

Current rating depends on diameter, emissivity, sun, wind and allowable temperature. IEEE 605 gives calculation methods; ratings rise noticeably once the surface weathers and its emissivity increases.

Deflection

Span length is often limited by gravity sag rather than stress. A common criterion is sag no more than about 1/150 to 1/200 of the span; check your utility standard.

Short-circuit forces

Peak fault current produces large electromagnetic forces between phases. The tube, insulators and fittings must resist them without permanent set.

Aeolian vibration

Long tubular spans can vibrate in steady wind. A damping conductor (old stranded cable) placed inside the tube or proprietary dampers suppress it.

Thermal expansion

Aluminium expands about 23 mm per 10 m per 100 °C. Use sliding supports and expansion fittings on long runs.

Corona

At EHV, choose a diameter large enough to keep surface gradient below the corona onset; end caps or corona balls finish open tube ends.

Fabrication

Joining, finishing and fittings

  • Welding: sections and fittings are commonly joined by TIG or MIG welding with ER4043 filler. The weld zone loses part of its T6 strength, so place welds away from mid-span where bending stress peaks.
  • Bolted and compression fittings: clamps and couplers in aluminium alloy avoid galvanic problems; clean contact areas and use jointing compound.
  • Plating: tin or silver plating at connection pads improves contact stability; most tube bodies are left bare.
  • Insulation: heat-shrink or other insulating covers are used in compact indoor installations and for animal protection.
  • Bending: large tubes are bent on mandrel benders; T6 needs generous radii, so plan bends in the layout or use cast or welded elbows.
  • Inspection: dimensional, visual, conductivity and tensile tests per lot, with straightness checked on long lengths.

Both alloys perform well mechanically. The decision usually comes down to whether the purchaser's specification calls for guaranteed conductivity; if it does, 6101 is the safe choice.

FAQ

6101 aluminium (aluminum) bus pipe: FAQ

What temper is used for substation bus pipe?

T6 is standard because it gives the highest strength (200 MPa tensile minimum) with 55 % IACS minimum conductivity. T63 is used where slightly higher conductivity is wanted.

Why is tubular bus better than flat bar outdoors?

A tube is stiffer for its weight, has lower AC resistance because of skin effect, and its smooth round surface resists corona at high voltage.

What sizes of bus pipe are common?

Nominal pipe sizes from 1 1/2 in to 6 in in schedule 40 or 80 are most common, plus metric ODs of about 60 – 250 mm.

How is aeolian vibration prevented?

By inserting a length of damping cable inside the tube or fitting dampers, and by limiting span lengths.

Can 6101 bus pipe be welded on site?

Yes, with TIG or MIG and ER4043 filler by qualified welders. Design for the reduced strength of the heat-affected zone.
