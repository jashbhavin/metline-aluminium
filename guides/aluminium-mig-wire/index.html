Aluminium MIG Wire: Grades, Selection and Use

Aluminium MIG wire is a solid filler wire fed continuously through a gas metal arc welding (GMAW) torch. The right grade depends mainly on the base alloy: ER4043 suits most 6xxx and casting alloys, while ER5356, ER5183 and ER5556 suit the magnesium-bearing 5xxx alloys used in marine and structural work. This guide covers grades, chemistry, selection, sizes, shielding gas and the feeding problems that cause most aluminium MIG defects.

Overview

What aluminium MIG wire is and how it differs from steel wire

In MIG (GMAW) welding the wire is both the electrode that carries the arc and the filler metal that forms the weld. For aluminium the wire is a bare, solid alloy wire, classified in North America under AWS A5.10 with an "ER" prefix (electrode or rod) and in Europe and most of Asia under EN ISO 18273 with an "S Al" prefix.

Aluminium wire behaves very differently from steel wire. It is soft, so it buckles easily in long feed paths and is crushed by knurled drive rolls. It conducts heat so well that welds need high current and fast travel. Its surface oxide melts at around 2,050 °C, far above the metal itself, so the oxide must be cleaned off or broken up by the arc. And molten aluminium dissolves hydrogen from moisture, oil and dirt, which comes out as porosity when the weld freezes. Almost every aluminium MIG problem traces back to one of these four facts.

Filler choice also matters more than with mild steel. The filler mixes with melted base metal in the weld pool, and some combinations crack as they solidify, lose strength at temperature, or change colour after anodising. Choosing the wire therefore starts with the base alloy, then considers strength, service temperature, corrosion environment and finish.

Four rules for aluminium MIG

  • Match the filler to the base alloy family; never pair a 4xxx filler with high-magnesium 5xxx plate
  • Use a feed system built for soft wire: U-groove rolls, plastic liner, short or push-pull gun
  • Keep wire and joint dry and clean; most porosity is hydrogen from moisture or oil
  • Use pure argon or argon-helium shielding gas only; never CO2 or oxygen mixes
Filler grades

The main aluminium MIG wire grades

ER4043 (Al-5Si)

The most widely used general-purpose wire. Silicon lowers the melting point and makes the weld pool very fluid, which gives smooth beads and strong resistance to hot cracking on 6xxx alloys and castings. Welds are relatively low in ductility and turn dark grey when anodised.

ER5356 (Al-5Mg)

The standard wire for 5xxx alloys and the most common choice for structural and marine fabrication. Stronger and more ductile than 4043, with a better colour match after anodising, and stiff enough to feed well. Not recommended for sustained service above about 65 °C.

ER5183 (Al-4.5Mg-Mn)

Higher manganese gives higher weld strength than 5356. Used for 5083 and 5456 plate in ships, cryogenic tanks and pressure vessels where the joint must match the strength of the base plate.

ER5556 (Al-5Mg-Mn)

Similar to 5183 with slightly higher strength; used on high-strength 5xxx alloys and on 7xxx structural alloys such as 7005 in some fabrications.

ER5554 (Al-2.7Mg)

Lower magnesium makes it the 5xxx filler of choice for parts operating above about 65 °C, such as tankers carrying hot products and wheels, usually on 5454 base metal.

ER4047 (Al-12Si)

Very high silicon gives the lowest melting point and highest fluidity. Used for leak-tight joints, heat-exchanger work and where shrinkage cracking must be minimised; also used as a brazing filler.

ER4943

A newer Al-Si filler with a small magnesium addition that raises as-welded strength over 4043 while keeping its crack resistance.

ER1100 / ER1188

Near-pure aluminium fillers for welding 1xxx alloys, mainly in electrical bus work and chemical equipment where conductivity or purity matter.

ER2319

An aluminium-copper filler for welding heat-treatable 2219 alloy in aerospace and cryogenic tanks, usually to AMS 4191.

For the complete family of fillers and their individual pages, see the guide to 16 aluminium welding wire grades.

Two rules prevent most filler mistakes. First, do not use a silicon (4xxx) filler on 5xxx alloys containing more than about 3 % magnesium: magnesium and silicon combine into brittle magnesium silicide in the weld and ductility collapses. Second, do not use a 5xxx filler with more than about 3 % magnesium (5356, 5183, 5556) for parts that run continuously above about 65 °C, because a magnesium-rich phase can form along grain boundaries and make the weld prone to stress-corrosion cracking. For those parts use ER5554 or a 4xxx filler on compatible base metal.

Weld strength

Weld strength and filler behaviour compared

Weld strength in aluminium is usually limited by the heat-affected zone of the base metal rather than by the filler. In 6061-T6, for example, the zone next to the weld anneals and over-ages, so the groove weld tensile strength falls to roughly 165 MPa (24 ksi) minimum, the value used in AWS D1.2 procedure qualification, whichever common filler is used. Filler choice matters most for fillet welds, where the weld metal carries the shear load, and for non-heat-treatable alloys such as 5083.

Choose the diameter by thickness and current. As a starting point, 0.8 – 0.9 mm wire suits sheet of about 1 – 3 mm with short-circuit or pulsed transfer; 1.0 – 1.2 mm suits most work from about 3 mm to 12 mm; 1.6 mm suits thick plate in spray transfer and high-deposition mechanised welding. Larger wire is stiffer and easier to feed, so many fabricators use 1.2 mm for general work even on moderately thin material with a pulsed power source.

Drive rolls

Use U-groove rolls and only enough tension to feed without slipping. V-groove or knurled rolls shave and flatten soft wire, sending swarf into the liner.

Liner

Use a PTFE, nylon or carbon-filled plastic liner, trimmed cleanly at both ends, and keep it as short and straight as possible.

Gun choice

For long leads or thin wire, use a spool gun or a push-pull gun that pulls the wire from the torch end.

Contact tip

Use tips made for aluminium, which are slightly oversized to allow for thermal expansion, and replace them as soon as they show wear or arcing.

Torch technique

Push the torch (forehand) at about 10 – 15° so the gas cover leads the pool; dragging causes soot and poor cleaning.

Joint preparation

Degrease with solvent, then brush with a stainless-steel brush used only for aluminium, shortly before welding.

Storage is a frequent and overlooked cause of porosity. Keep wire in its sealed bag until use, in a dry store at a stable temperature, and return part-used spools to a sealed bag with desiccant. Moving cold spools into a warm, humid workshop causes condensation on the wire surface, so let them reach room temperature before opening.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) MIG wire: frequently asked questions

Should I use ER4043 or ER5356?

Use ER4043 for 6xxx alloys and castings when crack resistance and a smooth bead matter, and for parts that run hot. Use ER5356 for 5xxx alloys, for higher shear strength and ductility, and when the part will be anodised. On 5083 or 5086 plate never use 4043.

Which wire do I use for 6061 aluminium?

ER4043 is the usual choice because it resists hot cracking. ER5356 is used when higher fillet shear strength or a better colour match after anodising is needed. Either way, the heat-affected zone limits groove weld strength to about 165 MPa unless the part is heat-treated after welding.

What gas do I need for aluminum MIG welding?

Pure argon for most work, or an argon-helium mix (25 – 75 % helium) for thick sections and faster welding. Gases containing CO2 or oxygen, used for steel, must not be used on aluminium.

Why does my aluminium wire keep tangling at the feeder?

Soft aluminium wire buckles if the feed path resists it. Use U-groove drive rolls with light tension, a plastic liner, aluminium contact tips and a short cable, or switch to a spool gun or push-pull gun.

What causes porosity in aluminium MIG welds?

Hydrogen, almost always from moisture, oil or hydrated oxide on the wire or joint, or from poor gas cover. Keep wire sealed and dry, degrease and brush the joint, and check gas flow and hoses for leaks.

Can ER5356 be used on parts that get hot?

Not for sustained service above about 65 °C. Prolonged heating can make high-magnesium welds sensitive to stress-corrosion cracking. Use ER5554 on 5454, or a 4xxx filler where the base metal allows.
