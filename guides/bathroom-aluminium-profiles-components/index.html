Bathroom Aluminium Profiles & Components

Bathroom products such as shower enclosures, mirror frames, towel rails, shelves and vanity cabinets rely on aluminium extrusions and sheet that stay attractive in constant humidity, steam and cleaning chemicals. This guide shows which alloys, tempers and finishes suit each part, and what to specify when you buy the aluminium. Metline supplies the profiles, sheet and machined blanks to bathroom product makers worldwide; we do not make the finished bathroom products.

Overview

Why aluminium is used in bathroom products

A bathroom is a demanding environment for any metal: humidity sits close to 100 % during and after a shower, condensation forms daily, and surfaces are cleaned with acids, chlorine bleach and limescale removers. Aluminium copes well because it forms a protective oxide film, and once that film is thickened by anodising or covered by a powder coating it resists staining and corrosion for many years.

The second reason is shape. Extrusion lets a designer put a glass channel, a seal groove, an LED slot and a screw port into a single profile, cut to length and mitred at the corners. That is why most shower door frames, sliding rails, mirror frames and towel bars are made from 6xxx-series extrusions, while cabinet bodies and shelves often use aluminium sheet or honeycomb panels.

Aluminium also weighs about a third as much as brass or stainless steel for the same volume, which makes wall-hung cabinets, large mirrors and frameless shower hardware easier to install, and it is fully recyclable at the end of the product's life.

What bathroom product makers need from the aluminium

  • An alloy that extrudes into thin, detailed sections with a clean surface
  • A consistent anodising or bright-finish response so that parts from different batches match
  • Enough strength and stiffness to carry glass, mirrors and wall loads without deflecting
  • Corrosion protection matched to wet-room conditions and aggressive cleaning products
Alloy selection

Which aluminium alloy for which bathroom part?

Three 6xxx alloys cover almost every bathroom application. They differ mainly in surface quality and strength.

AlloyCharacterTypical bathroom parts
6063-T5 / T6Excellent extrudability and anodising response; the standard architectural alloyShower enclosure frames, sliding rails, mirror and cabinet frames, trims, shelves
6463-T5 / T6Low-iron, bright-trim alloy that gives a highly reflective, uniform finish after chemical or electrolytic brighteningPremium mirror frames, towel bars, visible trims and decorative profiles imitating chrome or gold
6061-T6Higher strength, good machinability; less suited to very thin, complex sectionsHinge blocks, glass clamps, wall brackets, machined fittings and support arms
6060-T5 / T66Similar to 6063, widely used in Europe for fine-detail profilesSeal carriers, small trims and frame profiles
5052 / 3003 sheetFormable, corrosion-resistant non-heat-treatable sheetCabinet bodies, shelf trays, back panels, mirror backing

6463 versus 6063: both have similar magnesium and silicon contents, but 6463 limits iron to 0.15 % maximum and copper is kept within a narrow band. Low iron means fewer intermetallic particles at the surface, so after polishing and bright dipping the profile reflects light like a mirror instead of looking slightly milky. That is why 6463 is chosen for parts that must look like polished chrome, gold or nickel. For ordinary satin or matt finishes, 6063 is cheaper and gives an excellent result.

Properties

Strength of common bathroom profile alloys

Bathroom profiles are rarely strength-critical, but the temper still matters: a sliding door rail or a cabinet frame carrying a heavy mirror must not sag. The values below are typical figures; specified minimums for extrusions are set by EN 755-2 or ASTM B221 and depend on wall thickness.

Alloy and temperTensile strength (typical)Yield strength (typical)ElongationUse
6063-T5186 MPa145 MPa12 %General frames and trims
6063-T6241 MPa214 MPa12 %Door rails, longer spans
6463-T5Similar to 6063-T5Similar to 6063-T5—Bright decorative parts
6061-T6310 MPa276 MPa12 %Brackets, clamps, hinges

Glass weight is what usually governs the size of a shower profile. A 1000 × 2000 mm panel of 8 mm toughened glass weighs about 40 kg (glass density about 2.5 g/cm³), and a sliding door rail must carry this load through rollers without visible deflection. Larger enclosures therefore use deeper rail sections or 6063-T6 rather than T5.

Applications

Aluminium for each bathroom product

Shower enclosures and doors

Wall channels, door frames, sliding top rails, threshold and seal profiles, and handles. Typically 6063-T5/T6 extrusions, with 6061 machined parts for hinges and glass clamps. Profiles must accept seals and fit the glass thickness used, commonly 6, 8 or 10 mm.

Mirror frames and LED mirrors

Thin frame and back-frame profiles, LED light channels with diffuser grooves, corner keys and wall brackets. 6063 for satin finishes, 6463 for bright frames. See the mirror frame profile guide.

Towel rails and bars

Round, square or oval tube and solid bar, end caps and wall flanges. 6063 tube for standard bars and 6463 for polished or gold-effect finishes.

Shelves and storage

Shelf profiles, edge trims, corner baskets and wall rails, often in 6063 extrusion combined with 5052 or 3003 sheet trays.

Mirror cabinets and vanities

Cabinet frames, handle profiles, hinge mounting rails and panels, with honeycomb or foam-core panels where weight matters.

Shower systems

Shower rail tube, slide bars and overhead shower-head arms, usually anodised 6063 tube or machined 6061 components.

Surface finishing

Surface finishes for wet rooms

The finish decides both the look and the life of a bathroom part. Specify it as precisely as the dimensions.

FinishAppearanceWet-room guidance
Anodising (silver, black, bronze, gold)Metallic, transparent film that shows the metal textureSpecify a film thickness class; 15 µm minimum is a sensible choice for wet interiors, 20 µm for heavy use. Properly sealed film is essential.
Bright dip / electropolish + anodiseMirror-like, chrome-effect finishUse 6463 or another bright-trim alloy; thinner films preserve brightness but reduce durability.
Brushed + anodiseFine linear texture: brushed nickel, brushed gold, brushed rose goldBrushing is done before anodising; colour consistency needs one alloy and one anodising line per project.
Powder coatingMatt or satin colour: black, white, gun greyChoose a coater approved to Qualicoat or an equivalent scheme; check edge coverage on cut ends.
PVD over polished aluminiumGold, gunmetal or chrome tonesUsually applied by a specialist finisher on top of a prepared, coated surface.

Two common causes of bathroom failures are cleaning chemicals and mixed metals. Strong alkaline or acidic cleaners attack anodic films and powder coatings, so care instructions should recommend neutral cleaners. Stainless steel screws in direct contact with bare aluminium can cause galvanic corrosion in standing water; use coated fasteners, insulating washers or ensure the joint drains and dries. Design & fabrication

Design and fabrication considerations

  • Keep walls uniform: thin, even walls (typically 1.0 – 2.0 mm for frames) extrude more consistently and anodise more evenly than sections with heavy changes in thickness.
  • Put visible faces on the die's best surface: tell the extruder which faces are decorative so that die lines and handling marks are kept off them.
  • Design for drainage: bottom profiles and thresholds need weep paths so water does not sit in channels.
  • Allow for mitre joints: 45° corners on mirror and cabinet frames need corner keys or cleats; plan the screw ports and key grooves in the profile.
  • Match tolerances to the glass and seals: extrusion tolerances follow EN 755-9 or EN 12020-2 for precision 6060/6063 profiles; tighter channel widths should be agreed with the extruder.
  • Machine before finishing: holes, slots and threads should be cut before anodising so bare metal is not exposed afterwards.

FAQ

Bathroom aluminium (aluminum) profiles: FAQ

Which aluminium alloys are used for bathroom products?

6063 in T5 or T6 temper is the standard choice for frames, rails and trims. 6463 is used for bright, mirror-like decorative parts, and 6061-T6 for machined brackets, clamps and hinges. Sheet parts usually use 5052 or 3003.

Why is 6463 better for premium visible parts?

Its iron content is limited to 0.15 %, so the surface has fewer intermetallic particles. After polishing and bright dipping it gives a clearer, more reflective finish than 6063, which is why it is used to imitate chrome, nickel or gold.

Does aluminium corrode in a bathroom?

Bare aluminium can stain in constant humidity, but anodised or powder-coated aluminium performs very well. Specify a sealed anodic film of at least 15 µm or a quality-approved powder coating, avoid harsh cleaners and isolate stainless steel fasteners.

Can long shower profiles be anodised?

Yes. Profiles in normal stock lengths can be anodised; the maximum length depends on the anodising line used. Tell us the finished length so that profiles are cut and racked correctly before finishing.

Does Metline make shower enclosures or mirrors?

No. Metline supplies the aluminium: extruded profiles, tube, sheet and machined components, finished as specified. Our customers design, assemble and install the finished bathroom products.

Can you supply profiles to our own drawings?

Yes. Send the profile drawing with decorative faces marked, alloy, temper, finish and quantity. Custom profiles need a dedicated extrusion die, which is confirmed in the quotation.

Sourcing aluminium for bathroom products?

Send your profile drawings, finish requirements and quantities. We will reply with alloy advice, finish options and a delivered price.
