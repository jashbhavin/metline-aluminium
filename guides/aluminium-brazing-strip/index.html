Aluminium Brazing Strip & Clad Coil for Heat Exchangers

Aluminium brazing strip is clad brazing material slit into narrow coils, typically 0.15 – 3 mm thick, that feeds tube mills, folding lines and progressive presses. Most of it becomes high-frequency welded or folded flat tubes for radiators, heater cores and charge-air coolers, or headers and side plates. This guide explains the clad structures, how strip is turned into tubes, what to specify and how to avoid common problems.

Overview

What is aluminium brazing strip?

Brazing strip is the same roll-bonded composite as brazing sheet, a higher-melting core with an aluminium-silicon filler layer on one or both faces, but supplied as narrow, precision-slit coil. Its job is to feed continuous forming lines without stopping.

In a heat exchanger, the strip usually becomes the tube. A narrow coil is roll-formed into a flat or round tube and either welded along its seam by high-frequency (HF) welding, or folded into a "B" profile that is closed during brazing. The outside of the tube carries filler that will braze to the fins and headers; the inside is often lined with 7072 to protect it from coolant corrosion.

Strip also feeds presses that stamp headers, side plates, turbulators and small plates. Compared with cut sheets, coil feeding lets these parts be made at high speed with minimal scrap. For thin fin material, see our brazing foil guide; for wide sheet and plate, the brazing sheet guide.

What makes strip different from sheet

  • Narrow slit widths matched to tube circumference or part blank width
  • Tight width tolerance, low camber and clean burr-free edges for tube welding
  • Coils sized and wound (pancake or traverse) for continuous feeding
  • Clad face orientation controlled so the filler ends up on the correct side of the tube

Magnesium-bearing cores such as 6951 or 6061 do not braze reliably with standard potassium fluoroaluminate flux in CAB. Use caesium-modified flux or vacuum brazing, and confirm by trial.

Process

How brazing strip becomes heat exchanger tube

HF-welded flat tube

The strip is roll-formed into a tube, its edges heated by high-frequency current and forged together, the outer weld bead is scarfed off, and the tube is sized to a flat oval. Requires very consistent width, edge condition and thickness.

Folded B-tube

Double-clad strip is folded so that a central web is formed and the edges overlap; the tube is closed and the web joined during brazing. Allows very thin walls and internal reinforcement.

Round welded tube

Clad strip formed and welded into round tube for heat exchangers, connection tubes and manifolds.

Stamped plates and headers

Strip fed from coil into progressive dies to make header plates, end plates and turbulators.

Each route places different demands on the strip. HF welding is sensitive to edge burrs and to variation in width, because the seam must meet evenly. Folded tubes need good elongation and accurate clad ratios on both faces. Deep-drawn headers need O or H24 temper. Tell your supplier which route the strip is for, not just its dimensions.

Quality

Tolerances, testing and certification

  • Thickness: tight tolerance, because tube wall thickness sets burst pressure and weld quality
  • Slit width and camber: controlled for even seam welding and accurate tube perimeter
  • Edge condition: burr-free, with no cracks or slivers at the slit edge
  • Clad ratio: per face, checked on cross-sections across the width
  • Mechanical properties: as delivered and, on request, after a simulated braze cycle
  • Corrosion: SWAAT (ASTM G85 Annex A3) or similar tests are often used by heat exchanger makers to compare tube materials
  • Certificate: EN 10204 3.1 mill certificate with chemistry of each layer and mechanical results

Applications

Applications of aluminium brazing strip

  • Automotive radiators and heater cores: HF-welded or folded flat tubes, headers and side plates
  • Charge-air coolers and oil coolers: thicker tubes, turbulators and plates
  • EV thermal management: battery cooling tubes, cold plate components, chiller plates
  • HVAC and refrigeration: header tubes for microchannel coils, connection tubes and brackets
  • Off-highway and rail: heavy-duty radiators and coolers
  • Industrial plate-fin exchangers: side bars, separators and closure strips

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) brazing strip: frequently asked questions

What is brazing strip used for?

Mainly for making heat exchanger tubes by HF welding or folding, and for stamping headers, side plates and turbulators. The clad filler brazes the tubes to fins and headers in the furnace.

Why do radiator tubes have a 7072 layer inside?

7072 contains about 1 % zinc, which makes it anodic to the core. It corrodes preferentially and sideways, protecting the core from pitting through the tube wall on the coolant side.

What is the difference between welded and folded tubes?

Welded tubes are formed from single- or three-layer strip and seam-welded before brazing. Folded B-tubes use double-clad strip folded into shape and closed during brazing, which allows thinner walls and an internal web.

Which temper should I order for tube strip?

It depends on the forming route. HF tube mills commonly run H14 or H24 strip; severe folding and deep-drawn headers need O or H24. Agree the temper with your tube mill operator.

Can 6061-cored brazing strip be CAB brazed?

Not reliably with standard flux, because magnesium poisons potassium fluoroaluminate flux. Use caesium-modified flux, a low-Mg core, or vacuum brazing.

How should I specify slit width?

Give the nominal width and tolerance, the camber limit and the required edge condition, plus the coil inner and outer diameter and winding type for your line.
