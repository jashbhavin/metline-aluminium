Cabinet Handle Aluminium Profiles: Alloys, Shapes & Finishes

Cabinet handle profiles are thin-walled aluminium extrusions, almost always 6063 in T5 or T6 temper, that are cut to length and fixed to kitchen, wardrobe and office furniture doors as edge pulls, recessed channels or bar handles. This guide explains which alloy, temper, shape, wall thickness and finish to specify so that the profile looks right, survives daily use and fits your cabinet system. Metline supplies the extruded profile in mill finish or finished lengths to furniture makers and hardware brands worldwide.

Overview

What a cabinet handle profile has to do

A handle profile looks simple, but it is a decorative part that people touch thousands of times a year, in a kitchen full of steam, grease and cleaning chemicals. It must keep a sharp, straight line over lengths of up to 3 metres, carry the pull force of a heavy drawer without flexing, and keep its colour next to appliances and worktops for a decade or more.

Extruded aluminium suits this job better than most alternatives. A single die can produce a complex cross-section with a finger recess, a mounting flange and a groove for the door panel in one piece, and the profile can then be cut to any door width. Aluminium is light, so it adds little weight to the door hinges, and it takes anodised and painted finishes that match modern handle-less kitchen designs.

Handle profiles fall into two broad groups. Surface-mounted pulls (bar, edge and T-bar handles) are screwed to the face or top edge of the door. Integrated or recessed profiles (J-pull, G-profile, C-channel and L-channel "gola" systems) run along the carcass or the door edge so that the front looks handle-free. Both use the same alloys, but integrated profiles are longer, more structural and need closer straightness tolerances.

Key decisions when specifying a handle profile

  • Alloy and temper: 6063-T5 for most profiles, 6063-T6 or 6061-T6 for long or heavily loaded pulls
  • Cross-section and wall thickness: enough stiffness without making the die hard to fill
  • Finish: anodised, powder-coated, wood-grain transfer, brushed or polished, and the visible faces
  • Cut length, end machining, drilling and packing, so the profile arrives ready to fit
Chemical composition

6063 and 6061 composition for handle profiles

Almost every decorative handle profile is extruded from 6063. Its low copper and iron content gives a bright, even anodised colour, and it extrudes easily through the thin walls and sharp corners that handle designs need. 6061 is used when a profile must be stronger, for example a long bar handle on a tall pantry door or a profile that also acts as a structural frame member. Mechanical properties

Physical properties of 6063 (useful for design)

Density
2.70 g/cm³: a 1 m profile with a 120 mm² section weighs about 0.32 kg
Modulus of elasticity
68.9 GPa, about one third of steel, so stiffness comes from section shape
Thermal expansion
About 23.4 µm/m·°C: a 3 m profile grows roughly 1.4 mm over a 20 °C change
Melting range
About 615 – 655 °C, far above any kitchen temperature
Tempers

T5, T6 or T4: choosing the temper

6063-T5

Cooled from the extrusion press and then artificially aged. The default for straight handle profiles: strong enough, economical, and it anodises with a uniform tone.

6063-T6

Solution heat-treated (by press quench on thin walls) and aged to full strength. Choose it for long integrated rails, tall-door bar handles or thin walls that must resist denting.

6063-T4 / T1

Naturally aged and softer. Used when the profile is to be bent into curves or radiused handles, then aged to T5/T6 after forming if strength is needed.

6061-T6

Higher strength for pulls that double as frame members, aluminium cabinet frames or heavy drawer handles. Anodised colour is slightly less clear than 6063.

6061-T4

Formable condition of 6061 for bent or stretched handles that are aged after shaping.

Temper and finish

Anodising and powder curing happen after ageing. Powder curing at about 180 – 200 °C for a short cycle has little effect on T5/T6 strength.

Design

Wall thickness, tolerances and straightness

Most handle profiles have walls of about 1.0 – 2.0 mm. Walls thinner than about 0.8 mm are hard to extrude evenly and dent easily; walls much thicker than needed add cost and weight without improving the look. Keep wall thickness as uniform as possible across the section, because large differences cause uneven cooling, distortion and visible streaks after anodising.

Typical tolerance references

Cross-section dimensions
EN 12020-2 (precision profiles in 6060/6063) or EN 755-9; ASTM B221 / ANSI H35.2 for US orders
Wall thickness
Typically ±0.10 – 0.15 mm on 1 – 2 mm walls under EN 12020-2
Straightness
Critical for gola channels; EN 12020-2 limits deviation per metre and over the full length
Twist and flatness of visible faces
Specify if long profiles are fixed edge-to-edge with doors
Cut length
Typically ±0.5 mm for finished cut lengths; tighter on CNC-cut parts
Finish thickness
Anodising class (for example AA10, AA15) per EN ISO 7599 / ISO 7599; powder film usually 60 – 120 µm
  • Mark the visible (decorative) faces on the drawing so they are protected during handling and finishing
  • Round sharp external corners slightly (about 0.3 – 0.5 mm) for a better feel and more even anodising
  • Add small grooves or ribs to hide die lines on large flat faces
  • Allow for finish thickness in clip-on grooves: powder coating can add 0.1 – 0.2 mm per side
  • Design the groove for the actual board and edge-banding thickness, not the nominal value

Surface finishing

Surface finishes for handle profiles

Anodising

Clear (silver), champagne, bronze, black, gold and rose-gold tones. For kitchen interiors a film of 10 – 15 µm is normal; 15 – 20 µm suits bathrooms and heavier wear. The film is hard and will not peel.

Brushed or sand-blasted then anodised

Mechanical brushing ("wire drawing") or bead blasting before anodising gives a satin or matt look that hides fingerprints and light scratches.

Electrophoretic (E-coat) over anodising

A thin clear or coloured acrylic layer is deposited over the anodised film for extra stain and alkali resistance and a smoother feel.

Powder coating

Any RAL or custom colour, matt to gloss, to match cabinet fronts. Polyester powders are standard; specify architectural-grade powders if colour stability matters.

Wood-grain transfer

A printed film is sublimated into a powder base coat to imitate oak, walnut and other timbers, matching wood-effect fronts.

Polished and bright-dipped

Mechanical polishing and chemical brightening before anodising for a mirror-like chrome look, best on bright-quality 6063.

For colour consistency across a kitchen, order all handle profiles in one batch from one finishing line and approve a limit sample showing the lightest and darkest acceptable shade. Our guide to aluminium for anodised appearance parts explains why alloy and billet quality matter.

FAQ

Cabinet handle aluminium (aluminum) profiles: FAQ

Which aluminium alloy is best for cabinet handles?

6063 in T5 or T6 temper. It extrudes cleanly into thin, detailed sections and gives the most uniform anodised colour. Use 6061-T6 only where a handle or rail needs more strength than 6063 can give.

What anodising thickness should a kitchen handle profile have?

For dry kitchen interiors 10 – 15 µm is normal. For bathrooms, coastal homes or heavy use, specify 15 – 20 µm. Thicker films improve wear and corrosion resistance but slightly darken coloured finishes.

Can aluminium handle profiles be bent into curves?

Yes. Order the profile in a soft temper such as T4, bend it by press or CNC with a radius suited to the wall thickness, then age it if strength is needed. Finish after bending, otherwise the anodised or powder film can crack.

What is the difference between a J-pull and a G-profile handle?

A J-pull clips onto the top edge of the door and the fingers hook under its lip. A G-profile is fixed along the door edge and its curved section forms a recess behind the front. Both give a handle-free look; the choice depends on the cabinet system and board thickness.

Does Metline make finished cabinet handles?

No. Metline supplies the aluminium extrusion in mill finish or in finished, cut lengths for furniture makers and hardware companies, who fit, assemble and sell the handles.

Is aluminium or stainless steel better for kitchen handles?

Aluminium is lighter, cheaper for long profiles and available in many colours and integrated shapes. Stainless steel is harder and more scratch-resistant for solid bar handles. For handle-less fronts and colour-matched designs, aluminium is the usual choice.
