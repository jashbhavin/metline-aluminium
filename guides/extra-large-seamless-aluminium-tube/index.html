Extra-Large Seamless Aluminium Tube: Buyer's Guide

Extra-large seamless aluminium tube means hollow sections of roughly 200 mm to over 1,300 mm outside diameter, with walls from about 18 mm up to 200 mm or more, made without a longitudinal weld. They are produced by piercing and extruding a solid billet on very large vertical presses, then heat treated and machined. This guide explains how they are made, which alloys and standards apply, how to estimate weight, and what to put in an enquiry.

Overview

What counts as an extra-large seamless aluminium tube?

Most aluminium tube is produced on horizontal extrusion presses, which limits seamless round tube to a few hundred millimetres in diameter. Above that size, tube has traditionally been made by rolling plate and welding a seam, by forging and boring, or by ring rolling. Extra-large seamless tube fills the gap: a single hollow, with no weld, in diameters that once required fabricated pipe.

"Seamless" matters because a weld is a metallurgical discontinuity. In heat-treatable alloys such as 6061, 2024 and 7075, the heat-affected zone of a weld loses a large part of its strength, and in high-strength 2xxx and 7xxx alloys arc welding is generally not practical at all. A seamless tube keeps uniform properties around its circumference, which simplifies design for pressure, fatigue and bending, and avoids weld inspection and qualification.

Porthole (bridge-die) extrusions are sometimes sold as "tube", but they contain longitudinal seam welds formed inside the die. For pressure service, many codes and buyers specify seamless tube made from a hollow billet or by piercing, and standards such as ASTM B241 and EN 755-7 are written specifically for seamless product.

Why engineers specify large seamless tube

  • Uniform strength around the circumference, with no weld heat-affected zone
  • Access to high-strength 2xxx and 7xxx alloys that cannot be welded into pipe
  • Thick walls for pressure, impact or machining into flanged and stepped parts
  • A near-net hollow that saves material compared with boring a solid forging
Manufacturing route

How extra-large seamless tubes are produced

The industrial route for the largest sizes is vertical hot extrusion on hydraulic presses rated at several hundred meganewtons. The sequence below is typical; individual mills differ in detail.

  1. Billet preparationA large cast (or spray-formed) billet is homogenised, scalped to remove the cast skin and inspected ultrasonically. For aerospace alloys, spray-formed billets give a finer, more uniform structure than conventional direct-chill casting.
  2. HeatingThe billet is heated to the alloy's hot-working range, typically about 400 – 480 °C depending on grade, and transferred into the press container.
  3. PiercingA mandrel pierces the billet along its axis to create the bore, or a pre-bored hollow billet is used.
  4. Vertical extrusionThe ram forces the metal downward through an annular gap between the die and the mandrel, forming the tube in one stroke. Working vertically avoids the sagging and eccentricity that a very heavy hollow would suffer on a horizontal press.
  5. Heat treatmentHeat-treatable alloys are solution treated, quenched and aged in pit (well-type) furnaces where the tube hangs vertically, which keeps it straight and gives even heating along its length.
  6. Straightening and machiningTubes are straightened, then the outside is turned and the bore is bored or skived to the final size, removing extrusion surface and improving concentricity and roughness.
  7. InspectionDimensional, ultrasonic and eddy-current, hardness and metallographic checks, then preservation and packing.

Because tooling for each diameter is expensive, mills keep a library of die and mandrel sizes and produce the nearest standard outside and inside diameters, then machine to the customer's final dimensions. Asking for a size close to an existing tool set usually shortens the route and lowers cost; a supplier can tell you which nominal sizes are available.

Quality

Tolerances, machining and inspection

As-extruded large tube follows the tolerance tables of the product standard, which are relatively wide at these sizes. Where tighter control is needed, the outside is turned and the bore is bored to the drawing. Typical capabilities after machining include concentricity within about 0.5 mm, straightness of the order of 1 mm per metre and surface roughness of Ra 12.5 µm or better; confirm the figures that apply to your size before ordering.

Ultrasonic testing

Immersion or contact UT to detect internal discontinuities; aerospace orders typically reference AMS-STD-2154 class levels.

Eddy current

Surface and near-surface defect detection and conductivity checks, which also confirm temper on heat-treated tubes.

Dimensional checks

OD, ID, wall at several points around the circumference, ovality, straightness and length.

Mechanical tests

Tensile tests taken from the tube end in the direction the standard requires, plus hardness surveys.

Metallography

Grain size, recrystallised layer depth and absence of coarse grain bands, important for fatigue-critical parts.

Certification

EN 10204 3.1 mill certificate with heat number, chemistry and test results; third-party inspection on request.

Downstream work

Bending, joining and fittings

Large seamless tubes are often only the starting stock. Common downstream operations are:

  • Machining: flanges, steps, threads and grooves are cut directly into the heavy wall, replacing welded assemblies or forged rings.
  • Bending: induction or hot bending to large radii, usually in the annealed or as-extruded condition followed by heat treatment.
  • Welding: 5083 and 6061 tubes are welded with 5183 or 5356 and 4043 or 5356 fillers respectively; 2024 and 7075 tubes are joined mechanically.
  • Fittings: elbows, tees, reducers and flanges in matching alloys complete a piping run; see forged pipe and fittings.
  • Surface protection: anodising, conversion coating or painting, especially for 2024 and 7075, which need protection in corrosive service.
Design considerations

Design points for large aluminium tubes

Large aluminium tubes are often specified as replacements for steel pipe or forgings. The density is about one third of steel, but so is the elastic modulus (about 69 – 72 GPa against 200 GPa), and that changes how the part should be designed.

Pressure rating

Wall thickness for internal pressure is set by the design code, starting from the hoop-stress relation P = 2 S t / D, where S is the code allowable stress for the alloy, temper and temperature. Use the welded allowable stress for any part of the line that will be welded, even if the tube itself is seamless.

Stiffness and buckling

With a lower modulus, an aluminium tube deflects about three times as much as a steel tube of the same size. Large diameter-to-wall ratios also raise the risk of local buckling in compression and bending, so check both strength and stability.

Thermal expansion

Aluminium expands about twice as much as steel (roughly 23 µm/m·°C). Long runs need expansion loops or joints, and mixed aluminium-steel assemblies need allowance for differential movement.

Temperature limits

Heat-treated alloys lose strength with long exposure above about 100 – 150 °C. In contrast, 5083 and 6061 keep or improve their toughness at cryogenic temperatures, which is why they are used for liquefied gas service.

Galvanic contact

Isolate aluminium from steel, stainless and copper alloys in wet or marine service with insulating gaskets, sleeves and washers, or coat the more noble metal.

Thick-section properties

Very heavy walls cool more slowly during quenching, so minimum properties in 6xxx, 2xxx and 7xxx alloys can fall with increasing thickness. Agree test locations and acceptance values before production.

Common mistakes in large-tube enquiries

  • Giving only nominal pipe size without the finished OD, ID and tolerance
  • Specifying a 7xxx alloy and then planning to weld the tube into an assembly
  • Ignoring the machining allowance between the production size and the finished part
  • Using design allowables for thin sections on a tube with a 150 mm wall

FAQ

Extra-large seamless aluminium (aluminum) tube: FAQ

What is the largest seamless aluminium tube available?

Vertical extrusion presses in the industry produce seamless tubes up to about 1,320 mm outside diameter, with walls up to roughly 220 mm and lengths up to about 12 m. Availability of a given diameter and wall depends on existing tooling.

What is the difference between seamless and porthole aluminium tube?

Seamless tube is extruded from a hollow or pierced billet over a mandrel, so it has no internal weld lines. Porthole tube is formed in a bridge die where metal streams re-join, leaving longitudinal seam welds. Pressure and high-integrity applications usually specify seamless.

Can 7075 large-diameter tube be welded?

Not by normal arc welding: 7075 is crack-sensitive and loses much of its strength. 7075 tubes are machined and joined mechanically. For welded large tube, use 6061, 6082 or 5083.

How do I calculate the weight of a large aluminium tube?

Weight in kg/m = π × (OD − wall) × wall × density ÷ 1,000, with dimensions in mm and density in g/cm³. A 610 × 55 mm tube in 6061 weighs about 259 kg per metre.

Are large seamless tubes supplied machined?

They can be supplied as extruded or with the outside turned and bore machined to drawing. Machining removes the extrusion surface and improves concentricity, roughness and straightness.

Which standard should I order to?

ASTM B241 is the usual standard for seamless pipe and tube in pressure and general service, EN 755 for European projects, and AMS specifications for aerospace. Many orders combine a standard for material properties with a drawing for finished dimensions.

Why are spray-formed billets used for some large tubes?

Spray forming deposits atomised molten alloy onto a collector to build a billet with a very fine, uniform grain structure and little segregation. That allows highly alloyed grades such as 7034 and 7055, and some aluminium-lithium alloys, to be extruded into large sections with better toughness and fatigue performance than conventionally cast billets would give.

Do you ship large tubes internationally?

Yes. Metline ships worldwide on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms, with capped ends, dried bores, wrapped surfaces and timber cradles to protect the tubes in transit.
