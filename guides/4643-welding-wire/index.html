ER4643 / R4643 Aluminium Welding Wire

ER4643 is an aluminium-silicon-magnesium filler metal classified under AWS A5.10, made for 6xxx-series weldments that will be solution heat-treated and aged after welding. Its small magnesium addition lets the weld metal form Mg2Si and harden with the parent metal, which matters most in thick sections where the base metal adds too little magnesium to an ER4043 weld. Metline supplies ER4643 MIG wire on spools and R4643 TIG rod in straight lengths, with lot certificates, worldwide.

Overview

What is ER4643 filler metal?

ER4643 is best understood as a modified ER4043. The silicon content is cut from about 5 % to about 4 %, and 0.10 – 0.30 % magnesium is added. That combination was designed so that the weld metal behaves roughly like a mixture of four parts 4043 and one part 6061, which is the blend you would get naturally if enough base metal melted into the weld pool.

The reason this matters is heat treatment. A 6061 or 6082 structure is often welded in the T4 or T6 condition and then given a full solution heat treatment, quench and artificial ageing so the whole assembly, welds included, returns close to T6 strength. Weld metal can only respond to that treatment if it contains both silicon and magnesium. With ER4043, almost all of the magnesium has to come from dilution, that is, from parent metal melted into the pool.

In thin sheet and small fillets, dilution is high and ER4043 welds pick up enough magnesium. In heavy butt welds, typically above about 10 mm (3/8 in), the centre of the joint is mostly filler metal, so the weld stays soft after heat treatment even though the plate either side hardens. ER4643 supplies the missing magnesium from the wire itself, so the weld centre ages along with the parent metal.

When ER4643 is the right choice

  • The weldment is 6xxx (6061, 6063, 6082, 6005A, 6351) and will be solution heat-treated and aged after welding
  • Joint sections are heavy or multipass, so base-metal dilution in the weld centre is low
  • A silicon-type filler is preferred for its fluidity and resistance to hot cracking
  • Not needed if the part stays as-welded: without post-weld heat treatment ER4643 offers no real gain over ER4043

As-welded, an ER4643 joint in 6061-T6 is governed by the softened heat-affected zone, just as an ER4043 joint is, and design codes such as AWS D1.2 give the same reduced allowable strengths for both. The advantage only appears after a full post-weld solution heat treatment and ageing, when the weld centre can approach the strength of the parent metal instead of remaining a weak link.

Ageing alone (for example welding T4 material and then artificially ageing to T6) helps the heat-affected zone recover somewhat but does not fully harden the fusion zone. For predictable results the procedure should include solution treatment, rapid quench and ageing, qualified on test coupons before production.

A simple rule: if the assembly goes through a solution heat-treatment furnace after welding, consider ER4643; if it stays as-welded and needs more strength than ER4043, look at ER4943; if the base metal is a 5xxx alloy, use ER5356 or ER5183.

Applications

Where ER4643 is used

Heat-treated structural frames

Thick 6061 or 6082 frames, platforms and machine bases that go through a solution furnace after welding so welds and plate share the same temper.

Bicycle and sports frames

Many 6061 frames are welded in T4, then solution-treated and aged. ER4643 helps the joints harden fully along with the tubes.

Rail and vehicle structures

Selected 6xxx assemblies that are re-heat-treated, where the fusion zone must not limit fatigue or static strength.

Heavy-section repair

Rebuilding thick 6xxx parts that will be heat-treated afterwards, such as tooling plates and large fittings.

Castings to wrought alloys

Some A356 or 357 castings welded to 6xxx parts that are heat-treated as an assembly.

Pressure and process equipment

Where codes call for an Al-Si filler and heat treatment is specified in the procedure.

Handling

Storage, preparation and common mistakes

  • Keep it dry: store spools sealed, at stable temperature, and return part-used spools to the bag. Hydrated oxide on wire is a common cause of porosity.
  • Clean the joint: degrease first, then brush with a dedicated stainless brush just before welding.
  • Use the right feed kit: U-groove drive rolls, a polymer liner and contact tips sized for aluminium; a push-pull or spool gun for soft small-diameter wire.
  • Do not expect miracles as-welded: ER4643 only pays off when the procedure includes solution treatment and ageing.
  • Qualify the full sequence: test coupons should go through the same weld, solution treatment, quench and ageing as the production part.
  • Watch distortion: quenching welded assemblies can move them; plan fixtures or straightening.

FAQ

ER4643 aluminium (aluminum) welding wire: FAQs

What is the difference between ER4643 and ER4043?

ER4643 has less silicon (3.6 – 4.6 % against 4.5 – 6.0 %) and adds 0.10 – 0.30 % magnesium. The magnesium lets the weld metal harden during post-weld solution treatment and ageing, even in thick joints where little base metal mixes into the weld.

Is ER4643 worth using if I do not heat-treat after welding?

Usually not. As-welded, joint strength is controlled by the heat-affected zone and ER4643 performs much like ER4043. Choose ER4943 if you need higher as-welded strength from a silicon-type filler.

Can ER4643 be used on 6061 and 6063?

Yes. It was developed for 6xxx alloys such as 6061, 6063, 6082 and 6351, especially heavy sections that are solution heat-treated and aged after welding.

Does AMS 4189 still apply?

AMS 4189 was cancelled and replaced by the AWS A5.10 ER4643 classification. Older drawings that cite AMS 4189 are normally satisfied by ER4643 certified to AWS A5.10; confirm with your customer or inspector.

What is the difference between ER4643 and R4643?

The chemistry is identical. ER4643 is spooled wire used as the electrode in MIG welding; R4643 is straight cut rod fed by hand in TIG welding.

Will ER4643 welds match anodised parts?

No. Like other silicon-bearing fillers, ER4643 welds turn grey when anodised. If colour match matters and the joint stays as-welded, a 5xxx filler is the usual choice.
