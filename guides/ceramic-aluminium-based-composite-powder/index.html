Ceramic-Reinforced Aluminium Composite Powder for Additive Manufacturing

Ceramic aluminium-based composite powders are gas-atomised Al-Si-Mg alloy powders, typically AlSi7Mg or AlSi10Mg, containing fine titanium diboride (TiB2) or similar ceramic particles. In laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) and directed energy deposition (DED), the particles refine the grain, improve laser absorption and raise strength, stiffness and dimensional stability above standard AlSi10Mg. This guide explains the powder chemistry, particle size, quality limits, typical printed properties, process tips and safe handling.

Overview

Why add ceramic particles to aluminium printing powder?

AlSi10Mg is the workhorse aluminium alloy for laser powder bed fusion. It prints reliably because its near-eutectic silicon content resists solidification cracking, but its strength and stiffness are modest and it is sensitive to process settings. High-strength wrought alloys such as 6061 or 7075 crack badly when printed. Ceramic-reinforced powders aim to close that gap.

Fine TiB2 particles, usually well under a micron, act in three ways during printing. They provide nucleation sites that turn the columnar grains typical of laser melting into fine, equiaxed grains, which reduces anisotropy and cracking. They increase absorption of the laser beam, because aluminium itself reflects much of the infrared energy, so the melt pool becomes more stable and the process window wider. And once solidified, they strengthen and stiffen the metal by dispersion strengthening and load transfer.

The result, according to a growing body of published research, is printed material with tensile strength roughly 100 – 200 MPa higher than conventional AlSi10Mg, a modest increase in stiffness and, in well-optimised grades, useful ductility. Higher ceramic fractions push stiffness and strength further at the expense of elongation.

Benefits reported for TiB2-reinforced aluminium powders

  • Higher tensile and yield strength than standard AlSi10Mg
  • Higher elastic modulus and lower thermal expansion, improving dimensional stability
  • Fine, equiaxed microstructure with less anisotropy between build directions
  • Better laser absorption and a more forgiving process window
  • Improved wear and fatigue resistance in many studies
Chemical composition

Matrix alloys and ceramic content

Two matrix families dominate. A 7 % silicon matrix with relatively high magnesium (close to AlSi7Mg0.6 / A357) gives the best combination of strength and ductility after heat treatment. A 10 % silicon matrix (close to AlSi10Mg) prints very easily and offers lower thermal expansion and better thin-wall capability. Each is offered with a range of TiB2 contents.

Most TiB2-reinforced powders are pre-alloyed: the TiB2 is formed in the melt by reaction and the composite melt is then gas-atomised, so every powder particle contains reinforcement. This gives better uniformity than mechanically blending ceramic powder with aluminium powder, which can segregate during spreading.

Ask for a powder certificate per lot showing chemistry including TiB2 content, particle size distribution, O/N/H, flow and density, and SEM images of morphology. Consistency between lots matters more than any single value, because printing parameters are tuned to a powder.

Properties

Typical printed properties

Properties of printed parts depend on the machine, parameters, build orientation, wall thickness and post-processing as much as on the powder. The table gives indicative ranges from published research and supplier data for LPBF test bars, alongside standard AlSi10Mg for reference.

Heat treatment typically raises yield strength of the reinforced grades by a few tens of megapascals while elongation falls slightly, and the modulus is unchanged. Higher TiB2 content gives higher stiffness and strength, with lower elongation; the 10 % silicon matrix gives slightly higher stiffness and lower expansion than the 7 % matrix at the same TiB2 level.

Selection

Choosing matrix and TiB2 content

Low TiB2, 7 % Si matrix

Best ductility and toughness with a clear strength gain. A good first step for teams moving from AlSi10Mg, and for parts that see impact or assembly loads.

Low TiB2, 10 % Si matrix

Easy printing of thin walls and complex lattices, with better strength than plain AlSi10Mg.

High TiB2, 7 % Si matrix

High strength and stiffness for structural brackets, hinges and housings; elongation still useful after heat treatment.

High TiB2, 10 % Si matrix

Highest stiffness and lowest expansion, for precision and semiconductor equipment parts where dimensional stability dominates.

  • Match particle size to the process: fine cuts for LPBF, coarser for DED
  • Start from a validated parameter set for your machine, then optimise density and surface
  • Plan heat treatment (stress relief or a T6-type treatment) according to the required balance of strength and ductility
  • Design to the stiffness gain: thinner walls or lighter lattices can deliver the same rigidity
Processing

Printing and post-processing tips

  1. Store and dryKeep powder sealed under inert gas or dry air; dry before use if exposed, because moisture increases hydrogen porosity.
  2. Load and spreadCheck flow and spreading on the first layers; spherical, low-satellite powder gives even layers.
  3. PrintComposite powders absorb laser energy better than AlSi10Mg, so energy density usually needs adjustment; validate density by CT or sectioning.
  4. Stress relieveRelieve residual stress before removing parts from the plate to avoid distortion.
  5. Heat treat and machineApply the chosen heat treatment, then machine critical faces; PCD or fine-grain carbide tools suit the hard particles.
  6. Recycle powderSieve and blend used powder within agreed limits, tracking O and H content and particle size over reuse cycles.
Handling

Safe handling of aluminium powder

Fine aluminium powder is combustible and, as a dust cloud, can explode. Composite powders carry the same hazard. Handle them under the guidance of the safety data sheet and recognised standards such as NFPA 484 (combustible metals) and local ATEX or equivalent regulations.

  • Use conductive, earthed equipment and avoid sparks, open flames and static discharge
  • Collect spills with approved vacuum systems or by gentle sweeping; never use compressed air to clean
  • Use Class D extinguishing agents or dry sand for metal fires; never water
  • Wear respiratory protection and avoid inhaling fine powder
  • Store sealed containers in a cool, dry, ventilated area away from oxidisers and water
  • Manage wet-separator and filter waste as hazardous, reactive material
Applications

Where composite aluminium powders are used

Aerospace

Hinge arms, brackets, ducts and topology-optimised structural fittings that need forging-like strength in a printed part.

Semiconductor and precision equipment

Stiff, dimensionally stable frames and bonding-machine parts where high modulus and low expansion reduce vibration and drift.

Space and optics

Mirror mounts, instrument housings and lattices that must hold alignment through thermal cycles.

Automotive and motorsport

Lightweight suspension, powertrain and cooling components produced in low volumes.

Tooling and repair

DED cladding and repair of worn aluminium parts with a harder, wear-resistant deposit.

Lattices and heat exchangers

Thin-walled lattice structures that benefit from the fine microstructure and higher strength.

FAQ

Ceramic aluminium (aluminum) composite powder: FAQ

What is TiB2-reinforced aluminium powder?

A gas-atomised Al-Si-Mg alloy powder in which fine titanium diboride particles are distributed inside each powder particle. When printed, the TiB2 refines the grain and strengthens and stiffens the metal.

How much stronger is it than AlSi10Mg?

Published LPBF results typically show tensile strength roughly 100 – 200 MPa higher than standard AlSi10Mg, reaching about 450 – 550 MPa depending on TiB2 content, process and heat treatment.

Which particle size should I order?

15 – 45 µm or 15 – 53 µm for laser powder bed fusion, and 45 – 106 µm for directed energy deposition and cladding. Your machine maker's recommendation is a good starting point.

Can I use my AlSi10Mg parameters?

They are a starting point only. The composite absorbs laser energy better, so energy density usually needs adjusting. Validate density and properties with test coupons before production.

Can the powder be reused?

Yes, after sieving, within limits. Track oxygen, hydrogen and particle size over reuse cycles and blend with fresh powder as your quality plan requires.

Is the powder hazardous to ship and store?

Fine aluminium powder is combustible and is shipped as dangerous goods in sealed, inert-filled containers. Store it dry and follow the safety data sheet and NFPA 484 or local rules.

Moving beyond AlSi10Mg?

Send your machine, part and property targets. Metline will advise on composite powder options and documentation.
