Coffee Capsule Aluminium Foil

Single-serve coffee capsules use two different aluminium foils: a thicker, deep-drawable foil for the cup body and a thin, heat-seal-lacquered foil for the lid that the machine pierces. This guide explains which alloys, tempers, gauges and coatings suit each part, and what capsule makers should specify when they buy foil stock.

Overview

Why aluminium is used for coffee capsules

Ground coffee starts to stale within hours of grinding, because oxygen attacks the aromatic oils and moisture accelerates the process. A capsule has to hold a few grams of coffee for months, then survive brewing pressures of around 15–19 bar and temperatures above 90 °C. Aluminium foil does both jobs with a very thin wall.

Rolled aluminium foil above roughly 20 µm is effectively free of pinholes, so it forms an almost total barrier to oxygen, water vapour and light. That is why aluminium capsules can be flushed with nitrogen, sealed and stored without a secondary oxygen-barrier pack, whereas many plastic or compostable capsules need an outer bag. Aluminium also conducts heat well, deep-draws into thin-walled cups at high speed, and is fully recyclable without loss of quality.

The capsule maker does not buy finished foil off the shelf. Body and lid stock are usually ordered as lacquered or printed coils slit to the width of the press or lidding line, with the alloy, temper, gauge and coating system agreed in advance and confirmed by trials.

Two foils, two jobs

  • Body foil must deep-draw into a cup without cracking, wrinkling or earing, and carry an inner coating that tolerates hot water and coffee oils
  • Lid foil must heat-seal reliably to the body flange, keep oxygen out during storage and burst or be pierced cleanly in the machine
  • Both need food-contact compliant lacquers and tight gauge control so that high-speed lines run without stoppages
Alloy selection

Alloys used for capsule bodies and lids

Three alloy families cover almost every aluminium capsule on the market. The composition limits below are the registered Aluminum Association values; the mill certificate for each coil reports the actual analysis.

AlloyMain alloying elementsTypical use in capsulesWhy it is chosen
8011Fe 0.6 – 1.0 %, Si 0.5 – 0.9 %Body foil and lid foilIron-silicon particles give a fine grain and good strength-to-ductility balance for deep drawing; widely available
3003Mn 1.0 – 1.5 %, Cu 0.05 – 0.20 %Body foil for deeper or stiffer cupsHigher strength and dent resistance; holds shape on deep, complex forms
1235Al ≥ 99.35 %Lid foil, laminated lid structuresVery soft and ductile, low pinhole count at thin gauges, pierces cleanly
8079Fe 0.7 – 1.3 %, Si 0.05 – 0.30 %Lid foilHigher iron raises strength and puncture resistance of thin O-temper foil while keeping ductility

All capsule foils are normally supplied in the fully annealed O temper. Annealing removes the rolling oil and gives the high elongation needed for drawing or for a lid that must stretch slightly under brew pressure before it ruptures against the pyramid plate of the machine. Some lid designs use a light H-temper to stiffen the foil; this is agreed with the capsule designer.

Capsule body

Body foil: specification points that matter

The cup is formed in several stages on a multi-cavity press from lacquered coil. Defects in the foil appear immediately as split walls, wrinkled flanges or uneven height. Buyers should control the following:

PropertyTypical targetWhy it matters
GaugeAbout 0.08 – 0.12 mm (a common cup wall is near 0.10 – 0.11 mm)Thinner saves metal; too thin risks splitting and denting
Gauge toleranceCommonly ± 4 – 5 % or tighter by agreementUniform wall height and consistent press set-up
TemperO (annealed)Maximum elongation for deep drawing
EaringLow, agreed by cup testEaring creates uneven flanges that fail the seal
Inner coatingFood-contact lacquer, typically 3 – 6 g/m²Separates coffee from metal, resists hot water and oils
Outer coatingColour lacquer or print primer, sometimes embossedBrand colour, scuff resistance, machine recognition
SurfaceClean, dry, free of oil, pinholes and scratchesLacquer adhesion and visual quality

Earing is the waviness at the rim of a drawn cup caused by crystallographic texture in the foil. Mills control it through the rolling and annealing schedule; for capsule work it is worth asking for cup-test results on the first coils of a new supply.

Capsule lid

Lid foil: sealing, barrier and piercing

Lid foil is usually around 30 – 40 µm thick before embossing. It is coated on the inside with a heat-seal lacquer chosen to bond to the body material: aluminium cups use a lacquer compatible with the body inner coating, while plastic cups need a lacquer that bonds to polypropylene, polystyrene or PET. The outside is printed or colour-lacquered.

Seal strength

The lacquer must give a peel strength high enough to hold through filling, nitrogen flushing and transport, yet the seal must not leak under brew pressure. Seal temperature, dwell time and pressure are set on the lidding machine in trials.

Barrier

At 30 µm and above, rolled foil is essentially pinhole-free. The lid therefore keeps oxygen and moisture out as effectively as the body.

Piercing or rupture

Some systems pierce the lid with needles; others let it rupture against a patterned plate as pressure builds. Foil gauge, temper and embossing pattern control how cleanly and repeatably this happens.

Laminated lids

Some lids combine foil with a thin polymer layer for extra seal strength on plastic cups or to control tearing behaviour.

Embossing

A fine embossed pattern stiffens the lid, reduces wrinkles during sealing and helps the foil feed on high-speed lines.

Die-cut or reel-fed

Lids are either pre-cut and stacked, or cut and sealed in one step from a narrow coil. The coil format must suit the lidding line.

Coatings & food contact

Coatings and food-contact compliance

The lacquer system is as important as the metal. Inner coatings on body and lid are in direct contact with hot coffee, so they must comply with the food-contact rules of the market where the capsules are sold. In the European Union that means Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004 and good manufacturing practice under Regulation (EC) No 2023/2006; in the United States, coatings are typically formulated to FDA 21 CFR 175.300. The aluminium itself should meet the composition limits of EN 602 for food-contact semi-products.

  • Ask for a declaration of compliance for the coated foil, naming the lacquer system and the food simulants and conditions tested
  • Specify coating weight per side (g/m²) and the curing conditions, because under-cured lacquer can taint coffee or lose adhesion
  • Check that the heat-seal lacquer on the lid matches the body material and the coating on the body flange
  • Confirm sterilisation or hot-fill conditions if the capsule format involves them
  • For printed foil, check ink rub resistance and that no ink transfers to the inner side when coils are wound

Selection

How to select foil for a capsule project

  1. Define the capsule systemBody material (aluminium or plastic), capsule shape and depth, and how the machine opens the lid.
  2. Choose the body alloy and gauge8011-O suits most standard cups; move to 3003-O for deeper shapes or when dent resistance is a problem.
  3. Choose the lid alloy and gauge8011, 1235 or 8079 in O temper at about 30 – 40 µm, matched to the piercing or rupture mechanism.
  4. Agree the coating systemInner lacquer, heat-seal lacquer, outer colour or print, coating weights and food-contact documentation.
  5. Set the coil formatSlit width, coil outer diameter, core diameter (commonly 76 or 152 mm), winding direction and joint limits.
  6. Run trialsForming trials on the press and sealing trials on the lidding line, with peel and burst tests, before approving bulk supply.

A common mistake is to approve foil on a datasheet alone. Small differences in temper, lacquer cure or surface cleanliness can change forming and sealing behaviour, so a short trial run on the real tooling is essential.

FAQ

Coffee capsule aluminium (aluminum) foil: FAQ

What thickness of aluminium foil is used for coffee capsules?

Capsule bodies are usually drawn from about 0.08 – 0.12 mm foil, with many standard cups close to 0.10 – 0.11 mm. Lids are normally about 30 – 40 µm before embossing.

Which alloy is best for the capsule body?

8011-O is the most common choice because it deep-draws well and is widely available. 3003-O is used when the cup is deeper or needs more stiffness and dent resistance.

Can the same foil be used for body and lid?

The alloy can be the same (8011 is used for both), but gauge and coatings differ: the body needs a thicker foil with a forming-grade lacquer, while the lid needs thin foil with a heat-seal lacquer matched to the body.

Will an aluminium lid seal to a plastic capsule?

Yes, if the heat-seal lacquer is formulated for that polymer, such as polypropylene. The lacquer must be specified for the body material and confirmed in sealing trials.

Are aluminium capsules recyclable?

The aluminium is fully recyclable. In practice recovery depends on local collection schemes and on separating the used coffee grounds from the capsule.

What should I send for a quotation?

Alloy and temper, gauge and tolerance, coating system for each side, slit width, coil diameter and core, quantity and destination. Metline can supply samples for forming and sealing trials.

Sourcing foil for coffee capsules?

Send your body and lid specifications. We will reply with suitable alloys, coating options, samples and a delivered price.
