6061 Tooling Plate and Block for CNC Machining

For jigs, fixtures, mould plates and machined structures, 6061 is supplied as rolled T651 plate, forged T652 block or precision-milled blanks cut to size. Each route trades strength, flatness, internal soundness and cost differently. This guide explains how to choose between them, what surface and tolerance to ask for, and when cast tooling plate or another alloy is the better buy.

Overview

Why machinists choose 6061 for tooling

A good tooling material must cut quickly, hold size when material is removed, take a durable surface finish and be available in large sections at a sensible price. 6061-T651 meets all four: it machines at high spindle speeds with short chips, has low residual stress after stretching, anodises well and is rolled by mills worldwide.

It is not the hardest or most stable option. 7075-T651 is roughly twice as strong and is preferred for high-load mould inserts, while cast tooling plates are flatter and more stable but weaker. 6061 sits in the middle: strong enough for most fixtures and mould bases, stable enough for precision work, and economical in large sizes.

In short

  • Rolled 6061-T651 plate: the default for fixture plates and mould plates up to about 150 – 200 mm
  • Forged 6061-T652 block: for very thick, highly loaded or internally critical parts
  • Precision-milled blanks: ready-to-machine pieces that save set-up and clean-up time
  • Cast tooling plate (not 6061): when flatness and stability matter more than strength
Product forms

Plate, block and blank: the supply options

Rolled plate, T651

Rolled, solution treated, quenched, stretched 1.5 – 3 % and aged. Low residual stress and good flatness. Thickness typically 6 – 200 mm, widths to about 2500 mm.

Forged block, T652

Forged from ingot or billet, solution treated, compressed about 1 – 5 % to relieve stress, then aged. Dense, with a refined grain through the section; used for large mould blocks and highly stressed parts.

Saw-cut blocks

Cut from thick plate to your dimensions with a small allowance. The economical route for most fixture and mould work.

Precision-milled plate

One or both faces milled flat and parallel to a stated thickness, often with protective film. Goes straight onto the machine table.

Ground plate

Surface-ground for gauges, inspection bases and assembly plates that need very fine finish and flatness.

Six-side-milled blanks

Squared on all faces to close tolerances, so the first operation can start from a known datum.

Machining

Machining and finishing 6061 tooling stock

  • Use sharp carbide end mills with two or three flutes, high positive rake and polished flutes to avoid built-up edge
  • Run high spindle speeds with flood coolant or mist; 6061 tolerates aggressive feeds
  • Remove material evenly from both faces and finish after a relaxation pass to hold flatness
  • Use thread inserts in fixtures that are bolted and unbolted often; aluminium threads wear
  • Hard anodise (Type III) wear surfaces such as locating faces and slideways
  • Hold inspection at 20 °C; a 500 mm plate grows about 0.012 mm for every degree
Applications

Typical uses of 6061 tooling plate and block

  • CNC fixtures: sub-plates, tombstones, vice jaws and modular fixture bases
  • Moulds: prototype and short-run injection moulds, blow moulds, thermoforming tools and mould backing plates
  • Automation: robot end-effectors, gantry plates, test and inspection fixtures
  • Semiconductor and vacuum: chamber bodies, wafer-handling plates and frames
  • Machinery: machine beds, cover plates and structural brackets

FAQ

6061 tooling plate and block: frequently asked questions

Is 6061 tooling plate the same as cast tooling plate?

No. 6061 is a wrought alloy supplied as rolled plate or forged block. Cast tooling plates are made from different casting alloys, are flatter and more stable, but have roughly half the strength and do not anodise as evenly.

What is the difference between T651 and T652?

Both are solution treated and aged. T651 is stress-relieved by stretching and applies to rolled plate; T652 is stress-relieved by compression and applies mainly to forgings and blocks.

Is 6061 or 7075 better for moulds?

6061 is cheaper, anodises better and suits prototype and short-run moulds. 7075 is harder and stronger, giving longer life and better edge retention for production moulds.

Can you supply 6061 plate milled to thickness?

Yes. Plate and blocks can be supplied saw-cut, milled on one or both faces, ground, or six-side milled to your tolerances.

Do thick 6061 blocks need ultrasonic testing?

For moulds and critical tooling it is strongly recommended. Ask for testing to ASTM B594 or AMS-STD-2154 at the class your part requires.
