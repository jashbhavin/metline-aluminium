Aluminium Sandwich Wall Panels: Corrugated, Honeycomb and Insulated Cores

A sandwich wall panel is a flat or curved cladding element with two aluminium skins bonded to a core, used for ventilated facades, interior partitions, ceilings and modular buildings. For most wall work the choice is between a thin all-aluminium corrugated core panel, a stiffer honeycomb panel for large formats, and an insulated panel where the wall must also provide thermal resistance. This guide is written for facade fabricators, system companies and project buyers who need the panel material.

Overview

What is an aluminium sandwich wall panel?

In a wall panel, the sandwich construction does two jobs. It gives a large, flat, light panel that resists wind pressure between widely spaced rails, and it provides a durable coated face that defines the building’s appearance. The skins are usually 3003, 3105 or 5005 aluminium sheet with a PVDF or polyester coil coating; the core is aluminium (corrugated or honeycomb), mineral or an insulating foam.

Wall panels are typically part of a system: a sub-frame of aluminium rails and brackets, panel trays or cassettes with folded edges, gaskets or open joints, and a drained and ventilated cavity in front of the insulation and the structural wall. Metline supplies the aluminium materials for these systems, including the panels, coated skin coil and the extruded rails and edge profiles; the facade contractor designs, fabricates and installs the finished wall.

Why fabricators choose all-aluminium corrugated wall panels

  • No polymer core, so non-combustible classifications such as A2-s1,d0 are achievable
  • Thin (often 4 – 6 mm) yet stiff enough for large cassette panels
  • The core resists collapse when routed, folded and roll-curved
  • Fully recyclable metal at end of life
Corrugated composite panel

Aluminium corrugated composite wall panels

The corrugated core is a thin aluminium sheet formed into a wave, trapezoid or a three-dimensional cone pattern, then bonded between the two skins with a thermosetting adhesive under heat and pressure. A cone-shaped (3D) core supports the skins at points in both directions, so it behaves more uniformly than a single-direction wave and resists denting.

Because the core is metal, the panel can be routed on the back skin and folded into trays, curved on a three-roll bender and drilled for fixings without the core crushing as readily as a honeycomb. That makes it a good fit for curved corners, column cladding and soffits. Its main limitation is span: a 4 – 6 mm panel needs closer supports or stiffeners than a 20 mm honeycomb panel in high wind zones.

Honeycomb wall panel

Aluminium honeycomb sandwich wall panels

Honeycomb wall panels use an expanded aluminium honeycomb (usually 3003) between skins of about 1.0 – 1.5 mm, with total thicknesses of 10 – 25 mm. They are the choice for very large formats, for high-wind elevations and where perfect flatness matters, for example in lobbies, airports and large public buildings.

Honeycomb panels are stiffer per kilogram than corrugated panels but are more costly to produce, involve more adhesive area, and need edge closure to prevent moisture entering open cells. Shaping beyond gentle curves is harder, so complex geometry tends to favour corrugated or folded solid aluminium sheet. See our honeycomb sandwich panel guide for core grades.

Insulated sandwich panel

Insulated sandwich panels with aluminium faces

Insulated panels combine thin metal faces with a thick core of polyurethane (PUR/PIR), mineral wool or similar, typically 40 – 200 mm. They are factory-made to EN 14509 and act as the whole wall build-up in cold stores, cleanrooms, industrial buildings and modular units. Aluminium faces are chosen over steel for corrosion resistance in food, coastal and chemical environments.

Fire performance depends strongly on the core: mineral wool cores can achieve A2-s1,d0, while foam cores are combustible and their classification depends on the complete panel. Insulated panels are not a substitute for a rainscreen panel on a high-rise facade; check the building code requirements for each wall type.

Corrugated and honeycomb panels both reach the same fire class; the choice between them is mainly about span, flatness, shaping and cost. For a standard rainscreen with cassettes up to about 1.5 × 3 m and supports at normal spacing, corrugated panels usually do the job at lower cost. For very large panels, high wind loads or premium flatness, honeycomb is safer.

Applications

Where sandwich wall panels are used

Ventilated facades

Rainscreen cladding on concrete or steel-framed buildings, hung on aluminium rails with a drained cavity. Panels are formed into cassettes with hook-on or riveted fixings.

Interior wall systems

Partitions and feature walls in offices, hotels, hospitals and transport hubs, often on keel-slot or modular frames. Panels resist impact and are easy to clean.

Ceilings and soffits

Lightweight panels on suspension systems for public spaces, canopies and walkways, including curved and perforated designs.

Cleanrooms and healthcare

Smooth, non-porous, easy-to-clean walls for operating theatres, laboratories and pharmaceutical areas.

Modular and prefabricated buildings

Factory-finished wall panels for modular units, kiosks and site buildings, installed dry.

Transport hubs

Airports, metro and rail stations, where impact resistance, fire class and long-term appearance are critical.

Design

Design, fabrication and fixing guidance

  • Wind load: size panel thickness and support spacing to the project wind pressure; ask for tested load-span data for the chosen build-up.
  • Thermal movement: allow about 2.4 mm per metre per 100 °C; use slotted holes or sliding cassette hooks and joints of roughly 10 – 20 mm.
  • Folding: rout the back skin with the correct V or U groove for the panel thickness, and fold once; reworking cracks the face coating.
  • Edges: seal or close cut edges on honeycomb panels exposed to weather.
  • Fixings: use aluminium or stainless fasteners with isolating washers; avoid direct contact with copper or untreated steel.
  • Colour consistency: order each elevation from one coating batch and keep panel direction arrows consistent on metallic finishes.
  • Protective film: remove within the period stated by the coater after installation to avoid adhesive residue.

FAQ

Sandwich wall panels: frequently asked questions

Can an aluminium corrugated panel replace a honeycomb panel?

In most standard rainscreen and interior applications, yes: both reach A2-s1,d0 and the corrugated panel is thinner and usually cheaper. For very large panels, high wind loads or premium flatness, honeycomb remains the safer choice because of its higher stiffness.

What thickness of sandwich wall panel do I need?

Interior walls commonly use 3 – 4 mm corrugated panels; exterior cassettes 4 – 6 mm corrugated or 10 – 20 mm honeycomb. The final thickness depends on wind load, panel size and support spacing, so check against the manufacturer’s load-span data.

Are aluminum sandwich wall panels non-combustible?

All-aluminium corrugated and honeycomb panels typically achieve A2-s1,d0 to EN 13501-1. Panels with PE cores do not; FR cores are usually B-s1,d0. The classification report for the exact product and fixing system is what counts.

Which finishes are available?

PVDF and polyester coil coatings in RAL and custom colours, anodised, brushed, mirror, and printed wood or stone grain finishes. PVDF is recommended for exterior facades.

Does Metline install wall panel systems?

No. Metline supplies the aluminium materials: sandwich panels, coated skin coil and the extruded rails and profiles used in wall systems. Design, fabrication and installation are carried out by the facade contractor or system company.
