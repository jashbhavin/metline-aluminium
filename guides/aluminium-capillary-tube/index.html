Aluminium Capillary Tube

Aluminium capillary tube is cold-drawn seamless tubing with an outside diameter of roughly 0.5 to 10 mm and a bore that can be well under 1 mm. It is made mainly from 1050, 1060, 1070 and 3003 for fluid and refrigerant lines and from 6063 where a little more strength is needed. This guide covers alloys, sizes, standards, manufacturing, applications and how aluminium compares with copper.

Overview

What is an aluminium capillary tube?

A capillary tube is a tube whose bore is small enough that its length and diameter, rather than a valve, control how much fluid passes and how much pressure is lost. The term is also used more loosely for any very fine, thin-walled tube. In aluminium, capillary tubes are usually produced by extruding a hollow shell and then cold drawing it through dies, with annealing between passes, until the tube reaches its final size.

Aluminium brings low weight, good thermal conductivity, resistance to corrosion in clean environments and easy bending and flaring. Its main limitations against copper are lower strength at the same wall thickness, more difficult brazing and soldering, and the need to avoid direct contact with copper in damp conditions. These points decide where aluminium capillary tubing is a good choice and where copper remains the default.

Between ultra-fine capillary sizes and ordinary small tube there is a grey zone. Tubes from about 4.8 mm up to 20 mm outside diameter are often called small aluminium tubes rather than capillaries; they use the same alloys and drawing route but need less precise bore control.

Typical reasons to choose aluminium capillary tube

  • All-aluminium refrigerant circuits, avoiding copper-to-aluminium joints and galvanic corrosion
  • Lower weight in vehicles, portable equipment and instruments
  • Good heat transfer where the capillary also exchanges heat with a suction line or a plate
  • Easy forming into coils, bends and spirals
  • Fully recyclable material with no plating needed for corrosion protection indoors

For refrigerant and fluid service the soft 1xxx and 3003 alloys dominate, because the tube is bent and coiled during assembly and must not crack. 6063 is chosen when the tube is a mechanical part rather than a fluid line. Hard tempers (H14, H18) keep straight lengths stiff and straight; the annealed O temper is supplied for coils and for tubes that will be bent, flared or swaged.

Standard tolerance tables were written for ordinary tube sizes, so capillary tubing is usually ordered against a drawing that sets the limits for outside diameter, bore or wall thickness, ovality and straightness. Tell the supplier which dimension controls function: for a flow restrictor it is the bore; for a tube that is pushed into a fitting it is the outside diameter. Holding both very tightly at once raises cost.

How it is made

How aluminium capillary tube is manufactured

  1. Billet and extrusionA homogenised billet of the chosen alloy is extruded into a seamless hollow shell, or a coiled tube is produced by continuous extrusion.
  2. Cold drawingThe shell is drawn through a sequence of dies, often over a mandrel or plug, reducing diameter and wall step by step.
  3. Intermediate annealingDrawing work-hardens the metal; annealing between passes restores ductility so drawing can continue without cracking.
  4. Final temperThe tube is left hard-drawn (H tempers), fully annealed (O) or, for 6063, solution treated and aged.
  5. Cleaning and inspectionInternal cleaning removes drawing lubricant and particles, which matters for refrigerant and analytical lines. Diameter, wall and length are checked; eddy-current testing can detect wall defects.
  6. Cutting, coiling and packingTubes are cut to length or level-wound, end-capped to keep the bore clean and packed to prevent kinking.
Applications

Where aluminium capillary tubes are used

Refrigeration and air conditioning

Expansion capillaries and refrigerant lines in all-aluminium circuits, sometimes bonded to an aluminium suction line to form a heat exchanger. Copper remains common because it is easier to braze and draw to very tight bores, so aluminium is chosen where the whole circuit is aluminium.

Automotive and battery cooling

Small coolant and refrigerant lines, sensor tubes and thermal-management parts where weight counts.

Instrumentation

Pressure-sensing lines, thermostat and thermometer capillaries, pitot and sampling tubes.

Laboratory and analytical equipment

Low-pressure gas and fluid transfer lines where a clean, non-magnetic, light tube is needed.

Electronics and heat transfer

Small heat pipes, cold-plate tubes and cooling loops embedded in plates.

Mechanical and decorative parts

Antenna elements, pins, sleeves, guide tubes and model-making components, usually in 6063.

Aluminium versus copper capillary tube

PropertyAluminium (1060 / 3003)Copper (C12200)
Density2.70 g/cm³8.94 g/cm³
Thermal conductivityAbout 190 – 235 W/m·KAbout 340 W/m·K
Strength at equal wallLowerHigher
Brazing and solderingNeeds aluminium brazing alloys and fluxes; narrower process windowEasy with standard brazing alloys
CorrosionGood in clean, dry environments; vulnerable when in contact with copper and moistureGood, though formicary (ant-nest) corrosion can occur in some conditions
Tight bore tolerancesAchievable but more demandingLong-established for restrictor service (ASTM B360)

A practical rule: if the rest of the circuit is aluminium, use an aluminium capillary and avoid the copper-aluminium joint altogether. If the circuit is copper, keeping a copper capillary is usually simpler. Mixed joints need a proper transition fitting or a sealed, moisture-protected joint.

Procurement

How to specify aluminium capillary tube

  1. Alloy and temperFor example 3003-O for coiled refrigerant capillary, or 1070-H14 for straight instrument tube.
  2. Controlling dimensionsOD and wall, or OD and bore, with the tolerance on the dimension that matters most.
  3. Length or coilCut length with tolerance, or coil weight and inner diameter of the coil.
  4. CleanlinessState internal cleanliness and end-capping needs for refrigerant or analytical use.
  5. Testing and documentsEN 10204 3.1 certificate, plus eddy-current, leak or burst testing if required.
  6. Packing and termsProtective packing against kinking and delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP).

Common mistakes

  • Specifying tight tolerances on OD, bore and wall all at once when only one controls function
  • Ordering hard temper tube that must be bent tightly during assembly
  • Joining aluminium capillaries directly to copper without moisture protection
  • Forgetting internal cleanliness requirements for refrigerant circuits

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) capillary tube: frequently asked questions

What is the smallest aluminium capillary tube available?

Drawn aluminium capillary tube can be made with outside diameters around 0.5 mm and bores of about 0.3 mm. Whether a particular combination is possible depends on the alloy and the tolerance required, so confirm with a drawing.

Can aluminium replace copper capillary tube in a refrigerator?

It can in an all-aluminium circuit, and some appliance designs use an aluminium capillary bonded to an aluminium suction line. In a copper circuit it is usually simpler to keep copper, because copper-aluminium joints need careful design to avoid galvanic corrosion.

Which alloy is best for bending and coiling capillary tube?

1050, 1060 or 3003 in the annealed O temper. They bend to tight radii without cracking or collapsing; 3003 is a little stronger.

Are aluminium capillary tubes seamless?

Precision capillary tube is normally seamless, made by extrusion followed by cold drawing. Welded tube is used for larger, less critical sizes.

How is aluminium capillary tube supplied?

In straight cut lengths, often end-capped, or in level-wound coils for long continuous runs. Cut pieces with deburred, flared or swaged ends are also possible.

What certificate comes with the tube?

An EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate with chemical composition and, where specified, mechanical and dimensional results for the lot.
