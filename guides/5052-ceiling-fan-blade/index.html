5052 H12 / H22 / H32 Aluminium Sheet for Industrial Ceiling Fan Blades

5052 aluminium sheet is a standard material for roll-formed and press-bent blades on industrial and high-volume low-speed (HVLS) ceiling fans. It combines about 210 – 260 MPa tensile strength in H22 and H32 with a fatigue strength higher than 6061-T6, good formability for airfoil bends and excellent resistance to humid, dusty and mildly corrosive factory air. Metline supplies 5052 sheet and coil cut to blade lengths for fan makers worldwide.

Overview

What a ceiling fan blade asks of its material

An industrial ceiling fan blade is a long cantilever, often 1 – 3.5 m from hub to tip on HVLS fans, rotating for thousands of hours a year. Each revolution flexes it slightly under gravity and aerodynamic load, so the governing property is not static strength but resistance to fatigue, together with low mass to keep hub loads and motor power down.

5052 fits that profile well. Among the common non-heat-treatable alloys it has one of the highest fatigue strengths: about 117 MPa at 5 × 108 cycles in H32, compared with roughly 97 MPa for 6061-T6 and 69 MPa for 6063-T6 in published typical data. It gains its strength from magnesium and cold rolling rather than heat treatment, so bending the blade profile does not create soft heat-affected zones, and it keeps its properties without ageing.

Magnesium and chromium also give 5052 very good corrosion resistance in humid warehouses, livestock barns, food plants and coastal buildings, where fans often run in condensation, ammonia or salt-laden air. A light blade also matters for safety: less mass means lower energy if a blade ever comes loose, which is why fan designs combine light blades with mechanical retention and secondary safety cables.

Why fan makers choose 5052 sheet

  • High fatigue strength for long-life rotating blades
  • Bends cleanly into airfoil and stiffening-rib profiles in H22 and H32
  • Excellent corrosion resistance without coating, plus good powder-coat and anodise response
  • Uniform thickness and flatness help blade sets balance within tolerance

Manufacturing

Bent-sheet blades versus extruded blades

Industrial fan blades are made in two main ways. Sheet blades are roll-formed or press-brake bent from 5052 strip into a cambered airfoil, often with a folded leading edge, stiffening ribs or a closed hollow section riveted along its seam. Extruded blades are pushed through a die as a hollow airfoil profile, usually in 6063-T5/T6 or 6061-T6, and cut to length.

Engineering

Design and fabrication tips

  • Bend across the grain where possible, with inside radii of about 1 × thickness in H32 and slightly less in H22.
  • Avoid sharp notches at bolt holes and rivet lines near the root, where bending stress and fatigue are highest; deburr all holes.
  • Match blade weights within a set; consistent sheet thickness makes static and dynamic balancing much easier.
  • Isolate dissimilar metals at steel hubs with washers, sleeves or coatings to prevent galvanic corrosion in humid buildings.
  • Finish after forming: powder coat or anodise after bending to avoid cracking the film.
  • Validate fatigue: test complete blade assemblies, including fasteners, for the design life; fan air performance is usually rated to AMCA 230 and electrical safety to standards such as UL 507 or IEC 60335.

FAQ

5052 sheet for ceiling fan blades: FAQs

Which temper is best for bent fan blades?

H32 is the usual choice: stable properties and good bendability. H22 bends slightly more easily at the same tensile strength. H12 has higher yield but is less forgiving in tight bends.

Why not use 6061-T6 sheet for blades?

6061-T6 is stronger statically but less formable, and its fatigue strength is lower than 5052-H32. For bent-sheet blades 5052 is generally the better balance.

What thickness are industrial fan blades?

Most bent-sheet blades use about 1.5 – 3 mm, depending on blade length, chord and whether the section is closed or open.

Can 5052 blades be powder coated?

Yes. After pre-treatment 5052 takes powder coatings and anodising well. Coat after forming.

Do you supply sheet cut to blade size?

Yes. Sheet or slit coil can be cut to blade blanks, with protective film and export packing.
