Packaging alloy guide

5182 and 5052 Aluminium Sheet and Coil for Can Ends

Can end stock is thin, high-magnesium aluminium coil, usually 5182 and sometimes 5052, that is lacquered and pressed into the lids, easy-open ends and tabs of beverage and food cans. It must be strong enough to hold internal pressure at a very thin gauge, form a reliable score line and rivet, and carry food-contact coatings that survive retort and filling. This guide covers alloys, tempers, properties, coatings and tolerances; Metline supplies bare and coated end stock to can and closure makers worldwide.

Overview

Why can ends need a different alloy from bodies

The end of a beverage can is the most heavily engineered part of the package. It is flat, so it cannot rely on curvature for stiffness, yet it must resist internal pressures well above atmospheric in carbonated drinks, and in food cans it must survive retort sterilisation. It also carries an easy-open feature: a scored panel that must tear cleanly under a finger, but never leak, and a rivet that holds the tab.

These demands call for higher strength than the 3104 body alloy can give, so ends are made from 5182, an aluminium-magnesium alloy with about 4.5 % Mg. Magnesium in solid solution raises strength strongly when the alloy is cold-rolled, which lets end makers reduce gauge and metal weight while keeping buckle pressure.

End stock is almost always supplied pre-coated. The rolling mill or a coil coater applies the inside and outside lacquers and a wax lubricant, and the end maker presses ends straight from the coated coil. The coating bake changes the temper designation from H19 (bare) to H48 or H49 (coated), because the heat partly recovers the cold work.

Parts made from end stock

  • Beverage can ends (shells) and the converted easy-open end
  • Tabs (stay-on and ring-pull), often from separate tab stock
  • Food can easy-open ends (EOE) and sanitary ends
  • Aerosol cones and domes, closures and lids for specialty packaging

TemperMeaningTypical use
H19Extra-hard, cold-rolled, no thermal treatment after rollingBare end and tab stock before coating

The H4x designations are defined in EN 515 and ANSI H35.1 for strain-hardened products that are subsequently heated during painting or lacquering. Because the bake lowers strength slightly, mills roll the bare strip a little harder than the target so that the coated coil meets the H48 window.

Coatings

Coatings and lubricant on coated end stock

The coating system protects the metal from the product inside, protects the outside during filling and handling, and lets the end be pressed without galling. Coatings must comply with the food-contact regulations of the market where the can is sold, such as the US FDA 21 CFR 175.300 and EU Regulation 1935/2004 frameworks.

  • Solvent resistance: coating withstands repeated solvent rubs (for example MEK double rubs) without exposing metal
  • Sterilisation: no blush, loss of gloss, peeling or flaking after retort, for example 121 °C for 30 minutes
  • Acid and sulphur resistance: inner coating unaffected after retort in acid or sulphur test solutions
  • Adhesion: cross-hatch adhesion at the top rating
  • Porosity: low enamel-rater readings on formed ends

Both alloys have excellent corrosion resistance under lacquer. For pressurised beverage cans 5182 is the norm. For the can body itself, see our 3104 and 3004 can body stock guide.

Quality

Surface and coil quality requirements

  • No serious blisters, missed coating, scratches, corrosion, oil spots, peeling, streaks, colour variation, roll marks or periodic defects
  • Burr height on slit edges kept very small, typically within about 0.04 mm
  • Clean, tight coil ends without loose wraps
  • Even wax film across the width
  • Tab stock without curl that would interfere with feeding

Typical lot tests include tensile properties, thickness profile, coating weight, coating cure (solvent rubs), adhesion, retort resistance and lubricant weight.

FAQ

Can end aluminium (aluminum) stock: FAQ

Why are can ends made from 5182 and not 3104?

The end is flat and must resist internal pressure at a thin gauge, so it needs more strength than 3104 gives. 5182 contains about 4.5 % magnesium, which makes it much stronger after cold rolling.

What does H48 mean on coated end stock?

H48 is a full-hard strain-hardened temper that has then been heated during lacquering. The bake slightly softens the metal, so the mill rolls it harder to land in the H48 strength window after coating.

When is 5052 used for can lids?

For food can ends, closures and lids that are not highly pressurised, where its moderate strength is enough and good formability is useful. Pressurised beverage ends use 5182.

What inside coating is used on can ends?

Traditionally epoxy-based lacquers, now often replaced by BPA-free polyester, acrylic or vinyl systems. The coating is chosen for the contents and must comply with the food-contact rules of the destination market.

Can you supply bare H19 end stock for our own coil coating line?

Yes. Bare 5182-H19 and 5052-H19 coil can be supplied with a clean surface ready for pre-treatment and coating. Tell us the gauge, width, ID and target post-bake strength.

What thickness tolerance is typical?

About ± 0.005 mm for gauges up to 0.30 mm and ± 0.008 mm from 0.30 to 0.50 mm, or tighter by agreement.

Sourcing end and tab stock?

Send your specification, coating system and volumes. Metline will reply with matching 5182 or 5052 coil, test data and delivery options.
