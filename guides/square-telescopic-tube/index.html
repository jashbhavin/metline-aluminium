Telescoping tube design guide

Square Telescopic Aluminium Tube: Sizing, Fit and Alloy Selection

A square telescopic tube set is a series of square aluminium tubes in which each stage slides inside the next larger one, giving an adjustable length that cannot rotate. Whether the set slides smoothly, rattles or jams is decided by three numbers: the outside size step between stages, the wall thickness and the corner radius. This guide explains how to choose them, which alloys and locking methods suit which loads, and what to put in an enquiry.

Overview

What a square telescopic tube is, and why square

A telescopic (telescoping) tube is two or more tubes of decreasing size that nest inside one another. Pull the inner stage out and the assembly gets longer; push it in and it shortens. A pin, button, clamp or cam then fixes the chosen length. With a square section, the flat faces key the stages against each other, so the assembly cannot twist and holes on different stages stay lined up.

Round telescopic tubes slide and rotate freely, which is ideal for tripod legs, poles and handles that are locked by a twist collar. Square sets are chosen when the extended member must stay oriented: a sign post that has to face the road, a shelf upright with holes that must line up, a machine guard, a medical stand or a lift column carrying a bracket on one face. Square sections are also easier to drill accurately, to bolt to flat plates and to fit with standard angle brackets and connectors.

For a given outside size and wall, a square tube is also stiffer in bending than a round one. A thin-walled square of side b has a second moment of area of roughly (2/3)·b³·t, while a round tube of diameter b has about 0.39·b³·t. That is about 70 % more bending stiffness, for about 27 % more weight per metre. Torsional stiffness of a closed square section is also high, which is why square sets feel solid when a load is hung off one side.

What decides a good telescopic set

  • The clearance between the inside of one stage and the outside of the next
  • Matching corner radii, so the inner tube does not bind in the corners
  • Wall thickness that suits both the load and the size step
  • Straightness and twist tolerance over the full sliding length
  • A locking method chosen for the load, the vibration and how often it is adjusted
Fit

The nesting rule: outside size, wall and clearance

Every telescopic set obeys one relationship. The outside size of the next stage equals the inside size of the stage around it, minus the clearance you want:

ODnext = OD − 2 × t − c, where t is wall thickness and c is the total (two-sided) clearance across the flats.

Rearranged, it tells you the thickest wall a given size step allows: tmax = (step − c) ÷ 2. This single check catches the most common mistake in telescopic designs, which is choosing a standard wall and a standard size step that simply do not fit. A 1/4 in (6.35 mm) step with a 1/8 in (3.18 mm) wall, for example, leaves zero clearance: the next tube is exactly the same size as the hole it is meant to slide into.

Values are calculated from the nesting rule. Real tubes also carry size tolerances; check that the clearance stays positive with the largest inner tube inside the smallest outer tube.

How much clearance? A tight set (around 0.3 – 0.4 mm total on small sizes) slides with a precise, quiet feel but is sensitive to dents, powder-coat build-up and dirt. A looser set (0.8 – 1.5 mm) tolerates paint and site conditions but needs a positive lock, a clamp or plastic glide pads to stop play. Sets with large size steps commonly use thin UHMW-PE, nylon or acetal wear strips on the inner tube to take up the gap and give a low-friction, non-galling slide.

Corner geometry

Corner radius matching: why square sets jam

Corner radius causes more telescopic problems than any other dimension. The inside corner radius of a tube is roughly its outside radius minus the wall thickness. If the inner tube has a sharper outside corner than the inside corner of the outer tube, the inner tube touches in the corners before the flats, and the clearance you calculated across the flats never appears.

  • Rule: the outside corner radius of each inner stage should be equal to or larger than the inside corner radius of the stage around it.
  • Extruded tubes can be ordered with a specified corner radius on the die, which makes consistent sets straightforward.
  • Mixing sources (one stage from one supplier, the next from another) is a common cause of binding because corner radii differ even when flats match.
  • Check diagonally: measure the inner tube across corners and the outer tube's inside across corners, not only across the flats.
  • Twist matters too: a tube with even slight twist along its length can bind at full insertion even when a short sample slides freely.

Extruded aluminium square tube is normally supplied to the dimensional tolerances of EN 755-8 (porthole tubes) or ANSI H35.2 / ASTM B221, which cover size, wall, corner radius, straightness and twist. For telescopic use it is worth stating that stages must slide over the full length, and asking for sample lengths to be test-fitted before the full order is cut.

Material selection

Which aluminium alloy for telescopic tube?

Most telescopic sets are extruded from 6xxx alloys, which extrude accurately into thin walls with good surface quality and accept anodising. 7xxx alloys are used where weight is critical. A practical choice for most industrial sets is 6061-T6 or 6005A-T6 for loaded stages and 6063-T5/T6 for light, appearance-critical sets. Where the outer stage takes most of the bending and the inner stages are short, some designs mix alloys, but keep corner geometry and tolerances matched across the set. For alloy background see the 6061 square tube and 6063 square tube pages.

Locking methods

Locking methods compared

Spring button (snap button)

A spring-loaded button in the inner tube pops through holes in the outer tube. One-handed, fast and cheap; adjustment steps equal the hole pitch. Suits light loads and frequent adjustment.

Pin and hole

A through-pin, detent pin or gravity pin passes through aligned holes in both stages. Simple, positive and strong in shear; the standard for sign posts, scaffold-type uprights and outriggers.

Clamp collar or cam lever

A collar on the outer tube squeezes the inner tube at any position. Gives infinite adjustment without drilling the inner stage, but holding force depends on clamping and friction.

Knob and threaded pad

A threaded knob presses a pad against the inner tube through a nut on the outer stage. Stepless and cheap; best for light loads, since the pad can slip or mark the surface.

Plastic latch and glide cap

A moulded collar or latch on the end of the outer tube both locks and guides the inner tube, removing metal-to-metal contact and noise. Common in furniture and photo gear.

U-bolt or through-bolt

Bolts clamp or pass through both stages for semi-permanent settings in vibrating or heavy-duty structures.

Pre-drilled (perforated) square tubes, often with holes on all four faces at a regular pitch, turn a telescopic set into a modular system: the same tube accepts pins at any height and brackets on any face. When specifying perforated tube, state hole diameter, pitch, the faces to be drilled, and the position of the first hole from the end, so holes line up between stages. See also perforated aluminium tube.

Structural design

Overlap, bending and the load on each joint

The overlap is the length of the inner stage that stays inside the outer stage at full extension. When a telescopic member carries a side load, the joint acts like a short lever: the bending moment at the joint is resisted by two contact forces at either end of the overlap. Their size is roughly the moment divided by the overlap length, so halving the overlap doubles the bearing force on the tube walls.

  1. Set the overlapA common starting point is an overlap of at least 1.5 – 2 times the outside size of the inner tube for lightly loaded sets, and considerably more where the set is cantilevered under side load. Confirm by calculation for structural uses.
  2. Check bearing at the overlap endsDivide the joint moment by the overlap to get the contact force; check that the outer tube wall and any glide pad can carry it without denting.
  3. Check the pinFor pinned sets, check the pin in double shear and the hole in bearing in both tubes. Thin walls usually fail in hole bearing before the pin shears.
  4. Check the whole columnAn extended telescopic member is a stepped column. Check buckling with the smallest section governing, and remember that the play at each joint adds to deflection.
  5. Limit the stopAdd a mechanical stop so the inner stage cannot be pulled out past the minimum overlap.

This is general guidance. Scaffolds, access equipment, lifting devices and structures carrying people must be designed and verified to the applicable code by a qualified engineer.

Processing

Cutting, drilling, end work and surface finish

Telescopic sets are only as good as their machining. Cut ends square and deburr inside and out, because an internal burr on the outer tube catches the inner stage. Drill or punch holes on a jig so hole positions repeat between stages. Typical secondary operations include:

  • Cut to length with matched lengths for each stage of a set
  • Hole drilling and punching, single-face or through, at a fixed pitch
  • Slotting for clamp collars and spring-button travel
  • End work: flaring or swaging for a guide lip, end caps, plugs and feet
  • Anodising (typically 10 – 25 µm) gives a hard, low-friction surface and is thin enough not to upset fits
  • Powder coating adds roughly 60 – 120 µm per surface; allow for it in the clearance or mask the sliding faces

Anodising is usually the better finish for sliding stages because it adds little thickness and resists wear. Powder coating is tougher against impact and gives more colour options, but a coat on both sliding faces can consume a tight clearance entirely. A common compromise is to powder-coat the outer stage and anodise the inner stages.

Ordering

How to specify a square telescopic tube set

  1. Define the functionRetracted and extended length, number of stages, load and direction, how often it will be adjusted, indoor or outdoor use.
  2. Fix the geometryOutside size and wall of each stage, required clearance, corner radius, and length of each stage including minimum overlap.
  3. Choose alloy and finishFor example 6061-T6 or 6063-T6, anodised or powder-coated, with any masked faces.
  4. Add machiningHole diameter, pitch and faces; slots; end work; stops; and whether stages should be supplied matched as sets.
  5. Agree inspectionDimensional tolerances (EN 755-8 or ANSI H35.2), slide test over full length, mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1, and export packing.

FAQ

Square telescopic aluminum tube: frequently asked questions

How much clearance should a square telescopic tube have?

Total clearance across the flats (both sides together) is usually about 0.3 – 0.4 mm for small, precise sets and 0.8 – 1.5 mm for larger or painted sets. Tighter fits feel better but jam with dents, dirt or coating build-up; looser fits need glide pads or a positive lock.

How do I work out the size of the next stage?

Subtract twice the wall thickness and the clearance from the outer tube's outside size. A 50 × 1.5 mm outer tube has a 47 mm inside, so with 1 mm clearance the next stage is 46 mm.

Why does my inner tube bind even though the flats fit?

Usually the corner radius. If the inner tube's outside corners are sharper than the outer tube's inside corners, the tubes touch at the corners first. Twist or bow along the length is the other common cause.

Which alloy is best for telescopic square tube?

6063-T5/T6 gives the best surface and tolerances for light sets; 6061-T6 and 6005A-T6 are the usual choice for loaded posts and frames; 6082-T6 suits heavy columns; 7xxx alloys are used for very light outdoor and sports equipment.

Can telescopic tubes be powder coated?

Yes, but a typical powder coat adds about 60 – 120 µm per surface, which can use up a tight clearance. Anodise sliding stages, mask the sliding faces, or design the clearance to include the coating.

Can I buy only the inner or outer stage?

Yes. Send the dimensions of your existing stage, ideally including corner radius, and we will check the fit before supplying the matching aluminium tube.

Planning a telescopic tube set?

Send the stage sizes or the reach, load and number of stages. We will check the nesting fit and reply with aluminium tube options and delivery to your site.
