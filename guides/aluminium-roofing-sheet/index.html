Aluminium Roofing Sheet: Profiles, Gauges and Sizes

Aluminium roofing sheet is 3xxx or 5xxx series aluminium strip, usually 0.4 to 1.2 mm thick, roll-formed into corrugated, trapezoidal (box), standing-seam or tile profiles and finished in mill, PE or PVDF coating. It weighs about a third as much as steel sheet of the same thickness and does not rust at cut edges. This guide compares the profiles, gives gauge and weight tables, and explains how to specify the sheet and its base material.

Overview

What is aluminium roofing sheet?

A roofing sheet starts life as flat coil. A roll-forming line bends the strip through a series of roller stands until it has the ribs or corrugations that give it stiffness, then cuts it to length. The profile, not the metal thickness alone, is what lets a sheet under 1 mm thick span between purlins and carry wind and snow loads.

Aluminium sheet is formed from the same profiles used for coated steel, but the material behaves differently. It is lighter, it does not rust where it is cut or drilled, and it handles coastal salt and humid air well. Against that, its lower stiffness means an aluminium profile of the same shape and gauge spans a little less than steel, and its higher thermal expansion has to be catered for in the fixings. Designers therefore either choose a deeper profile, a thicker gauge or closer purlin spacing when switching from steel.

This page concentrates on the sheet itself: profiles, dimensions, gauges, weights and finishes. For the wider picture of every aluminium product used in a roof, including foil radiant barriers, panel face sheets and climate-based alloy selection, see the companion aluminium roofing material guide.

Specification summary

  • Base metal: 3105-H24/H26 or 3004-H34 for most painted roofs; 3003-H14 or 1100-H14 for light corrugated sheet; 5052-H32 for coastal and industrial exposure
  • Thickness: 0.4 – 0.7 mm for corrugated and tile profiles; 0.6 – 1.2 mm for long-span box and standing-seam profiles
  • Sheet length: cut to the roof slope length; long-span lines can produce single sheets of 12 m and more where transport allows
  • Finish: PE for moderate climates, PVDF for coastal, high-UV and architectural roofs
Profile types

Corrugated, trapezoidal, standing seam and tile profiles

Corrugated (sinusoidal)

The classic wave profile, commonly about 76 mm (3 in) pitch and 16 – 19 mm deep. Easy to curve and overlap, good for agricultural buildings, sheds, carports and curved roofs. Through-fixed at the crest.

Trapezoidal / box profile

Flat-topped ribs, typically 20 – 45 mm high at 100 – 200 mm pitch. Stiffer than corrugated, so it spans further and drains well on lower pitches. The standard for warehouses and factories.

Standing seam

Narrow pans, roughly 300 – 600 mm wide, joined by raised seams that are snapped or machine-folded together. Concealed clips let each pan expand freely, so it suits long single-piece runs and very low pitches.

Tile-effect (step tile)

Pressed steps and waves that imitate clay tiles, usually 0.4 – 0.7 mm thick in short sheets of 1 – 3 m. Used on houses, resorts and re-roofing where a traditional look is wanted at a fraction of the weight.

Pan or clip-lock

Flat-pan sheet with interlocking edges and concealed fixing, used for canopies, balconies and light commercial roofs where no exposed screws are wanted.

Curved and crimped sheet

Corrugated or box profiles crimp-curved or roll-curved for barrel roofs and canopies; aluminium curves more readily than steel at the same gauge.

Dimensions

Widths, cover widths and lengths

Two widths appear on every roofing sheet quotation. The input (uncoiled) width is the width of the flat coil fed into the roll-former. The effective cover width is the width of roof one sheet covers after forming and side-lapping. Deeper profiles consume more material, so the ratio between them drives the weight and the cost per square metre of roof.

Typical dimensional range

Coil input width
About 914, 1000, 1200 and 1250 mm for standard profiles; wider coil for special panels
Effective cover width
About 750 – 1050 mm for corrugated and box profiles; 300 – 600 mm for standing-seam pans
Sheet length
Cut to order, commonly 1 – 6 m for container shipment; longer sheets for site-formed or locally formed long-span roofs
Thickness
0.4 – 1.2 mm, with tolerance to EN 485-4 or ASTM B209 for the base coil
Flat sheet for trims
Coil-coated flat sheet, for example 1220 × 2440 mm, in the same colour for flashings and ridges

Sheets longer than about 11.8 m do not fit a standard 40 ft container. For very long single-piece roofs, coil is often shipped and formed near site on a mobile roll-former.

If you buy coil to form yourself, give the supplier the profile drawing or at least the input width and the forming line’s coil limits. If you buy formed sheet, specify the cover width and rib geometry, because two "box profiles" from different lines rarely interlock.

Temper matters as much as alloy. A hard H16 or H18 temper gives the stiffest sheet but can crack at tight seams and folds. Standing-seam pans and site-folded flashings are better served by H24 or H26, which have similar strength with better bendability. Remember that the paint-curing oven of a coil-coating line softens the strip slightly, so specify the temper required after coating.

Finish

Coatings and surface finishes for roofing sheet

Mill finish

Bare rolled aluminium. Durable, lowest cost; weathers to a dull grey. Common on farm buildings and industrial sheds.

Polyester (PE)

Coil-applied paint, typically about 18 – 25 µm. Good colour range and value for roofs in moderate climates.

SMP

Silicone-modified polyester with better UV and chalk resistance than standard PE.

PVDF

Fluoropolymer paint, typically 25 – 40 µm in two or three coats. Best colour and gloss retention for coastal, desert and architectural roofs; commonly tested to AAMA 2605.

Embossed

Stucco or orange-peel texture rolled into the strip. Reduces glare and hides minor handling marks and oil canning.

Anodised and laminated

Anodised sheet gives a metallic architectural look; film lamination adds scratch protection. Both are used more on flat architectural roofing than on roll-formed profiles.

Ask for a backer coat (a thin primer or polyester layer) on the underside of painted sheet that will sit over humid spaces; it protects the reverse face from condensation. Strippable protective film on the top face prevents scuffing during roll-forming and installation but should be removed soon after fixing, because film left in the sun can bond to the paint.

Installation principles

How aluminium roofing sheets are installed

Installation detail belongs to the roofing system supplier and the contractor, but buyers of the sheet should know the principles, because they affect what to order: fastener type, sheet length, sealant and trims.

  1. Check the structurePurlins or battens must be level, at the spacing the profile’s load table allows, and isolated from the aluminium if they are steel or treated timber.
  2. Start at the eavesLay the first sheet at the end away from the prevailing wind, so side laps face away from wind-driven rain, and work up the slope from eaves to ridge.
  3. Lap correctlySide-lap by at least one rib or corrugation, or as the profile design states; end laps on low pitches need sealant tape and a longer overlap.
  4. Fix through the crestThrough-fixed sheet is screwed through the crest of the rib, not the valley, with aluminium or stainless self-drilling screws and EPDM sealing washers. Do not overtighten; the washer should be compressed, not squeezed out.
  5. Allow for movementUse oversized pre-drilled holes or sliding clips on long sheets so thermal expansion does not elongate holes or buckle the sheet.
  6. Fit trims and clean upAdd ridge caps, barge flashings and closures, then sweep off drilling swarf the same day. Steel filings left on the roof rust and stain the coating.

Aluminium is not automatically quieter in rain than steel: drumming depends mainly on what is under the sheet. An insulated build-up or a solid deck reduces noise for any metal roof. Likewise, aluminium is fully compatible with solar mounting rails made from aluminium extrusions, but clamps and fasteners should still be stainless or aluminium and isolated from any copper earthing conductors.

Ordering

What to include in a roofing sheet enquiry

  • Formed sheet or flat coil for your own roll-former, with profile drawing or input width
  • Alloy and temper after coating (for example 3105-H24) and thickness with tolerance
  • Sheet lengths and quantities per length, or coil weight and ID for coil
  • Coating system, colour (RAL or sample), gloss, backer coat and protective film
  • Matching flat sheet or coil for flashings and trims
  • Standards: base metal to EN 485 / ASTM B209, coated strip to EN 1396, profile to EN 508-2 where required
  • Mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1, coating test report, export packing and delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF, DAP)

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) roofing sheet: frequently asked questions

What gauge of aluminium roofing sheet should I use?

For corrugated and tile-effect sheet on houses and sheds, 0.4 – 0.6 mm is common. For industrial box profiles 0.6 – 0.9 mm, and for long-span or standing-seam roofs in exposed locations 0.9 – 1.2 mm. Always confirm against the profile’s load-span table for your purlin spacing.

How much does an aluminium roofing sheet weigh?

Flat aluminium sheet weighs about 2.71 kg/m² per millimetre of thickness. A 0.5 mm sheet therefore weighs about 1.36 kg/m² flat, or about 1.8 kg per square metre of roof once corrugated, depending on the profile.

What is the difference between input width and cover width?

Input width is the flat coil width fed into the roll-former; cover width is the roof width one formed sheet covers after side-lapping. A 1000 mm coil might give a 762 mm cover in corrugated profile or about 900 mm in a shallow box profile.

Is aluminum roofing sheet better than steel?

It is lighter, does not rust at cut edges and lasts better near the sea. Steel is cheaper and stiffer, so it spans further at the same gauge. Aluminium is the better choice for coastal sites, re-roofing light structures and buildings where rust staining is unacceptable.

Can aluminium roofing sheets be used with solar panels?

Yes. Aluminium mounting rails sit well on aluminium roofs. Use aluminium or stainless clamps and fasteners with sealing washers, and keep copper earthing conductors from touching or draining onto the sheet.

Can I order coil instead of formed sheet?

Yes. Roof-sheet manufacturers usually buy coated or mill-finish coil slit to their roll-former’s input width. Give the width, thickness, coil ID, maximum coil weight and the paint side orientation.

Need aluminium roofing sheet or coil?

Send the profile or coil width, gauge, coating, colour and quantity. We will reply with the base material, certification and a delivered price.
