Aluminium Electrical Conduit

Aluminium electrical conduit is metal tubing, usually in 6063 alloy, that protects and routes electrical cables. It weighs about a third as much as steel conduit of the same size, does not rust, is non-magnetic and serves as a low-impedance earthing path. This guide covers rigid, EMT and flexible types, the standards behind them, size and weight tables, and where aluminium is and is not the right choice.

Overview

What is aluminium electrical conduit?

Conduit is the protective raceway through which building and industrial cables are pulled. Metal conduit shields the conductors from impact, crushing, moisture and fire spread, and in most systems it also acts as the equipment earthing (grounding) conductor. Aluminium conduit does all of this at a fraction of the weight of steel.

Rigid aluminium conduit has been used in North American industrial and commercial installations for decades and is recognised alongside galvanised steel in the US National Electrical Code (NEC) and the Canadian Electrical Code. It is specified heavily in petrochemical plants, wastewater works, food plants, coastal buildings and data centres, and increasingly on rooftop solar and EV-charging projects, where corrosion resistance and easy handling matter.

Metline supplies aluminium tube and pipe in conduit alloys and dimensions, couplings stock, bar for fittings, and related extrusions, to conduit manufacturers, fabricators and project buyers worldwide. Where a project requires UL-listed or CSA-certified conduit, the listing is held by the conduit manufacturer and must appear on the product marking; tell us the requirement and we confirm what can be supplied for that project.

Why engineers choose aluminium conduit

  • About one third the weight of steel conduit, so one installer can lift and fit lengths that would need two with steel
  • Naturally corrosion resistant: a self-repairing oxide film needs no galvanising or paint in most atmospheres
  • Non-magnetic: reduces inductive heating and voltage drop in AC circuits compared with steel conduit
  • Good conductivity makes the conduit a low-impedance equipment earthing path
  • Non-sparking and fully recyclable at end of life
Conduit types

Rigid, EMT and flexible aluminium conduit

Rigid aluminium conduit (ARC / RMC)

Heavy-wall, threaded conduit with the same outside diameter and wall as steel rigid conduit. Covered by ANSI C80.5 and UL 6A and installed under NEC Article 344. Supplied in 10 ft (3.05 m) lengths with tapered NPT threads and one coupling. Used outdoors, in industrial plants and in hazardous (classified) locations with suitable fittings.

Aluminium EMT (electrical metallic tubing)

Thin-wall, unthreaded tubing joined with set-screw or compression fittings. Covered by UL 797A and installed under NEC Article 358. Easy to bend by hand bender and cut on site. Used indoors and in dry or damp locations where heavy mechanical protection is not needed.

Flexible aluminium conduit

Helically wound, interlocked aluminium strip that bends freely, used for short connections to motors, transformers and vibrating equipment. Flexible metal conduit (FMC) is listed to UL 1 and installed under NEC Article 348; liquid-tight versions add a PVC or similar jacket.

Material

Alloys used for aluminium conduit

Conduit is made almost entirely from 6063, the architectural extrusion alloy, because it extrudes into seamless tube with a smooth bore, threads cleanly and resists corrosion well. Low copper content is important: copper reduces the corrosion resistance of aluminium, so conduit makers keep it to a minimum, and some describe their conduit as "marine grade" for this reason.

Rigid conduit is commonly supplied in the T1 temper (cooled from extrusion and naturally aged), which gives enough strength for threading and field bending while keeping the metal ductile. Smaller sizes are sometimes supplied in T5. EMT uses 6063 in a temper that allows tight field bends without cracking. The interlocked strip of flexible conduit uses a soft, formable aluminium alloy. Where a customer needs extra strength, for example for long unsupported spans or custom raceways, 6061-T6 tube is an alternative.

Composition limits are the registered Aluminum Association values for 6063 (ASTM B221 / EN 573-3 EN AW-6063).

In practice aluminium is the best choice for exposed runs in corrosive atmospheres, high-level and roof installations where weight and handling matter, and installations near sensitive equipment. Steel remains preferable where the conduit will be cast into concrete or buried without extra protection, or where the highest crush resistance is required. PVC dominates underground ducts and very wet locations but cannot serve as an earth path and needs expansion fittings on long runs.

Installation

Installation guidance for aluminium conduit

  • Cutting and threading: use sharp, fine-tooth blades and threading dies with cutting oil suited to aluminium; ream every cut end to remove burrs that could damage cable insulation
  • Bending: aluminium bends with standard benders but springs back slightly more than steel; use the bender maker’s aluminium settings and do not exceed the minimum radius for the trade size
  • Thread compound: apply an electrically conductive, anti-seize compound to threads to keep the earthing path reliable and prevent galling
  • Dissimilar metals: use aluminium, stainless steel or suitably coated fittings and straps; isolate aluminium from copper and bare steel in wet locations
  • Concrete and soil: wrap, coat or sleeve aluminium conduit where it enters concrete or the ground, as NEC 300.6 requires; chlorides and alkalis in fresh concrete attack aluminium
  • Expansion: on long straight outdoor runs, allow for expansion of about 2.3 mm per 10 m for each 10 °C change, using expansion fittings where needed
  • Supports: follow the support spacing in the electrical code for the trade size; aluminium conduit is listed with the same spacing rules as steel rigid conduit

Common mistakes

  • Burying aluminium conduit or casting it into concrete without supplementary corrosion protection
  • Using plain steel straps or fittings on coastal sites, creating galvanic corrosion at every support
  • Leaving cut ends unreamed, which damages cable jackets during pulling
  • Specifying EMT for areas exposed to severe mechanical damage, where rigid conduit is required

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) electrical conduit: frequently asked questions

Is aluminium conduit allowed by the electrical code?

Yes. The US National Electrical Code covers aluminium rigid metal conduit under Article 344 and aluminium EMT under Article 358, and the Canadian Electrical Code recognises it too. The product must be listed for the purpose, and aluminium in concrete or in contact with soil needs supplementary corrosion protection.

How much lighter is aluminum conduit than steel?

About one third the weight for the same trade size. A 2 in rigid aluminium conduit weighs roughly 1.8 kg per metre, compared with about 5.4 kg per metre for galvanised rigid steel.

Can aluminium conduit be used as the earthing conductor?

Yes. Rigid aluminium conduit and aluminium EMT are recognised as equipment grounding (earthing) conductors when installed with listed fittings. Aluminium’s good conductivity gives a low-impedance fault path; use conductive thread compound to keep joints reliable.

Can aluminium conduit be buried or cast in concrete?

Only with supplementary corrosion protection such as a factory coating, wrapping or sleeving, as required by NEC 300.6. Fresh concrete is alkaline and may contain chlorides, both of which attack bare aluminium.

Which alloy is used for aluminium conduit?

6063 is standard, typically in the T1 temper for rigid conduit, with low copper content for corrosion resistance. 6061-T6 tube can be used where extra strength is needed for custom raceways.

Does aluminium conduit reduce voltage drop?

Compared with steel conduit, yes. Because aluminium is non-magnetic, AC circuits in aluminium conduit have lower reactance and therefore lower voltage drop and less inductive heating of the raceway, which helps on long feeders.
