High-Reflectivity Aluminium Sheet for Lighting and Optical Use

High-reflectivity aluminium sheet is a bright-rolled, high-purity aluminium substrate with an anodised or vacuum-deposited (PVD) optical coating that reflects most of the light falling on it: roughly 86 – 90 % for anodised grades and up to about 98 % for PVD-enhanced grades. It is used for luminaire reflectors, LED backlights, daylighting systems, solar concentrators and UV curing lamps. This guide explains the coating types, how reflectance is measured and how to choose a grade; Metline supplies reflective sheet and coil worldwide, slit or cut to size.

Overview

How a high-reflectivity aluminium sheet works

Freshly deposited pure aluminium reflects about 91 – 92 % of visible light and keeps that performance well into the ultraviolet, which no other common metal does. A reflective sheet takes advantage of this, but it must also survive handling, forming, heat and humidity, so the bright metal is always protected by an oxide or a transparent coating.

A reflective sheet is built in layers. The base is a rolled aluminium strip, usually of high purity (99.7 – 99.9 % Al) so that few intermetallic particles break up the surface. The strip is rolled with polished work rolls to a mirror-bright finish. It is then either chemically or electrolytically brightened and anodised to give a thin, clear protective oxide, or it is coated in a vacuum line with a sequence of thin films: a reflective metal layer (high-purity aluminium or silver) followed by dielectric layers whose thicknesses are tuned so that reflected light waves reinforce one another.

The result is measured as total reflectance (all reflected light, whatever its direction) and specular reflectance (light reflected at the mirror angle). A luminaire that must shape a precise beam needs high specular reflectance; a diffuse light box or backlight needs high total reflectance with a matt or hammered surface.

What higher reflectance delivers

  • More light out of the luminaire for the same LEDs, or fewer LEDs for the same output
  • Lower heat load in the fitting, because less light is absorbed as heat
  • Better beam control with specular grades, smoother light with diffuse grades
  • Higher yield from solar concentrators and UV curing systems

Reflectance figures are typical values for flat, unformed material measured with an integrating sphere. Forming stretches the coating and can lower reflectance by a few percent; ask for data on the formed condition if your reflector is deep-drawn.

Ordinary anodised reflectors cannot go much above 90 %, because the anodic oxide both absorbs a little light and creates interference losses. PVD-enhanced grades get past this limit with optical multilayers. Silver-based layers give the highest visible reflectance but need careful sealing to prevent tarnish; aluminium-based enhanced layers are slightly lower in the visible range but more stable and far better in the ultraviolet.

Tempers range from soft (O) for deep drawing and spinning to H14 – H18 for flat or lightly bent parts. A harder temper keeps large panels flat and resists dents; a softer temper allows deeper forming without cracking the coating. Thickness is typically 0.25 – 3.0 mm, with 0.3 – 0.8 mm most common for luminaire reflectors, in widths up to about 1,250 mm.

Testing

How reflectance is measured and compared

Total reflectance is normally measured with an integrating-sphere photometer against a calibrated standard, following methods such as DIN 5036-3, and weighted to the sensitivity of the human eye. Solar reflectance uses a different, sun-weighted spectrum (for example ASTM E903), so a solar figure cannot be compared directly with a lighting figure.

  • Compare like with like: same standard, same weighting, same measurement geometry.
  • Ask for both total and specular (or diffuse) reflectance; two sheets with the same total reflectance can produce very different beams.
  • Check after forming and ageing: request data after a bend or stretch test and after humidity or temperature exposure if the fitting runs hot.
  • Check the colour: some coatings give a slightly warm or cool tint, which matters in architectural lighting.
Fabrication

Cutting, forming and handling reflective sheet

Cutting

Slit, sheared, laser or punched. Keep tools sharp and clean to avoid edge burrs and coating chips; leave the protective film on.

Forming

Bend and draw with polished or film-covered tools. Respect the minimum bend radius given for the grade, especially for PVD types.

Joining

Use rivets, clinching, tabs or adhesives. Welding destroys the optical surface locally.

Handling

Wear gloves; fingerprints and oils reduce reflectance and can etch the surface over time.

Temperature

Anodised grades tolerate typical luminaire temperatures well; check the rated maximum for PVD grades in hot fittings.

Cleaning

Dust with compressed air or a soft cloth and mild neutral cleaner; never use abrasives or alkaline cleaners.

Applications

Where high-reflectivity aluminium is used

  • Commercial and industrial lighting: downlight cones, high-bay and street-light reflectors, troffer louvres
  • LED backlights and light boxes: high total-reflectance back panels for displays and signs
  • Daylighting: tubular skylights and light pipes, where every reflection multiplies losses
  • Solar energy: concentrator mirrors and booster reflectors for thermal and PV systems
  • UV systems: curing lamps for inks and coatings, water and air disinfection, medical phototherapy
  • Optical and scientific: light-mixing chambers, test enclosures, horticultural grow-light reflectors
  • Architecture and interiors: reflective ceilings and decorative panels (material supplied to fabricators)

Metline supplies the reflective aluminium sheet and coil, slit or cut to blanks; the finished reflectors and fittings are made by luminaire and system manufacturers.

Selection advice

How to choose the right reflectance grade

  1. Define the optical jobBeam shaping needs specular; uniform light needs diffuse or textured. Decide which reflectance number really drives performance.
  2. Count the reflectionsLight pipes and mixing boxes reflect many times, so a few percent per bounce compounds quickly; this is where 95 – 98 % grades pay back.
  3. Check the spectrumFor UV, choose a UV-optimised aluminium layer; silver-based and thick anodised coatings perform poorly below 400 nm.
  4. Check forming and environmentDeep drawing, high temperature or humidity can rule out some coatings.
  5. Specify and documentState the grade, finish, alloy, temper, thickness, width, coil ID or sheet size, and request reflectance test data with the delivery.

Common mistakes are paying for 98 % sheet in a single-bounce reflector where an anodised grade would perform almost as well, using silver-based sheet in a UV lamp, and removing the protective film before forming.

FAQ

High-reflectivity aluminium (aluminum) sheet: frequently asked questions

How reflective is high-reflectivity aluminium sheet?

Anodised specular grades typically reach about 85 – 90 % total reflectance. PVD-enhanced aluminium grades reach about 95 %, and silver-based PVD grades up to about 98 %, measured on flat material with an integrating sphere.

What is the difference between total and specular reflectance?

Total reflectance counts all reflected light; specular reflectance counts only the light reflected at the mirror angle. Specular grades shape beams precisely, while diffuse grades spread light evenly.

Which grade is best for UV curing or disinfection lamps?

A UV-optimised grade with a pure aluminium reflective layer and a thin UV-transparent protective coating. Silver-based and conventional anodised sheet reflect UV much less efficiently.

Can high-reflectivity sheet be bent and deep drawn?

Yes, within the minimum bend radius and forming limits of the grade. Anodised grades in soft tempers form well; PVD coatings may lose a few percent of reflectance when strongly stretched.

What thickness is normally used for luminaire reflectors?

Most reflectors use 0.3 – 0.8 mm sheet. Thinner material suits small reflectors and backlights; thicker 3003 or 5052 suits large self-supporting panels.

Do you supply reflective aluminium in coil?

Yes. Metline supplies reflective aluminium as coil, slit strip and cut sheet worldwide, with protective film and reflectance data on request.
