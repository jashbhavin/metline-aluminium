6063 Aluminium T Bar: Sizes, Weights, Properties and Uses

6063 aluminium T bar is an extruded tee section, a flat flange with a central web, made in the architectural alloy 6063. It is supplied mainly in T52 and T5 for glazing bars, partitions, floor transitions and trims, and in T6 where it must carry more load. This guide lists common sizes with calculated weights, the properties you can rely on, finishes and the points to check before you order.

Overview

What is a 6063 aluminium T bar?

A T bar (tee bar or tee section) has a horizontal flange and a vertical web meeting at the centre of the flange. The flange gives a wide face for fixing, sealing or covering a joint; the web adds stiffness and can slot into a groove or carry a panel on each side.

Extruded in 6063, the tee becomes a precise, smooth and easily finished section. That is why 6063 tees are found in glazing systems, suspended ceilings, room dividers, floor and tile transitions, display systems and greenhouse roofs: places where the bar is visible and must look consistent after anodising or powder coating.

Sizes are given as flange width × overall height × thickness. Standard stock tees usually have equal flange width and height and a uniform thickness, but custom tees with unequal dimensions, a thicker web, a bulb on the web or rounded toes are common in proprietary glazing and partition systems.

Where tees beat angles

  • One tee can cover a joint between two panels and hold both, where two angles would be needed
  • The central web keeps the section symmetric, so it bends less sideways under load
  • The flange gives a clean visible face; the web is hidden in the joint or groove
Product types

Common types of 6063 T bar

6063-T52 tee

The usual stock temper in North America. Aged to a controlled strength window, so the bar notches, bends and crimps predictably. Popular for glazing bars, partitions and assembled frames.

6063-T5 and T6 tee

T5 is the general-purpose temper in most other markets; T6 adds strength for ceiling grids carrying heavier panels, roof glazing bars and greenhouse purlins.

Anodised tee

Clear, champagne, bronze or black anodising for architectural trim, door and window details and interior partitions.

Powder-coated tee

RAL colours and wood-grain effects to match window systems, shopfitting and furniture.

Decorative tee and transition strip

Thin flanges with brushed, polished or textured faces for floor and wall transitions, tile joints and panel dividers.

Custom tee profiles

Unequal flanges, gasket grooves, screw ports or bulb webs extruded on a dedicated die for system suppliers.

Design

How a 6063 tee behaves in service

Two-way stiffness

The web resists bending in its own plane, while the flange resists sideways bending. Orient the web along the direction of the main load, for example vertical in a ceiling grid carrying panels.

Deflection governs

Aluminium is about one third as stiff as steel. For long spans, check deflection first: a deeper web increases stiffness much faster than a thicker flange.

Thermal movement

6063 expands about 2.3 mm per metre per 100 °C. Long glazing or roof tees need slotted fixings or expansion gaps.

Dimensional stability

Press-quenched and aged tees have low residual stress, so they stay straight after cutting and notching. Store bars flat and supported to avoid sagging.

Working with 6063 tee

Cutting, notching, joining and finishing

  • Cutting: carbide-tipped saw with a lubricant; support both the flange and web close to the blade to prevent chatter, then deburr
  • Notching and coping: T52 and T5 notch cleanly for cross-tee joints in grids and frames
  • Drilling and machining: sharp, high-rake tools and high speeds; T6 gives a cleaner finish than softer tempers
  • Welding: TIG or MIG with ER4043 or ER5356; expect a softer heat-affected zone and finish damage, so mechanical fixing is usual for visible work
  • Bending: bend T52 or T4 about the stronger axis with formed tooling; avoid tight bends in T6 and bend before finishing
  • Anodising and coating: 6063 gives an even anodised colour; powder coating at 60 – 80 µm is common for exterior use

FAQ

6063 aluminium T bar: frequently asked questions

How much does a 6063 aluminium T bar weigh?

For a uniform-thickness tee, multiply t × (flange + height − t) in mm by 0.0027 for kg/m. A 40 × 40 × 3 mm tee is about 0.62 kg/m; a 2 × 2 × 1/4 in tee about 1.10 lb/ft.

Which temper is best for 6063 T bar?

T52 or T5 for glazing, trims and partitions, where predictable bending and a good finish matter. T6 where the tee spans further or carries heavier loads.

Can 6063 T bar be anodised?

Yes. 6063 is one of the best alloys for anodising, giving an even clear or coloured finish with good wear and corrosion resistance.

Is 6063 aluminum T bar suitable for outdoor use?

Yes. It resists atmospheric corrosion well; anodising or powder coating extends its life in coastal or polluted areas.

Can 6063 T bar be welded?

Yes, by TIG or MIG with 4043 or 5356 filler, but the area around the weld softens and finishes are damaged, so mechanical fixing is preferred for visible work.

Can you supply cut and machined T bar?

Yes. Cutting to length, notching, drilling and CNC machining to drawing can be arranged with the material supply.
