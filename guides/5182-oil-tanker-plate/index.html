5182 Aluminium Sheet for ADR Tank Trucks

5182 is a 4.5 % magnesium aluminium alloy whose high elongation and steady weldability make it a favoured shell material for ADR road tankers carrying petrol, diesel and other dangerous goods at ambient temperature. H111 is used for rolled shells and O for spun heads and pressed baffles. Metline supplies 5182 tanker sheet 3 – 12 mm thick and up to about 2600 mm wide, to EN 14286 and ASTM B209, as flat plate or cut kits, worldwide.

Overview

Why tanker builders use 5182

ADR, the European agreement on carrying dangerous goods by road, sets tank wall thickness and impact protection on the basis of a reference steel shell. Aluminium tanks may be thinner than an equivalent mild-steel shell in proportion to the material's strength and elongation, so an alloy that combines good strength with very high elongation lets the designer reach a light, compliant shell.

5182 was developed for formed automotive inner panels and beverage can ends, applications that demand strength and extreme formability. The same balance suits tank construction: 5182-O and H111 sheet stretch uniformly during rolling, spinning and pressing, and weld consistently along very long seams. Its manganese level (0.20 – 0.50 %) is lower than in 5083, which gives a slightly softer, more ductile sheet at a similar magnesium content.

In practice, 5182 sits between 5083 and 5754 in a tanker builder's choice: lighter-gauge capable like 5083, more forgiving to form, and well proven for ambient-temperature petroleum service across Europe, the Middle East and Asia.

What 5182 offers a tank builder

  • High elongation for rolling shells and spinning heads without cracking
  • Stable MIG and TIG welding with 5183 or 5356 filler
  • Wide sheet up to about 2600 mm reduces longitudinal seams
  • Good resistance to fuels, road salt and humid air without internal coating

Corrosion

Corrosion, sensitisation and NAMLT testing

All 5xxx alloys with more than about 3 % magnesium can become sensitised if they spend long periods between roughly 65 °C and 200 °C: magnesium-rich β phase forms along grain boundaries and the metal becomes prone to intergranular and stress-corrosion cracking. Ambient-temperature fuel tankers rarely approach this range, but tanks parked in desert sun, or carrying warm product, need attention.

NAMLT mass loss (ASTM G67)Interpretation
≤ 15 mg/cm²Resistant to intergranular corrosion
15 – 25 mg/cm²Intermediate; investigate further
> 25 mg/cm²Susceptible; not acceptable for exposed service

A NAMLT report can be supplied with the mill certificate on request. For cargo kept above about 65 °C, such as bitumen or heated chemicals, choose 5454 rather than 5182. A bright or highly reflective exterior finish lowers the skin temperature of tanks in hot climates and is often specified in the Gulf region.

Fabrication

Welding and forming 5182

Filler choice

ER5356 is widely used for general tank seams; ER5183 gives higher as-welded strength and is preferred for the most highly stressed joints or harsher service.

Cleaning

Degrease with solvent, then remove oxide with a stainless wire brush immediately before welding. Most porosity comes from moisture, oil or old oxide.

Heat input

Keep heat input and interpass temperature moderate to limit the softened zone and distortion on long seams.

Inspection

Penetrant, radiographic or ultrasonic testing of critical seams as required by the tank code; smooth weld toes improve fatigue life.

Spinning and pressing

O temper spins into heads readily. Avoid slow warm forming that could sensitise the alloy.

Surface protection

PE film protects bright or mirror sheet during fabrication; remove it before welding near the seam.

FAQ

5182 aluminium (aluminum) tanker sheet: FAQ

Should I choose 5182-H111 or 5182-O?

Use H111 for the rolled shell, where flatness and stable welding matter, and O for spun heads, pressed baffles and bulkheads, where maximum formability is needed.

Is 5182 approved for ADR tankers?

ADR does not list approved alloys; it sets design rules. 5182 supplied to EN 14286 is widely used for ADR tank shells, with wall thickness calculated by the tank designer under EN 13094 or the applicable standard.

Do I need NAMLT testing?

It is advisable where tanks may run warm or the specification calls for it. ASTM G67 mass loss of 15 mg/cm² or less indicates resistance to intergranular corrosion.

Which filler suits 5182?

ER5356 for most seams and ER5183 where higher as-welded strength or harsher service applies. Do not use 4xxx silicon fillers on high-magnesium alloys.

Is a mirror finish worth it in hot climates?

A highly reflective surface absorbs less solar heat, keeping cargo and shell cooler and limiting vapour loss, and it keeps a uniform appearance. Mill finish is fine for painted tanks.

Can you supply cut parts instead of flat sheet?

Yes. Shell plates cut and bevelled, heads and baffles to drawing, and kits packed by compartment can be arranged along with the mill certificates.
