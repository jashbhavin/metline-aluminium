Aluminium-Plastic Composite Strip for Cables and Optical Cables

Aluminium-plastic composite strip is soft aluminium tape coated on one or both sides with polyethylene or an ethylene copolymer. Cable makers fold it lengthwise around the cable core, where it forms a moisture barrier and an electrical screen, and the plastic layer heat-seals to the overlap and to the outer sheath. This guide explains the tape types, dimensions, performance tests and how to specify the strip; Metline supplies it in pads and slit coils to cable plants worldwide.

Overview

What the composite strip does in a cable

Water is the main enemy of telecom and fibre-optic cables. Moisture that diffuses through a polyethylene sheath can raise attenuation in copper pairs and, over years, weaken optical fibres. A thin, continuous metal layer stops that diffusion almost completely, and aluminium is the cheapest, lightest metal that can be formed into such a layer at line speed.

In the classic "APL" or "laminated aluminium polyethylene" sheath, the tape is longitudinally applied over the core with an overlap of a few millimetres. As the hot polyethylene jacket is extruded over it, the plastic coating on the tape melts and bonds to the jacket and to itself at the overlap. The result is a sealed, bonded sheath that resists water, bends with the cable and does not crack.

Besides blocking moisture, the aluminium layer acts as an electrostatic screen against external interference, and in metallic cables it can serve as a continuity or earthing path. In some designs the tape also gives modest protection against rodents and mechanical damage, although steel tape is used where real armouring is required.

Where composite aluminium tape is used

  • Outdoor and duct optical fibre cables (loose-tube and central-tube designs)
  • Telephone and data cables with polyethylene sheaths
  • Coaxial and LAN cables that need a foil screen bonded to the jacket
  • Control, instrumentation and some power cables as a radial moisture barrier
Product types

Single-sided and double-sided types

Telecommunications industry standards classify the strip by coating material and by whether one or both sides are coated. The type codes below are widely used in tender documents for optical cable materials.

Type codeCoatingSides coatedWhen it is chosen
YLPolyethyleneOneSheaths where only the outer jacket bonds to the tape
YLYPolyethyleneTwoOverlap sealed tape-to-tape and bonded to the jacket
ELEthylene copolymer (e.g. EAA)OneStronger adhesion to the metal and to PE jackets
ELEEthylene copolymer (e.g. EAA)TwoMost common for fibre cables needing a fully bonded, sealed sheath

Copolymer coatings such as ethylene acrylic acid (EAA) contain polar groups that bond chemically to the aluminium oxide surface, so they hold better after heat ageing and immersion than plain polyethylene. That is why EL and ELE types dominate modern optical cable production. The standard colours are natural (translucent) and blue, which helps operators tell coated and uncoated sides apart.

Base metal

The aluminium strip inside the tape

The metal layer is a thin, fully annealed (O temper) aluminium strip, usually from the 1xxx series such as 1050 or 1060, or 8xxx foil alloys where higher strength at thin gauges is wanted. Annealing matters: the tape must fold around the core and corrugate without cracking, and residual rolling oils must be removed so the coating adheres.

ASTM B736 covers aluminium and aluminium-alloy cable shielding stock, including coated strip, and is often cited for the test methods. In Chinese telecom tenders the YD/T 723 series is referenced; IEC cable standards set the sheath requirements that the tape helps meet. Ask for test results with each lot, not just a type certificate.

Winding

Winding, joints and packing

  • Pads must be wound tightly and evenly so they do not telescope or collapse when stood on edge
  • Only one strip is wound on each core; two tapes on one core are not acceptable
  • Overlapping joints are not allowed within a pad; butt-welded joints are normally permitted and marked
  • Edges must be clean and free of burrs, which can puncture the jacket or cause poor sealing
  • Pads are wrapped in moisture-barrier film with desiccant and packed on pallets or in wooden cases
  • Store dry and below about 40 °C; use first-in, first-out, as the copolymer can absorb moisture over time
Processing

Practical notes for the sheathing line

Forming

Guide the tape through a forming die so the overlap is consistent (usually 3 – 6 mm). An uneven overlap is the most common cause of seal failure.

Corrugation

Corrugated tape improves flexibility and crush resistance on larger cables. Use annealed metal; hard tape cracks at the corrugation roots.

Temperature

The jacket melt temperature must be high enough to activate the copolymer at the overlap. Too cool gives weak bonding; too hot can thin the coating.

Earthing

Where the tape is used as a screen, remove the coating at terminations so the metal can be bonded to earth.

Storage

Condition pads to line temperature before use to avoid condensation on the tape.

Inspection

Check peel and seal strength from the finished cable regularly, not only from incoming tape.

FAQ

Aluminium-plastic composite tape: FAQ

What is the difference between YLY and ELE tape?

Both are coated on two sides. YLY uses polyethylene, while ELE uses an ethylene copolymer such as EAA that bonds more strongly to aluminium and keeps its adhesion better after heat and water ageing. ELE is the usual choice for bonded optical cable sheaths.

Which aluminium thickness should I use?

0.15 mm is the most common choice for fibre and telecom cables. Use 0.10 mm for small, flexible cables and 0.20 mm where a stronger barrier or better corrugation strength is needed.

Why is the coating blue or natural?

Those are the standard colours. Colour helps identify the coated side and the tape type on the line and in the finished cable. Other colours may be possible by agreement.

Can the aluminium layer earth the cable?

Yes, it can serve as a screen or continuity conductor, but the coating is an insulator, so it must be removed at terminations to make the earth connection.

Are welded joints allowed in a pad?

Butt-welded joints are normally accepted if they are marked and keep full width. Overlapping joints are not accepted because they jam forming dies and leave gaps in the seal.

What aluminium (aluminum) alloy is used?

Annealed 1xxx-series aluminium such as 1050 or 1060 is typical, with 8xxx foil alloys used in some products. The key requirement is O temper with a clean, pre-treated surface.

Qualifying a new tape source?
