Aluminium Pipe Size Chart: SCH40 and SCH80 Dimensions and Weights

Aluminium pipe uses the same nominal pipe size (NPS) and schedule system as steel pipe: the NPS fixes the outside diameter and the schedule fixes the wall thickness. Schedule 40 is the standard wall and Schedule 80 the extra-heavy wall. The tables below give outside diameter, wall, inside diameter and weight for NPS 1/8 to 12 in aluminium, calculated for 6061 pipe.

Basics

How NPS and schedule numbers work

NPS is a name, not a measurement. From NPS 1/8 to NPS 12 the actual outside diameter is larger than the nominal size: a 1 in pipe has an OD of 1.315 in (33.4 mm), and a 2 in pipe 2.375 in (60.3 mm). From NPS 14 upward, OD equals the nominal size in inches. Because OD is fixed for each NPS, all schedules of a size fit the same fittings and clamps; only the bore changes.

The schedule number sets the wall thickness. Higher schedule means a thicker wall, a smaller inside diameter, more weight and a higher pressure rating. Aluminium pipe dimensions follow ASME B36.10M (Schedule 40 and 80) and ASME B36.19M (Schedule 5S and 10S), and the aluminium product standards ASTM B241 (seamless pipe) and ASTM B429 (structural pipe) reference these dimensions.

A note on the "S" schedules: up to NPS 8, Schedule 40 and 40S are identical, as are 80 and 80S. At NPS 10 and 12 they differ (for example, NPS 12 Schedule 80 is 0.688 in, but 80S is 0.500 in). Always state which one you need on the order.

Pipe or tube?

  • Pipe is ordered by NPS and schedule, and its OD is fixed by the size system
  • Tube is ordered by actual OD and wall (for example 50 × 3 mm or 2.000 × 0.125 in)
  • Pipe is normally used for fluids and handrails; tube for structures and precision parts
  • See our article on the difference between aluminium pipe and tube

High-strength aerospace alloys such as 2024 and 7075 are usually supplied as tube to OD × wall rather than in pipe schedules.

Pressure rating

Estimating the pressure rating of aluminium pipe

A first estimate of internal pressure capacity uses Barlow’s formula, P = 2 × S × t ÷ D, where S is the allowable stress for the alloy and temper at the design temperature, t the minimum wall (nominal wall less the mill tolerance, typically 12.5 % for seamless pipe), and D the outside diameter. Allowable stresses come from the design code, for example ASME B31.3 for process piping, not from the tensile strength.

  • Welding reduces strength. The heat-affected zone of 6061-T6 drops to near annealed strength, so codes give lower allowable stresses for welded 6xxx pipe.
  • Temperature matters. Allowable stress for 6xxx alloys falls significantly above about 100 – 150 °C.
  • Threads remove wall. Threaded joints use Schedule 80 so that enough metal remains under the thread.
  • Use seamless pipe for pressure. Specify ASTM B241 seamless; B221 and B429 structural pipe may contain extrusion seams.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) pipe sizes: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between SCH40 and SCH80 aluminum pipe?

Both have the same outside diameter for a given NPS. Schedule 80 has a thicker wall, a smaller bore, roughly 30 – 65 % more weight (depending on size) and a higher pressure rating. For example, NPS 2 is 0.154 in wall in Schedule 40 and 0.218 in in Schedule 80.

What is the outside diameter of 2 inch aluminium pipe?

NPS 2 aluminium pipe has an outside diameter of 2.375 in (60.3 mm) in every schedule. The inside diameter is 2.067 in for Schedule 40 and 1.939 in for Schedule 80.

How much does 1-1/2 inch Schedule 40 aluminium pipe weigh?

About 0.94 lb per foot (1.40 kg/m) in 6061, based on 1.900 in OD and 0.145 in wall. A 20 ft length weighs about 18.8 lb.

Do aluminium and steel pipe use the same sizes?

Yes. Aluminium pipe follows the same NPS outside diameters and schedule walls, so it fits standard fittings and clamps. It weighs about one third as much as steel pipe of the same size.

Which aluminium alloy is used for Schedule 40 handrail pipe?

6063-T6 or 6061-T6. 6063 gives a better anodised finish and bends more easily; 6061 is stronger. Common handrail sizes are NPS 1-1/4 (1.660 in OD) and NPS 1-1/2 (1.900 in OD).

Is the metric wall thickness the same as the inch value?

Yes: the metric wall is the inch wall converted, for example Schedule 40 NPS 1 is 0.133 in, or 3.38 mm. Some charts round these values loosely, so check against ASME B36.10M when the exact wall matters.
