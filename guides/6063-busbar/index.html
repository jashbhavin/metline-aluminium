6063 Aluminium Busbar and Electrical Flat Bar

6063 busbar is an extruded aluminium flat bar that trades a little conductivity (about 53 – 58 % IACS depending on temper) for a smooth surface, tight dimensions and easy bending. It is the practical choice for panel, switchgear and inverter busbars where currents are moderate and appearance or plating quality matters. This guide explains tempers, conductivity, sizing and how to specify it.

Overview

Why 6063 is used for busbar

A busbar has two jobs: carry current with acceptable temperature rise, and hold its shape between supports. Pure aluminium grades such as 1350 and 1060 do the first job best; 6063 does the second job better while keeping roughly 85 – 95 % of their conductivity.

6063 is the most extruded aluminium alloy in the world, which matters for busbar buyers in three ways. First, almost any rectangular section with square or radiused edges can be produced from an existing die, so tooling cost is rarely an issue. Second, 6063 leaves the press with a very clean, uniform surface, which gives consistent results when the bar is tin- or silver-plated at the joints. Third, it bends and punches readily, so one bar can be formed into the offsets and risers a compact panel needs.

The trade-off is conductivity. The magnesium and silicon that make 6063 heat-treatable also scatter electrons, so a 6063-T6 bar has noticeably higher resistance than a 1350 bar of the same size. For a busbar this means a slightly larger cross-section or a slightly lower current rating. Where the bar must carry very high current with minimal losses, 6101 or 1350 is usually the better choice; where moderate current, rigidity and finish matter, 6063 is hard to beat on cost.

Where 6063 busbar fits best

  • Low-voltage switchboards, motor-control centres and distribution panels
  • Inverter, UPS and drive busbars where plated joints and neat bends are needed
  • Battery-pack and energy-storage interconnects with moderate currents
  • Earth (ground) bars and neutral links that also act as mounting rails

Electrical & mechanical data

Conductivity and strength of 6063 by temper

Conductivity and strength move in opposite directions. Ageing to peak strength (T6) precipitates Mg2Si finely, which raises strength but leaves more solute in the matrix than an over-aged or annealed bar. Tempers

Which temper should you specify?

T5

Cooled from the press and artificially aged. Good balance of conductivity and stiffness, lowest cost, and the temper most extruders stock for busbar.

T6

Solution treated (often on the press) and aged to peak strength. Choose it for bars that span long distances between insulators, carry heavy short-circuit forces or are bolted at high torque.

T4

Naturally aged and relatively soft. Useful when the bar will be bent tightly and then aged to T6, or when maximum formability is needed.

T66

A higher-strength 6063 variant from tighter chemistry control. Rarely needed for busbar; it costs conductivity.

O

Fully annealed. Highest conductivity of the 6063 tempers but too soft for most self-supporting bars; used for flexible links and shaped connectors.

Over-aged tempers

Some buyers ask for a bar aged past peak strength to recover a few points of conductivity. Agree the target conductivity and minimum strength in writing before ordering.

Extruded flat bar

The normal route. A standard die gives consistent dimensions and a smooth surface; best value on any meaningful quantity.

Sawn from plate

For widths beyond the extrusion range, very thick sections or small quantities of odd sizes. Edges are machined rather than extruded.

Radiused-edge bar

Rounded corners reduce local electric-field concentration and make plating and insulation sleeving more reliable.

Fabricated busbar

Cut, punched, drilled, bent and plated to drawing so the bar arrives ready to install.

Processing

Bending, joining and surface treatment

Bending

Flatwise bends in T5/T6 normally need an inside radius of about 1 × thickness for bars up to 12 mm and 2 × thickness above that; edgewise bends need more. Run a trial bend on the first lot.

Punching and drilling

6063 punches cleanly. Deburr holes at joints, because burrs reduce contact area and raise joint resistance.

Bolted joints

Aluminium creeps under constant clamp load. Use Belleville (conical) washers or wide flat washers, contact grease and the torque from your joint design, and avoid plain spring washers.

Plating

Tin plating is the usual joint finish; silver for high-current or high-temperature joints; nickel where hardness and corrosion resistance matter. Plating needs a zincate or similar pre-treatment on aluminium.

Copper connections

Never bolt bare aluminium straight to bare copper in damp conditions. Use plated surfaces, bimetallic washers or copper-clad aluminium transition pieces.

Insulation

Epoxy powder coating, heat-shrink sleeving or insulating paint are applied after fabrication for touch-safe or compact layouts.

Ordering

How to specify 6063 busbar

  1. Define the sectionThickness, width, edge shape and length, or a drawing of the fabricated bar.
  2. State temper and conductivityFor example "6063-T5, conductivity ≥ 55 % IACS measured at 20 °C". Stating the minimum conductivity is more useful than the temper alone.
  3. Add finish and processingPlating type and thickness, insulation, holes and bends.
  4. Agree tests and documentsConductivity, tensile and bend tests as required; mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1.
  5. Confirm delivery termsEXW, FOB, CIF or DAP, with export packing suited to the finish.

FAQ

6063 aluminium (aluminum) busbar: FAQ

What is the conductivity of 6063 busbar?

Typically about 55 % IACS in T5 and about 53 % IACS in T6, roughly 31 – 32 MS/m. Annealed 6063 reaches about 58 % IACS. Ask for the measured value on the mill certificate if your design depends on it.

Is 6063 or 6101 better for busbar?

6101 is the dedicated conductor alloy: ASTM B317 guarantees minimum conductivity (55 – 59.5 % IACS by temper). 6063 is cheaper, more widely extruded and has a better surface, but its conductivity is not guaranteed by its product standard. Use 6101 when conductivity is a contractual requirement.

Can 6063 busbar be bent?

Yes. In T5 or T6, flatwise bends with an inside radius of about one thickness are normal for bars up to 12 mm. For tighter bends, form in T4 and age afterwards.

Does 6063 busbar need plating?

Not for the bar body, which forms a protective oxide film. Joint surfaces are commonly tin- or silver-plated, or cleaned and greased just before assembly, to keep contact resistance low and stable.

How much does a 6063 busbar weigh?

Multiply thickness × width (mm) × 0.0027 to get kg per metre. A 10 × 100 mm bar weighs about 2.7 kg/m, compared with about 8.9 kg/m for copper of the same size.

Do you supply 6063 busbar outside India?

Yes. Metline ships busbar worldwide from Mumbai with mill test certificates and export packing.
