6101 Aluminium Busbar: Tempers, Sizing and Specification

6101 is the dedicated aluminium alloy for busbar. ASTM B317 guarantees its conductivity for each temper, from 55 % IACS in T6 to 59.5 % IACS in T64, while giving several times the strength of pure EC aluminium. This guide shows how to pick a temper, size a 6101 bar against copper, design joints that stay cool and write a purchase specification.

Overview

Why 6101 is the standard busbar alloy

A busbar is judged on three things: how much heat it generates per ampere, whether it stays straight and tight at its joints for decades, and how much it costs and weighs. 6101 scores well on all three.

Pure EC aluminium (1350) conducts slightly better, but it is soft and creeps under bolt pressure, so its joints loosen over time unless carefully designed. General-purpose alloys such as 6063 and 6082 are stronger but their conductivity is not guaranteed and is noticeably lower. 6101 sits deliberately between them: its chemistry keeps manganese, chromium and other conductivity-killing elements at trace levels, and its ageing treatments are tuned to deliver a published minimum conductivity with useful strength.

For switchgear, busway and panel builders this means a predictable product. When a 6101-T61 bar is certified to ASTM B317, its conductivity will be at least 57 % IACS at 20 °C, and its tensile and yield strengths will meet set minimums for its thickness. That predictability is what allows designers to rely on temperature-rise test results from one batch to the next.

Benefits of 6101 busbar over copper

  • About 30 % of the density of copper, so a bar of equal resistance weighs roughly half as much
  • Lower and more stable material cost per ampere of capacity
  • Larger, stiffer sections for the same current, which helps with short-circuit forces
  • No tarnish-related contact problems when joints are plated or properly prepared

The 0.03 % caps on manganese and chromium are the critical lines on a 6101 certificate. A bar extruded from ordinary 6063 billet can look identical but will not reach the B317 conductivity minimums, so check the certified analysis as well as the measured conductivity.

For equal DC resistance, a 6101-T61 bar needs about 1.75 times the cross-section of copper. In practice the equal-temperature-rise ratio is smaller, because the larger aluminium bar has more surface area to shed heat. Two rules of thumb are widely used for converting a copper design:

  1. Widen the barKeep the thickness and increase the width by roughly a quarter to a third, for example replacing a 1/4 × 4 in copper bar with 1/4 × 5 in aluminium.
  2. Thicken the barKeep the width and increase the thickness by about half, for example 3/8 × 4 in aluminium for 1/4 × 4 in copper.
  3. Verify by testConfirm the rating by temperature-rise testing of the assembly, for example to IEC 61439-1 or UL 857 for busways. Bar orientation, spacing, enclosure ventilation and AC skin effect all change the result.

Even after upsizing, the aluminium bar typically weighs about half as much as the copper bar it replaces.

Product types

Types of 6101 busbar

Extruded flat bar

The standard form, with square or radiused edges. Uniform dimensions and good surface for plating.

Shaped and channel bus

Extruded channels, angles and integrated profiles that combine conductor and support in one section.

Plate-cut bar

Very wide or thick bars sawn from 6101 plate for rectifier and smelter buses.

Coil and strip

Thin 6101 strip for flexible links, laminated busbar layers and stamped connectors.

Flexible busbar

Stacked thin strips or braid joined at solid ends, absorbing vibration, thermal movement and misalignment.

Insulated busbar

Epoxy powder or heat-shrink covered bar allowing closer phase spacing and touch protection.

Joint design

Designing joints that stay cool

Most busbar failures start at joints, not in the bar. Aluminium forms a hard oxide film within seconds of exposure and creeps slowly under constant pressure; good joint design deals with both.

  • Prepare the surface: wire-brush or abrade the contact area under a jointing compound and assemble immediately, or use factory-plated (tin or silver) contact areas.
  • Keep pressure constant: use high-strength bolts with conical (Belleville) spring washers or thick flat washers. Plain split washers do not maintain load as the aluminium creeps.
  • Use enough overlap: overlap length of several bar thicknesses and multiple bolts spread current evenly; follow your design standard.
  • Avoid bare copper-to-aluminium contact: plate one or both surfaces, or use bimetallic transition plates, to prevent galvanic corrosion in humid conditions.
  • Retorque or inspect: thermographic inspection after commissioning finds any high-resistance joint before it fails.
  • Weld where suitable: TIG- or MIG-welded joints with ER4043 filler eliminate contact resistance entirely for permanent connections.

Bend radii depend on temper: T64 and T65 take the tightest flatwise and edgewise bends, while T6 needs the most generous radius. Agree a sample bend on the first delivery if the bar will be formed on automated equipment.

Ordering

How to specify 6101 busbar

  1. Standard and temperFor example "6101-T61 to ASTM B317/B317M".
  2. Section and lengthThickness × width, edge form, length or drawing.
  3. ConductivityState the minimum % IACS at 20 °C if higher than the standard minimum.
  4. Finish and fabricationPlating type and thickness, holes, bends, insulation.
  5. Documents and shippingEN 10204 3.1 certificate with chemistry, mechanicals and conductivity; EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP; export packing with interleaving.

FAQ

6101 aluminium (aluminum) busbar: FAQ

What is the difference between 6101-T6 and 6101-T61?

T6 is aged to peak strength (200 MPa tensile minimum) with 55 % IACS minimum conductivity. T61 is over-aged: lower strength (138 MPa minimum in thin sections) but higher conductivity, 57 % IACS minimum. T61 is the more common busbar temper.

How much larger must an aluminium busbar be than copper?

For equal DC resistance, about 1.75 times the cross-section for 6101-T61. For equal temperature rise, designers usually find a 25 – 50 % increase in width or thickness is enough, confirmed by testing.

Is 6101 busbar safe for bolted joints?

Yes, when joints are designed for aluminium: clean or plated contact faces, jointing compound, and conical spring washers to maintain clamp load. 6101 creeps less than 1350, which helps.

Can 6101 busbar be bent edgewise?

Yes, in T64 or T65, which have the best ductility. T6 needs generous radii and is not recommended for tight edgewise bends.

Which standards cover 6101 busbar?

ASTM B317/B317M in North America, IEC 60114 internationally, IS 5082 in India and EN 755 for extruded product properties and tolerances in Europe.

Can you supply plated or insulated 6101 busbar?

Yes. Metline supplies 6101 busbar cut, punched, bent, tin- or silver-plated or insulated to drawing, with mill test certificates, worldwide.
