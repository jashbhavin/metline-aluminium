Aluminium for Tank Trucks and Tank Trailers

Road tankers for fuel, chemicals, asphalt and food liquids are built mostly from 5xxx aluminium-magnesium plate because it is light, tough, weldable and resistant to corrosion, so more payload can be carried on every trip. Shells, heads and baffles use 5083, 5182, 5454 or 5754 in O or H111 temper, with 6xxx profiles for frames and walkways and 5183 or 5356 filler for welding. This guide explains which alloy goes where, the standards that apply, and how to specify the materials.

Overview

Why tankers are built from aluminium

A road tanker runs close to its gross weight limit on every loaded trip, so every kilogram saved on the tank is a kilogram of extra product. An aluminium tank barrel is typically far lighter than a carbon steel one of the same capacity, which is why aluminium has become the standard material for fuel tankers in Europe and many other markets.

The 5xxx alloys used for tank shells gain their strength from magnesium in solid solution rather than heat treatment, so they keep most of their strength after welding. They form a stable oxide film that resists petroleum products, many chemicals and road salt without paint, and they stay tough at low temperatures, so they do not become brittle in winter or in cryogenic service. Aluminium also does not spark when struck by steel tools in the way some metals can, and it is fully recyclable at the end of the vehicle's life.

What a tanker material must deliver

  • High strength combined with high elongation, because tank regulations reward ductility as well as strength
  • Reliable fusion welding with low porosity and good weld toughness
  • Corrosion resistance to the cargo, cleaning agents and the road environment
  • Formability for heads, baffles and the barrel shape without cracking
  • Traceable, certified material for type approval and inspection

The table shows specified values for O/H111 plate in EN 485-2 for the thickness range used in tank shells. Limits change slightly with thickness band, so the mill certificate for each plate is the controlling document.

Under ADR, the minimum shell thickness of an aluminium tank is derived from a reference steel thickness using a formula based on the product of tensile strength and elongation of the actual material (e₁ = 464·e₀ / ∛(Rm₁·A₁)²). That is why tank designers look closely at guaranteed elongation as well as strength, and why alloys combining both, such as 5083 and 5182, are popular for shells.

Fabrication

Welding, forming and corrosion control

  • Welding: MIG for long seams and TIG for fittings, with ER5183 for 5083 and other high-Mg plate and ER5356 for general 5xxx work. Remove the oxide by stainless brushing just before welding, keep plate and wire dry and clean, and use pure argon or argon-helium shielding to minimise porosity.
  • Avoiding cracks: use O or H111 temper for heads and tight forming, keep bend radii generous, and avoid 4xxx (Al-Si) fillers on high-Mg plate, which can give weak, crack-prone welds.
  • Hot service: alloys with more than about 3 % Mg (5083, 5182) can become sensitised to intergranular corrosion if held above about 65 °C for long periods; use 5454 for asphalt and heated cargoes.
  • Dissimilar metals: isolate steel chassis, stainless fittings and copper items from the aluminium with insulating pads, sleeves and sealants to prevent galvanic corrosion.
  • Cleaning: check that alkaline or acidic cleaning chemicals are compatible with aluminium; strong alkalis attack it.
  • Inspection: radiography or ultrasonic testing of seams and hydrostatic or pneumatic testing of the finished tank as required by the code.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) for tank trucks: frequently asked questions

What aluminum alloy is used for fuel tankers?

Most fuel tanker shells and heads are 5083 or 5182 in O or H111 temper, with 5754 also common. 5454 is used for hot cargoes such as asphalt, and 6xxx extrusions for frames and fittings.

Why is 5454 used for asphalt tankers?

Its magnesium content is below 3 %, so it does not become sensitised to intergranular corrosion when held at elevated temperature, unlike 5083 or 5182.

Which filler wire should be used for tanker welding?

ER5183 for 5083, 5182 and other high-magnesium plate where weld strength matters, and ER5356 for general 5xxx welding. Avoid 4043 on high-Mg alloys.

Is aluminium allowed for dangerous goods tanks under ADR?

Yes. ADR Chapter 6.8 allows aluminium alloy tanks, with minimum shell thickness calculated from the material's strength and elongation, and EN 13094 covers the design of low-pressure metallic tanks including aluminium.

What thickness of plate is used for tank shells?

Shell thickness comes from the design calculation and approval; for road fuel tankers it is commonly in the 4 – 8 mm range, with heads sometimes thicker.

Can Metline supply all the aluminium for a tanker build?

Yes. Metline supplies 5xxx plate and coil, tread plate, 6xxx profiles, tube, bar and welding wire to tanker builders worldwide, with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and export packing.
