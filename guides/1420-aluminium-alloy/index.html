Aluminium-lithium alloy guide

Russian 1420 (01420) Aluminium-Lithium Alloy

1420, also written 01420, is a Russian aluminium-magnesium-lithium alloy with about 5–6 % magnesium and 2 % lithium. It is roughly 10 % lighter and noticeably stiffer than conventional aluminium alloys, and, unusually for an Al-Li alloy, it can be fusion welded. It was developed for welded aircraft structures and is supplied mainly as sheet, plate and extrusions against aerospace specifications.

Overview

What makes 1420 different

Lithium is the lightest metal. Each 1 % of lithium added to aluminium lowers density by about 3 % and raises the elastic modulus by about 6 %. Combined with magnesium, which is also lighter than aluminium, this gives 1420 a density near 2.47 g/cm³, compared with 2.64 g/cm³ for an Al-6 % Mg alloy such as АМг6 and 2.78 g/cm³ for 2024.

1420 was developed in the Soviet Union in the 1960s under the metallurgist I. N. Fridlyander and became the first aluminium-lithium alloy used in welded primary structure. Its magnesium content makes it behave in welding much like a 5xxx alloy, so welded fuselage sections and fuel tanks could be built without rivets, saving further weight.

The trade-offs are moderate strength and fracture toughness by modern standards, sensitivity to processing, and the need for specialised melting because lithium is reactive. Later Russian alloys such as the scandium-modified 1421 and the Al-Cu-Li family addressed some of these limits, but 1420 remains the reference Al-Mg-Li grade.

Key points for buyers

  • About 10 % lighter than ordinary aluminium alloys, with higher stiffness
  • Fusion weldable, unlike most Al-Cu-Li grades
  • Good general corrosion resistance thanks to the Al-Mg base
  • Specialist aerospace material: supply depends on specification and approvals

The real advantage shows in specific properties. Dividing modulus by density, 1420 is roughly 13–16 % stiffer per kilogram than 5083 or 2024, which matters for buckling-critical panels and stiffness-driven structures.

Fabrication

Welding and fabrication

Fusion welding

TIG and other arc processes are used with matched Al-Mg-Li or Al-Mg fillers. Thorough cleaning and removal of the surface layer are essential to avoid porosity from lithium-rich oxide and hydrogen.

Weld properties

Weld zones lose some strength relative to aged parent metal, as with any precipitation-hardened alloy; design allowables come from qualified procedures.

Forming

Form in the quenched or annealed condition, then age. Formability is lower than for 5xxx alloys.

Machining

Machines like other medium-strength aluminium alloys; collect and store swarf separately and dry.

Applications

Where 1420 is used

  • Welded fuselage sections and cockpit structures of military aircraft
  • Welded integral fuel tanks
  • Lightweight panels, window and door frames in aircraft
  • Rocket and missile structures where welded light construction pays
  • Specialist lightweight structures outside aerospace, subject to cost

Western equivalents in purpose rather than chemistry are Al-Li alloys such as 8090 and 2090, and for welded tanks, 2195 and 2219; none is a direct drop-in replacement.

FAQ

1420 aluminium-lithium alloy: frequently asked questions

Is 1420 a normal Al-Mg alloy?

No. It contains about 2 % lithium as well as 5–6 % magnesium, which lowers density to about 2.47 g/cm³ and raises stiffness compared with ordinary Al-Mg alloys.

Can 1420 be welded?

Yes. It is one of the few aluminium-lithium alloys designed for fusion welding, and was used for welded aircraft fuselage sections and fuel tanks.

What is the Western equivalent of 1420?

There is no direct equivalent. 8090 is the closest in purpose among Al-Li alloys; substitution needs engineering approval.

How much lighter is 1420 than 2024?

About 11 % by density (2.47 vs 2.78 g/cm³), with a slightly higher elastic modulus.

What does the 0 in 01420 mean?

It is an older way of writing the same Russian grade number; 1420 and 01420 refer to the same alloy.
