Thick plate selection guide

6061-T6 vs 6061-T651 Thick Plate: How to Choose

6061-T6 and 6061-T651 plate have the same chemistry and essentially the same strength; the difference is that T651 is stretched after quenching to remove residual stress. For thick plate that will be machined, that stretch decides whether a part stays flat or bows when pockets are cut. This guide explains the two tempers, how properties change with thickness, and how to specify thick 6061 plate for moulds, fixtures and machined structures.

The key difference

What separates T6 from T651?

Both tempers start the same way: the rolled plate is solution heat-treated at about 530 °C, quenched in water and then artificially aged at about 160 – 175 °C to precipitate Mg2Si. Quenching a thick plate cools the surface much faster than the core, which locks in compressive stress at the surfaces and tensile stress in the middle.

In T651, the plate is stretched permanently by 1.5 – 3 % between quenching and ageing (the "51" suffix). Stretching yields the whole cross-section and evens out those internal stresses, typically reducing them by around 80 – 90 %. In T6, the stretch is omitted, so the quench stresses stay in the plate.

Strength is not the reason to choose one over the other. Standards set the same minimums for both, and typical values are the same. What changes is machining behaviour: remove material from one face of a T6 plate and the remaining stresses rebalance, so the part bows, twists or springs when unclamped. A T651 plate machined the same way moves far less.

Properties by thickness

How 6061 plate properties change with thickness

Thick plate cools more slowly at mid-thickness during the quench, so less magnesium silicide stays in solution and the core reaches slightly lower strength after ageing. Standards account for this by lowering minimum elongation and, in the heaviest bands, tensile strength.

Typical properties of 6061-T6/T651 are about 310 MPa tensile, 276 MPa yield, 12 % elongation and 95 HB. Design to the minimums for your thickness band, whichever temper you buy.

Selection

When to order T6 and when to order T651

Order 6061-T651 when…

  • The part will be CNC machined with pockets, slots or one-sided removal
  • Flatness after machining is critical: fixture bases, mould plates, vacuum chambers
  • More than about a quarter of the plate thickness will be removed
  • The thickness is within what the mill can stretch

Order 6061-T6 when…

  • The plate is thinner sheet used flat or lightly formed
  • The part is welded or used largely as rolled, with little machining
  • The section is thicker than the mill can stretch
  • You will rough-machine, stress-relieve or age, then finish-machine anyway

For the very heaviest sections, alternatives include forged and compressed 6061 block (temper T652, stress-relieved by compression) and cast tooling plate, which has no rolling or quench stresses at all but lower strength.

Ultrasonic testing, flatness and tolerances

Thick plate can hide porosity, inclusions or centreline defects that only appear when a deep pocket is cut. For moulds, aerospace tooling and pressure-retaining parts, specify ultrasonic inspection up front.

  • Ask for tensile specimens from mid-thickness (or T/4) when the core carries load
  • State whether the plate is for structural use or tooling; it changes the testing that matters
  • Specify a machining allowance on each face if surfaces must be fully cleaned up
Machining thick plate

Machining thick 6061 without distortion

  1. Choose T651Start with stress-relieved plate wherever thickness allows.
  2. Machine symmetricallyRemove material evenly from both faces, flipping the part between roughing passes.
  3. Rough, rest, finishRough to within 1 – 2 mm, unclamp so the part can relax, then re-clamp lightly and finish.
  4. Control heatUse flood coolant and sharp, high-rake carbide tools; heat input causes local movement.
  5. Clamp gentlyExcess clamping force bends the plate; it springs back when released.

6061-T651 produces short, broken chips and a bright finish at high spindle speeds. It takes clear, coloured and hard anodising well, which makes it a common choice for fixtures and machine components.

Common mistakes

Mistakes buyers make with thick 6061 plate

  • Ordering T6 for a heavily machined part to save a little cost, then losing far more in scrapped or re-worked parts when they distort
  • Designing to typical values rather than the specified minimum for the actual thickness band; above 100 mm the minimum tensile strength is lower
  • Leaving out ultrasonic testing on mould and tooling plate, where a hidden void found halfway through machining wastes days of spindle time
  • Using thick 6061 where flatness matters more than strength; cast tooling plate holds flatness better for vacuum tables and inspection bases
  • Forgetting the machining allowance; saw-cut faces and rolled surfaces usually need a few millimetres of clean-up per side
  • Mixing standards on one order, for example ASTM tolerances with EN property tables; state one governing specification
Applications

Where thick 6061 plate is used

30 – 50 mm

Fixture plates, CNC sub-plates, jig bodies, robot end-effector bases and automation frames.

50 – 150 mm

Injection and blow mould plates, vacuum-forming tools, machine beds and semiconductor chamber bodies.

Over 150 mm

Large mould blocks, heavy machine structures, aerospace assembly tooling and test rigs.

Alternative grades for thick plate: 6082-T651 (the European equivalent, slightly stronger), 7075-T651 (about twice the strength, for high-load tooling), 5083 (best for welded and marine parts) and cast tooling plate (best flatness).

FAQ

6061-T6 and T651 aluminum plate: frequently asked questions

Is 6061-T651 stronger than 6061-T6?

No. Both have the same minimum and typical properties. T651 is stretched to relieve residual stress, which improves stability during machining, not strength.

What does the "51" in T651 mean?

Under ANSI H35.1, "51" means the product was stress-relieved by stretching a controlled amount after solution treatment: 1.5 – 3 % for plate. "52" means stress relief by compression, used for forgings and some blocks.

How thick can 6061-T651 plate be?

It is limited by the stretching capacity of the mill. Plate to about 150 – 200 mm is widely available, and some mills stretch thicker. Beyond that, plate is usually supplied as T6, or forged block in T652 is used.

Why did my 6061-T6 plate bow after machining?

Quench stresses in un-stretched plate rebalance when material is removed. Use T651, machine symmetrically and finish after a rest pass to avoid it.

Should thick 6061 plate be ultrasonically tested?

For moulds, aerospace tooling and parts with deep pockets, yes. ASTM B594 or AMS-STD-2154 class A is a common requirement and catches internal defects before machining starts.

Can T6 plate be converted to T651?

Not practically. Stretching must happen between quenching and ageing. A T6 plate can be thermally stress-relieved, but this lowers strength.
