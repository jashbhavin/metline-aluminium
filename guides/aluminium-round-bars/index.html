Aluminium Round Bar: How to Choose the Alloy, Type and Size

Aluminium round bar (rod) is solid cylindrical stock made by extrusion, cold drawing, forging or casting, in alloys from soft 1100 to aerospace 7075. For most machined parts the choice comes down to 6061 or 6082 for all-round use, 2011 for high-speed turning, and 2024 or 7075 when strength is the priority. This guide compares the alloys, explains how production route affects tolerance and properties, and gives the size, weight and standards information needed to order the right bar.

Overview

Choosing aluminium round bar in three decisions

Buying round bar well means answering three questions in order: which alloy and temper gives the strength, machinability and corrosion resistance the part needs; which production route gives the tolerance, surface and internal soundness required; and which diameter and length minimise waste. Getting the first decision wrong is costly, while the other two mainly affect price and machining time.

Round bar is the most widely used aluminium stock in machine shops. CNC turning centres and Swiss-type lathes feed it continuously; milling shops saw it into blanks for flanges, hubs and pistons; fabricators use it for shafts, pins, handrails and fixings. Because the part is usually machined, the bar’s alloy controls tool life, chip form and surface finish as much as the final strength.

This guide is written for engineers and buyers comparing options. For a concise product specification of round bar, including size range and stock forms, see the round bar specification page; for individual alloys, follow the links in the related section.

Quick recommendations

  • General machined parts: 6061-T6/T6511 (ASTM drawings) or 6082-T6 (EN drawings)
  • High-volume turned parts: 2011-T3, or lead-free free-machining grades such as 6262 or 2011 variants where RoHS limits apply
  • High strength: 7075-T6/T651 or 2024-T351/T4
  • Marine and welded parts: 5083 or 5086 in O/H111/H112
  • Elevated temperature: 2618-T61 or 2219
  • Decorative and anodised: 6063-T6 or 6060

Alloy families

What each alloy family is good for

6xxx structural bar

6061, 6082, 6005 and 6063 combine moderate to good strength with weldability, anodising response and corrosion resistance. They are the default for brackets, shafts, fixtures, manifolds and structural pins. 6061 dominates ASTM markets and 6082 European ones.

Free-cutting bar

2011, 2007 and 6262 contain elements that break chips into short pieces, so automatic lathes run fast without chip tangling. Traditional grades contain lead or bismuth; check the chemistry where RoHS or end-of-life rules apply.

High-strength 2xxx

2024, 2014 and 2017 offer high strength and good fatigue performance for aircraft fittings, fasteners and highly stressed machine parts. Copper content lowers corrosion resistance, so parts are usually anodised or painted.

High-strength 7xxx

7075 is the strongest common bar alloy and machines to a fine finish. T73 tempers trade some strength for much better stress-corrosion resistance. 7050 is used for thick, highly loaded aerospace parts.

Marine 5xxx

5083, 5086 and 5052 resist seawater and keep their strength after welding because they are not heat-treated. Strength comes from magnesium content and cold work.

Heat-resistant and special

2618 and 2219 keep useful strength at elevated temperature; 4032 has low thermal expansion and good wear resistance for forged pistons; 1100 and 6101 serve electrical uses.

A useful rule: if the bar surface stays on the finished part, as in a shaft running in a bush or a pin in a reamed hole, choose cold-finished bar. If everything will be machined away, extruded bar is cheaper. For diameters beyond what local presses can extrude, or for fatigue-critical parts thicker than about 150 mm, ask for forged bar. Working with round bar

Machining, finishing and common mistakes

  • Choose the stretched temper for heavy machining. T6511 or T651 bar has lower residual stress, so long slender parts stay straighter after turning.
  • Do not expect 6061 to chip like 2011. 6061 gives longer chips on lathes; use chip breakers, high pressure coolant or a free-cutting grade for unattended turning.
  • Anodise the right alloy. 6063 and 6061 give clear, even anodic films; 2011 and 2024 anodise darker and less uniformly; 7075 hard-anodises well.
  • Protect copper-bearing alloys. 2011, 2014, 2024 and 7075 need anodising, painting or a corrosion-resistant design in damp or marine service.
  • Weld only weldable alloys. 6061, 6082 and 5083 weld readily; 2011, 2024 and 7075 are generally not fusion-welded for structural joints.
  • Buy the right diameter. Allow enough stock for clean-up on extruded bar, but avoid ordering much larger than needed; each extra few millimetres of diameter adds weight quickly because weight rises with the square of diameter.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) round bar: frequently asked questions

What is the most common aluminium round bar alloy?

6061-T6 (or T6511) worldwide, and 6082-T6 in Europe. Both give a good balance of strength, machinability, weldability and corrosion resistance at moderate cost.

Which aluminium round bar is strongest?

Among common grades, 7075-T6 has the highest strength, with typical tensile strength around 570 MPa, followed by 2014-T6 and 2024. For thick, highly loaded aerospace parts 7050 is also used.

Which aluminium bar is best for CNC turning?

2011-T3 is the classic free-cutting grade and produces short chips at high speed. 6262 is an alternative with better corrosion resistance. 6061 and 7075 also machine well but give longer chips on lathes.

What is the difference between extruded and cold-finished round bar?

Extruded bar is hot-formed through a die and is the economical choice for parts that will be fully machined. Cold-finished bar is drawn, turned or ground after extrusion for tighter diameter tolerance, better roundness and a bright surface.

How do I calculate the weight of an aluminum round bar?

Weight in kg per metre equals diameter in mm squared × 0.0021206 for 6xxx alloys. A Ø50 mm bar weighs about 5.30 kg/m. Multiply by about 1.04 for 7075.

Can aluminium round bar be welded?

6061, 6082, 6063 and the 5xxx alloys weld readily by TIG or MIG. 2011, 2024 and 7075 are generally unsuitable for structural fusion welding.
