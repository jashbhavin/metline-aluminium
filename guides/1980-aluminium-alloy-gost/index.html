1980 (V48-4) Aluminium Alloy: GOST Grade Guide

1980, known in older documents as V48-4 (В48-4), is a Russian heat-treatable, weldable Al-Zn-Mg alloy developed for welded and riveted structures that work in seawater. It is used only in the quenched and artificially aged condition, 1980T1, where sheet guarantees about 365 MPa tensile and 295 MPa yield strength. This guide explains the grade, its standards, properties and the checks to make before you substitute or order it.

Overview

What is 1980 aluminium alloy?

1980 is a four-digit designation in the Russian system for wrought aluminium. The leading 1 marks an aluminium-base alloy and the 9 places it in the Al-Zn-Mg family, the same chemical group as Western 7020 and 7039. The letter-and-number name V48-4 (В48-4) is its older laboratory designation, and both names still appear on drawings and certificates.

The alloy was developed by the Russian shipbuilding materials institute (CRISM "Prometey") as a high-strength aluminium that can still be fusion welded. That combination is unusual: most aluminium alloys that reach yield strengths around 300 MPa are copper-bearing 2xxx or 7xxx aerospace grades, which crack when welded and corrode quickly in seawater. 1980 keeps copper very low and relies on zinc and magnesium for strength, with manganese, chromium and zirconium to control grain structure and resist stress-corrosion cracking.

In practice, 1980 sits between the non-heat-treatable marine alloys such as 1561 (AMg61) and 5083, which weld easily but are limited to roughly 130 – 200 MPa yield, and the much stronger but non-weldable 1941 and 1943 Al-Zn-Mg-Cu alloys from the same institute, which are reserved for riveted structures.

Key points for engineers and buyers

  • Used in service only as 1980T1: solution treated, quenched and given a two-stage artificial age
  • Weldable; the strength and corrosion resistance of welds can be restored by a special post-weld heat treatment of the structure
  • Designed for welded and riveted hulls, decks and superstructures working in seawater
  • No exact ISO, EN or ASTM equivalent: any substitute needs approval from the designer or classification body
Standards

GOST and OST documents that cover 1980

1980 is not listed in the general interstate grade standard for wrought aluminium (GOST 4784), which mainly covers grades with international counterparts. It is instead specified through shipbuilding product standards and industry (OST) documents. Always confirm which document and edition governs your order, because acceptance values differ between them.

Fabrication

Welding, heat treatment and corrosion behaviour

Weldability

Al-Zn-Mg alloys are among the few high-strength aluminium families that weld well by MIG and TIG. Low copper and zirconium additions reduce hot cracking. Filler choice and welding procedure must follow the project's approved procedure specification.

Natural re-ageing

A useful property of Al-Zn-Mg alloys is that the heat-affected zone partly recovers strength by natural ageing at room temperature over the weeks after welding, unlike 6xxx alloys.

Post-weld heat treatment

For 1980T1 the developer states that weld strength and corrosion resistance can be brought back to base-metal level by a special heat treatment of the welded structure.

Stress corrosion

All high-strength Al-Zn-Mg alloys can be sensitive to stress-corrosion cracking, particularly in the short-transverse direction. The two-stage age of T1 is designed to improve resistance; avoid high sustained tensile stresses across the thickness.

Seawater service

The alloy is intended for structures working in seawater. Exposed welds and crevices are normally protected by coatings, and contact with copper alloys or carbon steel must be insulated.

Machining and forming

In T1 the alloy machines cleanly. Cold forming is limited at full strength; tight bends are better made before final ageing, following the governing process documents.

Substitution

Equivalents and how to evaluate a substitute

1980 has no one-to-one counterpart in ISO, EN or ASTM. The nearest Western alloys belong to the weldable Al-Zn-Mg group, but their zinc-to-magnesium balance, strength and corrosion behaviour differ, so a substitute is a design decision, not a purchasing one.

AlloySystemTypical yield (aged)How it compares with 1980
7020 (EN AW-7020)Al-Zn-Mgabout 280 – 300 MPa (T6)Closest common Western grade; lower magnesium, widely used for welded rail and military structures
7039Al-Zn-Mgabout 340 MPa (T64)Higher magnesium, used for armour plate; less common today
7005Al-Zn-Mgabout 290 MPa (T6/T53)Mainly extrusions, e.g. bicycle frames
1561 / 5083Al-Mgabout 130 – 200 MPaNon-heat-treatable marine alloys; easier to weld and more corrosion resistant, but weaker
  1. Compare chemistryCheck Zn, Mg, Cu, Mn, Cr and Zr ranges, not just the series number.
  2. Compare acceptance valuesTensile, yield, elongation and any corrosion or bend tests in the governing standard.
  3. Check welding procedureA different alloy needs a re-qualified welding procedure and filler.
  4. Get approvalWhere drawings name 1980 / V48-4, a substitute must be approved by the designer, customer or classification society.

FAQ

1980 / V48-4 aluminium alloy: frequently asked questions

Are 1980 and V48-4 the same alloy?

Yes. 1980 is the four-digit Russian grade number and V48-4 (В48-4) is the older letter designation of the same Al-Zn-Mg alloy. Both may appear on drawings, so quote whichever your document uses.

What does the T1 temper mean for 1980?

In the Russian system T1 means quenched and artificially aged. For 1980 the ageing is done in two stages to balance strength and stress-corrosion resistance. It is not the same as the American T1 temper.

Can 1980 aluminium be welded?

Yes. It was designed as a weldable high-strength alloy for marine structures. Welds lose some strength, but the developer states that a special post-weld heat treatment restores weld strength and corrosion resistance to base-metal level.

Is there an ASTM or EN equivalent of 1980?

No exact one. 7020 is the closest common Western Al-Zn-Mg alloy, but its composition and guaranteed properties differ, so a substitution must be approved by the designer or classification body.

How strong is 1980T1 compared with 5083?

Sheet of 1980T1 guarantees about 295 MPa yield strength, roughly double that of 5083-H111 and well above 5083-H116. The trade-off is greater sensitivity to stress corrosion and the need for controlled heat treatment.

Can Metline supply 1980 cut to size for export?

Yes. We source sheet, plate, profiles and bar to the stated GOST, OST or TU, cut to size on request, and ship worldwide with mill test certificates and export packing.
