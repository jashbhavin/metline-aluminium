Aluminium Rectangular Tubing: A Practical Buyer's Guide

Aluminium rectangular tubing is a hollow extruded section, most often in 6061-T6 or 6063-T5/T6, that is stiffer in one direction than the other, which makes it the efficient choice for beams, frames and rails loaded mainly from one side. This guide covers alloy selection with correct specified properties, inch and metric size charts with weights and bending properties, tolerances, fabrication and how rectangular tube compares with square and round tube.

Overview

What rectangular tubing is and why engineers use it

A rectangular tube puts most of its metal far from the neutral axis in the deep direction. For the same weight it is much stiffer and stronger in bending about its strong axis than a square or round tube, which is why it is used for beams, chassis rails, gantry members and frame sides.

Almost all aluminium rectangular tubing is extruded. Standard architectural and structural sizes are made in porthole dies, which form solid-state seam welds along the length; these are sound for structural use. Where a truly seamless tube is required, for example for pressure or some aerospace uses, rectangular tube can be drawn from seamless round tube, but this is a specialist and more expensive route.

Flat faces make rectangular tube easy to join: it bolts, rivets and welds flat against plates and other members, accepts T-slot and clamp hardware, and gives a clean, modern look in furniture, shopfitting and architecture. The choice between sharp-corner and radiused-corner tube is mostly aesthetic and practical: sharp corners mate neatly with other flat parts, while radiused corners coat more evenly and are kinder to hands.

Specify these five things on any order

  • Alloy and temper, for example 6061-T6 or 6063-T52
  • Outside width × height × wall, and sharp or radiused corners
  • Length (stock 6 m / 20 ft, or cut lengths) and quantity
  • Standard and tolerance class: ASTM B221 or EN 755-2 with EN 755-9 / EN 12020-2
  • Finish and any processing: anodised, powder coated, cut, drilled, bent or machined

6063 and 6061 values are specified minimums from ASTM B221 for extruded tube. Some catalogues quote 6061-T6 at 290 MPa tensile and 6063-T5 at 190 MPa; those figures belong to rolled sheet or are typical rather than guaranteed values. Design to the specified minimum and check the mill certificate.

For most buyers the decision is between 6063 and 6061. Pick 6063 when appearance, anodising and cost drive the choice and loads are modest. Pick 6061-T6 when the tube carries real structural load, is welded into a frame, or must resist denting. In Europe, 6082-T6 and 6005A-T6 fill the role that 6061-T6 plays elsewhere.

As a quick example from the metric chart: a 50 × 100 × 3 mm rectangular tube weighs about 2.33 kg/m and has a strong-axis section modulus of about 22 cm³ and a moment of inertia of about 112 cm⁴. A 75 × 75 × 3 mm square tube of exactly the same weight has a section modulus of about 20 cm³ and a moment of inertia of about 75 cm⁴. If the load is mainly vertical, the rectangular tube is around 10 % stronger and 50 % stiffer for the same metal.

Design example

Sizing a rectangular tube beam: a worked example

Because aluminium's modulus of elasticity (about 69 GPa) is roughly one third of steel's, an aluminium beam sized only for strength often deflects more than the design allows. In most frames and platforms, stiffness governs, so check deflection first and stress second.

  1. Define the load caseExample: a 2 m simply supported beam carrying a 1 kN point load at mid-span, using 50 × 100 × 3 mm tube in 6061-T6 with the 100 mm side vertical.
  2. Check bending stressBending moment M = P × L / 4 = 500 N·m. With a section modulus of about 22.4 cm³, the bending stress is about 22 MPa, a small fraction of the 240 MPa minimum yield.
  3. Check deflectionDeflection = P × L³ / (48 × E × I) = 1000 × 2000³ / (48 × 69 000 × 1 121 000) ≈ 2.2 mm, or about span/930. That satisfies a typical span/250 or span/360 limit comfortably.
  4. OptimiseWith so much margin, a thinner wall or a 6063-T6 tube may work. Turning the same tube on its side (50 mm vertical) would cut stiffness by about two thirds, so orientation matters as much as size.
  5. Allow for welds and holesIf the beam is welded near mid-span, or drilled through the flanges, reduce the allowable stress accordingly and check the local area.
  • Common mistake: designing an aluminium frame by copying steel sizes. Equal deflection usually needs a deeper section, not just a thicker wall.
  • Common mistake: using catalogue "typical" strengths for design. Use specified minimums from ASTM B221 or EN 755-2.
  • Common mistake: ignoring local wall crushing at supports and bolt points; add crush tubes, bearing plates or a thicker wall.
  • Common mistake: using 6063-T5 for a welded structural frame where 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 was needed.

If your design relies on a sliding fit, such as telescoping tubes, specify the critical dimensions and ask for a fit check, because the standard tolerances of two separate sizes can add up to more clearance than you expect.

For decorative work, also state which faces are visible. Extrusion die lines and seam welds can show through a clear anodised finish, and the die and handling can be arranged to keep them on hidden faces when this is agreed in advance.

Working with rectangular tube

Cutting, bending, welding and finishing

Cutting and machining

Saw with carbide-tipped blades designed for non-ferrous metal and use lubricant. 6061-T6 and 6082-T6 machine more cleanly than 6063, which is softer and can be gummy.

Bending

Bending the "easy way" (about the weak axis) is far simpler than the "hard way". Mandrels or filler support prevent the inner wall from buckling. 6063-T5 and T4 bend more readily than 6061-T6.

Welding

MIG or TIG with ER4043 or ER5356. Expect the heat-affected zone of 6061-T6 to lose a substantial part of its strength; place welds away from the most highly stressed points or design to welded allowables.

Mechanical joining

Bolting through tube walls needs crush tubes or inserts so the wall does not collapse; blind rivets and rivet nuts work well for thin walls.

Anodising

Clear, black and bronze anodising give a hard, durable finish. 6063 gives the most uniform appearance; 6061 can look slightly greyer.

Powder coating

Any colour, with Qualicoat or AAMA quality classes for exterior use. Pre-treatment is essential for adhesion.

Applications

Where aluminium rectangular tubing is used

  • Transport: trailer and truck body frames, rail vehicle interiors, bus structures, EV battery tray frames.
  • Industrial equipment: machine guards, conveyor frames, gantries, linear-motion supports, inspection platforms.
  • Building and architecture: mullions, louvres, pergolas, balustrade posts, partitions and signage frames.
  • Marine: boat rails, gangways, pontoon frames and walkways, usually in 6061-T6 or 5xxx alloys.
  • Solar and energy: mounting rails and support frames for photovoltaic arrays.
  • Furniture and retail: tables, shelving, display systems and lightweight frames.
  • Aerospace and defence: ground-support equipment, shelters and drawn high-strength tube for secondary structure.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) rectangular tubing: frequently asked questions

What is the strongest aluminum rectangular tubing?

Among common structural alloys, 6061-T6 and 6082-T6 are the strongest, with a specified minimum yield of about 240 MPa for 6061-T6 under ASTM B221. 7075 and 2024 tubes are stronger but harder to weld and much more expensive.

Is 6061 or 6063 better for rectangular tube?

6061-T6 is about twice as strong in yield as 6063-T5 and is better for load-bearing and welded frames. 6063 has a smoother surface, anodises more evenly and costs less, so it suits architectural and decorative work.

How much does aluminium rectangular tubing weigh?

Weight in kg/m = 2 × wall × (width + height − 2 × wall) × 0.0027, with dimensions in mm. For example, 2 × 4 × 1/8 in tube weighs about 1.68 lb/ft (2.50 kg/m).

Can rectangular aluminium tubing be bent?

Yes. Bending about the weak axis is straightforward; bending about the strong axis needs mandrels or internal support to stop the walls buckling. Softer tempers bend more easily, and some parts are bent in T4 and then aged.

What lengths are available?

Standard lengths are about 6 m (20 ft) and sometimes 12 ft or 24 ft in imperial markets. Tubes can be cut to any length, with saw-cut tolerances of around ±0.5 mm.

Do you supply rectangular tubing internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies rectangular tubing worldwide from Mumbai, with mill certificates, export packing and shipment on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
