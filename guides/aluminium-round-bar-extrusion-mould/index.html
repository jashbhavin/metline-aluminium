Extrusion sizing guide

Aluminium Round Bar Extrusion Dies and Standard Sizes

Extruded aluminium round bar is produced by forcing a hot billet through a solid die with a circular opening, so each diameter needs its own die (often called a mould). Choosing a diameter that extruders already hold dies for avoids a tooling charge and a die trial on the first order. This guide lists common metric and inch round bar sizes with their weights, explains how round bar dies work, and shows how alloy, press size and tolerance affect what can be supplied.

Overview

What is a round bar extrusion die?

A round bar die is a disc of hardened hot-work tool steel, usually H13 (1.2344), with one or more circular openings machined through it. The opening has a short, polished "bearing" land that sizes the bar and a relieved back section that lets the bar leave without dragging. Because there is no mandrel or bridge, round bar dies are solid dies: the simplest and most robust type of extrusion tool.

Even a simple die has to be designed for the alloy and press, machined, hardened, nitrided and tried on the press before production metal is accepted. The first trial may show an oval bar, surface lines or uneven flow, and the bearing is then corrected. For that reason extruders keep libraries of existing round bar dies in the diameters the market uses most, and a buyer who picks one of those sizes can go straight to production.

When the diameter is new to the extruder, a one-time die charge is normally quoted and the die then stays available for repeat orders. Because round dies are inexpensive compared with complex hollow profile dies, the charge is rarely decisive for large orders, but it matters for samples and small batches, and the die trial adds time to the first delivery. Picking a standard diameter is therefore the simplest way to cut cost and lead time.

Before you fix a round bar diameter

  • Check the size charts below and design to a standard metric or inch diameter where you can
  • If the part is machined anyway, order the next standard size up rather than a new die for an odd diameter
  • For tight diameter tolerance or a bright surface, consider cold-finished bar instead of a new extrusion die
  • State alloy and temper with the diameter: 6063 and 7075 of the same size may run on different dies and presses
  • For very large diameters, ask whether extruded, forged or cast-and-machined bar is the better route
Process

Single-hole and multi-hole round bar dies

Single-hole dies

One opening per die. Used for medium and large diameters, where the cross-section already takes a large share of the billet. The bar runs straight from the die centre, which gives very even flow and good roundness.

Multi-hole dies

Two, four, six or more openings in one die. Used for small rod, where a single opening would give an extrusion ratio too high for the press. The holes are arranged symmetrically so metal flows out of each one at the same speed.

Bearing length

The polished land that sizes the bar. Longer bearings slow metal flow and improve surface on soft alloys; shorter bearings suit hard alloys that need less friction.

Die steel and nitriding

H13 tool steel is hardened and then nitrided to give a hard, wear-resistant bearing surface. Dies are re-nitrided periodically during their life.

Extrusion ratio

Container area divided by total die-opening area. Too high and the press stalls or the surface tears; too low and the bar may lack proper grain refinement. Die and press are matched to keep the ratio in a sensible range.

Press size

Small rods run on small presses with small billets; diameters above roughly 150 – 200 mm need large containers and high-tonnage presses, which fewer extruders have.

Hard alloys such as 2014, 2024 and 7075 need more pressure and run far slower than 6060 or 6063, so the same diameter may be extruded on a larger press or with fewer holes in the die. That is why a supplier may quote a common diameter quickly in 6082 but need a different die for 7075.

Weight calculation

How to calculate round bar weight

Round bar weight formulas

Metric
kg/m = d² (mm) × 0.0021206 for density 2.70 g/cm³
Imperial
lb/ft = d² (in) × 0.9189 for density 0.0975 lb/in³
Worked example, metric
Ø50 mm 6082: 50 × 50 × 0.0021206 = 5.30 kg/m; a 3 m bar weighs 15.9 kg
Worked example, inch
2 in 6061: 4 × 0.9189 = 3.68 lb/ft; a 12 ft bar weighs about 44 lb
Alloy correction
Multiply the 6xxx weight by about 1.03 for 2011/2024, 1.04 for 7075, 0.985 for 5083

Small differences between catalogue weights from different suppliers usually come from the density each one assumes. For freight planning, add packing weight and allow for the actual length tolerance. The aluminium bar weight calculator handles any diameter and length.

Temper "51" suffixes (T6511, T4511) indicate the bar has been stretched after quenching to relieve stress and straighten it, which matters for bars that will be machined heavily. Very large diameters may be supplied as T6 without stretching if the press stretcher cannot handle the section; check this for parts that must stay straight after machining.

Tolerances

Diameter tolerances, straightness and surface

Extruded round bar is supplied to the tolerance tables of the ordering standard. Tolerances widen as the diameter grows and are tighter for the softer, easy-to-extrude alloys.

Standards for extruded and drawn round bar

Europe, extruded
EN 755-2 (mechanical properties) and EN 755-3 (round bar tolerances on dimensions and form)
Europe, cold-drawn
EN 754-2 and EN 754-3; tighter diameter tolerance and better surface than extruded bar
USA, extruded
ASTM B221 with dimensional tolerances from ANSI H35.2
USA, cold-finished
ASTM B211, used for precision and free-machining rod
Straightness
Per the relevant standard; stretched tempers are straighter, and roll straightening is available
Ovality
Included in the diameter tolerance; most critical on large single-hole bars and short die life

If you need tolerances tighter than extruded bar can hold, for example h9 or h11 fits for direct use in bearings or collets, specify cold-finished or precision-ground bar rather than a new extrusion die. See our precision ground bar guide.

Ordering

How to order round bar from a standard die

  1. Pick the sizeChoose a diameter from the charts, or send yours and ask for the nearest standard die size.
  2. Specify the materialAlloy, temper, standard (EN 755, ASTM B221, ASTM B211, AMS where applicable), length and length tolerance.
  3. Add processingCut-to-length blanks, chamfering, ultrasonic testing or anodising if needed.
  4. Confirm documentsMill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 with chemistry and mechanical properties for the cast and batch.
  5. Packing and deliveryBundled and wrapped bars in wooden cases or on pallets, shipped on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms worldwide.

FAQ

Round bar extrusion dies: frequently asked questions

Why does a non-standard diameter cost more?

Every diameter needs its own die. If no die exists, one has to be made and trialled, which adds a one-time tooling charge and time to the first order. Standard diameters run from dies the extruder already holds.

What is the largest aluminium round bar that can be extruded?

Diameters up to about 300 – 350 mm are extruded on large presses, though only some presses can handle them. Larger bars are usually forged or machined from cast billet.

Can I order a metric bar when my drawing is in inches?

Only if the dimension allows it. A 25 mm bar is 0.4 mm smaller than a 1 in bar, so it will not fit a 1 in bore or collet. Order the inch size from an inch die when fit matters, or machine down from the next larger metric bar.

Why do different suppliers list different weights for the same diameter?

They assume different densities. 6xxx alloys are about 2.70 g/cm³, 2011 and 2024 about 2.78 – 2.83 g/cm³ and 7075 about 2.81 g/cm³, so weight per metre varies by up to about 4 % for the same size.

Is extruded bar accurate enough for precision parts?

For parts that will be machined, yes. If the bar surface is used as-supplied in a bearing or collet fit, choose cold-finished (ASTM B211, EN 754-3) or precision-ground bar for tighter tolerance and a better surface.

Which aluminum alloys can be extruded into round bar?

Almost all wrought alloys: 6060, 6063, 6061, 6082 and 6005 extrude most easily; 2011, 2014, 2024 and 7075 need slower speeds and more pressure; 5083 and 6101 are also produced as round bar for marine and electrical use.
