Aluminium Solar Absorber Sheet & Strip

Solar absorber sheet is thin, high-purity aluminium coil, usually 1060 or 1100 at around 0.4 mm, that forms the heat-collecting plate inside flat-plate, air and PV/T solar collectors. It is supplied either bare, for manufacturers who apply their own coating, or with a selective PVD coating that absorbs about 95 % of sunlight while radiating only a few per cent of the heat back. Metline supplies both forms in coil, slit strip and cut sheet to collector makers worldwide.

Overview

What solar absorber sheet does in a collector

In a solar thermal collector, the absorber is the dark plate under the glass. It converts sunlight into heat and passes that heat into the fluid tubes (risers) bonded to its back, or into air flowing past it. Its two jobs, absorbing radiation and conducting heat sideways to the tubes, define what the material must do.

Aluminium does the second job well. 1060 and 1100 have high thermal conductivity, low density and excellent formability, so a thin sheet spreads heat efficiently to the risers while keeping the collector light. The first job is done by the surface: bare aluminium reflects most sunlight, so the absorber is coated, either with a black paint or, in modern collectors, with a spectrally selective coating.

Metline supplies two products: bare aluminium substrate coil for coating lines and absorber makers who coat in-house, and selective-coated absorber coil ready for laser or ultrasonic welding to riser tubes. In both cases the aluminium is supplied as coil, slit strip or cut sheet; the collector itself is made by the customer.

How it works

Why a selective coating beats ordinary black paint

Any hot surface radiates heat as infrared. A black paint absorbs sunlight well, but it is also an efficient infrared emitter, so at operating temperature it loses a large share of its heat by radiation. A selective coating is engineered to be "black" in the visible and near-infrared range of sunlight (roughly 0.3 – 2.5 µm) and "shiny" in the longer infrared range (beyond about 3 µm) where a 60 – 120 °C plate radiates.

SurfaceSolar absorptance αThermal emittance εEffect in the collector
Bare 1060 / 1100 aluminiumLow (reflects most sunlight)Very lowNot usable as an absorber without a coating
Black paint (non-selective)High, about 0.90 – 0.95High, about 0.85 – 0.90Absorbs well but loses much heat by radiation, especially at higher temperatures
Selective black paintAbout 0.90 – 0.93Moderate, about 0.3 – 0.5Lower-cost improvement for low-temperature collectors
PVD selective coatingAbout 0.93 – 0.96Low, about 0.04 – 0.10High efficiency, particularly when the plate runs well above ambient

Values are typical published ranges for commercial coatings; the actual α and ε of a coated batch are given on its optical test report.

PVD (physical vapour deposition) coatings are produced in roll-to-roll vacuum lines using magnetron sputtering, electron-beam evaporation or both. The usual structure is a thin infrared-reflecting base (the aluminium itself or a metal layer), a cermet absorbing layer of metal particles in a ceramic matrix, often titanium or chromium based, and a transparent anti-reflection top layer. Because the process is dry and vacuum based, it avoids the liquid chemistry and wastewater of older black chrome or black nickel electroplating.

The gain from selectivity grows with temperature difference. For a domestic hot water collector at 60 °C on a cold, sunny day, cutting emittance from about 0.9 to about 0.05 removes most of the radiative loss from the plate, which raises efficiency noticeably at mid and high operating temperatures and in low irradiance.

Material choice

Why 1060 and 1100 are used, and how aluminium compares with copper

Commercially pure aluminium is chosen because it combines the highest thermal conductivity of the aluminium alloys with very good formability and a clean, uniform surface for coating. Alloying elements that raise strength also lower conductivity, and an absorber does not need structural strength.

Property1060 aluminium1100 aluminiumCopper (C11000 / Cu-ETP)
Aluminium or copper content≥ 99.60 % Al≥ 99.00 % Al (with 0.05 – 0.20 % Cu)≥ 99.90 % Cu
Thermal conductivity (typical)About 230 W/m·KAbout 220 W/m·KAbout 390 W/m·K
Density2.70 g/cm³2.71 g/cm³8.9 g/cm³
Conductivity per unit massHighHighAbout half that of aluminium
FormabilityExcellentExcellentExcellent
Relative material costLowLowHigh

Copper conducts heat better, so for the same fin efficiency a copper absorber can be thinner. Aluminium compensates with thickness: a 0.4 mm aluminium absorber performs comparably to a thinner copper sheet, at a fraction of the weight and material cost. That is why aluminium absorbers, often welded to copper or aluminium risers, now dominate flat-plate collector production.

1060 has slightly higher purity and conductivity; 1100 has a small copper addition that gives a little more strength. Both coat and weld well. Choose by the coating line's experience and the forming required.

Applications

Applications: flat-plate, air, heat pipe and PV/T collectors

Flat-plate collectors

A full-plate absorber or several fin strips are laser or ultrasonically welded to a harp or serpentine of risers. The most common use of absorber coil.

Solar air collectors

The absorber heats air flowing behind or through it for space heating and crop drying; profiled or perforated absorber sheet increases heat transfer.

Heat pipe and evacuated tube collectors

Aluminium fins with a selective coating clamp around heat pipes inside glass tubes or in heat pipe manifold designs.

PV/T (photovoltaic-thermal)

An aluminium thermal absorber is laminated or bonded to the back of PV cells to remove heat, raising electrical efficiency and producing warm water or air.

Process heat and pool heating

Larger collector fields for industrial low-temperature heat, and unglazed absorbers for swimming pools.

Building-integrated collectors

Facade and roof-integrated solar thermal panels where low weight is an advantage.

Compatibility with each system depends on operating temperature, stagnation temperature, joining method and the corrosion environment. In particular, aluminium should not carry the heat transfer fluid directly unless the fluid chemistry is controlled; in most collectors the fluid runs in copper or protected aluminium risers and the absorber stays dry inside the collector box.

Fabrication

Joining, forming and handling absorber sheet

  • Laser welding: the standard joining method for full-plate absorbers; a line weld bonds the sheet to the riser. Coating suppliers specify parameters so the weld seam does not damage the coating beyond a narrow line.
  • Ultrasonic welding: a solid-state process widely used to join aluminium fins to copper risers without melting, avoiding brittle Al-Cu intermetallics.
  • Roll-bond and clamping: some designs press-fit fins around tubes or use roll-bonded panels with integral channels.
  • Forming: 1060 and 1100 in H14 bend and emboss easily; tighter forming may call for O or H24 temper.
  • Handling: coated surfaces scratch easily; use gloves, keep the coated side protected and avoid stacking sheets face to face without interleaving.
  • Storage: keep coils dry and above the dew point to prevent condensation stains, particularly after sea transport.

ISO 22975-3 is the reference method for assessing the long-term durability of selective absorbers in vented flat-plate collectors, and it underpins absorber qualification in the Solar Keymark scheme. Ask for the coating's qualification evidence alongside the batch optical report. Export packing uses moisture-barrier wrapping, desiccant, edge protectors and eye-to-sky or eye-to-wall coil positioning on wooden pallets or cases, so that coatings and coil edges arrive undamaged.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) solar absorber sheet: frequently asked questions

What absorptance and emittance can a selective coating on aluminium reach?

Commercial PVD selective coatings on aluminium typically reach a solar absorptance of about 0.94 – 0.96 and a thermal emittance of about 0.04 – 0.10, depending on the coating type. The actual values for each batch are reported on its optical test certificate.

Is aluminium as good as copper for an absorber?

Copper conducts heat better, but aluminium is about one third of the density and much cheaper. A slightly thicker aluminium absorber gives comparable fin efficiency at lower weight and cost, which is why aluminium absorbers are now widely used in flat-plate collectors.

Why is 0.4 mm the usual thickness?

It balances lateral heat conduction to the risers, weldability and weight. Thinner sheet saves material but conducts less heat sideways, so riser spacing must shrink; thicker sheet adds weight and cost. Other thicknesses from about 0.3 to 0.6 mm can be supplied.

Can the absorber be used in PV/T panels?

Yes. Aluminium absorber sheet is used as the thermal layer behind PV cells, laminated or bonded to the module. Specify the lamination process so the surface and temper suit it.

Do you supply coated absorber or bare substrate?

Both. Bare 1060 or 1100 substrate coil suits customers with their own coating lines; selective-coated coil suits collector makers who weld it directly to risers.

Which test confirms the coating will last?

ISO 22975-3 assesses the durability of selective absorber surfaces under high temperature, water condensation and high humidity with sulphur dioxide, using changes in absorptance and emittance as the failure criterion, and is used for Solar Keymark absorber qualification.
