Fiberglass Honeycomb Panel with Aluminium Core

A fiberglass honeycomb panel is a sandwich of two thin glass-fibre reinforced plastic (FRP) skins bonded to a lightweight aluminium honeycomb core. The result is a flat, stiff, corrosion-resistant panel weighing roughly 3 – 6 kg/m², used for truck and reefer bodies, rail interiors, marine bulkheads, cleanroom walls and equipment housings. This guide explains how the panels are built, how to specify them and where their limits lie.

Overview

What is a fiberglass honeycomb panel?

Sandwich panels work like an I-beam spread over an area. The two skins carry bending loads as tension and compression, while the core keeps them apart and carries shear. Doubling the core thickness roughly quadruples the bending stiffness for only a small increase in weight, which is why a 20 mm honeycomb panel can be as stiff as a much heavier solid sheet.

In an FRP honeycomb panel, the skins are glass-fibre fabric impregnated with epoxy (or sometimes polyester or phenolic) resin, and the core is an expanded aluminium honeycomb made from 3003 or 5052 foil. The FRP faces resist impact, scratching, moisture and chemicals and can be gel-coated, textured or coloured. The aluminium core provides high shear and compressive strength, does not absorb water and does not creep under sustained load the way polypropylene or paper cores can.

Compared with all-aluminium honeycomb panels, FRP skins give better resistance to dents, a non-conductive and thermally less conductive surface, and an easy-to-clean finish for hygienic or refrigerated spaces. Compared with panels on plastic cores, the aluminium core keeps the panel flat and dimensionally stable over a wide temperature range.

Typical reasons to choose FRP over aluminium skins

  • Better dent and impact resistance for vehicle walls and floors
  • Smooth, washable gel-coat surface for food, cold-chain and cleanroom use
  • Electrically insulating faces and lower thermal bridging than metal skins
  • No corrosion of the skin in salt spray or chemical splash zones

Performance

How FRP honeycomb panels perform

Stiffness and strength

Bending stiffness is set mainly by skin thickness and core depth; strength by skin strength, core shear strength and bond quality. Panel makers verify these by beam flexure testing (ASTM C393) on the actual construction.

Compression and point loads

The honeycomb's flatwise compressive strength (ASTM C365) decides how well floors resist wheels, feet and pallet jacks. Smaller cells and heavier foil raise it.

Moisture

Neither the aluminium core nor cured epoxy absorbs significant water, but edges must be sealed so water cannot travel through the cells. Unsealed cut edges are the most common cause of panel failure outdoors.

Corrosion

FRP skins do not corrode. 5052 cores resist humidity and salt spray well; for marine use specify corrosion-protected core foil and sealed edges.

Thermal behaviour

FRP faces conduct heat far less than aluminium skins. For insulated reefer bodies, honeycomb panels are combined with foam layers because the open aluminium core itself is not an insulator.

Long-term load

Aluminium honeycomb does not creep at normal service temperatures, so floors and shelves keep their flatness under sustained loads.

Fire safety

Fire performance: what to check

Fire is where FRP panels need the most care. The aluminium core is non-combustible, but epoxy and polyester resins burn and produce smoke. Standard FRP honeycomb panels are therefore not suitable where non-combustible materials are required, for example on many building facades. Flame-retardant resin systems and phenolic skins improve performance substantially, and the right test depends on the application:

ApplicationTypical fire standardNote
Railway vehicles (Europe)EN 45545-2Hazard levels HL1 – HL3; flame-retardant or phenolic skins usually needed

Always ask for a fire test report on the exact construction (skin, resin, adhesive, core and thickness). A report for a different build does not transfer.

Working with panels

Cutting, fixing and edge sealing

  1. CuttingCut with fine-tooth carbide or diamond saws, or CNC routers with dust extraction. Glass-fibre dust is abrasive and an irritant, so use extraction and protective equipment.
  2. Inserts and fixingsThrough-bolts crush the core unless the hole is reinforced. Use potted inserts (resin-filled core around a threaded insert) or solid edge and hardpoint fills where fixings carry load.
  3. Edge closureSeal every cut edge with an aluminium U-profile, edge filler resin or sealant so moisture cannot enter the cells.
  4. Joining panelsJoin with extruded aluminium H-profiles, corner profiles, or bonded splines. Structural adhesives spread load better than discrete fasteners.
  5. Grooving and foldingV-grooving the back skin and core lets panels be folded into boxes and corners; the groove is then bonded and sealed.

For more on joining methods and their effect on panel strength, see honeycomb core joining methods.

Applications

Where fiberglass honeycomb panels are used

  • Truck, van and reefer bodies: side walls, roofs and floors where low weight raises payload and FRP faces stand up to loading damage
  • Rail interiors: wall linings, ceilings, partitions and floors built to EN 45545-2 fire requirements
  • Marine: bulkheads, cabin linings, deck coverings and furniture bases in humid, salty environments
  • Modular and mobile buildings: wall and roof panels for site cabins, shelters and equipment containers
  • Cleanrooms and food plants: washable wall and ceiling panels with smooth gel-coat faces
  • Industrial equipment: housings, guards, maintenance platforms and access panels

A typical example is a delivery-van side wall: a 20 mm panel with 1 mm FRP skins on a 5052 core weighs a little over 5 kg/m², takes rivnut or bonded fixings at potted hardpoints, and gives a smooth face for graphics.

FAQ

Fiberglass honeycomb panel: FAQ

What is the core of a fiberglass honeycomb panel?

In the panels described here the core is expanded aluminium honeycomb in 3003 or 5052 alloy, typically with 6 – 10 mm cells. Some panels on the market use polypropylene, paper or aramid cores, which behave differently in strength, creep and moisture.

How much does an FRP aluminium honeycomb panel weigh?

Roughly 3 – 6 kg/m² for common builds. A 20 mm panel with 1 mm glass/epoxy skins on a medium-density aluminium core weighs about 5.4 kg/m².

Can fiberglass honeycomb panels be used outdoors?

Yes, provided all edges are sealed and the gel coat or surface finish is UV resistant. FRP skins and aluminium cores do not corrode, but water entering through open cut edges must be prevented.

Are FRP honeycomb panels fireproof?

No. The aluminium core does not burn, but standard resins are combustible. Flame-retardant or phenolic skins can meet rail, marine and many interior fire standards; check the test report for the exact construction and application.

Is a fiberglass panel better than an aluminium-skinned honeycomb panel?

It depends on the use. FRP skins resist dents and chemicals and give a washable, insulating surface. Aluminium skins are stiffer for the same thickness, non-combustible as a face material, and easier to coat for architectural finishes.

Can panels be cut and drilled on site?

Yes, with carbide or diamond tools and dust extraction. Seal cut edges afterwards and use potted inserts or reinforced hardpoints for any fixing that carries load.
