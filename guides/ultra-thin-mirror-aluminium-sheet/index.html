0.08 mm Ultra-Thin Mirror Aluminium Sheet

Ultra-thin mirror aluminium is bright-rolled, high-purity aluminium in gauges from about 0.08 mm, finished by anodising or vacuum coating to give total light reflectance from around 86 % up to 95 %. At 0.08 mm it uses about 84 % less metal than a 0.5 mm mirror sheet, which makes it attractive for reflector liners, laminates and decorative films. This guide explains grades, finishes, measurement and where thin gauges work best.

Overview

What is ultra-thin mirror aluminium?

Mirror aluminium is sheet or coil whose surface has been rolled on polished work rolls to an extremely smooth, bright finish and then protected or enhanced so that it reflects light specularly, like a mirror. "Ultra-thin" refers to gauges well below the 0.3 – 1.0 mm normally used for luminaire reflectors, down to about 0.08 mm.

At 0.08 mm the material is, strictly, in foil territory: EN 546 treats aluminium thinner than 0.2 mm as foil, and ASTM uses a limit of about 0.15 mm. In practice it is still sold as "mirror sheet" because it is made by the same bright-rolling and finishing routes and is used for the same optical purposes, either self-supporting in small parts or laminated to a carrier.

The economic case is simple. Aluminium weighs 2.70 kg per square metre per millimetre, so a 0.08 mm sheet weighs 0.216 kg/m² against 1.35 kg/m² for 0.5 mm. Thin gauges cost more per kilogram to roll and finish, but far less metal is needed per square metre of reflective surface, so for laminated or lined products the cost per square metre is often much lower.

When a thin mirror gauge makes sense

  • The mirror surface is laminated to plastic, paper, foam, board or composite panel
  • It lines or wraps an existing structure, such as a reflector housing or light pipe
  • Weight matters, as in portable, automotive or aerospace interiors
  • Large areas are needed and the sheet does not have to carry load itself

Reflectance figures depend on the measurement standard. European suppliers usually quote total reflectance to DIN 5036-3 with an integrating sphere, while some data sheets use ASTM E1651. Diffuse (scattered) reflectance is quoted separately: a true mirror has low diffuse reflectance, while satin and "hammertone" grades scatter more light on purpose. Always compare grades measured to the same method.

Polishing, electropolishing and chemical brightening improve smoothness and brightness; anodising and lacquer protect the surface. Coloured mirror finishes (gold, champagne, rose gold and similar) are produced by coloured lacquers or coatings over the bright aluminium.

The rule of thumb: the purer the aluminium, the brighter the anodised mirror, because alloying elements and intermetallic particles show up as haze in the oxide layer. Claims of 94 – 95 % reflectance from ordinary alloyed sheet without a PVD layer should be questioned and checked against a measured data sheet.

Performance

Durability, ageing and heat

Does reflectance drop over time?

Yes, slowly. Dust, fingerprints, humidity and pollutants reduce it, and unprotected aluminium forms a thicker oxide over time. Anodised and PVD grades with a sealed top layer keep reflectance far better than bare or lightly lacquered material.

Cleaning

Use soft cloths with mild, neutral detergent and clean water. Abrasives, alkaline cleaners and solvents can damage anodised or lacquered layers.

Heat

Anodised reflectors tolerate the operating temperatures of most luminaires, but lacquered and coloured coatings have lower limits. Check the coating's rated temperature for lamps or solar receivers.

Outdoor and solar use

For outdoor or solar concentrator use, choose a grade with a weather-resistant protective layer tested for UV and humidity; standard indoor reflector grades can degrade outdoors.

Corrosion

Salt-spray and humidity test results should be part of the data sheet when the sheet will be used near the coast or in humid interiors.

Handling

Thin gauges dent and crease easily; keep the protective film on until after forming and installation.

Working with it

Cutting, forming and laminating thin mirror sheet

At 0.08 – 0.2 mm, mirror aluminium can be slit, guillotined, die-cut and laser-cut, but its edges are sharp and the surface marks easily. Leave the protective film on during all cutting and forming, and use clean, polished tooling to avoid pick-up marks. For bends, the anodic layer of anodised grades can craze if the radius is too tight; follow the supplier's minimum bend radius for the specific grade and temper.

Lamination is the most common route for the thinnest gauges. Thin mirror aluminium is bonded to plastic sheet, honeycomb or foam panels, board or composite panels with pressure-sensitive or heat-activated adhesives. The bond side should be primed or treated for adhesion, and the laminate built so it does not curl when the temperature changes.

Applications

Applications of ultra-thin mirror aluminium

  • Lighting reflectors: liners for downlights, troffers, high-bays and LED optics, where thin gauge reduces weight and cost
  • Tubular daylight systems: highly reflective liners for light pipes that carry daylight into buildings
  • Solar thermal and concentrators: reflective surfaces for collectors and booster reflectors, using weather-resistant grades
  • Architectural decoration: mirror faces for composite panels, ceilings, columns and interior trims
  • Automotive and transport interiors: trim strips, badges and lamp reflectors
  • Signage and displays: mirror letters, point-of-sale displays and decorative labels

For thicker reflector sheet and optical grades, see the high-reflectivity aluminium sheet guide.

Selection

How to choose the right mirror aluminium

  1. Define the reflectance targetDecorative use rarely needs more than 85 %; efficient luminaires use 86 – 88 % anodised or 95 % PVD-enhanced grades.
  2. Decide whether the sheet carries loadIf it must be self-supporting, choose 0.3 mm or more; if it is laminated or a liner, 0.08 – 0.2 mm often works.
  3. Match the environmentIndoor, humid, outdoor or high-temperature use each needs a suitable protective layer.
  4. Check forming needsTight bends and deep drawing need a softer temper and a grade rated for forming.
  5. Compare like with likeAsk for reflectance to the same standard (DIN 5036-3 or ASTM E1651) and for a sample before ordering.

The most common mistakes we see are buying on a headline reflectance number without knowing the test method, using an indoor reflector grade outdoors, and ordering a gauge that is too thin for a self-supporting part. Another is removing the protective film before forming, which almost always leaves handling marks on the mirror face. Agree the finish, film type, coil inside diameter and packing (sealed, desiccated cases for sea freight) at the quotation stage so the material arrives ready for your line.

FAQ

Ultra-thin mirror aluminium (aluminum) sheet: frequently asked questions

How thin can mirror aluminium sheet be?

Mirror-finished aluminium is available from about 0.08 mm. At that gauge it is usually laminated or used as a liner, because it cannot support itself over large spans.

What reflectance can I expect?

Lacquered bright-rolled sheet reaches roughly 80 – 86 %, anodised high-purity mirror about 85 – 88 %, and PVD-enhanced aluminium about 95 % total reflectance, measured to DIN 5036-3.

What is the difference between mirror and high-reflectivity aluminium?

Standard mirror aluminium is bright-rolled and protected for appearance and moderate reflection. High-reflectivity grades use high-purity substrates, brightening and anodising, often with PVD reflection-enhancing layers, to reach 90 – 95 % for optical and lighting uses.

Does the reflectivity of mirror aluminium change over time?

It declines slowly with dirt, humidity and surface oxidation. Sealed anodised or PVD protective layers and regular gentle cleaning keep the loss small.

Is thin mirror aluminium really cheaper?

Per square metre it often is, because far less metal is needed. Per kilogram it costs more, so compare quotes per square metre including any carrier or lamination.

Can mirror aluminium be bent?

Yes, within the minimum bend radius for the grade and temper. Tight bends can craze the anodic layer, so keep the protective film on and use softer tempers for deep forming.

Need thin mirror aluminium for reflectors or laminates?
