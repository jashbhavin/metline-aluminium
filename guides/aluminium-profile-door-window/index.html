Building profiles guide

Aluminium Profiles for Doors and Windows

Aluminium doors and windows are assembled by fabricators from extruded profiles, usually 6063 or 6060 in the T5 or T6 temper, with wall thicknesses of roughly 1.2 to 3 mm and an anodised, powder-coated or PVDF finish. This guide covers the main profile families, alloy and temper choices, finish classes, tolerances and packing, so that fabricators and project buyers can specify the aluminium correctly. Metline supplies the profiles and accessories; the windows and doors themselves are made by the fabricator or system company.

Overview

What door and window profiles have to achieve

A window or door frame is a set of extruded aluminium sections cut, machined and joined at the corners, with glass, gaskets, hardware and, in insulated systems, polyamide thermal barriers. The profile shape does most of the engineering work: it carries wind load, holds the glazing, drains water and locates the seals.

Aluminium displaced steel and wood in most commercial glazing because extrusion can put complex functional geometry into a single section: screw ports, gasket grooves, glazing beads, drainage chambers and hardware channels are all formed in one pass through the die. The metal does not rot, rust or swell, takes durable decorative finishes, and is fully recyclable at the end of the building's life.

Compared with uPVC, aluminium frames are far stiffer, so sightlines can be slimmer and openings larger; the trade-off is thermal conductivity, which is why insulated (thermal-break) profiles now dominate in cold and temperate climates. For a broader introduction see our separate window profile guide and door profile guide; this page focuses on the full range of profile families and how to specify them.

Types

Main families of door and window profiles

Thermal-break profiles

Inner and outer aluminium sections joined by glass-fibre-reinforced polyamide strips (typically PA66 GF25) that are inserted and rolled in. Frame U-values fall sharply; used for casement, tilt-and-turn, lift-and-slide and entrance doors in insulated buildings.

Non-insulated profiles

Single-piece sections for warm climates, interior partitions, shopfronts and secondary glazing, where thermal performance is not required and cost and slim lines matter.

Sliding window and door profiles

Frames with tracks and interlocks, sashes and rollers channels. Heavy lift-and-slide doors use deeper, thicker sections to carry large glass panels.

Casement and hinged profiles

Outer frame, sash and mullion sections with gasket grooves and Euro-groove hardware channels for hinges and multipoint locks.

Curtain wall and shopfront sections

Mullions, transoms and pressure plates for stick or unitised facades; see the curtain wall profile page.

Louvre and sun-shade profiles

Blade and frame sections for ventilation louvres, sun shading and screens, often aerofoil or Z-shaped and hollow for stiffness.

Roller shutter and garage door slats

Roll-formed or extruded slats, guide rails and end bars. Extruded slats are stronger and used for security shutters.

Accessory profiles

Glazing beads, corner cleats, flyscreen frames, sills, couplers and trims supplied with the main system.

Wood-effect and clad profiles

Standard profiles finished with sublimated woodgrain or combined with timber in aluminium-clad wood systems.

Material

Alloys, tempers and properties

6063 and its European sibling 6060 extrude at high speed through complex hollow dies and give the smooth surface needed for decorative finishes. 6005 and 6082 are used for heavier structural members where higher strength is required.

Alloy and temperMin. tensile strengthMin. yield strengthTypical use
6063-T5150 MPa110 MPaStandard frames, sashes, beads and trims
6063-T6205 MPa170 MPaLarger doors, mullions and wind-loaded members
6060-T5 / T6Slightly below 6063 at the same temperEuropean system profiles; excellent anodising finish
6005-T5 / 6005A-T6Higher than 6063-T6Heavy mullions, sliding door frames, structural sections
6082-T6Highest of the groupStructural supports and brackets rather than visible frames

6063 minimums from ASTM B221 (thickness up to 12.5 mm). European profiles are specified under EN 755-2, whose minimums vary by thickness band; the certificate shows the values met by the lot.

Hardness is the quickest check on site or at goods-in. Webster hardness readings of about 8 – 12 HW are typical for correctly aged 6063-T5; markedly lower readings point to under-ageing and soft profiles that may deflect or let screws strip.

Design

Wall thickness and section design

Wall thickness is set by structural need, by the screw ports and by local rules. Thin walls save metal and cost but reduce stiffness and screw holding, and they show distortion after finishing.

Profile typeCommon wall thicknessWhat drives it
Interior partitions and light frames0.8 – 1.2 mmAppearance and handling only
External window frames and sashes1.2 – 2.0 mmWind load, screw retention, hardware fixing
External doors and sliding door frames1.8 – 3.0 mmDoor weight, hinge loads, abuse resistance
Curtain wall mullions2.0 – 3.5 mm and moreSpan and wind pressure
Louvre blades1.0 – 2.0 mmSpan between supports and wind
  • Several national building standards set minimum wall thicknesses for load-bearing members of external windows and doors; check the rule in the project country before approving a die.
  • Keep walls as uniform as practical: large thickness differences cause uneven cooling, poor flatness and colour variation after anodising.
  • Design screw ports for the fastener size and allow for the coating thickness on visible faces.
  • For thermal-break profiles, the crimping zones (knurled grooves) must be held to tight tolerances or the shear strength of the composite falls.

Specify the finish class, not just a colour. A powder coating to Qualicoat class 1 on a seafront building will chalk and fade years before a class 2 or PVDF system, and anodising thinner than 15 µm is not suitable for exterior use. Agree colour and gloss on approved samples, and request the finish certificate with each delivery.

Logistics

Packing finished profiles for export

Finished profiles are easily scratched, so packing matters as much as the extrusion. Typical arrangements are:

  • Anodised and powder-coated profiles: protective film on visible faces or individual paper or foam wrapping, bundled with shrink-film and wooden battens.
  • Polished or high-gloss profiles: soft interleaving and heat-shrink film on each length.
  • Export: bundles in wooden crates or on steel frames, with desiccant for long sea voyages; labels showing profile number, length, finish, colour and quantity.
  • Lengths: commonly 5.8 – 6.5 m to fit 20 ft and 40 ft containers; cut lengths on request.

Ask for the protective film to be suitable for the outdoor exposure time on site, since some films leave residue if left in sunlight for months.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) door and window profiles: frequently asked questions

Which aluminum alloy is used for window and door profiles?

6063 is the standard alloy worldwide, with 6060 widely used in Europe. Both extrude complex hollow shapes with a smooth surface for anodising or coating. 6005 or 6082 is chosen for heavy structural members.

What is the difference between T5 and T6 profiles?

T5 profiles are cooled at the press and artificially aged; T6 profiles are fully solution treated before ageing and are stronger. 6063-T5 has a minimum yield of 110 MPa and 6063-T6 about 170 MPa under ASTM B221.

How thick should aluminium window profiles be?

External window frames and sashes typically have walls of about 1.2 – 2.0 mm, and external doors 1.8 – 3.0 mm. The right thickness depends on wind load, opening size, hardware and any minimum set by local building rules.

What is a thermal-break profile?

An aluminium profile split into inner and outer parts joined by glass-fibre-reinforced polyamide strips. The polyamide interrupts heat flow through the frame, cutting its U-value and preventing condensation on the inside face.

Is anodising or powder coating better for windows?

Anodising gives a hard, metallic finish that does not peel, best in natural and bronze tones. Powder coating offers any colour and good durability; for coastal or high-rise projects choose Qualicoat class 2 powders or PVDF.

Does Metline make complete windows and doors?

No. Metline supplies the aluminium: extruded profiles to your drawings or system, thermal-break profiles, sheet and accessories, finished and cut to length if needed. The windows and doors are fabricated by your own workshop or system partner.
