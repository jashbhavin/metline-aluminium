4043 and 4047 Aluminium Wire Rod for Welding (O, H12, H14, H18)

4043 (about 5 % silicon) and 4047 (about 12 % silicon) are the two main aluminium-silicon filler alloys. Both give a fluid weld pool and strong resistance to hot cracking; 4047 flows even more freely and is also a brazing alloy. They are supplied as finished MIG and TIG wire, and as wire rod in O, H12, H14 or H18 temper for further drawing or straight-length filler. This guide explains the difference and how to choose.

Overview

What silicon does in a filler alloy

Silicon lowers the melting point of aluminium and narrows its freezing range. In a weld, that means a more fluid pool that wets the joint well and, critically, liquid metal that can feed shrinkage as the weld solidifies, preventing solidification cracks.

Many base alloys, especially the heat-treatable 6xxx series, are crack-sensitive if welded without filler or with a filler of similar chemistry. Diluting the weld with 4043 or 4047 moves the weld metal out of the crack-sensitive composition range. That is why 4043 is among the most widely used aluminium fillers in the world, alongside the magnesium-bearing 5356.

4047 takes the effect further. At 11 – 13 % silicon it is close to the aluminium-silicon eutectic (about 12.6 % Si), so it melts over a narrow range of about 577 – 582 °C. It flows into tight joints, gives minimal shrinkage and is used both as a welding filler (ER4047) and as a brazing filler (BAlSi-4).

Neither 4xxx filler should be used on 5xxx base metals with more than about 2.5 % magnesium (such as 5083 or 5456): the weld forms brittle magnesium silicide. Use 5356 or 5183 for those. And because 4xxx welds are not heat-treatable in the way 6xxx base metal is, an as-welded 6061-T6 joint is designed to a reduced strength, about 165 MPa (24 ksi) minimum tensile in AWS D1.2.

Product forms

Wire rod tempers and what they are used for

O (annealed)

Soft rod for further drawing to fine welding wire, and for coiling straight-length TIG rod with no spring-back.

H12 / H14

Partly work-hardened rod for straight-length TIG filler and for cutting into brazing rings and preforms.

H18 (full hard)

The temper of finished MIG wire: stiff enough to push through liners and contact tips without kinking.

Spooled MIG wire

Usually 0.8, 1.0, 1.2 and 1.6 mm on 0.5 – 7 kg spools or larger drums for robots.

Straight TIG rod

Typically 1.6 – 5.0 mm diameter in about 1 m lengths, marked with the alloy.

Larger rod

Up to about 15 mm for further processing, feedstock and specialised filler.

Porosity in aluminium welds is almost always caused by hydrogen, and the filler surface is a major source. Store wire in a dry, warm area, keep spools in their bags until use and never leave a spool on a machine in a humid shop overnight without cover.

Applications

Where 4043 and 4047 are used

  • Structural and general fabrication: 6061 and 6063 frames, trailers, railings and bicycle frames (4043)
  • Automotive: radiators, condensers, intake manifolds and repairs to cast wheels and housings
  • Heat exchangers and HVAC: torch and furnace brazing of tube-to-header joints (4047)
  • Electronics and power: heat sinks, cold plates and housings where low distortion matters
  • Casting repair: filling defects in silicon-bearing castings
  • Pressure-tight assemblies: tanks and housings where fluidity helps achieve leak-tight seams

FAQ

4043 and 4047 welding wire: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 4043 and 4047?

4043 has about 5 % silicon and is a general-purpose filler. 4047 has about 12 % silicon, melts at a lower, narrower range, flows more freely and is also used as a brazing alloy.

Can I weld 6061 with 4043?

Yes. 4043 is one of the two standard fillers for 6061 and is preferred when crack resistance or post-weld heat treatment matters. 5356 gives higher shear strength and a better colour match after anodising.

Can 4043 or 4047 be used on 5083?

No. On 5xxx alloys with more than about 2.5 % magnesium the weld forms brittle Mg2Si. Use 5183 or 5356.

Why is MIG wire supplied in H18 temper?

Full-hard wire is stiff enough to feed through the gun liner without buckling. Softer tempers are used for drawing stock and straight TIG rods.

Will 4043 welds match the colour of anodised parts?

No. Silicon-rich welds turn grey (4043) or dark grey (4047) when anodised. Use 5356 where appearance after anodising matters.

What causes porosity with aluminium wire?

Hydrogen from moisture, oil or hydrated oxide on the wire or base metal. Keep wire dry and sealed and clean the joint before welding.
