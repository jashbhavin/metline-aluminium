Flat-Oval Aluminium Radiator Tube

Flat-oval radiator tube is the thin, welded aluminium tube that carries coolant through a brazed radiator core. It is roll-formed from brazing-clad strip, high-frequency welded and sized to a precise width, height and wall, so it joins to fins and headers in a single brazing cycle. This guide explains profiles, clad constructions, sizes, quality checks and how to specify tube for a new core or a replacement supply.

Overview

What is a flat-oval radiator tube?

A modern aluminium radiator is a brazed sandwich of three parts: rows of flat tubes that carry coolant, corrugated louvred fins between them that shed heat to the air, and headers (tube plates) that receive the tube ends and connect to the tanks. The tube is the pressure-retaining part, so its profile, wall and weld decide how much heat the core can move and how long it survives.

A flat-oval section has two long parallel faces joined by rounded ends. The flat faces give a large contact area with the fins and a thin coolant channel, which raises the heat-transfer coefficient on the liquid side; the low height keeps the air-side pressure drop down so more air passes through the core. Compared with the round tubes used in older copper-brass radiators, a flat tube of the same cross-sectional area puts far more of its surface against the fins.

Most radiator tube is made by roll-forming brazing-clad aluminium strip into a closed shape and joining the seam by high-frequency (HF) induction welding. The outside clad layer melts during furnace brazing and becomes the filler metal that bonds tube to fin and tube to header. Other tube families, such as folded B-type tube and extruded multi-port microchannel tube, compete for the same job and are compared later in this guide.

What the tube has to deliver

  • Burst and pressure-cycle resistance at a wall of only 0.2 – 0.4 mm
  • A leak-tight weld that survives sizing, bending at the tube ends and brazing
  • Enough clad metal on the outside to form fillets with every fin crest
  • Internal corrosion resistance against glycol coolant and external resistance against road salt
  • Straightness and dimensional consistency so automated core builders can stack thousands of tubes
Dimensions

Flat-oval tube size range and common sections

Tube is described as width × height × wall (W × H × T) in millimetres. Width matches the core depth or one row of a multi-row core; height is the minor dimension that sets coolant channel size; wall is the strip gauge. The ranges below cover passenger-car, commercial-vehicle and off-highway radiators.

Material construction

Brazing-clad constructions: what each layer does

Radiator tube strip is a roll-bonded composite. Each layer is a different alloy with a specific job, and the combination is chosen to suit the brazing process and the coolant and road environment the radiator will see.

Braze clad: 4343 or 4045

Aluminium-silicon alloys that melt below the core. 4343 contains about 6.8 – 8.2 % Si and 4045 about 9.0 – 11.0 % Si; the higher silicon of 4045 gives more fluid filler and larger fillets. The clad is on the outside, where it bonds to fins and header.

Core: 3003 or 3005

The structural layer. 3003 (Al-Mn-Cu) is the standard core. 3005 adds a little magnesium for more strength after brazing, but magnesium must be kept low for flux brazing because it reacts with the fluoride flux.

Water-side liner: 7072

An Al-Zn alloy (about 0.8 – 1.3 % Zn) on the inside of the tube. It is electrochemically more active than the core, so it corrodes preferentially and spreads attack sideways instead of letting a pit run through the wall.

Clad thickness is usually expressed as a percentage of total strip thickness per side, commonly in the range of about 5 – 15 %. Too little braze clad leaves weak fillets; too much causes erosion of the thin core during brazing. Your brazing furnace profile and fin design should be considered together with the clad ratio. Manufacturing

How HF-welded radiator tube is made

  1. Slit clad stripBrazing-clad coil is slit to the exact developed width of the tube section. Width tolerance and edge quality at this stage control weld quality later.
  2. Roll-formA train of forming stands progressively bends the strip into an open oval with the edges meeting at the top.
  3. High-frequency weldAn induction coil heats the strip edges to welding temperature and squeeze rolls forge them together. The outside bead is trimmed or flattened.
  4. Size and straightenSizing rolls bring the tube to final width and height. Straightness matters because core builders stack tubes automatically.
  5. Eddy-current test and cutThe tube passes an eddy-current coil that flags weld defects online, then a flying cut-off produces lengths to the core drawing.
  6. Inspect and packDimensions, burst pressure and weld structure are checked on samples; tubes are bundled and packed to protect the thin clad surfaces.

For water-glycol radiators running at modest pressures, welded flat-oval tube is usually the most economical choice. Where the design pushes wall thickness down to save weight, a folded B-type tube with its internal web keeps pressure strength. For refrigerant circuits, extruded microchannel tube is standard. Applications

Where flat-oval aluminium radiator tube is used

Passenger cars

Single-row radiators with 16 – 26 mm tubes, thin walls and 4343/3003/7072 construction. Weight and core depth are tightly constrained.

Trucks and buses

Wider 32 – 40 mm sections and heavier walls resist pressure cycling, vibration and longer service intervals.

Construction and agricultural machinery

Cores face dust, debris and high ambient temperatures. Thicker walls and double-sided clad sections are common.

Heater cores

Narrow 12 – 16 mm tubes in compact cabin heaters, often with the same clad system as the main radiator.

Generator sets and industrial coolers

Stationary radiators for engines, compressors and hydraulic systems, built to custom sizes.

Aftermarket replacement cores

Replacement radiator makers match OE tube sections from samples so cores fit existing tanks and headers.

Specifying

How to specify radiator tube for a quote

  • Section: width, height and wall, with tolerances, or a drawing of the profile
  • Material: clad construction (e.g. 4343/3003/7072) and clad ratio per side if fixed by your process
  • Temper and condition: as-welded condition and any requirement for straightness or end preparation
  • Length: cut length and tolerance, or coil-to-length supply if you cut in-house
  • Testing: burst pressure, eddy-current acceptance level, and metallography frequency
  • Brazing route: controlled atmosphere (flux) brazing or vacuum brazing, which affects alloy choice
  • Commercials: annual volume, call-off pattern, packing and destination port, Incoterm (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP)

If you are switching supply for an existing radiator, send two or three sample tubes. Cross-sections reveal the clad layers and their thickness, which removes most of the guesswork in matching the original.

FAQ

Flat-oval radiator tube: frequently asked questions

Why do radiator tubes use a 4343/3003/7072 construction?

Each layer has a job. The 4343 outer clad melts during brazing and bonds the tube to the fins and header; the 3003 core carries the pressure; the 7072 inner liner corrodes sacrificially so coolant-side pits spread sideways rather than perforating the thin wall.

Can you match an existing flat-oval tube from a sample?

Yes. A sample tube is cross-sectioned to measure width, height, wall and clad layers, and a matching section is developed. A drawing with tolerances speeds this up.

What wall thickness should I choose?

Most passenger-car tubes run 0.20 – 0.28 mm. Trucks, buses and off-highway radiators usually use 0.30 – 0.40 mm for higher pressure-cycle and erosion margin. The right figure depends on design pressure, coolant flow velocity and expected service life.

What is the difference between 4343 and 4045 braze clad?

4045 has more silicon (about 9 – 11 % against 6.8 – 8.2 %), so it melts at a slightly lower temperature range and flows more freely, giving larger fillets. 4343 gives less erosion of thin cores. The choice depends on fin design and furnace profile.

Is flat-oval tube suitable for vacuum brazing?

Vacuum brazing normally uses magnesium-bearing filler alloys such as 4004 or 4104 rather than 4343. Tell us which brazing route you use so the clad system matches it.

How is aluminum radiator tube packed for export?

Tubes are bundled by count, protected against crushing and moisture, and packed in wooden cases or crates for sea or air freight, with the mill test certificate and packing list.

Need radiator tube for a new or existing core?

Send the section, clad construction, volumes and destination, or a sample tube. We will confirm material, testing and delivery terms.
