Alloy and temper guide

6061-T6 Aluminium: What the Temper Means, Properties and Product Forms

6061-T6 is 6061 alloy that has been solution heat-treated, quenched and artificially aged to its peak routine strength: typically 310 MPa tensile and 276 MPa yield. It is the default temper for machined parts, structural extrusions and general engineering sheet worldwide. This guide explains how T6 is produced, what each product standard guarantees, how T6 behaves in welding and machining, and when another temper is the better buy.

The basics

What does "T6" mean on 6061 aluminium?

The designation has two parts. "6061" identifies the alloy: a 6xxx-series aluminium strengthened by magnesium and silicon, with a little copper and chromium. "T6" is the temper code defined in ANSI H35.1 and EN 515: solution heat-treated, then artificially aged, with no deliberate cold work in between.

The 6061 chemistry on its own gives only modest strength. The alloy becomes useful once heat treatment turns its dissolved magnesium and silicon into billions of nanometre-scale Mg2Si precipitates that block dislocation movement. The T6 cycle is tuned to stop at the point where those precipitates give the highest strength, which is why T6 is sometimes described as "peak-aged".

Because the same temper code applies across product forms, 6061-T6 sheet, bar, extrusion and forging look identical on a drawing. They are not identical on the certificate: each form is covered by its own specification with its own guaranteed minimums, and those minimums differ by up to 30 MPa. That difference is the most common source of confusion for buyers and is covered in the specified-minimums section below.

6061-T6 in one paragraph

  • Typical properties: 310 MPa tensile, 276 MPa yield, 12 % elongation, about 95 HB
  • Specified minimums range from 260 MPa (extrusions) to 290 MPa (sheet and plate) tensile
  • Good corrosion resistance, very good machinability, readily welded with a strength penalty near the weld
  • T651, T6511 and T6510 are T6 with stress relief by stretching; the strength is the same
Process

How the T6 temper is produced

  1. Solution heat treatmentThe metal is held at about 530 °C (985 °F), just below the start of melting, until the magnesium and silicon dissolve fully into the aluminium. Furnace temperature uniformity matters: too cool and strength is lost, too hot and grain boundaries can melt.
  2. QuenchThe metal is cooled rapidly, usually in water or a polymer solution for plate and forgings, or by water spray or air at the press for extrusions. This traps the alloying elements in a supersaturated solid solution. Slow quenching in thick sections is why minimums fall as thickness increases.
  3. Stress relief (optional)For T651 and T6511, the product is stretched by about 1 – 3 % within hours of quenching. This removes the quench stresses that would otherwise make a part distort as it is machined.
  4. Artificial ageingThe metal is reheated to a moderate temperature: typically about 160 °C (320 °F) for around 18 hours for rolled and drawn products, or about 175 °C (350 °F) for around 8 hours for extrusions and forgings. Fine Mg2Si precipitates form and strength rises to its peak.
  5. Testing and certificationTensile specimens from each lot are tested against the product standard, and conductivity or hardness is often checked as a quick confirmation of correct heat treatment.

Times and temperatures above are typical industrial practice summarised from AMS 2770 and ASM handbook data; mills adjust them for furnace loading and section size.

If 6061 is cooled from extrusion and aged without a separate solution treatment, the result is designated T5. If it is solution-treated but left to age at room temperature, it is T4. Both are weaker than T6, and T4 slowly gains strength for several days after quenching before stabilising.

The practical consequence: a part machined from extruded 6061-T6511 bar is guaranteed about 28 MPa less tensile strength than the same part machined from rolled T651 bar or plate, even though both are "6061-T6". If your stress calculation relies on 290 MPa, say so on the drawing by naming the product standard, not just the alloy and temper.

TemperConditionTypical tensile / yieldWhen to choose it

6061-T651T6 plus stretching (plate, rolled bar)310 / 276 MPaPlate and bar that will be heavily machined

Choosing T6 when a part will be machined deeply on one side is a classic mistake: the unrelieved quench stress is released as metal is removed and the part bows. The material cost of T651 or T6511 is little different, so specify the stress-relieved version for any machined component.

Fabrication

Machining, welding, forming and finishing 6061-T6

Machining

T6 is hard enough to break chips cleanly and give a bright finish. Use sharp carbide tools with high positive rake, high spindle speeds and flood coolant. Softer tempers (O, T4) are gummy and tend to build up on the tool edge.

Welding

MIG and TIG weld well with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. Heat softens a band about 25 mm either side of the weld; design codes typically take the welded tensile strength as about 165 MPa (24 ksi). Re-solution treating and ageing after welding restores T6 strength but can distort the assembly.

Forming

T6 has limited ductility. Allow bend radii of roughly 1.5 – 3 × thickness for sheet and bend across the rolling direction. For tighter forming use O or freshly quenched T4.

Elevated temperature

Prolonged exposure above about 150 °C over-ages the precipitates and permanently lowers strength. Short exposures, such as powder-coat curing at about 180 – 200 °C for 10 – 20 minutes, cause only a small loss.

Anodising and coating

Takes clear, coloured and hard (Type III) anodising well; the finish is slightly less bright than 6063 because of the higher alloy content. Powder coating and painting are routine.

Corrosion

Good resistance in air and fresh water. In seawater 6061-T6 performs acceptably when anodised or painted; for permanent immersion, 5083 or 5086 is the safer choice.

FAQ

6061-T6 aluminium (aluminum): frequently asked questions

What is the yield strength of 6061-T6?

Typically about 276 MPa (40 ksi). The guaranteed minimum is 240 – 241 MPa (35 ksi) for sheet, plate, bar and extrusions under ASTM and EN standards. Design to the minimum.

Is 6061-T6 the same as 6061-T651?

They have the same strength. T651 has also been stretched after quenching to relieve internal stress, so it stays flat or straight when machined. Order T651 for plate and bar that will be machined.

How hard is 6061-T6?

About 95 Brinell, roughly 60 HRB or 107 HV. That is hard enough to machine cleanly but much softer than steel, so wear surfaces are often hard-anodised.

Can 6061-T6 be welded without losing strength?

No. Welding softens the heat-affected zone to around 165 MPa tensile. Either size the joint for the welded strength, place welds in low-stress areas, or re-heat-treat the welded assembly to T6.

Can 6061-T6 be bent?

Moderately. Use generous radii (about 1.5 – 3 × thickness for sheet) and bend across the grain. For tight bends, form in O or T4 and age afterwards.

What is the maximum service temperature for 6061-T6?

It keeps most of its strength up to about 100 °C. Long-term exposure above roughly 150 °C over-ages the alloy and the strength loss is permanent.

Does Metline supply 6061-T6 internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies 6061-T6 in all common forms from Mumbai to customers worldwide, with mill test certificates and export packing.
