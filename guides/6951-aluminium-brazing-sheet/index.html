Clad brazing sheet guide

6951 Aluminium Brazing Sheet: Five-Layer Clad Construction

6951 brazing sheet is a roll-bonded composite with a heat-treatable 6951 core, an aluminium-silicon filler alloy on the outside and, in the five-layer version, a 3003 interlayer between filler and core. The interlayer slows silicon diffusion into the core during brazing, which protects post-braze strength and corrosion life in demanding heat exchangers. This guide explains the construction, chemistry, brazing window and how to specify it.

Overview

What is 6951 brazing sheet?

Brazing sheet is aluminium sheet with a thin skin of low-melting Al-Si filler metal bonded to one or both faces. When an assembly of plates, fins and tubes is heated in a furnace, the skin melts, flows by capillary action into every joint and solidifies, so hundreds of joints are made in one cycle without separate filler wire.

Most brazing sheet uses a 3003 or modified 3xxx core. 6951 is chosen instead when the heat exchanger needs more strength after brazing. It is a lean Al-Mg-Si-Cu alloy with a solidus around 615 °C, high enough to survive a brazing cycle near 600 °C, yet it still contains enough magnesium and silicon to respond to precipitation hardening. After brazing, the furnace cycle acts as a solution treatment; natural or artificial ageing then restores much of the strength that a 3003 core would simply lose.

The five-layer construction (filler / interlayer / core / interlayer / filler) adds a 3003 barrier on each side. During brazing, silicon from the molten filler diffuses into whatever metal sits beneath it. With a 6951 core that diffusion can lower the local melting point, cause erosion of thin sections and leave a band that corrodes preferentially. The 3003 interlayer absorbs this silicon and keeps the core chemistry intact.

When 6951 five-layer sheet makes sense

  • Plate-fin, bar-and-plate or drawn-cup heat exchangers that must carry high internal pressure
  • Parts that must be thin for weight but still pass burst and pressure-cycle tests after brazing
  • Units exposed to salt spray, road chemicals or condensate, where long corrosion life is required
  • Vacuum-brazed aerospace, defence and industrial units that are aged after brazing
Construction

How the five layers work together

LayerAlloyFunctionTypical share of total thickness (each side)
Outer filler4104 (Al-10Si-1.5Mg-Bi)Melts at about 560 – 590 °C and forms the joints; Mg and Bi help break up oxide in vacuumAbout 10 – 18 %
Interlayer3003 (Al-Mn)Diffusion barrier that limits Si penetration and acts as a sacrificial bandAbout 8 – 12 %
Core6951 (Al-Mg-Si-Cu)Carries the load; age-hardens after the brazing cycleBalance, usually 50 – 70 %

Clad ratios are agreed per order. Ratios above are common starting points, not a standard requirement; the right value depends on gauge, joint design and how much filler the assembly needs.

Clad ratio is always quoted as a percentage of total sheet thickness per side. On 0.5 mm sheet, a 12 % filler layer is only 60 µm thick, which is why thin-gauge brazing sheet needs very consistent rolling. Too little filler leaves gaps in fillets; too much causes excess flow, erosion of fins and blocked channels.

Three-layer sheet (filler / core / filler) is cheaper and is perfectly adequate for many 3003-core parts. With a 6951 core, however, the five-layer build is the more robust route, because the core is more sensitive to silicon pick-up and its magnesium can migrate toward the surface. Single-side variants (filler on one face only, sometimes with a water-side liner on the other) are used where only one face makes joints.

Two details matter for brazing. First, 4104 contains magnesium on purpose: in a vacuum furnace, Mg evaporates from the filler and reacts with residual oxygen and water vapour, which disrupts the oxide film and lets the filler wet the joint without flux. Second, the 6951 core also contains up to 0.8 % Mg. That is helpful for strength but is a problem for flux brazing, because magnesium reacts with standard potassium fluoroaluminate flux and degrades it.

Properties

Mechanical behaviour before and after brazing

Brazing sheet is normally delivered in an annealed (O) or partially annealed (H2x) temper so that it can be stamped, drawn or bent into plates and cups. The brazing cycle then resets the microstructure of the core. What matters to the designer is therefore the post-braze, post-aged strength, which must be confirmed on brazed coupons from the actual furnace cycle.

What to ask for on the certificate

As-delivered properties
Tensile strength, 0.2 % proof strength and elongation of the clad sheet in the delivery temper
Clad ratio

Chemistry
Cast analysis of the 6951 core and of each cladding alloy
Post-braze data
Where required, tensile results on coupons put through a simulated brazing cycle and the agreed ageing practice

Clad sheet is weaker than bare 6951 in proportion to the softer clad layers, and brazed properties depend heavily on the furnace cycle and quench rate. Design to values measured on brazed coupons from your own process, not to handbook figures for the bare alloy.

Process

Vacuum brazing, CAB and the process window

The 4104 filler is designed for vacuum brazing. Assemblies are degreased, fixtured and heated in a vacuum furnace, typically held at around 10-5 mbar or better, to a peak of roughly 590 – 605 °C for a short soak. The filler melts, the magnesium getter does its work and the joints form without flux residues, which is important for aerospace, defence and electronics cooling parts where flux contamination is unacceptable.

Controlled atmosphere brazing (CAB) in nitrogen with fluoride flux is the volume process for automotive heat exchangers. A 6951 core can be brazed this way, but its magnesium content reduces flux activity, so results depend on flux type and loading, clad design and furnace profile. If CAB is the intended route, discuss it before ordering: an Mg-free filler such as 4343 or 4045 and a well-designed interlayer are normally preferred, and trials on the production line are essential.

  1. CleanRemove rolling and stamping oils with an alkaline or solvent degrease; residual hydrocarbons are a common cause of poor wetting.
  2. FixtureHold parts with consistent joint gaps, usually well under 0.1 mm, using stainless or Inconel fixtures that allow for thermal expansion.
  3. HeatRamp through a preheat stage (often around 450 – 560 °C) to even out temperature across thick and thin sections.
  4. BrazeHold briefly at peak temperature, long enough for complete filler flow, short enough to limit core erosion and sagging.
  5. Cool and ageCool at a controlled rate, then natural or artificial ageing to develop the 6951 core strength.

The key number is the solidus. Al-Si fillers melt from about 577 °C, so the core must stay solid while the filler flows. 3003 gives a large margin, 6951 a workable one, and 6061 almost none, which is why 6061 is used for machined manifolds and brackets rather than as a brazing-sheet core.

Applications

Where 6951 brazing sheet is used

  • Aerospace and defence: vacuum-brazed plate-fin heat exchangers, avionics cold plates and environmental control system coolers
  • Automotive and commercial vehicles: oil coolers, transmission coolers and high-pressure plates where strength after brazing limits wall thickness
  • EV thermal management: battery and power-electronics cold plates that must resist coolant pressure with thin walls
  • HVAC and refrigeration: brazed plate evaporators and chiller components with long service life targets
  • Industrial: compressor after-coolers, hydraulic oil coolers and air-separation plate-fin units

A typical bar-and-plate oil cooler pairs 6951 five-layer separator plates with 3003 fins and 3003 or 6063 bars. The fins do not need the strength; the plates do, because they contain the pressure. For full material sets see our fin stock and heat exchanger tube pages.

Ordering

How to specify 6951 brazing sheet

  1. ConstructionState the build (e.g. 4104/3003/6951/3003/4104), clad ratio per side and tolerance on clad ratio.
  2. DimensionsGauge, width, coil ID and OD or sheet length, and the tolerance standard (ANSI H35.2 or EN 485-4).
  3. Temper and formingDelivery temper, bend or cup-draw requirement, and any surface cleanliness limit.
  4. ProcessVacuum or CAB, peak temperature and any post-braze ageing, so the clad design can be matched.
  5. DocumentsEN 10204 3.1 certificate with core and clad chemistry, clad ratio measurement and mechanical results; export packing with desiccant.

FAQ

6951 brazing sheet: frequently asked questions

Why use a 3003 interlayer instead of a three-layer sheet?

During brazing, silicon from the molten filler diffuses into the metal beneath it. A 3003 interlayer takes up that silicon and protects the 6951 core from erosion, local melting and a corrosion-prone diffusion band. The result is better strength retention and longer corrosion life, at a modest extra cost.

Can 6951 brazing sheet be used in a CAB (Nocolok) furnace?

It can, but with care. The core contains up to 0.8 % magnesium, which reduces flux effectiveness. CAB users normally pair it with an Mg-free filler such as 4343 or 4045, adjust flux loading and run trials. 4104-clad sheet is intended for vacuum brazing.

Does 6951 regain strength after brazing?

Yes. The brazing cycle acts as a solution treatment for the core. Natural ageing at room temperature, or artificial ageing at moderate temperature, then precipitates Mg2Si and raises strength well above what a 3003 core can reach. Confirm values on brazed and aged coupons.

What clad ratio should I order?

Common starting points are about 10 – 18 % filler and 8 – 12 % interlayer per side, but the right ratio depends on gauge, joint gaps and fillet size. Thin sheet with many joints usually needs a higher filler ratio; thick plate needs less.

What is the difference between 4104, 4343 and 4045 filler?

4104 is Al-Si with magnesium and bismuth, designed for fluxless vacuum brazing. 4343 (about 7.5 % Si) and 4045 (about 10 % Si) contain no intentional magnesium and are the usual fillers for flux brazing. Pick the filler to suit the furnace process first.

Can Metline supply the fins and tubes as well?

Yes. Metline supplies the complete set of aluminium for a brazed heat exchanger, including 3003 and 3102 fin stock, round and flat tubes and other clad sheet, all with mill test certificates, to customers worldwide.
