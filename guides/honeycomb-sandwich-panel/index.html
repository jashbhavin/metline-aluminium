Honeycomb Sandwich Panel

A honeycomb sandwich panel is two thin facing sheets bonded or brazed to a honeycomb core, a build-up that gives the stiffness of a much thicker solid plate at a fraction of the weight. The facing material decides appearance, durability and impact resistance; the core decides shear strength and weight. Metline supplies aluminium-faced honeycomb panels and aluminium honeycomb core for panels with other facings, cut to size, worldwide.

Build-up

What makes up a honeycomb sandwich panel

Every honeycomb sandwich panel has the same three layers: a facing on each side and a honeycomb core between them, joined by adhesive or by brazing. Change any one layer and the panel changes character, which is why the same word covers a 6 mm interior ceiling panel and a 50 mm aircraft floor.

LayerCommon choicesWhat it controls
Facings (skins)Aluminium 3003, 5052, 5005, 6061; coated or anodised. Also CFRP, GFRP, stone veneer, laminatesBending strength, surface finish, impact and scratch resistance
CoreAluminium honeycomb (3003, 5052, 5056); aramid, thermoplastic or paper in other marketsShear strength, compression, weight, temperature limit
JoiningEpoxy film, two-part epoxy or polyurethane adhesive; vacuum brazing for all-aluminium panelsBond durability, fire behaviour, maximum temperature
Edges and insertsExtruded aluminium edge profiles, folded skins, potted insertsSealing, fixing and load introduction

Bending stiffness of a sandwich comes mainly from the facings and the distance between them. For two equal facings of thickness tf and modulus E, separated by a distance d between their centre lines, the bending stiffness per unit width is approximately D = E × tf × d² / 2. Doubling the core thickness therefore roughly quadruples stiffness, while the weight hardly changes.

Weights

Aluminium honeycomb panel weights

Panel weight is the two skins (2.70 kg/m² per millimetre of aluminium) plus the core (core density × core thickness). The table assumes a 50 kg/m³ aluminium core, a common density for architectural and interior panels; heavier structural cores add proportionally more, and adhesive adds roughly 0.3 – 0.6 kg/m² depending on system.

Calculated values for guidance. Solid aluminium plate of equal bending stiffness would be several times heavier.

Worked comparison. A 20 mm panel with 1 mm aluminium skins has facing centre lines 19 mm apart, so D ≈ 69 GPa × 1 mm × (19 mm)² / 2 ≈ 12.5 kN·m per metre width. A solid aluminium plate needs to be about 13 mm thick to match that stiffness, and it would weigh about 35 kg/m² against roughly 6.3 kg/m² for the sandwich: more than five times heavier. That ratio, not the absolute weight, is why sandwich panels dominate large flat surfaces in buildings and vehicles.

The same arithmetic shows what the core must do. The skins only work together if the core carries the shear between them without excessive deflection and supports them against local buckling. On short, heavily loaded spans, check core shear strength and shear deflection; a low-density core that is fine for a ceiling can be the weak link in a floor.

Applications

Where honeycomb sandwich panels are used

  • Building: facade cladding, soffits, ceilings, partitions, doors and canopies where large flat panels must stay flat.
  • Transport: rail floors, side walls and ceilings, bus and coach panels, truck and trailer bodies, lift cars.
  • Marine: bulkheads, ceilings, cabin furniture and superstructure linings on yachts and passenger ships.
  • Industrial: machine enclosures, cleanroom walls, work platforms, inspection tables and equipment covers.
  • Aerospace and defence: floors, interiors, shelters and ground-support equipment with weight limits.

For each sector, the fire and durability rules differ: EN 13501-1 for buildings, EN 45545-2 for rail, IMO requirements for passenger ships and FAR 25.853 for aircraft interiors. Those requirements are met by the complete panel, so state them on the enquiry.

FAQ

Honeycomb sandwich panels: frequently asked questions

How much does an aluminium (aluminum) honeycomb panel weigh?

A 20 mm panel with 1 mm aluminium skins and a 50 kg/m³ core weighs about 6.3 kg/m² before adhesive, or roughly 19 kg for a 1220 × 2440 mm sheet. A solid aluminium plate of equal stiffness would weigh many times more.

What skin thickness should I choose?

0.5 – 0.8 mm for ceilings and light interior panels, 1.0 mm for facades and partitions, and 1.5 mm or more for floors, platforms and panels exposed to impact.

Can the facings be different on each side?

Yes. Asymmetric build-ups, for example a coated face skin and a mill-finish back skin, are common. Very different skins can cause bowing, so keep thickness and material similar when flatness matters.

Are honeycomb panels suitable for outdoor facades?

Yes, with PVDF-coated aluminium skins, a durable adhesive, sealed edges and a fire classification that meets local building rules.

Can Metline supply panels with carbon-fibre or glass-fibre skins?

We supply the aluminium honeycomb core and aluminium sheet for those panels; the composite facings are sourced and bonded by the panel fabricator.

Need honeycomb sandwich panels?

Send thickness, skins, finish, size and quantity. We will confirm the build-up and quote delivered to your site or port.
