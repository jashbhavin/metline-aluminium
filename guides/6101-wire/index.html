6101 Aluminium Wire

6101 wire is drawn from a conductor-grade aluminium-magnesium-silicon alloy and heat-treated to give more strength than pure EC aluminium wire while keeping good conductivity. It is used for stranded conductors, earthing and lightning-protection conductors, braids and cold-headed parts. This guide covers composition, tempers, sizes and how to write an order.

Overview

What 6101 wire is and why it is used

Pure aluminium wire (1350 or 1370) has the best conductivity of any aluminium, but it is soft. When a conductor must carry its own weight over a span, resist abrasion, or hold a crimp or clamp without creeping, a heat-treatable alloy is used instead. 6101 and its close relative 6201 are the two conductor alloys of the 6xxx family.

6101 wire starts as continuously cast and rolled rod in the T4 condition. It is cold-drawn to size, which raises strength, and then artificially aged so that fine Mg2Si particles precipitate. The ageing step also draws dissolved magnesium and silicon out of solution, recovering much of the conductivity lost during alloying. The result is a wire that is several times stronger than annealed EC wire with only a moderate reduction in conductivity.

Typical reasons to choose 6101 wire

  • Higher strength than 1350 wire for self-supporting or abraded conductors
  • Better creep resistance at clamps, lugs and compression fittings
  • Good corrosion resistance in outdoor and earthing service
  • Suitable for cold heading into rivets and fasteners

Properties

Tempers and properties of 6101 wire

Unlike extruded 6101 bar, drawn 6101 wire has no single ASTM product standard with fixed temper limits, so tensile strength, elongation and conductivity are agreed between buyer and producer, and the mill certificate reports measured values for each lot. The useful conditions are:

ConditionHow it is madeTypical use
T4 (rod)Solution treated and quenched on the casting-rolling lineDrawing stock; not a final wire temper
Drawn, as-drawnCold drawn from T4 rod without ageingIntermediate; high strength, lower conductivity
Drawn and aged (T8-type)Cold drawn, then artificially agedConductor wire, earthing wire, braid
Aged before drawing (T6 then drawn)Aged rod drawn to final sizeWire for cold heading and rivets
AnnealedFully softened after drawingMaximum ductility for forming and winding

Conductivity of 6101 depends strongly on its ageing condition. As a reference point, extruded 6101 conductor tempers are specified at 55 – 59.5 % IACS minimum under ASTM B317. For wire, state the minimum conductivity (or maximum resistivity at 20 °C) and minimum tensile strength you need on the order.

Applications

Where 6101 wire is used

Stranded conductors

Strands in all-aluminium-alloy and composite conductors where strength above 1350 is needed.

Earthing and lightning protection

Solid and stranded aluminium-alloy conductors for down conductors and earthing networks; aluminium alloy conductors are covered in standards such as EN 62561-2.

Braids and shields

Fine wire braided into flexible connectors and cable screens.

Rivets and cold-headed parts

Light-duty rivets, pins and decorative fasteners where conductivity or corrosion resistance matters.

Metal spraying and welding

Wire of matching composition for repair or spraying of 6101 conductors.

Clad and plated wire

Base wire for copper-clad or tinned aluminium conductors.

FAQ

6101 aluminium (aluminum) wire: FAQ

Is 6101 wire stronger than 1350 wire?

Yes. Drawn and aged 6101 wire is several times stronger than annealed 1350 and noticeably stronger than hard-drawn 1350, at the cost of some conductivity.

What is the difference between 6101 and 6201 wire?

6201 contains more magnesium and silicon, so it is stronger and slightly less conductive. 6201-T81 is the standard alloy for AAAC overhead conductors under ASTM B398; 6101 is used where a little more conductivity is preferred.

What is the Werkstoff number for 6101?

The former German designation is E-AlMgSi0.5, Werkstoff 3.3207. Some catalogues wrongly list 3.2315, which belongs to 6082.

Can 6101 wire be used for rivets?

Yes. Wire drawn from aged rod heads well and suits light-duty rivets and pins where conductivity or corrosion resistance matter more than high strength.

How is 6101 wire packed for export?

In tightly strapped coils or on spools, wrapped against moisture and palletised or cased.
