Anodised Aluminium Coil for Fabrication

Anodised aluminium coil (also called pre-anodised or coil-anodised aluminium) is rolled aluminium strip that has been anodised continuously, as a moving web, before it reaches the fabricator. It arrives with a uniform, hard oxide finish already in place, so panels, trims, reflectors and housings can be slit, stamped and formed without a separate finishing step. Metline supplies pre-anodised coil and strip in architectural, decorative and reflective grades to customers worldwide.

Overview

What pre-anodised coil is, and why fabricators buy it

Anodising thickens the natural oxide on aluminium into a controlled, porous layer of aluminium oxide that is then sealed. Because the layer is grown from the metal itself, it cannot peel like paint. It is hard, resistant to weathering and abrasion, and keeps the metallic look of the aluminium beneath.

In batch anodising, finished parts are racked and dipped one load at a time. In coil anodising, a continuous strip runs through cleaning, etching or brightening, anodising, colouring and sealing tanks at a steady speed. The process is the same chemistry, but every metre of the coil sees identical conditions, which gives very consistent colour and film thickness across large areas and from one coil to the next.

For a fabricator, that means no racking marks, no colour mismatch between panels anodised in different loads, and no finishing bottleneck after forming. The trade-off is that the film is applied before forming, so bend radii, film thickness and alloy must be chosen together. The rest of this page explains how.

Where pre-anodised coil makes most sense

  • Large, flat or gently formed panels where colour must match across a whole facade or ceiling
  • High-volume stamped parts such as trims, nameplates, light reflectors and appliance panels
  • Thin gauges that are awkward to rack and anodise as individual parts
  • Projects that want a metallic finish without coating solvents or post-finishing on site

Purity and iron and silicon content decide clarity. The purer the aluminium, the more transparent the anodic film and the brighter the finish, which is why high-purity 1xxx grades are used for reflectors. For architecture, an "anodising quality" 5005 with controlled chemistry is the usual choice, because it gives an even appearance and matches 6063 window and curtain-wall extrusions anodised to the same colour.

Anodised colour is alloy-sensitive. Order panels and adjoining extrusions with matched samples, and do not mix alloys on one elevation.

Finishes

Surface finishes and colours

Clear (natural)

Uncoloured film that shows the texture of the metal. The most durable and most widely specified architectural finish.

Electrolytic colours

Champagne, light to dark bronze and black produced by depositing metal salts in the pores. Very lightfast, so suitable for exteriors.

Dyed colours

Organic dyes give gold, red, blue and other bright colours. Some dyes fade outdoors; check lightfastness before exterior use.

Brushed and satin

Mechanically brushed or chemically etched before anodising for a soft, directional or matt look that hides handling marks.

Bright and mirror

Chemically or electrochemically brightened high-purity or 5657 strip for specular trims and reflectors; reflectance depends on base metal and process.

Functional finishes

Thin films for printing and bonding, anti-fingerprint and easy-clean top treatments for interior panels.

Fabrication

Forming pre-anodised coil without crazing the film

Anodic oxide is a ceramic. It stretches very little, so when the strip is bent the film on the outside of the bend develops fine cracks called crazing. On thin films these cracks are almost invisible and do not affect corrosion much indoors; on thick films they can be visible and open the surface to weathering. Good results come from planning the bend before choosing the film.

  • Match film to forming: the deeper the forming, the thinner the film should be. Thick architectural films suit flat panels and gentle bends.
  • Use generous radii: bend radii of several times the thickness reduce crazing; tight hems and sharp folds are best done in thin-film material.
  • Choose the temper: softer tempers form more easily but the film still crazes; temper mainly controls springback and panel stiffness.
  • Bend across the grain: where possible, place bends perpendicular to the rolling direction.
  • Keep the protective film on: leave PE film in place through slitting, stamping and forming to avoid scratches on the visible face.
  • Protect cut edges: slit and sheared edges are bare aluminium; in exterior or marine use, design edges to be hidden or sealed.

Pre-anodised coil can be stamped, roll-formed, perforated and embossed. It is not suitable for welding on visible faces, because welding destroys the finish locally; use mechanical fixing or adhesive bonding. The anodic layer is electrically insulating, so earthing points need the film removed locally.

Choose batch anodising when parts are deeply formed, welded or machined, or when the finish must cover every edge. Choose pre-anodised coil when you want the anodised look on large flat or moderately formed areas and need efficient, consistent supply. Applications

Applications of anodised aluminium coil

  • Building envelopes: facade panels, soffits, column cladding, sunshade blades and flashings, typically 5005 in AA15 – AA25
  • Interiors: ceilings, wall panels, lift interiors and corridor protection in public and healthcare buildings
  • Lighting: reflectors and louvres from high-purity, brightened 1xxx strip
  • Automotive: bright trim strips, emblems and interior decorative panels in 5657 and 5005
  • Appliances and electronics: control panels, front fascias and housings
  • Signage and nameplates: printable thin-film strip for labels, plates and displays

FAQ

Anodised (anodized) aluminium coil: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between anodised coil and anodised sheet?

The finish is the same chemistry. Coil is anodised continuously as a strip, giving very even colour over long lengths and suiting slitting, stamping and roll-forming lines. Sheet may be cut from anodised coil or anodised piece by piece; it suits smaller batches.

Can pre-anodised aluminium coil be bent?

Yes, within limits. The oxide film crazes on the outside of tight bends, so use thinner films and generous radii for formed parts, and keep thick films for flat or gently curved panels.

Which alloy is best for anodised facade panels?

5005 in anodising quality is the usual choice: it gives a clear, uniform finish that matches 6063 extrusions anodised in the same colour and has enough strength for panels.

What film thickness should I specify for outdoor use?

AA15 for normal exterior exposure, AA20 for polluted or moderate coastal sites and AA25 for severe marine or high-wear exposure, following ISO 7599 guidance and good sealing.

Does anodised aluminium coil fade?

Clear and electrolytically coloured (bronze, champagne, black) finishes are very lightfast. Some organic dyes fade in sunlight, so check lightfastness data before using dyed colours outdoors.

Can Metline cut anodised coil to size?

Yes. Metline supplies anodised coil and strip slit to width or cut to length, with protective film where needed, mill certificates and export packing, shipped worldwide from Mumbai.
