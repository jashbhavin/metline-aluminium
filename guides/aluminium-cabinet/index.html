Materials guide for cabinet makers

Aluminium Materials for Cabinets

An aluminium cabinet is built from three groups of material: extruded frame and door profiles (usually 6063-T5 or T6), flat sheet or sandwich panels for the carcass and shelves (5052, 3003, honeycomb or composite panels), and aluminium fittings. This guide explains which grade and form suits each part of kitchen, wardrobe, bathroom, tool and outdoor cabinets, and what to specify when you buy the material.

Overview

Why build cabinets from aluminium?

Wood, MDF and particleboard swell when they absorb water, can be attacked by termites and lose screw-holding strength over time. An aluminium cabinet does none of these things. That is why aluminium has become a common choice for kitchens and bathrooms in humid and coastal climates, for outdoor kitchens, laboratories, hospitals, boats and caravans, and for industrial tool and storage cabinets.

Aluminium is about one third the weight of steel and does not rust, so a cabinet carcass can be light without needing paint for protection. It is non-combustible, does not emit formaldehyde or other volatile compounds as some board products can, and is fully recyclable at the end of the cabinet's life. The extrusion process also lets a designer build grooves for panels, hinges, slides and seals directly into the profile, which cuts assembly time.

The trade-offs are cost per square metre, which is higher than particleboard, and a different look and feel. Wood-grain transfer coatings and laminate-faced honeycomb panels close much of the appearance gap; the cost is usually justified by a service life measured in decades rather than years in wet areas.

Where aluminium cabinets are used

  • Kitchen, laundry and bathroom cabinets in humid or coastal homes
  • Outdoor kitchens and barbecue islands exposed to rain and sun
  • Hospital, laboratory and clean-room storage that must be washed down
  • Industrial tool cabinets, workshop storage and mobile tool trolleys
  • Marine, caravan and motorhome furniture where weight matters
  • Electrical, telecom and battery enclosures (see the enclosure notes below)

For most domestic cabinets, a 6063 frame with 3003 or 5052 sheet panels is the standard combination. Choose 6061-T6 only where loads justify it: it costs more, its surface after extrusion is less refined and it anodises with a slightly less uniform colour than 6063.

Extrusions

Profile types used in cabinet construction

Square and rectangular tube

The simplest carcass frame: 20 × 20 to 50 × 50 mm tube cut to length and joined with corner brackets or welded. Wall thickness of 1.2 – 2 mm is common for furniture.

T-slot framing

Metric 20, 30, 40 series and inch 10 and 15 series T-slot profiles. Panels drop into the slots and brackets bolt anywhere along the length, which suits workshop and industrial cabinets that may be reconfigured.

Channels

U, C, J, F, H and hat channels hold panel edges, form door tracks and act as shelf supports.

Angles

Equal and unequal angles reinforce corners, edge exposed panels and act as shelf ledges.

Purpose-designed cabinet profiles

Custom sections combining a panel groove, hinge channel, seal slot and handle in one extrusion. A die costs money once but removes many fabrication steps on every cabinet.

Handle profiles

J-pull, G-pull and edge-pull handles extruded in 6063 and fixed along the top edge of doors and drawer fronts for handle-free styling.

Ask for profiles cut to finished length and pre-punched for hinges and connectors when the volume justifies it: precise cutting at the material stage removes the most error-prone step from the cabinet shop.

Surface finishing

Surface finishes for aluminium cabinets

Anodising

An oxide layer grown from the metal itself, clear or coloured (champagne, bronze, black). For indoor furniture 10 – 15 µm is typical; 20 µm or more for outdoor and wet-area use. Specify to ISO 7599 or an equivalent class.

Powder coating

Polyester powder in any RAL colour and in matt, satin or textured finishes. Pre-treatment quality decides how well the coating resists blistering in kitchens and bathrooms.

Wood-grain transfer

A powder base coat with a sublimated wood pattern. Gives oak, walnut or teak looks on profiles and sheet while keeping aluminium underneath.

Brushed and polished

Mechanical finishes, usually followed by clear anodising to protect them from fingerprints and corrosion.

Electrophoretic coating

A thin, even lacquer often applied over anodising for a smooth, durable surface on visible profiles.

PVC protective film

Applied at the mill or after finishing to protect sheets and profiles during cutting, transport and assembly.

Hardware

Aluminium fittings and accessories

Many cabinet fittings are themselves aluminium extrusions, castings or pressings. Matching their alloy and finish to the frame avoids colour mismatch and galvanic corrosion.

  • Hinges and pivots: concealed or surface hinges, usually steel or zinc with aluminium mounting plates
  • Drawer slides: steel ball-bearing slides fixed to aluminium drawer sides; isolate dissimilar metals in wet areas
  • Corner brackets and connectors: die-cast or extruded corner keys for tube and T-slot frames
  • Sliding door tracks and pulleys: extruded top and bottom tracks with nylon or steel rollers
  • Feet, plinths and castors: adjustable levelling feet, plinth profiles and castors for mobile cabinets
  • Seals and edge strips: rubber or PVC seals in extruded grooves keep dust and moisture out
  • Gas struts, locks and dividers: for lift-up doors, secure storage and drawer organisation

Use stainless steel (A2 or A4) fasteners rather than plain carbon steel, which corrodes in contact with aluminium in damp conditions.

Procurement

How to specify aluminium for a cabinet project

  1. List the partsSeparate frame profiles, sheets or panels, and fittings. Give a drawing or standard size for each profile.
  2. Set alloy and temperFor example 6063-T5 to EN 755-2 for profiles and 5052-H32 to EN 485 or ASTM B209 for sheet.
  3. Define the finishAnodising thickness and colour, powder RAL code and gloss, or wood-grain pattern; state which faces are visible.
  4. Lengths and cuttingStock lengths (often 6 m) or cut-to-size; any punching, slotting or notching.
  5. Quantities and packingInterleaved, filmed and boxed packing protects finished surfaces in transit.
  6. Documents and deliveryEN 10204 3.1 certificates on request, plus delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP).

Common mistakes

  • Using sheet that is too thin for large doors, which then flex and rattle
  • Choosing 6061 for appearance-critical profiles where 6063 would finish better
  • Ordering profiles and sheet with different finish suppliers, causing colour mismatch
  • Plain steel screws in wet areas, leading to corrosion stains
  • Leaving honeycomb panel edges unsealed in kitchens and bathrooms

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) cabinets: frequently asked questions

Which aluminium alloy is used for kitchen cabinet profiles?

6063 in T5 or T6 temper. It extrudes the thin, grooved shapes cabinet frames need, has a smooth surface and gives an even colour when anodised or powder coated.

What thickness of aluminium sheet should I use for cabinet panels?

Back panels and small shelves commonly use 0.8 – 1.2 mm; side panels and doors 1.5 – 2 mm sheet, or a honeycomb or composite panel for large doors that must stay flat. The right thickness depends on panel size and how it is supported.

Are aluminium cabinets waterproof?

The aluminium itself is unaffected by water and will not swell or rot. Joints, edges and fittings still need correct sealing and stainless fasteners to keep water out of the cabinet interior.

Can aluminium cabinets look like wood?

Yes. Wood-grain transfer coatings on profiles and sheet, or laminate-faced honeycomb panels, give a timber appearance while the structure stays aluminium.

Is honeycomb panel better than solid sheet for cabinet doors?

For large doors, yes: a honeycomb panel is much stiffer and flatter for the same weight. Solid sheet is cheaper and easier to bend, and suits smaller doors, drawer fronts and backs.

No. Metline supplies the aluminium materials: profiles, tubes, sheet, honeycomb and composite panels, cut and finished to your specification, for cabinet manufacturers and project buyers worldwide.
