Aluminium Foam Panels for Sound Absorption

Aluminium foam panels absorb sound through the friction and resonance of air moving in their pores, while remaining non-combustible, moisture-proof and washable, which fibrous absorbers are not. Open-cell foam or treated closed-cell foam, mounted over an air gap or built into a perforated sandwich panel, can reach high absorption across speech frequencies. This guide explains how the panels work, which build to choose and how to read acoustic test data.

Overview

Why use aluminium foam for building acoustics?

Most sound absorbers are mineral wool, glass wool, polyester fibre or polymer foam behind a perforated or fabric face. They work well, but they can shed fibres, absorb moisture, harbour dirt and, in the case of polymers, burn. Aluminium foam offers a porous absorber that is metallic throughout: it can be washed, used outdoors and in swimming pools, and installed where non-combustible materials are mandatory.

The trade-off is cost and the need for correct design. A closed-cell foam slab used as cast reflects most sound, because air cannot enter the cells. Its absorption is unlocked by opening the cells (by rolling, drilling or slicing) and by mounting it with an air cavity behind. Open-cell foam absorbs without treatment but is more fragile and usually protected by a perforated face.

Physics

How aluminium foam absorbs sound

Viscous friction

Sound waves push air back and forth through narrow pores and cracks. Friction against the cell walls turns acoustic energy into heat. This is the main mechanism in open-cell foam.

Helmholtz resonance

Opened cells behind small apertures act as tiny resonators. Combined with an air gap they produce strong absorption peaks, which designers tune to the target frequency.

Panel and cavity effects

A thin foam plate over an air cavity behaves as a membrane absorber at lower frequencies. Increasing the cavity depth moves absorption down the frequency scale.

Mass for insulation

Blocking sound between rooms (insulation) depends mainly on mass and decoupling, not porosity. Foam composites therefore combine an absorptive core with dense facings.

Absorption and insulation are different properties. Absorption, reported as the sound absorption coefficient α (0 to 1) or as NRC or αw, controls echo and reverberation inside a room. Insulation, reported as the weighted sound reduction index Rw in decibels, controls how much sound passes through a wall. An acoustic brief should state which one is needed.

Always ask for the mounting condition with any absorption figure. ISO 354 tests use defined mountings such as type A (directly on a hard surface) or type E with a stated cavity depth (for example E-200 for a 200 mm suspended ceiling). The same panel can show very different values depending on mounting, so compare like with like.

Applications

Where aluminium foam acoustic panels are used in buildings

  • Walls: perforated foam-core linings in auditoriums, sports halls, atriums and corridors, where impact resistance and cleanability matter.
  • Ceilings: lay-in, clip-in or suspended panels; light weight reduces load on the suspension grid.
  • Partitions: solid-faced foam composites in dry-assembled office and hotel partitions, combined with mass layers for Rw targets.
  • Doors and panels: foam cores in acoustic door leaves (see aluminium foam door panels).
  • Wet and outdoor areas: swimming pools, stations, car parks and tunnels, where fibrous absorbers degrade.
  • Plant rooms and HVAC: linings near fans and compressors, where heat, oil mist and fire rules exclude polymers.
  • Roads and railways: outdoor barriers, covered on the foam noise barrier page.

In ordinary dry offices, mineral wool systems usually remain the economical choice. Aluminium foam earns its premium where moisture, hygiene, fire rules, outdoor exposure or a durable exposed-metal look are part of the brief.

Fire & durability

Fire behaviour, moisture and durability

The aluminium foam core is metallic and non-combustible and releases no smoke on its own. A composite panel, however, includes adhesives, films or veneers, so its reaction-to-fire class is determined for the complete panel, for example under EN 13501-1 in Europe or ASTM E84 in North America. Ask for the test report of the exact build you intend to use.

Aluminium foam does not absorb water into the metal, and well-made panels show negligible moisture uptake, which keeps dimensions and acoustic performance stable in humid spaces. Outdoors, the foam forms a natural oxide layer; coastal or polluted sites may call for a coated or anodised face. Panels are fully recyclable as aluminium at the end of their life.

Specifying

How to specify aluminium foam acoustic panels

  1. Set the acoustic targetReverberation time or NRC/αw for absorption, Rw for insulation, with the frequencies that matter.
  2. Choose the buildSingle plate, perforated sandwich or multi-layer composite; core thickness, cell type and perforation pattern.
  3. Define mountingDirect fix, cavity depth, suspension system and edge details.
  4. Confirm fire and finishRequired reaction-to-fire class and the face finish, colour and texture.
  5. Approve samples and dataTest reports for the chosen build and mounting, plus a physical sample for appearance.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) foam acoustic panels: FAQs

Does closed-cell aluminium foam absorb sound?

Only weakly as cast, because air cannot enter sealed cells. Rolling, drilling or slicing to open the cells and mounting the panel over an air gap raises absorption substantially.

What NRC can aluminium foam panels achieve?

Well-designed perforated or open-cell builds typically reach around 0.6 – 0.9. The figure depends heavily on thickness, perforation and mounting, so check test data for the exact build.

Are aluminium foam acoustic panels fireproof?

The foam core is non-combustible. The fire class of a composite panel depends on its adhesives and facings and must be confirmed by test for that build.

Can aluminium foam panels be used outdoors or around pools?

Yes. They do not absorb water or rot, which makes them suitable for pools, tunnels, stations and outdoor barriers where fibrous absorbers fail.

What is the difference between sound absorption and sound insulation?

Absorption reduces echo inside a room and is rated by α or NRC. Insulation stops sound passing through a wall and is rated by Rw in dB; it depends mostly on mass and decoupling.

How thick are aluminium foam acoustic panels?

Composite panels are commonly 9 – 18 mm thick; single foam plates are sliced to the thickness the acoustic design needs.
