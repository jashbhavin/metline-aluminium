6063-T6 Aluminium Tubular Bus Pipe for Substations and Switchyards

6063-T6 tubular bus pipe is seamless aluminium pipe used as a rigid conductor in substations, switchyards and generator connections. It combines about 53 % IACS conductivity with enough strength to span between insulators, and its round shape keeps corona low at high voltage. This guide covers sizes with calculated weights and resistance, the standards that apply, how 6063 compares with 6101 and 6061, and the design points engineers check.

Overview

What is aluminium tubular bus pipe?

In an air-insulated substation, rigid bus connects transformers, breakers, disconnectors and line bays. Rigid tubular bus replaces stranded conductors where spans are short to medium, where clearances must be held precisely, and where low maintenance and a tidy layout matter.

A tube is efficient for this job. Alternating current crowds towards the outer surface of a conductor (skin effect), so the metal at the centre of a solid bar carries little current; a tube puts all its metal where it works. The round section also has the same stiffness in every direction, and a smooth large diameter lowers the surface voltage gradient that causes corona discharge, radio interference and audible noise at higher voltages.

6063-T6 seamless pipe in ANSI schedule 40 dimensions is the most widely used bus tube in North America and many export markets. It offers a practical balance: conductivity of about 53 % IACS, a guaranteed yield of around 170 MPa to carry its own weight, ice and short-circuit forces between supports, and good weldability to aluminium bus fittings.

Why 6063-T6 for rigid bus

  • Higher conductivity than 6061-T6 (about 53 % vs 43 % IACS) with useful structural strength
  • Seamless extruded pipe gives uniform properties around the circumference
  • Standard pipe sizes match the widely published bus fittings and connectors
  • Corrosion resistant outdoors without coating; low weight eases erection on insulator posts

Engineering

What engineers check when designing tubular bus

Ampacity

Current rating depends on allowable temperature rise, ambient temperature, wind, solar gain and surface emissivity. A weathered or painted surface radiates heat better than bright metal. Ratings are calculated to IEEE 605 or the national equivalent.

Span and deflection

The bus must carry its own weight, ice and wind between supports within a deflection limit, often expressed as a fraction of the span. A larger diameter is the most effective way to increase stiffness.

Short-circuit forces

Fault currents create large electromagnetic forces between phases. The tube, insulators and fittings must withstand them; Schedule 80 or 6061-T6 is used where forces are high.

Aeolian vibration

Wind can set long tubes vibrating. Damping conductors (stranded cable inside the tube) or proprietary dampers are installed on longer spans.

Thermal expansion

Aluminium expands about twice as much as steel. Expansion fittings or sliding supports are needed so the bus does not load insulators as temperature changes.

Corona

At higher voltages the surface gradient must stay below the corona onset level. Larger diameters, smooth surfaces and corona caps or bells at tube ends are used.

Installation

Welding, connections and surface finishes

  • Welding: fittings are commonly welded with ER4043 filler by MIG or TIG; welding softens the tube locally, so welds are placed near supports where bending stress is low
  • Bolted connections: clean contact surfaces, use contact compound and spring washers, and tighten to the fitting manufacturer's torque
  • Connecting to copper: use bimetallic transition pieces or tin-plated contact areas to avoid galvanic corrosion
  • Bending: large-radius bends can be made with mandrel tooling; most layouts use straight runs with angle fittings
  • Finishes: usually mill finish; tin or silver plating on contact areas, and paint or a dark finish where higher emissivity is wanted
  • Handling: protect pipe ends and surfaces from dents and scratches, which can raise local electric stress at high voltage
Applications

Where 6063 bus pipe is used

  • Outdoor air-insulated substations and switchyards: main bus, bay connections and equipment jumpers
  • Generator and transformer connections at power stations
  • Industrial plant distribution, such as smelters, steelworks and chemical plants
  • Renewable energy collector substations for wind and solar farms
  • Rail traction substations and high-current DC systems

A typical example: an outdoor 132 kV substation uses 4 in Schedule 40 6063-T6 tube for its main bus, supported on post insulators, with welded or bolted fittings, damping cable on the longer spans and expansion fittings at regular intervals.

FAQ

6063-T6 bus pipe: frequently asked questions

Why is 6063-T6 used for bus pipe instead of pure aluminium?

Pure aluminium conducts better but is too soft to span between insulators without sagging. 6063-T6 keeps about 53 % IACS conductivity while giving a minimum yield of about 172 MPa.

What is the difference between 6063 and 6101 bus pipe?

6101 is a conductor alloy with slightly higher conductivity (about 55 – 59 % IACS depending on temper) and is covered by ASTM B317. 6063-T6 is stronger than the high-conductivity 6101 tempers and is the common choice for outdoor substation bus.

How much current can 4 inch Schedule 40 6063 bus pipe carry?

It depends on the allowed temperature rise, ambient temperature, wind and surface condition. Published ratings for this size are typically in the low thousands of amperes; calculate the rating for your conditions to IEEE 605 or ask the conductor supplier for rated tables.

Is 6063 bus pipe seamless?

Bus pipe for substations is normally seamless extruded pipe to ASTM B241, which gives uniform properties around the circumference. Welded tube is not usually accepted for high-voltage bus.

Does aluminum bus pipe need painting?

No. 6063 resists outdoor corrosion without coating. Contact areas may be plated, and some designers paint the tube to raise emissivity and current rating.

Can you supply metric bus tube?

Yes. Metric outside diameters and walls for IEC-style substations can be supplied; send the OD, wall and length.
