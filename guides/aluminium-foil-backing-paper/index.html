Aluminium Foil Backing Paper & Paper-Backed Foil

Paper-backed aluminium foil is a laminate of thin annealed foil, usually 6 – 9 µm of 1235 or 8011, bonded to a lightweight base paper so that the pack gets the foil's barrier and shine together with the paper's stiffness, printability and fold. Foil backing paper is the base paper made for this lamination. This guide explains the structure, the foil and paper grades used for cigarette liners, chocolate and butter wraps, labels and insulation facings, and what to put in the specification.

Overview

What is paper-backed aluminium foil?

Thin aluminium foil is an almost perfect barrier to light, oxygen, moisture and aroma, but on its own a 7 µm foil tears easily, wrinkles and is hard to print and feed on packing machines. Laminating it to paper fixes those weaknesses. The paper carries the tensile load through the machine, takes print and embossing, and gives the dead-fold and crease-retention that wrappers need, while the foil provides the barrier and the metallic look.

The paper used for this purpose is called foil backing paper or foil lining paper. It is a thin, strong, very smooth paper, usually single-side glazed, designed to bond tightly to foil and to run on high-speed laminators and packing lines without breaks. Its grammage, normally in the 21 – 50 g/m² band for tobacco and confectionery and heavier for labels and technical laminates, is matched to the foil gauge and the end use.

There are two quite different products that both look like "foil paper". Foil-paper laminate contains a genuine aluminium foil layer. Vacuum-metallised paper has only a very thin evaporated aluminium coating, a fraction of a micron thick; it is cheaper and recycles with paper, but it is a much weaker barrier. Specify which one you need.

What paper-backed foil delivers

  • Barrier to moisture, oxygen, light and aroma from a continuous foil layer
  • Machine strength, stiffness and dead-fold from the paper
  • A bright or matt metallic surface that can be printed, lacquered or embossed
  • Grease and odour resistance for food and tobacco
  • Lower foil usage than an unsupported foil of the same handling strength

Lamination is done either wet, with a water-based adhesive such as a casein, starch or synthetic latex system that dries through the paper, or by wax or hot-melt bonding for wraps that must be heat-sealable or grease-resistant. For food and tobacco, adhesive and inks must be approved for the intended contact and must not transfer odour.

The foil

Foil alloys, gauges and properties

Fully annealed (O temper) foil is used because it is soft, folds flat and has the cleanest surface after the annealing furnace burns off rolling oil. 1235 is a commercially pure alloy (at least 99.35 % Al) prized for low pinhole counts in very thin gauges; 8011 contains more iron and silicon, giving higher strength, and is widely used for thicker wraps and labels.

Thin foil can contain occasional pinholes; their number falls sharply as gauge rises and is reduced further by lamination, because the paper and adhesive bridge them. Roll quality matters as much as paper properties: even winding tension across the width, clean square ends, accurate width and consistent core size all affect splicing and running speed. For food contact, the paper must comply with the rules of the destination market, for example EU Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004 and national paper recommendations, or US FDA 21 CFR 176.170.

Applications

Applications of paper-backed aluminium foil

Tobacco inner liners

The classic use: 6 – 7 µm foil on light backing paper keeps cigarettes fresh, carries embossing and folds sharply around the bundle. See cigarette packaging foil.

Chocolate and confectionery

Printed or coloured laminate wraps that hold their fold and protect from light and moisture. See chocolate foil.

Butter, margarine and cheese

Grease-proof wraps that keep out light and oxygen and prevent taint.

Labels

Neck and body labels for beer and spirits; see beer label foil.

Insulation facings

FSK and foil/kraft laminates as vapour retarders and radiant barriers on mineral wool and duct wrap.

Industrial and decorative

Ream wraps, gift wrap, lamp reflectors and decorative laminates.

Quality

Quality checks for foil-paper laminates

  • Bond strength: peel test between foil and paper; weak bond causes delamination at folds and embossing.
  • Appearance: no wrinkles, blisters, dull patches or adhesive streaks; even colour on lacquered or printed foil.
  • Curl and flatness: laminate should lie flat at the packer's humidity; curl comes from moisture imbalance between paper and adhesive.
  • Dimensions: roll width, length, core diameter and splice count per roll as agreed.
  • Odour and migration: for food and tobacco, residual solvent and odour checks and compliance declarations.
  • Documents: certificate of analysis for the foil, paper test report and food-contact declaration where required.
Sustainability

Recyclability and alternatives

Laminates are harder to recycle than mono-materials. Paper mills can repulp some foil-paper laminates and recover the fibre, with the aluminium separated as reject; dedicated processes can also recover aluminium from laminates. Where recyclability in the paper stream is the priority and the barrier need is modest, vacuum-metallised paper is the usual alternative. Where shelf life, light protection or aroma barrier is critical, a genuine foil layer remains the more reliable choice.

Down-gauging the foil and paper, using water-based adhesives and avoiding unnecessary plastic layers all reduce the environmental footprint of a laminate without losing its function.

FAQ

Paper-backed aluminium (aluminum) foil: FAQs

What is foil backing paper?

A thin, strong, smooth base paper made for laminating to aluminium foil. It gives the foil machine strength, printability and fold, typically at 21 – 50 g/m² for tobacco and confectionery wraps.

Which aluminium alloy is used for paper-backed foil?

Annealed 1235-O and 8011-O are the most common, with 8079-O for some wraps. 1235 is favoured for the thinnest gauges, 8011 for thicker, stronger foils.

What is the difference between foil paper and metallised paper?

Foil paper contains a real aluminium foil layer, usually 6 – 12 µm, and is a strong barrier. Metallised paper carries an evaporated coating well under one micron thick; it is cheaper and paper-recyclable but a weaker barrier.

Is paper-backed aluminium foil food safe?

It can be, when the foil, paper, adhesive and inks all meet the food-contact rules of the market, such as EU Regulation 1935/2004 or US FDA regulations. Ask for the compliance declaration for the exact laminate.

How thick is the foil in cigarette packaging?

Typically 6 – 7 µm, laminated to a light backing paper, then embossed and sometimes printed or coloured.

Can paper-backed foil be recycled?

Some paper mills accept it and recover the fibre while separating the aluminium. Recyclability depends on local facilities; metallised paper is easier to recycle with paper.
