Aluminium Foam for Maritime and Naval Applications

Closed-cell aluminium foam is a metal sponge: solid aluminium cell walls around sealed gas pores, at a fraction of the density of solid metal. On ships it is used as a light, non-combustible core for bulkhead and deck sandwich panels, as an acoustic and vibration damping lining in engine rooms, and as a crushable energy absorber. This guide explains its properties, where it fits on a vessel and how to specify it.

Overview

Why aluminium foam suits ships

Ship designers fight weight above the waterline, fire risk in accommodation and machinery spaces, and noise and vibration from engines and propellers. Aluminium foam addresses all three in one material, which is why naval architects and outfitting companies have adopted it for interior and protective structures.

Foam is produced by releasing gas inside molten aluminium (typically with a titanium hydride foaming agent and a calcium or ceramic stabiliser) or by heating a compacted mix of aluminium powder and foaming agent. The result is a rigid block of closed cells a few millimetres across, which is then sliced into panels or foamed directly between face sheets.

Because the cells are sealed, closed-cell foam does not soak up water, and panels at densities below 1 g/cm³ float. The aluminium matrix does not burn or release toxic smoke, it can be recycled with other aluminium scrap, and it absorbs large amounts of energy by crushing progressively at a nearly constant stress.

Properties that matter on a vessel

  • Low density: typical marine panels weigh a fraction of solid aluminium or steel plate of the same thickness
  • Non-combustible metal structure with no organic binder
  • High bending stiffness when used as the core of a sandwich panel
  • Vibration damping and, when treated, useful sound absorption
  • Progressive crushing for impact and blast energy absorption
  • Closed cells that do not absorb water

Applications

Where aluminium foam is used on a vessel

Bulkheads and partitions

Foam-cored sandwich panels with aluminium face sheets form light, stiff, non-combustible partitions in accommodation and service spaces, replacing heavier steel-and-mineral-wool constructions.

Deck and floor panels

Sandwich panels spread loads over light stiffeners. Foam cores add damping that reduces structure-borne noise transmitted into cabins.

Engine room acoustic linings

Perforated or surface-opened foam panels with an air gap behind absorb machinery noise, survive oil mist and heat, and do not shed fibres like mineral wool.

Enclosures and sound boxes

Acoustic hoods around generators, compressors and pumps use foam panels for absorption plus a solid skin for insulation.

Protective and crash structures

Foam crushes at nearly constant stress, so it is used in layered protective panels and fenders to absorb impact or blast energy before it reaches the main structure.

Buoyancy and void filling

Closed-cell foam adds reserve buoyancy in compartments and stiffens thin-walled sections without significant weight.

Approvals

Fire safety and classification considerations

Materials used on classed ships must satisfy SOLAS fire-protection requirements and the rules of the classification society (for example DNV, ABS, Lloyd's Register or Bureau Veritas). Non-combustibility is demonstrated by testing to the IMO Fire Test Procedures (FTP) Code, which uses the ISO 1182 furnace test.

  • Aluminium alloy structures have a lower melting point than steel, so fire divisions made with aluminium normally need insulation to meet A- or B-class integrity times
  • Foam panels used as linings or partitions should be approved as part of a tested construction, not as a loose material
  • Any adhesive used to bond face sheets to foam must also be assessed for fire, smoke and toxicity
  • For structural use, panel strength and stiffness must be proven by testing to the class society's requirements
  • Keep records of density, panel thickness and batch for each delivery so test reports can be linked to supplied material
Design

Design, joining and corrosion protection

Sandwich panel design

Bending stiffness comes from the face sheets separated by the core. Choose foam density for shear strength and local crushing resistance under point loads.

Joining

Panels are bonded, mechanically fastened through inserts or framed in extruded aluminium profiles. Direct welding into the foam is not practical; weld to frames or face sheets instead.

Seawater exposure

Use marine-grade face sheets such as 5083 or 5052 and seal cut foam edges. Panels in wet spaces should be coated or anodised.

Galvanic contact

Isolate aluminium foam panels from steel hull structure and copper-alloy fittings with non-conductive spacers or sealants to prevent galvanic corrosion.

Acoustic treatment

Closed-cell foam reflects sound unless the surface is opened by drilling, perforating or rolling; an air cavity behind the panel moves absorption to lower frequencies.

Machining

Foam can be sawn, waterjet cut and milled. Cut faces show open cells, which should be filled or edged where moisture or dirt could collect.

Ordering

How to specify aluminium foam for marine projects

  1. Define the functionStructural core, acoustic lining, energy absorber or buoyancy filler. The function sets density and treatment.
  2. Give dimensionsPanel length, width and thickness, plus tolerances and any edge or cut-out details.
  3. Set the density or performance targetA density range, or the stiffness, crush stress or sound absorption you need.
  4. Choose facings and finishBare foam, foam with bonded aluminium face sheets, perforated or coated surfaces.
  5. List approvals and testsClass society, FTP Code tests, density reports, mechanical or acoustic test data; mill certificates for face-sheet alloys.
  6. Agree deliveryPacking in crates to protect edges and faces; EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms to your yard.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) foam for marine use: FAQ

Does aluminium foam float?

Closed-cell foam with a density below 1 g/cm³ floats, and most marine grades are well below that. Cut edges expose some open cells, so seal them where long immersion is possible.

Is aluminium foam fire-proof?

It is non-combustible and gives off no toxic smoke, but like all aluminium it softens and eventually melts at much lower temperatures than steel. Fire divisions still need a tested construction with insulation where class rules require it.

Can aluminium foam absorb engine noise?

Yes, once the closed surface is opened by perforating or rolling and the panel is mounted with an air gap. Untreated closed-cell foam mostly reflects sound, though it still damps vibration.

How is foam joined to the ship structure?

By adhesive bonding, mechanical fasteners through inserts, or by framing panels in aluminium extrusions that are welded or bolted to the structure. Isolate it from steel to prevent galvanic corrosion.

What density should I choose?

Lower densities suit acoustic linings and buoyancy; medium and higher densities suit sandwich cores and energy absorbers where crush strength matters. Give us the performance target and we will propose a density range.

Is aluminium foam approved by classification societies?

Approval applies to a specific product and construction, not to foam in general. Agree the class society, rules and tests at the start so test samples match the panels to be supplied.

Looking for lightweight, non-combustible panels for a vessel?

Tell us the application, panel sizes, density target and class requirements. We will propose foam material and test documentation.
