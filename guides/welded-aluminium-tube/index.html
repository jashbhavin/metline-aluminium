Welded Aluminium Tube: Processes, Alloys and Selection

Welded aluminium tube is made by forming flat strip or sheet into a round or shaped section and joining the edges with a continuous longitudinal or helical seam. It is the economical choice for heat exchangers, HVAC lines, furniture, frames and large ducts, where a seamless extruded or drawn tube would cost more than the job needs. This guide explains how each welding route works, which alloys suit it, what the standards say and when you should insist on seamless instead.

Overview

What is welded aluminium tube?

Every aluminium tube is made in one of two ways. Seamless tube is pushed or drawn from a solid billet, so the wall is one continuous piece of metal. Welded tube starts life as coil or sheet that has already been rolled to the final wall thickness; rolls bend it into a tube and the edges are fused together. The finished tube has a seam, but its wall thickness is as uniform as the strip it came from.

That difference drives the economics. A tube mill can run continuously at high speed from a coil, scrap is low and wall thickness can be thinner than an extrusion press can reliably produce. Pre-finished strip can even be painted, lacquered or clad with a brazing alloy before it is formed. Against that, the seam and the zone around it are metallurgically different from the parent metal, and that matters for pressure, fatigue and some heat-treatable alloys.

In practice, welded tube dominates in automotive radiators and condensers, air-conditioning tubing, solar and HVAC equipment, furniture, ladders, display systems and very large diameter ducts, while seamless tube remains standard for hydraulic lines, high-pressure gas, aerospace and critical structural members.

Key points for buyers

  • Welded tube is formed from strip, so wall thickness is very even and thin walls are practical
  • The weld seam is the property-limiting feature: specify seam testing for pressure or fatigue duty
  • Non-heat-treatable alloys (1xxx, 3xxx, 5xxx) are the natural fit; 6xxx welded tube needs heat-treatment planning
  • Large diameters beyond normal extrusion limits are made by plate rolling and arc welding or by spiral forming
Manufacturing routes

HF, ERW, arc and spiral welding compared

Suppliers use the terms loosely, so it helps to know what each route really is. High-frequency (HF) welding is a form of electric resistance welding (ERW): a high-frequency current, introduced by an induction coil or by sliding contacts, flows along the converging strip edges and heats them almost instantly; squeeze rolls then forge them together without filler metal. Older low-frequency ERW is rarely used for aluminium today, so in most quotations "ERW" and "HFW" describe the same kind of tube.

RouteHow the seam is madeTypical size windowBest suited to
HF induction / contact (HFW, ERW)Edges heated by HF current and forged by squeeze rolls; no filler; outside and often inside bead scarfedSmall to medium diameters, thin to medium wallsHeat-exchanger tube, HVAC, furniture, frames, pre-painted tube
Laser or TIG seam welding on a millEdges melted by laser or arc as the tube passes; narrow fused seamSmall diameters, thin wallsPrecision and decorative tube, alloys sensitive to HF welding
Formed and arc-welded (TIG / MIG)Sheet or plate rolled to a cylinder, seam butt-welded with fillerLarge diameters, heavier wallsProcess pipe, tanks, large ducts and columns
Spiral (helical) formingStrip wound helically; seam welded or mechanically lock-seamedVery large diameters, long lengthsVentilation ducts, exhaust and chimney liners

The seam geometry on an HF mill decides weld quality. Strip edges should meet parallel through the wall (an "I" approach) or with a very slight V so that oxide is squeezed out; a wide or inverted V heats the inner and outer surfaces unevenly and can trap oxide in the bond line. Good mills control edge preparation, squeeze pressure and weld power together and monitor them continuously.

Spiral lock-seam duct is not welded at all. If your application needs a gas-tight or pressure-bearing joint, specify a welded seam explicitly.

Alloys

Which alloys are used for welded tube?

Alloys that gain their strength from cold work or solid solution behave best, because the heat of welding does not destroy a precipitation-hardened structure. The typical values below are for the strip temper before welding (Aluminum Association / ASM typical data); the tube near the seam will be closer to the annealed value.

Two cautions apply. First, avoid 5xxx alloys with more than about 3 % magnesium for permanent service above 65 °C, because they can become sensitised to intergranular and stress corrosion; 5454 exists for that reason. Second, 2xxx and 7xxx aerospace alloys are almost never supplied as welded tube: they crack during fusion welding or lose most of their strength in the heat-affected zone.

Standards

Standards for welded aluminium tube

Many enquiries still quote ASTM B313 and B547, but both have been withdrawn by ASTM (B313 in 2018 and B547 in 2019) without a direct replacement. They are still useful as technical references and can be agreed as a contract basis, but buyers should state the edition they mean. In Europe, the EN 1592 series remains the dedicated product standard for HF seam-welded tube.

StandardScopeStatus / remarks

ASME B31.3Process piping design, including weld joint factorsApplies where welded tube is used as pressure pipe

For pressure piping, the design code matters more than the product standard. ASME B31.3, for example, applies a longitudinal weld joint quality factor to welded pipe, typically 0.85 for ERW pipe, which reduces the allowable pressure compared with seamless pipe of the same size unless additional testing is specified.

Inspection

Seam quality, testing and documents

The seam is where welded tube can fail, so inspection concentrates there. Typical defects are cold welds (edges touched but did not bond), oxide entrapment, porosity in arc-welded seams, and excessive inside bead that restricts flow or traps contamination. The tests below are chosen according to duty; ask for them in the order rather than assuming they are standard.

TestWhat it revealsWhen to specify
Eddy-current testing in lineLack of fusion, cracks and pinholes along the seamHeat-exchanger, refrigerant and fluid tube
Flattening and flaring / expansion testsDuctility of the seam; a weak bond opensAll pressure and expanded-joint applications
Leak test (air under water or helium)Through-wall defectsRefrigerant, coolant and gas lines
Hydrostatic or burst testPressure capacity of the finished tubePressure piping and vessels
Radiography or ultrasonic of arc weldsPorosity, lack of penetrationLarge formed-and-welded pipe
Macro / micro sectionBond line, bead shape, HAZ widthQualification and first-article approval

Metline supplies welded tube with a mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 showing chemistry and mechanical results, plus dimensional reports and any additional test records agreed in the order. Third-party inspection can be arranged on request.

Choose seamless for hydraulic and high-pressure gas lines, aircraft and other fatigue-critical tube, and any part that will be heavily bent, flared or expanded at a location you cannot keep away from the seam. Choose welded tube where volumes are high, walls are thin, the strip benefits from pre-finishing or the diameter is beyond extrusion limits. Our seamless tube guide covers the other side of the decision.

Working with welded tube

Bending, joining and finishing welded tube

Bending

Place the seam on the neutral axis (at the side of the bend, not the inside or outside) to keep it out of the zones of highest strain. Use mandrels on thin walls.

Expanding and flaring

Tube for expanded joints in heat exchangers needs inside bead removal and a ductile seam. Flare and expansion tests confirm it.

Brazing

Clad 4343/3003 tube brazes in controlled-atmosphere furnaces with non-corrosive flux. Unclad tube can be joined with Al-Si filler rings or wire.

Fusion welding

TIG and MIG with 4043 or 5356 filler, chosen by base alloy. For 5xxx tube use 5356 or 5183; for 6xxx, 4043 resists hot cracking.

Heat treatment

6061 or 6063 tube welded from O or T4 strip is aged or fully heat-treated after forming to reach T6 values; ask what temper the seam actually is.

Finishing

Anodise after forming for uniform colour; the seam can appear as a faint line. Pre-painted strip gives a finished surface straight from the mill.

Applications

Where welded aluminium tube is used

  • Automotive thermal systems: radiator, condenser, heater and charge-air cooler tubes, many in brazing-clad 3003 strip
  • Battery and power-electronics cooling: coolant manifolds and serpentine tubes for electric vehicles and energy storage
  • HVAC and refrigeration: connecting lines, headers and evaporator tubing
  • Furniture and consumer goods: chairs, tables, ladders, display stands, pre-painted or anodised
  • Construction and industry: handrails, light frames, conveyor guards and machine enclosures
  • Marine and offshore: 5083 and 5086 arc-welded pipe for seawater, ventilation and ballast systems
  • Ducting and exhaust: large-diameter spiral or arc-welded ducts, chimney liners and air-separation pipework

Metline supplies the tube itself and the strip, sheet and plate it is made from, cut to length or prepared to drawing on request. It does not supply finished assemblies such as heat exchangers or furniture.

FAQ

Welded aluminium (aluminum) tube: frequently asked questions

Is welded aluminium tube as strong as seamless?

Away from the seam the wall has the strength of the strip it was made from. The seam and heat-affected zone are usually softer, especially in cold-worked or heat-treated alloys, so pressure and fatigue designs use reduced values unless the tube is heat-treated and tested after welding.

What is the difference between HFW and ERW aluminium tube?

High-frequency welding is a type of electric resistance welding. In current aluminium tube production the two terms usually describe the same process: HF current heats the strip edges and rolls forge them together without filler.

Are ASTM B313 and B547 still valid?

ASTM withdrew B313 in 2018 and B547 in 2019. They can still be named in a contract as a reference, but you should state the edition. EN 1592 remains current for HF seam-welded tube in Europe.

Which alloy is best for welded heat-exchanger tube?

3003 for general use, or 3003 clad with 4343 or 4045 when the tube will be furnace-brazed. Long-life automotive designs sometimes use modified 3xxx cores with improved corrosion resistance.

Can welded aluminium tube be anodised?

Yes. Anodising after forming gives a uniform finish, although the seam may show as a faint line, particularly on bright or coloured finishes. Specify an anodising-quality strip if appearance matters.

Can Metline supply welded aluminium tube worldwide?

Yes. Metline supplies welded and seamless aluminium tube, along with the strip and sheet used to make it, to customers worldwide with mill test certificates and export packing.
