Current Collector Aluminium Foil for Batteries

In a lithium-ion cell the cathode coating is spread on a thin aluminium foil that carries electrons between the active material and the cell terminal. That foil, the current collector, is usually 10 – 20 µm thick, 99.3 – 99.7 % pure aluminium in the hard H18 temper, and it can be supplied plain, carbon-coated or perforated. This guide explains why aluminium is used, the foil types available, the properties that matter on a coating line, and how to write a purchase specification.

Fundamentals

What a current collector does

Battery active materials such as lithium iron phosphate (LFP) or nickel-manganese-cobalt oxide (NMC) are poor electronic conductors on their own. The current collector is the conductive backbone that supports the coated electrode and gathers current from every point of it.

A collector has four jobs. It must conduct electrons with very little resistance; it must give the coating something to adhere to through thousands of charge cycles; it must stay chemically stable at the electrode's working voltage in contact with the electrolyte; and it must be as thin and light as possible, because it stores no energy. Every micron removed from the collector leaves more room and mass for active material, which is why foil thickness has fallen steadily as cell makers chase energy density.

In a conventional lithium-ion cell, the positive electrode (cathode) uses aluminium foil and the negative electrode (anode) uses copper foil, typically around 6 – 8 µm. Getting this the right way round matters, and it is a point some supplier literature gets wrong.

Why aluminium on the cathode and copper on the anode

  • At the high cathode potential (about 3 – 4.3 V against lithium) copper would oxidise and dissolve, while aluminium is protected by a passive surface film
  • In LiPF6 electrolytes that film is reinforced by aluminium fluoride, which is why aluminium survives cathode voltages
  • At the low anode potential, below roughly 0.3 V against lithium, aluminium reacts with lithium to form alloys and would crumble, so copper is used there
  • Where the anode works at higher potential, as with lithium titanate (LTO), or in sodium-ion cells, where sodium does not alloy with aluminium, aluminium foil can be used on both electrodes
Product types

Types of current collector aluminium foil

Plain (bare) foil

Rolled 1xxx foil, cleaned and degreased so that the slurry wets it evenly. The default for most LFP and NMC cathodes and for supercapacitor electrodes. Low cost and mature; performance depends on surface cleanliness and thickness consistency.

Carbon-coated foil

Plain foil with a thin conductive carbon primer (carbon black, graphite or graphene in a binder), usually around a micron or less per side. It lowers contact resistance, improves adhesion of the cathode layer and shields the aluminium from electrolyte attack. Widely used with LFP for high-rate and long-life cells. See our carbon-coated foil guide.

Porous / perforated foil

Foil with many fine through-holes, made by mechanical or chemical perforation. It lets ions pass through the collector, which helps in pre-lithiation and in some supercapacitor and hybrid designs, and it reduces collector weight. Strength and handling on the line fall as open area rises.

Alloyed high-strength foil

Foils with small manganese or iron additions (for example 8xxx or 3xxx-type chemistries) for higher tensile strength, so very thin gauges survive high-speed coating and calendering without breaks.

Embossed or roughened foil

Textured surface that increases the contact area with the coating and improves mechanical keying. Used where adhesion is the limiting factor.

Foil for dry electrodes

Solvent-free dry electrode processes press a powder film onto the collector, which places more emphasis on surface primers and flatness than on slurry wettability. Carbon-primed foil is often used.

Modified foils, including coated and porous variants, are covered in more depth in the modified aluminium foil for batteries guide, and plain battery foil by alloy in the aluminium foil for lithium-ion battery guide.

Material

Alloys and tempers used for collector foil

Purity is a trade-off. Higher-purity aluminium conducts better and is more resistant to corrosion, but it is softer and more likely to tear when rolled very thin. Slightly less pure grades, with controlled iron and silicon, roll to thinner gauges with higher strength. Almost all collector foil is supplied in the H18 (full hard) temper because it gives the strength needed to run tensioned webs through coaters and calenders.

A practical rule: start with plain 1235 or 1070 H18 foil, then move to carbon-coated foil if cell testing shows high interface resistance, coating delamination after cycling, or poor high-rate performance. Porous foil is a design decision for specific cell architectures rather than a general upgrade.

Applications

Where current collector foil is used

  • Electric vehicle cells: cylindrical, prismatic and pouch cells with NMC, NCA or LFP cathodes
  • Stationary energy storage: LFP cells for grid, solar and backup systems, where long cycle life favours carbon-coated foil
  • Consumer electronics: phone, laptop and wearable cells, which push towards the thinnest gauges
  • Sodium-ion batteries: aluminium on both electrodes, which removes copper from the bill of materials
  • Supercapacitors (EDLCs): activated carbon electrodes on high-purity, often etched or carbon-coated foil
  • Lithium primary and specialty cells: for medical, aerospace and industrial devices, built to the cell maker's own specification

Pouch cells also use aluminium in the packaging laminate, a different product: see 8021 foil for pouch cell packaging.

Handling

Storage and handling of collector foil

  1. Keep it sealedFoil is shipped in sealed, moisture-barrier packing with desiccant. Leave it sealed until it is needed on the line.
  2. Acclimatise before openingLet coils reach room temperature before opening to avoid condensation on cold metal, which leaves water stains and oxide spots.
  3. Control the roomStore in a clean, dry area away from acids, alkalis and salt air; carbon-coated foil is more sensitive to humidity than bare foil.
  4. Handle by the coreLift coils by the core, never the outer wraps, and avoid knocks to the edges, which create dents that show up as coating defects.
  5. Use first in, first outSurface energy of bare foil can fall over time as the surface picks up contamination, so check dyne level on older stock before use.
Ordering

Writing a purchase specification

  1. MaterialAlloy, temper (normally H18), thickness and tolerance, and the standard or internal specification it must meet.
  2. Mechanical and surfaceMinimum tensile strength and elongation, wettability (dyne), pinhole limit, surface roughness or coating weight for coated foil.
  3. GeometrySlit width and tolerance, core material and inside diameter, maximum outside diameter or coil length, number of joints allowed.
  4. Packing and documentsVacuum or moisture-barrier packing, mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 with chemistry and mechanical results, sample coils for qualification.
  5. LogisticsAnnual volume, call-off pattern and delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP).

FAQ

Current collector aluminium (aluminum) foil: FAQ

Is aluminium foil used for the anode or the cathode?

For the cathode (positive electrode) in standard lithium-ion cells. Aluminium reacts with lithium at the low anode potential, so graphite and silicon anodes use copper foil. In sodium-ion and LTO-anode cells aluminium can be used on both sides.

What thickness is current collector aluminium foil?

Most cathode foil is between 10 and 16 µm, with 12 – 15 µm the most common and thinner 8 – 10 µm foil used where energy density is the priority. Thinner foil needs higher strength to run on the coater without breaking.

Which alloy is best for battery cathode foil?

1235 H18 is the most widely used because it rolls cleanly to thin gauges. 1070 and 1060 are chosen where conductivity matters more, and stronger alloyed foils are used for very thin gauges on fast lines.

What is the benefit of carbon-coated aluminum foil?

A thin conductive carbon primer reduces the contact resistance between coating and foil, improves adhesion and protects the aluminium from electrolyte attack. The benefit is greatest with LFP cathodes, high charge rates and long-life cells.

What dyne level should battery foil have?

Specifications commonly ask for at least 30 – 32 mN/m measured with dyne pens, which shows the surface is clean enough for water- or NMP-based slurries to wet evenly. Agree the test method with the supplier.

Can Metline supply foil cut to my coater width?

Yes. Foil can be supplied slit to the required width on the requested core, with sealed export packing and mill test certificates. Send the full specification and expected volume for a quotation.
