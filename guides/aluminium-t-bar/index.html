Aluminium T Bar (Tee Section): Sizes, Weights and Alloy Selection

An aluminium T bar is an extruded section shaped like a capital T: a flat flange with a single stem (web) at its centre. It is used to stiffen panels, join or support sheets and glazing, edge floors and build light frames, and it is extruded mainly in 6063 for architectural trim and 6061 or 6082 for structural duty. This guide gives standard sizes with calculated weights and section properties, explains rounded- and square-inside tees, and shows how to select alloy, temper and finish.

Overview

What is an aluminium T bar?

A T bar (tee bar, T section or T profile) has two elements: the flange, the horizontal top of the T, and the stem or web, which projects at 90° from its centre. It is an open, solid profile, so it is extruded on a flat die and is economical to tool, and its single plane of symmetry gives it useful stiffness in one direction while the flange provides a flat surface for fixing.

Sizes are given as flange width × overall height × thickness, for example 40 × 40 × 3 mm or 2 × 2 × 1/4 in. When the flange and stem have different thicknesses, both are stated (tf and tw). Make sure your drawing says whether the height is overall (top of flange to tip of stem) or stem only, because both conventions are used in catalogues.

Aluminium T bars do the same jobs as rolled steel tees but weigh about a third as much, never rust and can be anodised or powder coated. Small tees are mostly decorative and joining sections; larger ones act as stiffeners, purlins, floor bearers and lightweight beams. Because the centroid sits close to the flange, a tee is strong when the stem is in compression (stem down on a simply supported span with the flange on top) and weaker when the stem tip is in compression, which matters for cantilevers and continuous spans.

Typical reasons to choose a tee

  • Joins two panels edge to edge: the flange covers the joint while the stem sits between the panels
  • Stiffens a sheet or plate when the flange is riveted, bonded or welded to it
  • Supports glazing, infill panels and ceiling tiles on its flange
  • Gives a clean edge or divider strip for floors, worktops and tiles
Product types

Rounded-inside, square-inside and special tees

Rounded-inside (structural) tee

Has a fillet where the stem meets the flange and often rounded toes. The fillet reduces stress concentration and improves fatigue and buckling behaviour; it is the normal form for 6061 and 6082 load-bearing tees and for ASTM B308 standard structural profiles.

Square-inside (architectural) tee

Sharp inside corner and crisp edges for a neat appearance. Common in 6063 and 6060 for trims, dividers, glazing bars and decorative frames, and easier to fit tight against panels.

Unequal tee

Flange wider than the height (e.g. 50 × 25 mm) for cover strips and joint covers, or a tall stem (e.g. 50 × 100 mm) for stiffeners and bearers.

Bulb and lipped tees

Custom tees with a thickened stem tip or lips on the flange, used as ship stiffeners, rails and snap-fit profiles.

Thin and small tees

Sections from about 12 mm with walls near 1.5 mm for trim, model making, electronics and interior details.

Heavy-duty tees

Thick sections, 8 mm and above, for floors, platforms, ladders and light beams in transport and industry.

All of these are produced the same way: a homogenised 6xxx billet is preheated, pushed through a flat steel die with a T-shaped aperture, quenched at the press exit (by air for 6063, by water mist or spray for thicker 6061 and 6082), stretched about 1 – 3 % to straighten it and relieve stress, cut to length and artificially aged to T5 or T6. Because the die is a simple solid die, tees in standard sizes are widely tooled and a new die for a custom tee is relatively inexpensive compared with a hollow profile. Larger tees, or tees with very thin stems relative to the flange, need larger presses and slower speeds so that the stem fills completely and stays straight.

Engineering

Design notes for aluminium tees

A tee is not a symmetrical section, so its behaviour depends on which way it is loaded. Keep these points in mind when using a T bar as a structural member:

  • Orientation: with the flange on the compression side (stem hanging down on a simple span) the slender stem tip is in tension and the tee is efficient. Reverse the load and the stem tip can buckle locally at a much lower stress.
  • Lateral-torsional buckling: long, unbraced tees twist under bending. Brace the flange at intervals or attach it continuously to a panel.
  • Stem slenderness: codes such as the Aluminum Design Manual and EN 1999-1-1 limit the stem height-to-thickness ratio before allowable stress is reduced.
  • Deflection: aluminium's modulus is about 69 GPa, one third of steel, so check deflection first; the Ix values above give the stiffness.
  • Welding: a weld across a 6xxx tee softens a heat-affected zone; place welds away from peak bending, or use bolts or rivets.
  • Dissimilar metals: isolate tees from steel or copper in wet conditions with washers, tapes or coatings to prevent galvanic corrosion.

Where a tee is acting as a stiffener on a plate, the plate works with the flange as a combined section, so a modest tee can stiffen a large panel substantially. Ship and vehicle structures use this principle with tees welded or riveted to 5xxx or 6xxx plate.

Finishes and processing

Surface finishes and processing

Mill finish

As extruded; for hidden or later-painted parts.

Anodised

Clear, black, bronze, champagne or gold, typically 10 – 25 µm; hard anodising for wear surfaces. 6063 gives the most uniform colour.

Powder coating

Any RAL colour for frames, furniture and architectural trim.

Electrophoretic coating

A thin, even lacquer film often applied over anodising for building profiles.

Brushed, polished, sandblasted

Decorative or matte surfaces for visible interior work and equipment.

Machining

Cutting to length and angle, drilling, punching, milling slots, tapping and CNC machining to drawing; marking for traceability.

Tees bend more easily about the axis that puts the flange on the outside of the bend. Tight bends are best made in T4 or O temper and then aged; T6 tees need large radii and trial bends.

Tolerances & quality

Tolerances, standards and inspection

T bars are supplied to the dimensional tolerances of the ordering standard unless the drawing sets tighter limits. For extruded profiles these are ANSI H35.2 (called up by ASTM B221) or EN 755-9. Tolerances depend on the size of the circumscribing circle, the alloy group and whether a dimension is across metal (thickness) or across a space. Angularity between flange and stem, flatness of the flange, straightness and twist are each specified separately.

Buyer advice

Common mistakes when buying T bar

Ambiguous height

Ordering "50 × 50" without stating whether 50 mm is overall height or stem length can give a tee that is a full flange thickness too short or too tall.

Wrong inside corner

Square-inside architectural tees will not sit over a radius on a mating part, and rounded-inside tees leave a gap against flat panels. Specify the corner.

Choosing 6063 for load

6063-T5 has roughly half the yield strength of 6061-T6. For bearers, stiffeners and brackets, specify 6061, 6082 or 6005A.

Ignoring orientation

A tee designed with the stem in tension can fail early if installed upside down. Mark orientation on drawings for structural tees.

Inch and metric mix-ups

A 1 in tee is 25.4 mm, not 25 mm. Panels and glazing designed around one will not fit the other.

Forgetting finish allowances

Powder coating adds roughly 60 – 120 µm per face, which matters when tees slot into tight panel grooves.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) T bar: frequently asked questions

How are aluminium T bar sizes measured?

As flange width × height × thickness. Most catalogues give the overall height from the top of the flange to the tip of the stem; if flange and stem thicknesses differ, both are stated. Confirm the convention on the drawing.

Can aluminum T bars be welded?

6061, 6063 and 6082 tees weld well by TIG or MIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler, but the heat-affected zone loses part of its T6 strength. 5083 and 5086 marine tees keep more strength after welding. 7075 and 2024 tees should be bolted or riveted.

Which T bar alloy is best for marine use?

5083 or 5086 for welded stiffeners in seawater, and 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 for anodised or painted fittings above the waterline.

What is the difference between 6061 and 6063 T bar?

6061-T6 is about 30 % stronger and machines better, so it suits structural tees. 6063 has a smoother surface and anodises more evenly, so it suits trims, glazing bars and decorative frames.

Can T bars be bent?

Yes, with care. Bend with the flange on the outside where possible, use generous radii, and form tight bends in T4 or O temper before ageing.

How much does a 40 × 40 × 3 mm aluminium T bar weigh?

About 0.62 kg/m for a sharp-inside-corner tee, slightly more with a root fillet.

Do you supply custom T sections?

Yes. Metline supplies standard tees and custom T profiles extruded to your drawing, with cutting, drilling and finishing on request and mill certificates for every lot.
