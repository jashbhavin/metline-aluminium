Aluminium Round Tube: Alloys, Types, Sizes and Standards

Aluminium round tube is hollow cylindrical stock made by extrusion, cold drawing or seam welding, in alloys from 1100 to 7075. For most structural and general uses 6061-T6 or 6063-T5/T6 extruded tube is the right choice; seamless drawn tube is used where pressure, tight tolerance or a fine surface matters, and welded tube where low cost per metre counts. This guide explains how to choose between them, and gives size, weight and standards data for ordering.

Overview

What to decide when buying aluminium round tube

Round tube looks like a simple product, but four choices determine whether it suits the job: the alloy and temper, the manufacturing route (seamless, porthole extruded, cold-drawn or welded), the outside diameter and wall thickness, and the standard that governs tolerance and testing. Most problems with tube, such as splits during bending, leaks under pressure or poor anodising, trace back to one of these choices rather than to the supplier.

Round tube is used across almost every industry: frames, handrails and furniture; hydraulic and pneumatic lines; heat-exchanger and HVAC tubing; masts, antenna and telescopic poles; bicycle, motorsport and aircraft structures; and conveyor rollers and machine guards. Its circular section gives equal stiffness in every direction and the best torsional stiffness per kilogram of any open or closed shape, which is why it is preferred for shafts, struts and members that twist.

For a short product specification of the tube Metline supplies, see the round tube specification page. Pipe in nominal pipe sizes and schedules is covered separately on the aluminium pipe page.

Quick recommendations

  • General structural tube: 6061-T6 (ASTM markets) or 6082-T6 (EN markets)
  • Architectural, handrails, furniture: 6063-T5 or T6 for the best anodised surface
  • Lightweight high-strength structures: 7075-T6, 2024-T3 or 7005 / 7020 for welded frames
  • Marine and offshore: 5083, 5086 or 6082 with a suitable finish
  • Bending, flaring and HVAC: 3003 or 1100 in O or H14
  • Pressure lines: seamless tube to ASTM B241 or B210, never porthole tube unless the code allows it

The distinction between seamless and porthole tube matters most. Porthole tube contains seam welds formed in the solid state as the metal rejoins inside the die. Modern practice makes those welds sound for structural use, and porthole tube is the bulk of tube sold for frames and furniture. Pressure-vessel codes and many aerospace specifications, however, require seamless tube. If a drawing says "seamless", order to ASTM B241, B210 or EN 755-7 and check that the certificate states it. More detail is in our guides to seamless tube, drawn tube and welded tube.

Tempers such as 6063-T832 (drawn and aged) and 7005 or 7020 (weldable Al-Zn-Mg tube for frames) are also available; their properties are reported on the mill certificate.

Two common mistakes: choosing 6063 for a load-bearing frame because it is the cheapest extruded tube, and choosing 7075 for a welded structure. 6063 has only about two thirds of the yield strength of 6061, and 7075 is not suitable for fusion welding. For welded high-strength frames, the Al-Zn-Mg alloys 7005 and 7020 are the usual answer because they recover much of their strength by natural ageing after welding.

Weight calculation

How to calculate round tube weight

Round tube weight formulas

Metric
kg/m = 0.0084823 × wall (mm) × (OD − wall) (mm), density 2.70 g/cm³
Imperial
lb/ft = 3.676 × wall (in) × (OD − wall) (in), density 0.0975 lb/in³
Worked example, metric
50 × 3 mm: 0.0084823 × 3 × 47 = 1.196 kg/m; a 6 m length weighs about 7.2 kg
Worked example, inch
2 × 0.125 in: 3.676 × 0.125 × 1.875 = 0.862 lb/ft; a 20 ft length weighs about 17.2 lb
Alloy correction
Multiply by about 1.03 for 2024, 1.04 for 7075 and 0.985 for 5083

Tube weight rises almost in proportion to wall thickness, while bending stiffness rises with roughly the cube of the diameter. Increasing diameter at the same wall is therefore usually the most weight-efficient way to make a tube stiffer, as long as the wall does not become so thin that it dents or buckles locally. Use the tube weight calculator for other sizes.

Tolerances on OD, wall, ovality, straightness and length are set by the standard named on the order. EN 755-7 and EN 755-8 have different tables for seamless and porthole tube, and ASTM orders take tolerances from ANSI H35.2. If concentricity or wall uniformity is critical, for example for telescopic poles or tube that will be bored, say so: seamless drawn tube holds wall thickness more evenly than as-extruded tube.

Working with round tube

Bending, welding, joining and finishing

Bending

Soft tempers (O, T4) and 3003, 5052 or 6063 bend most easily. As a starting point, mandrel bending of 6061-T6 needs a centre-line radius of several times the OD; thin walls need a mandrel and wiper die to prevent wrinkling. For tight bends, bend in T4 or O and heat-treat afterwards.

Welding

6061, 6063, 6082 and the 5xxx alloys weld by TIG or MIG with 4043 or 5356 filler. Heat-treated 6xxx tube loses strength in the heat-affected zone, so welded frames are designed to reduced allowable stresses.

Mechanical joining

Swaging, flaring, crimping and compression fittings suit 3003, 5052 and 6061 in softer tempers. Avoid galvanic contact with copper and plain steel fittings in wet service.

Machining

Tube is turned, bored and threaded like bar; support thin walls to prevent chatter and distortion, and use sharp tools with high positive rake.

Anodising

6063 and 6060 give the most uniform decorative anodising; 6061 anodises well; 2024 and 7075 take hard anodising but look darker.

Corrosion

5xxx and 6xxx tube resists the weather and seawater well; 2024 and 7075 should be anodised or painted in damp or marine service.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) round tube: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between seamless and structural aluminium tube?

Seamless tube is extruded over a mandrel and has no joint in the wall. Structural (porthole) tube is extruded through a bridge die, so the metal splits and re-welds inside the die, leaving longitudinal seam welds. Both are strong enough for structures, but pressure applications normally require seamless tube.

Which is better for a frame, 6061 or 6063 tube?

6061-T6 for anything load-bearing: its typical yield strength is about 276 MPa against 214 MPa for 6063-T6 and 145 MPa for 6063-T5. 6063 is better where appearance and anodising matter more than strength, such as handrails and furniture.

How do I calculate aluminum tube weight?

Weight in kg/m = 0.0084823 × wall × (OD − wall), with dimensions in millimetres. A 50 × 3 mm tube weighs about 1.20 kg/m. In inches, lb/ft = 3.676 × wall × (OD − wall).

Can 7075 aluminium tube be welded?

Not for structural joints: 7075 is prone to hot cracking and loses strength when fusion-welded. Use 6061 or the weldable 7xxx alloys 7005 and 7020 for welded frames.

What is the difference between tube and pipe?

Tube is ordered by actual outside diameter and wall thickness. Pipe is ordered by nominal pipe size and schedule (for example NPS 2 Schedule 40), where the OD is fixed for each size and wall varies with schedule.

Which standards apply to aluminium round tube?

In the US, ASTM B221 for extruded tube, B241 for seamless tube, B210 and B483 for drawn tube and B313 for welded tube. In Europe, EN 755 for extruded and EN 754 for cold-drawn tube.
