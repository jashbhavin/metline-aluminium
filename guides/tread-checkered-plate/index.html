Aluminium Tread Checker Plate in Coil and Sheet

Aluminium tread checker plate (also called chequer, diamond, durbar or treadplate) is rolled aluminium sheet or plate with a raised pattern on one face that gives grip underfoot and stiffens the sheet. It is supplied as cut sheets or as coil for fabricators who cut their own lengths. This guide covers grades, patterns, coil versus sheet supply, sizes, tolerances under EN 1386 and how to choose the right plate for floors, vehicles, steps and boxes.

Overview

What is aluminium tread checker plate?

Tread plate is made by hot or cold rolling aluminium through a pair of rolls, one of which is engraved with the pattern. The raised ribs are formed from the metal of the sheet itself, so they cannot wear off or peel like a coating. The underside stays flat.

The pattern does three jobs: it gives a slip-resistant walking surface, it hides scratches and dents that would show on flat sheet, and it adds a little stiffness. Because aluminium does not rust, tread plate is used where steel chequer plate would need painting and regular maintenance: truck and trailer floors, toolboxes, ramps, stair treads, boat decks, wall protection and cold-store floors.

Names vary by region. "Diamond plate" is common in North America, "chequer plate" and "durbar" in the UK, India and Australia, and "tread plate" in European standards. They all describe the same product; what matters is the alloy, the temper, the base thickness and the pattern type.

Bright 3003 diamond plate

The familiar shiny pattern on truck toolboxes and trim. Chosen for appearance; strong enough for boxes and cladding but not ideal for heavily loaded floors.

5052 and 5754 floor plate

The default for vehicle and marine floors. Higher strength, excellent corrosion resistance and good weldability with 5356 filler.

6061-T6 structural plate

Used where the plate spans between supports and carries load. Highest strength of the common choices, but loses strength near welds.

Pattern height usually ranges from about 0.5 mm on thin lentil and fine patterns to about 2.5 mm on heavy 5-bar plate. EN 1386 defines the patterns, their nominal raised heights for each thickness band and the permitted deviations. A pattern that is too shallow loses grip, especially when wet or oily, so state the pattern height when slip resistance matters.

Slip resistance depends on pattern, wear, contamination and footwear, not on the alloy. If a slip-resistance rating is required, specify the test method (for example a ramp or pendulum test) and test the actual product.

Selection advice

How to choose and specify tread plate

  1. Define the loadA step or toolbox lid needs far less than a vehicle floor or a ramp crossed by forklifts. For spans between supports, size the base thickness and alloy by calculation, using the base thickness only.
  2. Match the environmentUse 5xxx alloys for marine, coastal and de-icing salt exposure; 3003 or 1xxx indoors or for decorative work.
  3. Choose the patternFive-bar or 3-bar for working floors, diamond for appearance and North American style, lentil for interiors and thin gauges.
  4. Specify finish and protectionBright finish for show, mill finish for working floors, PE film to protect bright surfaces during fabrication.
  5. State the standard and documentsEN 1386 or ASTM B632 / B209, with an EN 10204 3.1 certificate.

Common mistakes

  • Calculating strength using total height including the pattern, which overstates stiffness
  • Ordering bright 3003 diamond plate for a heavily loaded vehicle floor
  • Bending across the pattern without enough radius, which cracks the ribs on the outside of the bend
  • Welding 6061-T6 floor plate without allowing for strength loss in the heat-affected zone
  • Fixing aluminium tread plate directly to carbon steel frames in wet service without isolation
Working with tread plate

Cutting, bending, welding and finishing

Cutting

Shear, saw, laser, plasma or waterjet. Put the pattern face up on shears to avoid marking it, and deburr edges for safety.

Bending

Bend with the pattern on the outside and allow generous radii, particularly in harder tempers. Soft tempers (O, H111, H22) bend most easily.

Welding

5xxx and 6xxx tread plate weld well by MIG and TIG; 5356 filler is typical for 5052, 5754 and 5083, and 4043 or 5356 for 6061.

Finishing

Bright plate can be polished; mill-finish plate can be anodised, painted or powder coated. Clean the pattern valleys thoroughly before coating.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) tread checker plate: FAQ

What is the difference between tread plate, checker plate and diamond plate?

They are regional names for the same product: aluminium plate with a raised anti-slip pattern. Diamond plate usually means the single-diamond pattern; checker or chequer plate is often used for 5-bar and other patterns.

Which alloy is best for a trailer or truck floor?

5052 or 5754 in H32, H114 or similar tempers is the usual choice: strong, corrosion resistant and weldable. 6061-T6 is used where more strength is needed and welds are limited; bright 3003 is better kept for boxes and trim.

Is thickness measured including the pattern?

Normally no. Tread plate is ordered by base thickness, and the raised pattern height is specified separately. Use the base thickness for strength calculations.

Can tread plate be supplied in coil?

Yes, thinner gauges are commonly supplied in coil for fabricators who cut their own lengths or need long floors without joints. Heavier plate is usually supplied as flat sheets.

How much does aluminium tread plate weigh?

The base weighs 2.70 kg per m² for each millimetre of thickness; the pattern adds a few percent. A 1220 × 2440 mm sheet with a 3 mm base weighs about 24 kg before the pattern allowance.

Do you ship tread plate internationally?

Yes. Metline supplies aluminium tread checker plate in sheet and coil worldwide from Mumbai, with mill certificates and moisture-proof export packing on pallets or in wooden cases.
