Aluminium Hex Tube: Sizes, Alloys and Applications

Aluminium hex tube is an extruded hollow section with a hexagonal outside and either a round or hexagonal bore. It combines the spanner flats and anti-rotation of hex bar with much lower weight, and the bore can be threaded or used to pass cables and shafts. Most hex tube is 6061-T6/T6511 for strength or 6063-T5/T6 for appearance; this guide covers sizes, weights, section properties and how to specify it.

Overview

What aluminium hex tube is and why it is used

Hex tube is made by extruding a heated billet through a die with a mandrel or bridge that forms the bore. The result is a hollow bar with six flats outside, made in long lengths and cut to size. Because the shape comes from the die, almost any combination of across-flats size, wall thickness and bore shape can be produced.

Engineers choose it for three reasons. First, the flats: a hex tube part can be turned with a spanner, clamped flat in a fixture and prevented from rotating, which is why adjustable rod ends, tie rods and turnbuckles often use it. Second, the bore: a round bore can be tapped with left- and right-hand threads at opposite ends for length adjustment, and a hexagonal bore makes a sliding, torque-transmitting socket for a matching hex shaft. Third, weight: removing the core takes away material that contributes little to bending or torsional stiffness.

Hex tube is also used for its look. Hexagonal sections in anodised 6063 appear in furniture legs, lighting, shelving, display systems and architectural details, where they give a crisper appearance than round tube.

Typical reasons to specify hex tube

  • Spanner flats and anti-rotation on a hollow, lightweight part
  • Threaded bore for adjustable links, standoffs and rod ends
  • Hex bore to slide over and drive a hex shaft
  • Cable or fluid routing through a structural member
  • A distinctive decorative profile that anodises well
Section types

Round-bore and hex-bore hex tube

Hex outside, round bore

The most common type. The round bore can be drilled, reamed or tapped and suits threaded rod ends and bushings. Wall thickness varies between flats and corners, so specify the minimum wall the design needs.

Hex outside, hex bore

Uniform wall all round. Used as a sliding sleeve or coupling over hex shafts and in telescoping assemblies where rotation must be locked.

Thin-wall hex tube

Walls of roughly 1–2 mm for frames, displays, lighting and furniture where loads are low and appearance and weight matter.

Thick-wall hex tube

Walls of about 4 mm and more for tie rods, linkages, tool bodies and machined fittings where threads or cross-holes need material around them.

Custom hex profiles

Hex sections with internal splines, screw ports, slots or multiple bores, made with a dedicated extrusion die.

Machined hex tube parts

Cut lengths, threaded ends, chamfers, cross-holes and slots added after extrusion to make finished components from tube stock.

6061 and 6063 hollow sections are usually extruded through porthole dies, where the metal splits around the mandrel support and re-welds inside the die. This is normal and the welds are sound in these alloys. 7075 is harder to extrude as a porthole hollow, so 7075 hex tube is less common and is sometimes made from seamless round tube or machined from bar instead.

Comparison

Hex tube compared with round tube, square tube and hex bar

Is a hexagonal tube stiffer than a round or square tube? It depends on what is held equal. At the same outside width, a hex tube is stiffer than a round tube because it contains more material, and less stiff than a square tube of the same width for the same reason. At equal weight, the three shapes are close in bending, and round tube remains the most efficient in torsion. Choose hex for its flats and anti-rotation, not because it is inherently stronger.

Shape (width W, solid)AreaSecond moment IMain advantage
Round, diameter W0.785 W²0.049 W⁴Best torsional efficiency, easy to bend and turn
Hexagon, W across flats0.866 W²0.060 W⁴Spanner flats, anti-rotation, orientation-independent stiffness
Square, side W1.000 W²0.083 W⁴Highest bending stiffness at a given width, flat mounting faces

Compared with solid hex bar, hex tube saves weight and leaves a bore for threads or cables, at the cost of lower load capacity in compression and lower thread engagement. If a part sees high axial load or needs deep threads in solid material, hex bar may be the better stock; see our aluminium hex bar guide.

Working with hex tube

Machining, welding and finishing hex tube

Cutting

Saw with carbide blades for square, burr-free ends. For decorative parts, specify cut length tolerance and end finish.

Threading

Tap round bores with sharp, polished taps and cutting fluid. In 6061-T6 thread engagement of about 1.5–2 × diameter is common for load-bearing joints; use thread inserts where parts are frequently adjusted.

Welding

6061 and 6063 weld readily by TIG or MIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. The heat-affected zone loses strength, so keep welds away from highly stressed threaded ends.

Anodising

6063 gives the most uniform decorative finish; 6061 anodises well in clear and black. Anodising adds a few microns to dimensions and coats threads, so mask or chase threads if needed.

Powder coating

Durable coloured finish for furniture, railings and displays; not used on precision fits or threads.

Bending

Hex tube can be bent with shaped tooling, but the flats tend to distort. Bend in O or T4 temper and age afterwards where tight bends are needed.

Applications

Where aluminium hex tube is used

  • Motorsport and vehicles: adjustable tie rods, toe links and turnbuckles with left- and right-hand threaded ends, usually 6061-T6 anodised black
  • Drones and UAV frames: lightweight arms and standoffs in 6061-T6 or 7075-T6
  • Automation and fixtures: spacers, guide sleeves, clamp bodies and sliding couplings over hex shafts
  • Robotics: link members, cable-carrying standoffs and anti-rotation sleeves
  • Furniture and lighting: table and chair legs, lamp stems, shelving posts, mostly 6063-T5 anodised
  • Architecture and display: balustrade elements, decorative grilles and exhibition frames
  • Tools and hardware: tool handles, extension bars and connectors where flats help grip
Ordering

How to specify aluminium hex tube

  1. SectionAcross-flats size, wall thickness (hex bore) or bore diameter (round bore), or a section drawing for custom shapes.
  2. Alloy and temper6061-T6/T6511 for structural and threaded parts, 6063-T5/T6 for decorative parts, 7075 only where strength justifies it.
  3. Standard and tolerancesASTM B221 with ANSI H35.2 tolerances, or EN 755-2 with EN 755-8/-9 tolerances. Note any tighter requirement.
  4. Length and processingMill lengths or cut lengths, plus threading, chamfering, cross-holes or slots if needed.
  5. Finish and documentsMill finish, anodised colour or powder coat; EN 10204 3.1 certificate; packing and Incoterms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP).

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a round bore so large that the wall at the flats is too thin to hold threads
  • Specifying 7075 hollow sections without checking extrusion feasibility
  • Forgetting that anodising coats internal threads
  • Assuming hex tube is stiffer than square tube of the same width

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) hex tube: frequently asked questions

What alloy is aluminum hex tube made from?

Mostly 6061-T6 or T6511 for structural and threaded parts and 6063-T5 or T6 for decorative uses. 6082-T6 is common for EN-specified work, and 7075 is used occasionally where maximum strength is needed.

Can the bore of a hex tube be threaded at both ends?

Yes. Round-bore tube is often tapped with a right-hand thread at one end and a left-hand thread at the other, so a rod can be adjusted in length by turning the tube. Check that the wall at the flats is thick enough for the thread.

How do I calculate the weight of hex tube?

For uniform-wall tube, area = 0.866 × (AF² − (AF − 2t)²). Multiply area in mm² by 0.0027 to get kg/m for 6061. A 25 mm AF tube with a 2 mm wall weighs about 0.43 kg/m.

Is hex tube stronger than round tube?

At the same outside width a hex tube is stiffer because it contains more metal. At equal weight, hex and round tubes are similar in bending and round tube is better in torsion. Hex is chosen mainly for its flats.

What is the difference between hex tube and hex bar?

Hex bar is solid and suits high-load parts and deep threads. Hex tube is hollow, lighter, and leaves a bore for threads, shafts or cables.

Can you supply custom hex tube sections?

Yes. Custom across-flats sizes, walls and bore shapes are extruded from a dedicated die to your drawing, and tube can be cut, threaded or machined to order.
