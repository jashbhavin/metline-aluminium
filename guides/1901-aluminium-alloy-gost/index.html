GOST armour alloy guide

1901 Aluminium Alloy (GOST System)

1901 is a Russian weldable aluminium-zinc-magnesium alloy, with 5.4–6.2 % zinc and 2.4–3.0 % magnesium, best known as an armour material for light military vehicles. It combines high strength after ageing with the ability to be welded into hulls and protective structures. This guide covers its GOST composition, tempers, welding behaviour, cautions and the Western alloys it is compared with.

Overview

What is 1901 aluminium alloy?

Grades numbered 19xx in the Russian four-digit system belong to the Al-Zn-Mg group, the family that also contains 1915 (equivalent to 7005) and 1920 (В92). 1901 is one of the strongest weldable members of that group, designed for armour plate where ballistic resistance, low weight and weldability must be combined.

Aluminium armour became attractive in the 1960s because a thicker aluminium plate of the same areal weight as steel makes a stiffer hull with fewer internal stiffeners. Soviet infantry fighting vehicles of that era used 1901 for parts of the hull; in the West, the comparable development was 5083 and later 7039 armour plate.

Like all high-zinc Al-Zn-Mg alloys, 1901 gains strength from fine MgZn₂-type precipitates formed during ageing. It also shares the family weakness: sensitivity to stress-corrosion cracking if composition, heat treatment and residual stress are not controlled. Chromium, manganese and zirconium are added for exactly that reason.

Key points for buyers

  • High-strength, weldable Al-Zn-Mg alloy aimed at armour and protective structures
  • Heat-affected zones recover much of their strength by natural ageing after welding
  • Stress-corrosion resistance depends on correct ageing and on limiting residual stress
  • Ballistic performance must be proven by testing for each thickness and design

GOST gives a nominal density of 2.78 g/cm³, so a 1500 × 3000 × 20 mm plate weighs about 250 kg. Some supplier literature refers to the alloy as АБТ-101; confirm the designation against GOST 4784 and the product specification on any order.

Al-Zn-Mg alloys have a wide quenching window, so thick plate can be quenched in air or mild water spray without the severe residual stresses of 2xxx or high-copper 7xxx alloys. That is part of what makes them weldable: after welding, the heat-affected zone, which has been briefly solution treated, regains much of its strength over weeks of natural ageing at room temperature.

Guaranteed mechanical properties and ballistic acceptance criteria come from the governing military or aerospace specification. We do not publish generic values for 1901 because they differ by thickness, condition and standard.

Al-Zn-Mg armour generally gives better protection per unit weight than 5083 against armour-piercing small-arms rounds, while 5083 is tougher and more tolerant of corrosion and fabrication. Many vehicles combine both, with 7xxx plates where threat is highest.

Fabrication

Welding and fabrication guidance

Filler choice

Use Al-Mg fillers such as ER5356, ER5183 or ER5556. Silicon fillers (4043) give weaker, less ductile welds on Al-Zn-Mg plate.

Heat input

Keep heat input low and interpass temperature controlled to narrow the softened zone; multi-pass MIG is standard for thick plate.

Natural re-ageing

Allow time for the HAZ to recover by natural ageing before final testing; specifications often set a minimum waiting period.

Stress corrosion

Avoid high residual tensile stress in the short-transverse direction; do not leave plate edges and HAZ unprotected; follow the specified post-weld treatment.

Cutting

Waterjet or saw cutting avoids the heat-affected edges of plasma cutting; machine away heat-affected edges where required.

Protection

Conversion coat and paint the finished structure, sealing edges and weld toes.

Applications

Where 1901 is used

  • Hulls and engine decks of light armoured and airborne vehicles
  • Add-on and spaced armour panels
  • Protective enclosures, hatches and partitions
  • Welded structural parts of military vehicles where weight is critical

Outside defence, weldable Al-Zn-Mg alloys like 7020 and 7005 are used for rail vehicles, bicycle frames and bridges, but 1901 itself is rarely specified there.

FAQ

1901 aluminium alloy: frequently asked questions

What kind of alloy is 1901?

A weldable aluminium-zinc-magnesium alloy (7xxx type) in the Russian GOST 4784 system, with 5.4–6.2 % Zn and 2.4–3.0 % Mg plus Cr, Mn, Zr and Ti.

Is 1901 weldable?

Yes. It is welded with Al-Mg fillers such as 5356 or 5183, and the heat-affected zone recovers much of its strength by natural ageing after welding.

What is the Western equivalent of 1901?

There is no exact equivalent. 7039 is the closest armour alloy in purpose; 7020 is a related weldable structural alloy. Substitution requires testing and approval.

Does 1901 suffer from stress-corrosion cracking?

Like all high-zinc Al-Zn-Mg alloys it can, if heat treatment, residual stress and protection are not controlled. Chromium and zirconium additions and correct ageing reduce the risk.

What thickness of 1901 plate is used for armour?

Typically tens of millimetres, chosen by ballistic testing for the threat level and vehicle design.
