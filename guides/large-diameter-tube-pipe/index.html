Large pipe guide

Large Diameter Aluminium Tube and Pipe

Large diameter aluminium pipe covers round tube from about 300 mm up to more than 1.5 m in diameter, made by seamless extrusion, forging and boring, or by forming and welding plate and strip. The right route depends on diameter, wall thickness, pressure and alloy: seamless for pressure and critical service, spiral- or seam-welded for very large, thin-walled lines. This guide compares the options and explains sizes, standards, fittings and how to order.

Overview

What is large diameter aluminium pipe?

Most extruded aluminium tube and pipe is below about 300 mm (NPS 12) in diameter, the practical limit of ordinary horizontal presses. Larger sizes are made by heavier presses or by entirely different processes, and each process brings its own limits on wall thickness, length, alloy and quality.

Aluminium is chosen for large pipe because it is light (about one third of the weight of steel), does not rust, stays tough at cryogenic temperatures and is non-magnetic and non-sparking. That makes it a standard material for LNG and industrial gas lines, cryogenic transfer, ship and offshore piping, chemical process lines handling media that attack steel, large ducts, fire mains on vessels, and structural uses such as columns, towers and rollers.

In this guide, "pipe" refers to products sized by nominal pipe size (NPS) and schedule, and "tube" to products ordered by outside diameter and wall thickness. In large sizes the two often overlap, and most buyers specify outside diameter and wall directly.

Why aluminium for large pipe

  • About 34 % of the weight of steel pipe of the same size, which cuts support and handling costs
  • Naturally corrosion resistant, with no coating needed in many atmospheres and in seawater for 5xxx alloys
  • Gains strength and keeps ductility at cryogenic temperatures
  • Non-magnetic and non-sparking for sensitive or hazardous areas
  • Fully recyclable at the end of service

As a rule, choose seamless pipe when the line carries pressure, cyclic loads or hazardous media, or when a code requires it. Welded pipe is economical for large, thin-walled lines at low pressure, provided the joint efficiency of the weld is taken into account in the design. Forged and bored tube is reserved for short, heavy, critical parts.

For cryogenic and marine service, 5083 is usually the first choice because it keeps its strength after welding and has no ductile-to-brittle transition. For general process piping, 6061-T6 gives more strength in the as-supplied condition, but welded joints fall to a lower strength, which codes such as ASME B31.3 account for in allowable stresses.

Engineering

Wall thickness and pressure: how it is worked out

For a thin-walled pipe, the hoop stress from internal pressure is approximately p × D / (2 × t), where p is pressure, D the outside diameter and t the wall. Codes such as ASME B31.3 turn this into a required wall thickness using an allowable stress for the alloy and temper, a weld joint quality factor, and allowances for corrosion, threading and manufacturing tolerance.

Three aluminium-specific points change the answer compared with steel. First, allowable stresses are lower, so walls are thicker for the same pressure. Second, for welded construction in heat-treatable alloys such as 6061-T6, codes base allowable stress on the much lower as-welded strength, which often makes 5083 the more efficient choice for welded systems. Third, allowable stresses fall faster with temperature above about 100 – 150 °C, so aluminium piping is generally used from cryogenic temperatures up to moderate service temperatures.

Quality

Inspection and testing

  • Dimensional checks: outside diameter, ovality, wall thickness at several positions, straightness and end squareness
  • Mechanical tests: tensile tests per lot, flattening or expansion tests where the standard requires
  • Non-destructive testing: ultrasonic or eddy-current testing of seamless pipe; radiography or ultrasonic testing of welds in welded pipe
  • Pressure test: hydrostatic or pneumatic test to the standard or project specification
  • Surface: visual inspection for dents, scratches and die lines; internal cleanliness for gas and cryogenic service
  • Certification: EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate; 3.2 with third-party or class surveyor witness on request
Practical advice

Selection tips and common mistakes

  • Specifying seamless where welded would do: for large, low-pressure ducts and cooling water lines, spiral-welded pipe is often far cheaper and available in longer lengths.
  • Specifying welded where code needs seamless: check the design code and the classification society before choosing welded pipe for pressure service.
  • Ignoring weld strength in 6061: the heat-affected zone of welded 6061-T6 drops to well below parent strength; 5083 keeps more of its strength when welded.
  • Mixing metals without isolation: use insulating gaskets, sleeves or bonded transition joints where aluminium pipe meets steel or copper.
  • Underestimating handling: thin-walled large pipe dents easily; specify internal spiders or end protectors and padded supports for transport.
  • Forgetting thermal expansion: aluminium expands about twice as much as steel (about 23 × 10−6 per K), so long lines need expansion loops or bellows.

FAQ

Large diameter aluminium (aluminum) pipe: FAQ

What is the largest diameter aluminum pipe available?

Seamless aluminium pipe is produced up to about 1,300 mm OD on the largest presses, and spiral-welded pipe up to about 1,500 mm. Rolled and welded shells can be made larger. Feasibility depends on wall thickness, alloy and length.

Should I choose seamless or spiral-welded pipe?

Seamless for pressure service, cyclic loading, hazardous media or where the code requires it. Spiral-welded pipe suits large, thin-walled, low-pressure lines such as ducts and cooling water, where it is more economical.

Which alloy is best for large diameter LNG or cryogenic pipe?

5083 in O or H112 temper is the usual choice: it stays tough at cryogenic temperatures, welds well and keeps good strength in the weld zone. 6061-T6 is also used where higher parent strength is needed.

How much does a large diameter aluminium pipe weigh?

Weight per metre is π × (OD − wall) × wall × 0.0027 kg with dimensions in mm. For example NPS 24 schedule 10S (610 mm × 6.35 mm) weighs about 32.5 kg/m, and a 1,000 × 50 mm seamless tube about 403 kg/m.

Can you supply fittings with the pipe?

Yes. Flanges, elbows, tees, reducers and caps in matching alloys can be supplied with the pipe, along with cutting, end bevelling and certification.

What certificates are provided?

An EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate with chemistry and mechanical results; NDT and pressure test reports where specified; 3.2 certification with third-party or class surveyor witness on request.
