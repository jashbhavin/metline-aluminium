8011 Aluminium Bottle Caps: ROPP Caps, Screw Caps & Vial Seals

Most aluminium bottle caps, from 30 × 60 mm wine closures to 20 mm injection-vial seals, are deep-drawn from 8011 closure sheet in H14 or H16 temper, roughly 0.17 – 0.25 mm thick. The alloy combines enough strength to hold thread and torque with the low earing and clean drawing a high-speed cap press needs. This guide explains cap types, sizes, liners, material specifications and how to specify caps or cap stock, which Metline supplies to bottlers and closure makers worldwide.

Overview

Why aluminium caps are made from 8011

An aluminium cap starts as a flat, lacquered sheet less than a quarter of a millimetre thick. It is punched into a disc, drawn into a cup, decorated, lined and, for roll-on caps, threaded on the bottle itself by the capping head. Every step depends on the sheet behaving the same way from coil to coil.

8011 is an aluminium-iron-silicon alloy. The iron and silicon form fine intermetallic particles that strengthen the metal and refine its grain without the need for heat treatment, so the sheet gains strength through cold rolling and partial annealing (the H14 and H16 tempers). Compared with pure 1xxx aluminium, 8011 is stiffer and more resistant to denting and stretching under capping loads; compared with 3105 or 5052, it is cheaper and draws with very low earing when the rolling and annealing schedule is controlled.

That is why 8011 has become the default closure alloy for roll-on pilfer-proof (ROPP) caps on wine, spirits, water and edible oil, and is widely used for pharmaceutical vial seals and twist-off lids. 3105 is the usual alternative where a cap maker wants a little more strength or a different drawing response; we cover it in our 3105 closure strip guide.

What closure buyers need from the sheet

  • Low and consistent earing (typically 2 – 3 % or less) so drawn shells have an even rim and skirt
  • Tight gauge tolerance, usually around ±0.005 – 0.01 mm, so drawing and thread rolling stay stable
  • Strength that holds after lacquer curing, because stoving at around 200 °C softens the metal slightly
  • A clean, degreased surface that gives reliable lacquer and ink adhesion
Product range

Types of aluminium bottle caps

The word "cap" covers several quite different closures. They share the same base metal but differ in how they seal, how they show tampering and which liner they carry.

ROPP caps (roll-on pilfer-proof)

Supplied as plain, unthreaded shells. The capping head presses the cap onto the bottle and rolls the thread and the tamper band into the metal so they follow the glass exactly. Used on still wine, spirits, water, juices and oils.

Long wine caps

The familiar 30 × 60 mm screw cap for still wine, with a long decorative skirt that imitates a capsule over a cork. The liner controls how much oxygen reaches the wine.

Pre-threaded screw caps

Caps with formed threads, applied by screwing rather than rolling. Common on small bottles, cosmetics and some food jars.

Vial seals and flip-off caps

Crimp seals for 13, 20 and 32 mm injection vials. A rubber stopper does the sealing; the aluminium skirt is crimped under the vial lip to hold it. Flip-off caps add a coloured plastic button.

Pull-ring and tear-off caps

Caps with a scored tear strip and ring, used on infusion bottles, sauces and some beverages where the cap is removed without tools.

Twist-off lids and easy-open ends

Wide-mouth closures for jars and cans, made from lacquered 8011 or 3xxx/5xxx strip depending on diameter and pressure.

Sealing

Cap structure and liner options

The aluminium shell holds the closure on and shows whether it has been opened, but the liner is what actually seals against the bottle lip. Choosing the right liner matters as much as choosing the metal.

  • Shell: a deep-drawn 8011 cup, often knurled on the skirt for grip, with a perforated tamper band on ROPP caps
  • Top panel: flat or slightly domed, carrying the brand print, embossing or hot-foil stamping
  • Thread zone: rolled onto the bottle during capping (ROPP) or pre-formed (screw caps)
  • Liner: a foam, film, foil or elastomer disc, or a flowed-in compound, chosen for the product and its processing
Liner typePropertiesRecommended for
PE / EPE foamCushioning, good leak-tightness, economicalStill water, juices, edible oil, sauces
PVDC-faced film (Saranex-type)Moderate, controlled oxygen transmissionWines intended for drinking within a few years
Tin-foil barrier (Saran-tin type)Very low oxygen transmissionWines for long cellar ageing, aroma-sensitive spirits
Induction foil linerHermetic seal welded to the lip; clear tamper evidenceNutraceuticals, powders, condiments shipped long distances
Rubber / bromobutyl stopperPierceable, sterilisable, chemically inertInjection vials (used with crimp seals)
PVC-free flowed compoundSeals under pressure and pasteurisationCarbonated drinks, twist-off food jars

Liner choice also drives regulatory compliance: liners and lacquers that touch the product must be approved food-contact or pharmaceutical materials in the destination market, for example under US FDA 21 CFR or EU Regulation 1935/2004.

Customisation

Decoration, colour and finishing options

Colour and lacquer

Base coats in gold, silver, white, black or Pantone-matched colours; matt, gloss, metallic and soft-touch finishes. The inner lacquer protects the metal from the product.

Printing

Top and side printing by offset lithography on flat sheet before drawing, or by dry offset and screen on finished caps; multi-colour logos and batch codes.

Embossing and hot-foil

Raised or recessed logos on the top panel and hot-foil stamping for a premium look on wine and spirits caps.

Knurling

Vertical knurls on the skirt improve grip and hide minor marks; patterns vary by cap maker and brand.

Security features

Tamper bands, hidden marks and serialised printing help fight counterfeiting of high-value spirits.

Packing

Bulk cartons for hopper feeding, layer packing for automatic lines, or palletised and shrink-wrapped for export.

Aluminium caps also suit sustainability goals. Aluminium can be recycled repeatedly without losing its properties, and caps left on glass bottles are increasingly recovered in glass and metal sorting streams. Brands that move from a cork and capsule to an aluminium screw cap also remove a separate foil or PVC capsule.

Process

From 8011 closure sheet to finished cap

  1. Rolling and annealingThe closure sheet is rolled to final gauge and partly annealed to H14 or H16, with the schedule set to give low earing.
  2. Coating and printingSheet or coil is degreased, coated with inner and outer lacquers, printed and stoved, typically around 180 – 210 °C.
  3. Blanking and drawingDiscs are punched and drawn in one or more stages into a cup of the required height; earing shows up here as an uneven rim.
  4. Forming detailsKnurls, tamper-band perforations, beads and vent slots are formed, and the rim is trimmed.
  5. LiningPre-cut liners are inserted, or compound is flowed in and cured.
  6. Inspection and packingCaps are checked for height, diameter, coating adhesion, liner position and appearance, then packed for automatic feeding.

For cap makers, the main material-related causes of scrap are uneven earing, gauge variation across the coil width, poor flatness and lacquer adhesion failures. Ask for earing to be tested in the delivered (or paint-baked) condition and for gauge to be reported across the width, not just at the centre.

Specification checklist

How to specify aluminium caps or 8011 cap stock

Cap specification (agreed with your closure maker)

  • Product and how it is filled (still, carbonated, hot-fill, pasteurised, sterilised)
  • Bottle type and neck finish drawing (glass or PET)
  • Cap type and size, for example 30 × 60 mm ROPP
  • Liner type and any oxygen, CO2 or sterilisation requirements
  • Colours (Pantone references), print, embossing and hot-foil
  • Annual volume, trial quantity and destination

For 8011 cap stock

  • Alloy and temper (8011-H14, H16) and the governing standard
  • Gauge and tolerance, width and slit edge quality
  • Mechanical limits and earing, and whether measured after paint-bake
  • Surface: mill finish, degreased or pre-lacquered, with coating system
  • Coil inner diameter, outer diameter or coil weight, and packing
  • Certificates required (EN 10204 3.1, food-contact declarations)

Delivery terms are quoted EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP. Cap stock ships in coils on wooden pallets or in cases, eye-to-sky or eye-to-wall, wrapped against moisture.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) bottle caps: frequently asked questions

Why is 8011 used for bottle caps instead of pure aluminium?

The iron and silicon in 8011 make it stronger and stiffer than 1xxx aluminium at the same gauge, so caps resist denting and hold their thread under capping load, while the alloy still draws cleanly with low earing. It is also economical and widely available as closure stock.

What thickness is aluminium cap stock?

Most ROPP and screw caps are drawn from sheet about 0.20 – 0.25 mm thick; small caps and vial seals often use about 0.17 – 0.20 mm. The exact gauge depends on cap diameter, height and the cap maker's tooling.

What is the difference between a ROPP cap and a screw cap?

A ROPP cap arrives as a plain shell; the capping machine rolls the thread and tamper band into it on the bottle, so it fits each neck exactly. A pre-threaded screw cap already has its thread and is screwed on.

Which liner should I use for wine screw caps?

For wines drunk within a few years, PVDC-faced film liners give a small, controlled oxygen ingress. For wines meant for long ageing, tin-foil barrier liners let in very little oxygen. Winemakers choose the liner to suit the wine style.

Are aluminum bottle caps safe for food and medicines?

Yes, when made from food-contact grade alloy (for example to EN 602) and coated with approved inner lacquers and liners. The product touches the lacquer and liner, not bare metal, and those materials must comply with the rules of the destination market.

Are aluminium caps recyclable?

Yes. Aluminium can be recycled repeatedly without loss of quality, and caps are recovered in metal sorting streams. Leaving the cap on the bottle often helps it get captured rather than lost as a small loose item.

Can Metline supply cap stock for my own cap line?

Yes. We supply 8011 closure sheet and strip in H14, H16 and other tempers, slit to your width, mill finish or pre-lacquered, with mill test certificates, to closure makers worldwide.
