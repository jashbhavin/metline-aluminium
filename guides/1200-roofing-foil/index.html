Roofing foil guide

1200 Aluminium Foil for Roofing

1200 is a commercially pure aluminium (99.0 % Al minimum) that is rolled into foil for roof radiant barriers, insulation facings, underlays and colour-coated roofing laminates. Bright aluminium reflects about 95 % of radiant heat and has an emittance near 0.03–0.05, so a foil layer facing an air space cuts heat gain through hot roofs. This guide covers grade, product types, performance standards and the installation details that decide whether it works.

Overview

Why 1200 aluminium is used for roofing foil

Heat enters a building through a hot roof mainly as radiation from the underside of the roof covering. A surface with low emittance radiates very little heat, and a surface with high reflectance sends incoming radiation back. Bright aluminium does both, which is why a thin foil can reduce heat flow far more than its thickness suggests.

1200 is a good fit for this job. Its 99.0 % purity gives a bright, corrosion-resistant surface; it rolls easily to thin gauges; and it is slightly stronger than 1050 or 1060 because it carries more iron and silicon, which helps with handling and lamination. 8011 and 8079 are alternatives where higher strength or better pinhole performance at very thin gauges is needed.

Roofing foil is almost never used on its own. It is laminated to kraft paper, woven polyethylene, polyester film, bubble film or foam, or bonded to bitumen and metal sheet. The foil supplies reflectance and vapour resistance; the backing supplies strength, puncture resistance and fixing.

Products

Main types of 1200 roofing foil

Radiant barrier laminates

Foil on one or both faces of kraft paper, woven PE or reinforced scrim. Fixed under rafters or over purlins with the foil facing an air gap.

Bubble and foam foil insulation

Foil laminated to one or two layers of polyethylene bubble or foam. Adds a small amount of resistive insulation and acts as a vapour barrier.

Insulation facings

Foil-scrim-kraft (FSK) and foil-faced boards for mineral wool, PIR and glass wool used in roofs and ducts.

Colour-coated foil

Polyester or PVDF lacquered foil for visible underlays, bitumen membrane surfacing and decorative laminates.

Foil tape

Pressure-sensitive foil tape to seal laps and penetrations so the vapour barrier stays continuous.

Self-adhesive bitumen flashing

Embossed or coloured foil bonded to butyl or bitumen for roof flashings and repairs.

Products sold as "roofing foil" in 0.3–3 mm thickness are in fact sheet or coil, not foil. Corrugated or profiled roofing in that range is normally made from 3003, 3105 or 5052, not 1200.

Under ASTM C1313, a radiant barrier surface must have an emittance of 0.1 or less. Clean bright 1200 foil achieves this easily; a lacquer or clear coating raises emittance, and dust on an upward-facing surface raises it over time.

Design

Installation rules that decide performance

  1. Keep an air spaceFoil only reduces radiant transfer across an air gap. A gap of about 20–25 mm (roughly 1 in) or more on the foil side is a practical minimum; foil sandwiched tight between two solid layers acts only as a vapour barrier.
  2. Face the foil the right wayDownward-facing foil stays clean and keeps its low emittance. Upward-facing foil collects dust, which steadily reduces its benefit.
  3. Seal lapsOverlap joints and tape them with foil tape where the laminate is also the vapour control layer.
  4. Think about condensationFoil is a vapour barrier. In cold climates it belongs on the warm side of the insulation; in hot humid climates follow local guidance to avoid trapping moisture.
  5. Avoid galvanic contactSeparate foil from copper and wet treated timber; use compatible fixings.
Applications

Where 1200 roofing foil is used

  • Metal-roofed warehouses, factories and poultry houses, under sheeting and above purlins
  • Residential attics, under tiles or rafters as a reflective underlay
  • Insulation facings for mineral wool and foam boards
  • Surfacing for bitumen membranes and flashing tapes
  • Duct and pipe insulation jackets

Benefits are largest in hot, sunny climates with air-conditioned spaces, where radiant gain through the roof dominates. In cold climates, foil contributes more as a vapour barrier than as insulation.

FAQ

1200 aluminium roofing foil: frequently asked questions

How much heat does aluminium roofing foil reflect?

Clean, bright aluminium reflects about 95 % of radiant heat and has an emittance around 0.03–0.05. Real savings depend on the air gap, orientation, dust and climate.

Does roofing foil need an air gap?

Yes. The low-emittance surface must face an air space to reduce radiant transfer. Without a gap it works mainly as a vapour barrier.

Is 1200 better than 8011 for roofing foil?

1200 is purer and slightly more corrosion resistant; 8011 is stronger and has fewer pinholes at thin gauges. Both are widely used; choose by gauge and laminating process.

Can roofing foil be used in cold climates?

Yes, mainly as a vapour control layer on the warm side of insulation. Its radiant benefit is smaller than in hot climates.

What thickness of foil is used for roof insulation?

Laminated radiant barriers typically use foil from about 6 to 25 µm; exposed or coated foils are thicker. The backing provides most of the strength.
