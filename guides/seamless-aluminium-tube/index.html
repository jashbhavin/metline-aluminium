Seamless Aluminium Tube: Processes, Grades, Standards and Sizes

Seamless aluminium tube is extruded or drawn over a mandrel from a hollow or pierced billet, so its wall has no longitudinal weld line of any kind. That makes it the required choice for pressure lines, hydraulic and fuel tubing, aerospace structures and any tube that will be heavily machined, expanded or fatigue-loaded. This guide explains how seamless tube is made, which alloys and specifications to order, the standard tolerances and weights, and how to write an enquiry that gets the right product first time.

Overview

What makes an aluminium tube "seamless"?

Every aluminium tube starts as a solid or hollow billet. What separates a seamless tube from the rest is how the hole is formed. In a seamless tube the hole is made by a mandrel that passes through the centre of a hollow or pierced billet, so the metal flows around it as a single, continuous ring. Nothing is split and rejoined, and the finished wall is metallurgically uniform all the way round.

Two other kinds of tube are sold alongside seamless tube, and buyers often mix them up. Porthole or bridge-die tube (called "structural" tube in ASTM B221 and "porthole tube" in EN 755-8) is extruded from a solid billet through a die that splits the metal into several streams and then rewelds them under pressure in a welding chamber. The result looks identical to seamless tube, and the solid-state extrusion welds are sound enough for frames, furniture and architecture, but they are lines of different grain structure along the length. Welded tube is roll-formed from strip and joined by high-frequency or other welding, leaving a visible or trimmed seam.

Those seam lines are where the differences start to matter. Under internal pressure, during flaring or expanding, in high-cycle fatigue and in heavily machined parts, a weld line can be the first place a crack starts. Standards reflect this: ASTM B221 states that its structural tube is not intended for pressure applications, and points pressure users to ASTM B241 seamless pipe and tube and B210 drawn seamless tube. Aerospace hydraulic specifications such as AMS 4081 and AMS 4083 likewise cover seamless drawn tubing only.

When you should insist on seamless tube

  • Any line that carries gas or liquid under pressure, including hydraulic, pneumatic, fuel, oil and refrigerant tubing
  • Tubes that will be flared, swaged, expanded, bent tightly or bored to a close internal finish
  • Fatigue-loaded structures: aircraft and helicopter parts, motorsport and bicycle frames, booms and masts
  • Drawings or purchase specifications that call up ASTM B241, B210, B234, B345 or an AMS seamless tubing spec
  • Thick-walled tube used as a hollow machining blank for bushings, rings, cylinders and sleeves
Production

How seamless aluminium tube is produced

All seamless tube begins on an extrusion press fitted with a mandrel. Smaller diameters and tight tolerances are then reached by cold drawing; very large diameters may be produced on heavy presses or by forging and rolling. The route chosen decides the size range, the tolerance class and the surface finish you can expect.

  1. Billet preparationHomogenised DC-cast billet is cut to length. It is either cast hollow, bored, or pierced on the press by the mandrel itself. The billet is heated, typically to around 400 – 500 °C depending on alloy.
  2. Direct mandrel extrusionThe ram pushes the billet through the die while the mandrel holds the bore. Friction between billet and container is high, so press force and temperature rise along the push; this route suits large diameters and thick walls and gives a robust, economical tube.
  3. Indirect (backward) extrusionThe die moves into a stationary billet, so there is no container friction. Lower and steadier pressure gives more uniform properties along the length, better concentricity and less surface defect, which is why indirect presses are favoured for high-strength 2xxx and 7xxx tube and aerospace grades.
  4. Heat treatmentHeat-treatable alloys are solution treated and quenched (on the press for some 6xxx alloys, in a separate furnace for 2xxx and 7xxx), then stretched to straighten and relieve stress, and naturally or artificially aged to the ordered temper.
  5. Cold drawingFor smaller or more precise tube, the extruded "mother tube" is annealed and pulled through a hardened die over a plug or floating mandrel. Each pass reduces diameter and wall, raises strength by cold work and improves surface finish. Several passes with intermediate anneals may be needed.
  6. Finishing and inspectionTubes are straightened, cut, inspected for dimensions and surface, and tested as the specification requires: tensile tests, flattening or flaring tests, eddy-current or ultrasonic inspection, and hydrostatic or pneumatic tests for pressure tube.

Alloy selection

Common alloys for seamless tube and what each is for

Almost any wrought alloy can be made into seamless tube, but a dozen grades cover most orders. The high-strength 2xxx and 7xxx alloys and the high-magnesium 5xxx alloys are hard to push through porthole dies and weld poorly in the welding chamber, so in practice tube in those grades is nearly always seamless.

A short decision rule: start with 6061-T6 for any general engineering or pressure duty; move to 5083/5086 if the tube lives in seawater or must be welded without heat treatment; choose 3003 or 1100 when formability, brazing or conductivity matter more than strength; and go to 2024 or 7075 only when the strength-to-weight requirement justifies the extra cost and the corrosion protection they need.

Two practical consequences follow from the table. First, drawn tube in a strain-hardened temper (such as 3003-H14 or 5052-H32) gains its strength from cold work, so it softens if heated for brazing or welding; design the joint for annealed (O-temper) properties. Second, heat-treatable tube welded in the T6 condition loses strength in the heat-affected zone; 6061-T6 drops to roughly T4-level strength next to the weld unless the assembly is solution treated and aged again.

Product types

Types and shapes of seamless aluminium tube

Seamless extruded tube

Hot-extruded over a mandrel and supplied in the extruded or heat-treated temper. Economical for medium and large diameters and thick walls, to commercial extruded tolerances. Ordered to ASTM B241, B221 (seamless) or EN 755.

Cold-drawn seamless tube

Extruded mother tube reduced through dies for close tolerances, thin walls and a smooth surface. The usual choice for hydraulic, fuel, instrument and heat-exchanger tube. Ordered to ASTM B210, B234, AMS specs or EN 754.

Seamless pipe (SCH 40 / SCH 80)

Seamless tube made to nominal pipe sizes and schedule wall thicknesses so it fits standard flanges and fittings. ASTM B241 covers 6061 and 6063 pipe widely used in process and pneumatic systems.

Seamless bus pipe

Round tubular conductors in 6063, 6101 or 1xxx alloys for outdoor substations. The tube shape gives a large surface for heat loss, low skin-effect losses and a stiff span between insulators.

Large-diameter and thick-wall tube

Produced on heavy presses or by forging and ring rolling for cylinders, rollers, sleeves and pressure shells. Often supplied as rough-machined hollows. Sizes are confirmed project by project.

Coiled seamless tube

Small-diameter 1xxx or 3xxx tube in long annealed coils for refrigeration, air-conditioning and instrument lines, where joints must be minimised.

Shape options follow the same processes. Round tube is by far the most common, but seamless square, rectangular, oval (elliptical) and streamlined (aerofoil) tubes can be drawn from round mother tube through shaped dies. Streamlined tube is used for aircraft struts and fairings; oval and flattened tube appears in heat exchangers and furniture. Non-round seamless shapes carry corner-radius and convexity tolerances in addition to width and wall tolerances.

  • Thin-wall tube: light structures, heat-dissipation parts, telescopic poles and cosmetic components
  • Thick-wall tube: pressure vessels, hydraulic cylinders, deep-sea housings and machining blanks
  • Small-diameter and capillary tube: instruments, medical devices, sensors and refrigeration capillaries
  • Precision tube: close OD/ID tolerances and fine surface for honing, bearing seats and telescoping fits
  • Anodised or polished tube: decorative, optical and consumer parts where a uniform appearance is required

A typical example shows how the choice is made. A hydraulic return line on an aircraft is drawn from 6061 to AMS 4083 (T6) because the specification demands seamless, eddy-current-inspected tube with flaring and flattening tests. A substation bus run, by contrast, is extruded 6063-T6 or 6101 seamless tube chosen for conductivity, stiffness between supports and good weathering, with tight tolerances only where it meets the clamps. A hydraulic cylinder barrel starts as thick-wall 6061-T6 seamless extruded tube that is then bored and honed, so concentricity and the absence of seam lines matter more than outside-diameter tolerance.

How to specify

How to order seamless aluminium tube, and mistakes to avoid

  1. Define the dutyState what the tube does: pressure line (give design pressure and temperature), structural member, machining blank, conductor or heat-exchanger tube. This decides whether seamless is mandatory and which code applies.
  2. Choose alloy and temperFor example 6061-T6, 3003-O, 5083-H112 or 2024-T3. If parts will be welded, brazed or formed after delivery, say so, because it may change the temper you should order.
  3. Give dimensions properlyAny two of OD, ID and wall (never all three as hard limits), or NPS plus schedule for pipe; length (fixed or random) and quantity in pieces, metres or kilograms.
  4. Name the specificationASTM B241, B210, B234, B345, an AMS number or EN 755 / EN 754. Add any special tolerance, straightness, surface or inspection requirement.
  5. Agree testing and documentsMill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1, hydrostatic or eddy-current testing where the standard requires it, and third-party inspection if your project needs it.
  6. Agree packing and deliveryTubes are capped, wrapped and packed in wooden cases or bundles for export, and shipped on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.

Common mistakes on seamless tube orders

  • Ordering "6061 tube to ASTM B221" for a pressure line: B221 allows structural (porthole) tube unless seamless is stated
  • Specifying OD, ID and wall all as fixed dimensions, which over-constrains the tube and conflicts with the tolerance tables
  • Asking for extruded tube when drawn tolerances are really needed, or paying for drawn tube where extruded tolerances would do
  • Copying a cancelled federal spec (WW-T-700, QQ-A-200) without agreeing the current replacement
  • Forgetting that brazing or welding anneals strain-hardened tube and softens T6 tube near the joint
  • Treating an EN 754-8 or EN 755-8 order as seamless; those parts cover porthole tube

FAQ

Seamless aluminium (aluminum) tube: frequently asked questions

How can I tell whether an aluminium tube is seamless or porthole extruded?

They look the same. The reliable way is the paperwork: the certificate should name a seamless specification (ASTM B241, B210, B234, B345 or an AMS seamless spec) or state "seamless". Physically, a macro-etched cross-section of porthole tube usually shows extrusion weld lines as faint radial bands; seamless tube shows a uniform grain ring.

Is seamless aluminium tube stronger than welded tube?

The base metal has the same properties, but a seamless tube has no weld or extrusion-weld line, so it performs better under internal pressure, in fatigue and in expanding, flaring and tight bending. That is why pressure and aerospace specifications require it.

What is the difference between extruded and drawn seamless tube?

Extruded seamless tube is hot-extruded over a mandrel and held to commercial extruded tolerances. Drawn seamless tube is then cold drawn through dies, giving much closer diameter and wall tolerances, thinner walls, a smoother surface and higher strength in strain-hardened tempers.

Which alloy is best for seamless aluminium pressure pipe?

6061-T6 and 6063-T6 to ASTM B241 are the most common for process, compressed-air and gas lines. 5083 and 5086 are chosen for seawater and cryogenic service. The allowable pressure is set by the piping code (for example ASME B31.3), the alloy, temper, wall thickness and temperature.

How much does seamless aluminium tube weigh?

Multiply π/4 × (OD² − ID²) in mm by 2.70 and divide by 1000 for kg per metre. A 50 × 5 mm 6061 tube weighs about 1.91 kg/m; a 100 × 10 mm tube about 7.63 kg/m.
