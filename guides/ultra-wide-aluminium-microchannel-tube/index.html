Ultra-Wide Thin-Wall Aluminium Microchannel Tube

Ultra-wide microchannel tubes are flat, multi-port aluminium extrusions, from about 100 mm to several hundred millimetres wide, with thin walls and many parallel internal channels. A single extrusion can replace two or more welded profiles in battery cooling plates, tray floors and large heat exchangers, cutting weld seams, leak paths and weight. This guide covers design, alloys, tolerances, coatings and testing for engineers and buyers.

Overview

What is an ultra-wide microchannel tube?

Conventional microchannel (multi-port) tube for air-conditioning condensers is typically 12 – 50 mm wide and 1 – 3 mm high, with walls of a few tenths of a millimetre. Ultra-wide microchannel tubes keep the same principle, a flat hollow profile divided into many small parallel channels, but scale the width up to cover a battery module, cold plate or panel in one piece.

The channels give a very large wetted surface for the coolant, while the flat outer faces sit directly against battery cells, power semiconductors or fins. Because the part is extruded in one piece, the channels, ribs and mounting features are all integral, and there is no weld along the length that could leak or distort.

Producing these sections is demanding. The die has to feed metal evenly into dozens of thin webs across a wide section, and the press must deliver enough force through a container large enough for the width. Flat (oval) containers help, because they allow a wider profile from a given press size than a round container. Wide thin-wall extrusions therefore have a narrower process window than ordinary profiles, and die design is the key to flatness and wall consistency.

Benefits of one wide extrusion over welded assemblies

  • Fewer friction-stir or MIG weld seams, so fewer leak paths and less distortion
  • Less material and lower weight, because overlap and weld lands are removed
  • Flatter contact faces for better thermal contact with cells and modules
  • Fewer parts, dies and process steps in the assembly line

A typical example: a cooling plate around 500 mm wide and 10 mm high with walls of 1 – 2 mm might replace two friction-welded halves. Removing the centre weld removes the lapped lands on either side of it, which typically saves several per cent of the weight per metre and gives a continuous flat face. The exact saving depends on wall thickness and channel layout, so it should be calculated from the actual section drawing.

Alloys for wide microchannel tubes

Alloy and temperMain propertyTypical use

Two families serve two purposes. 3xxx and 1xxx alloys are used where the part will be furnace-brazed, because their melting range sits safely above Al-Si filler and they resist corrosion in coolant. 6xxx alloys are chosen where the tube must also carry structural load, and are usually joined by friction-stir welding, MIG, adhesive or mechanical fastening rather than brazing. Chemistry follows EN 573-3 and Aluminum Association limits; mechanical properties follow EN 755-2 or ASTM B221.

For cooling plates, flatness and twist over the length often matter more than the nominal wall thickness, because they control thermal contact with the cells or the gap filler thickness. Specify flatness across the width and along the length, wall tolerance, and any surface requirements on contact faces. Standard profile tolerances are usually too loose for wide cooling plates, so critical tolerances should be written on the drawing and agreed with the extruder before die making.

Surface treatment

Coatings for brazing and corrosion protection

Pre-flux coating

A flux-containing coating applied to the tube before brazing, so separate fluxing of the assembly is reduced or eliminated and joint filling is more consistent.

Si-flux coating

A mix of silicon powder and non-corrosive fluoroaluminate flux that forms its own filler metal during controlled-atmosphere brazing, letting unclad fins be brazed to the tube.

Zinc arc spray

A thin zinc layer sprayed on the outside of tube in line with extrusion. On brazing, zinc diffuses into the surface and forms a sacrificial layer that protects the tube walls from pitting.

Electrophoretic (E-coat)

A uniform paint film for corrosion protection and electrical insulation on structural or non-brazed parts; also a primer for further coating or bonding.

Insulating films and powder coat

Dielectric layers on battery-contact faces where cells must be electrically isolated from the cooling plate.

Bare mill finish

For parts that will be cleaned, bonded or coated by the customer after machining.

Coatings change the process window downstream. Zinc arc spray, for example, must be matched to the brazing cycle so that enough zinc diffuses into the surface without migrating too deep, and flux-coated tube must be stored dry and handled cleanly so the coating stays intact until it reaches the furnace. Tell us the brazing method, furnace profile and coolant early, so the coating weight and type can be specified correctly.

Selection rule: choose pre-flux or Si-flux for brazed assemblies, zinc arc spray where coolant-side or external corrosion is a risk, and E-coat or insulating films where electrical isolation and appearance matter.

Processing

Cutting, forming and leak testing

  • Cutting: precision sawing or flying-saw cutting to length, with burr control so channels are not blocked.
  • End preparation: end flattening, channel opening, machining of header slots, chamfers and flanges for manifold connection.
  • Bending: wide multi-port sections can be bent, but tight radii risk collapsing or cracking the internal webs; bend radius should be validated on samples for the actual section.
  • Joining: furnace brazing for 3xxx tubes with headers; friction-stir welding, laser welding or adhesive bonding for 6xxx plates.
  • Leak and pressure testing: air-under-water, pressure-decay or helium leak testing to the drawing, with burst tests on samples during validation.
  • Inspection: dimensional and flatness checks, wall thickness on sections, chemistry and tensile testing, with records kept per lot.
Applications

Where wide microchannel tubes are used

EV battery cooling plates

Wide 6063 or 3003 multi-port plates placed under or between modules, carrying glycol coolant close to the cells.

Battery tray floors

Taller 6061 or 6063 sections that combine the structural floor of the tray with integral coolant channels.

Energy-storage systems

Liquid-cooled cold plates for stationary battery racks and containerised storage.

HVAC and condensers

Wider multi-port tubes for compact, high-capacity heat exchangers and refrigerated transport units.

Power electronics

Cold plates for inverters, telecom base stations and data-centre equipment.

Solar and thermal panels

Absorber plates and thermal panels where a flat face and parallel channels are needed.

FAQ

Ultra-wide aluminium (aluminum) microchannel tube: frequently asked questions

What is a wide thin-wall extrusion?

It is a flat aluminium profile that is very wide relative to its wall thickness, for example several hundred millimetres wide with walls of one to a few millimetres, usually with many internal channels. Such sections need careful die design and suitable press capacity.

Can microchannel aluminium tube be bent?

Yes, but the minimum radius depends on the section height, web layout and temper. Tight bends can collapse or crack internal webs, so the bend radius should be proven on samples of the actual profile.

Which alloy is best for a battery cooling plate?

6063-T5/T6 is common for extruded cooling plates joined by welding or bonding; 3003 is preferred when the plate is furnace-brazed to headers. 6061 or 6005A is used where the plate also carries structural load.

What is the extrusion ratio and why does it matter?

It is the cross-sectional area of the billet divided by that of the profile. Wide thin-wall sections have high ratios, which demand more press force and careful die design to keep walls uniform and the profile flat.

What certificates and test reports are supplied?

An EN 10204 3.1 material certificate with chemistry and mechanical properties, dimensional and flatness reports, and leak or pressure test records where specified on the drawing.

Does Metline supply complete cooling plates or battery trays?

No. Metline supplies the aluminium tubes and profiles, cut to length and processed to drawing (end preparation, coating). Assembly into finished cooling plates, trays or heat exchangers is done by the customer.
