Aluminium Microchannel Tube (Multi-Port Extrusion): Complete Guide

Aluminium microchannel tube, also called multi-port extrusion (MPE) or parallel-flow flat tube, is a thin flat extrusion with a row of small internal channels. Brazed to louvred fins and headers, it forms compact, light and refrigerant-efficient heat exchangers for car air conditioning, residential and commercial HVAC, heat pumps and battery cooling. This guide covers alloys, sizes, coatings, brazing, corrosion protection and how to specify the tube.

Overview

What is an aluminium microchannel tube?

A microchannel tube is a flat aluminium extrusion, typically 1 – 3 mm high for air-conditioning use, containing anything from three to more than forty parallel ports separated by thin webs. Refrigerant or coolant flows through the ports; air flows over brazed fins on the flat faces. The many small channels give a very large internal surface for the volume of the tube and let thin walls hold high pressure.

Compared with the older round-tube-plate-fin (RTPF) coil made from copper tube and aluminium fins, an all-aluminium microchannel heat exchanger is lighter, more compact, needs considerably less refrigerant charge, and avoids galvanic corrosion between copper and aluminium. That is why it replaced round tubes in automotive condensers decades ago and is now widely used in residential and commercial air-conditioning condensers, heat pumps and chillers.

The tube is made by hot extrusion through a porthole (bridge) die, or by continuous rotary extrusion from aluminium rod. Both routes produce a seamless profile with no weld, so the channels are uniform along the length. After extrusion the tube is usually zinc-sprayed in line and either cut to length or coiled.

Why heat exchanger makers use microchannel tube

  • High heat transfer per unit volume and weight
  • Lower refrigerant charge than round-tube coils of similar capacity
  • All-aluminium construction: no copper–aluminium galvanic couple and simple recycling
  • Thin walls rated for high pressure, including CO2 (R744) systems with suitable designs
  • Seamless extrusion: consistent ports and no weld seam to leak
Alloys

Alloys for microchannel tube

Microchannel tube alloys must extrude through very fine dies at high speed, braze well in a controlled-atmosphere brazing (CAB) furnace and resist corrosion after brazing. That rules out most high-strength alloys and favours low-alloy 1xxx and 3xxx grades.

AlloyMain alloyingStrengthsTypical use
1100 / 1050 / 1070Essentially pure aluminiumEasiest to extrude, highest conductivity, very thin walls possibleResidential AC coils, evaporators, small condensers
3102Mn 0.05 – 0.40The classic MPE alloy: extrudes almost as easily as 1xxx with better strength and corrosion behaviourAutomotive and HVAC condensers
3003Mn 1.0 – 1.5, Cu 0.05 – 0.20Higher strength and pressure capacity; harder to extrudeHigher-pressure and larger tubes
Long-life 3xxx variantsControlled Mn, Si, Fe, Cu, Zn, TiImproved post-braze corrosion resistance and strengthAutomotive and coastal HVAC, CO2 systems
6063Mg 0.45 – 0.9, Si 0.2 – 0.6Good extrudability and strength; Mg interferes with standard CAB fluxMechanically joined or adhesive-bonded cooling plates, not standard CAB-brazed coils

Composition limits follow the Aluminum Association registrations. Magnesium above roughly 0.3 – 0.4 % reacts with potassium fluoroaluminate flux and impairs CAB brazing, which is why Mg-bearing alloys are avoided in brazed microchannel coils.

Ask your heat exchanger customer which alloy is qualified on their drawing; switching alloy, even between 1xxx and 3102, can change extrusion behaviour, brazing and corrosion life, so it normally needs requalification.

Surface coatings

Zinc, flux and Si-flux coatings

CoatingWhat it doesTypical loading
Zinc arc sprayZinc diffuses into the tube surface during brazing, creating a sacrificial layer that corrodes laterally instead of pitting through the wallCommonly about 6 – 15 g/m² (for example 8, 10 or 13 g/m² nominal)
Pre-flux coatingPotassium fluoroaluminate flux in a binder, applied before delivery so the core can be brazed without separate fluxingCommonly about 8 – 25 g/m²
Si-flux coatingSilicon powder plus flux in a binder: provides the filler metal on the tube itself so bare (unclad) fins can be usedSet by the brazing design
UncoatedFor cores where flux and filler come from clad fins and headers and corrosion protection is provided elsewhere—

Zinc load needs a balance. Too little and the tube is not protected; too much and the zinc-rich layer and fillets can corrode quickly, sometimes leading to fin detachment. The right value depends on the alloy, braze cycle and service environment, and is normally fixed by the heat exchanger maker's qualification.

Joining

Brazing and joining microchannel tube

  1. Core assemblyTubes are cut to length and stacked with louvred fins between them; tube ends are inserted into slotted header pipes.
  2. FluxingNon-corrosive potassium fluoroaluminate flux is applied, unless the tube or other parts are pre-fluxed.
  3. CAB brazingThe core passes through a nitrogen-atmosphere furnace, typically peaking around 600 °C. The Al-Si cladding on fins and headers (or the Si coating on the tube) melts and forms the joints.
  4. Cooling and testingAfter brazing, the core is leak-tested (often with helium) and pressure-tested to the customer specification.

Brazing anneals the tube, so the design must rely on post-braze strength. For mechanically assembled designs, such as some battery cooling plates, tubes can instead be joined by adhesive bonding, mechanical expansion into fittings or laser welding, which opens the door to 6xxx alloys.

Related brazing materials: condenser header pipe, fin stock, brazing sheet and brazing flux.

Quality

Corrosion protection and testing

Thin-walled tubes in coastal or road-salt environments fail by pitting through the wall. Microchannel coils resist this by combining a corrosion-resistant core alloy, a zinc diffusion layer that sacrifices itself laterally, and fins that are slightly more anodic than the tube so they protect it.

TestPurpose
SWAAT (ASTM G85 Annex A3)Acidified synthetic seawater fog cycle; the industry's standard accelerated test for brazed aluminium heat exchangers
Neutral salt spray (ASTM B117 / ISO 9227)General corrosion screening of tubes and coatings
Burst and proof pressureConfirms the tube and joints hold the required pressure with margin
Helium or air leak testDetects leaks in finished cores
Dimensional and visual inspectionHeight, width, wall, port integrity and surface defects
Coating weight checkZinc and flux loading per m² measured by stripping or XRF

Pressure requirements depend heavily on the refrigerant. Coils for R134a, R1234yf, R410A and R32 operate at very different pressures, and CO2 (R744) gas coolers run far higher still, so wall thickness, port size and alloy must be chosen for the specific system. State the required burst pressure on the drawing.

Ordering

How to specify and order microchannel tube

  1. ProfileDrawing with width, height, wall and web thickness, port number and shape, and tolerances; or an existing section from the tooling library.
  2. AlloyThe alloy qualified for your heat exchanger, for example 1100, 3102 or a long-life 3xxx grade.
  3. CoatingZinc arc spray load, pre-flux or Si-flux coating, or uncoated.
  4. Form and lengthCut lengths with length tolerance, or coils with coil weight and inner diameter.
  5. Testing and documentsBurst pressure, coating weight, dimensional sampling plan, mill certificate (EN 10204 3.1); delivery on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) microchannel tube: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between microchannel tube and MPE tube?

They are the same product. Microchannel tube, multi-port extrusion (MPE), multi-port tube and parallel-flow flat tube all describe a flat aluminium extrusion with several internal channels.

Which alloy is best for microchannel tube?

3102 is the traditional choice for brazed condensers because it extrudes easily and resists corrosion well. 1100 suits very thin residential coils, 3003 and long-life 3xxx alloys give more strength and corrosion life, and 6063 is used only where the tube is not CAB-brazed.

Why is microchannel tube zinc-sprayed?

During brazing the zinc diffuses into the surface, forming a sacrificial layer. Corrosion then spreads sideways through this layer instead of pitting straight through the thin wall, extending the life of the coil.

Can microchannel tube be used for CO2 (R744) systems?

Yes, with designs that have suitable wall and web thickness, port size and alloy. CO2 systems run at much higher pressures than conventional refrigerants, so burst requirements must be specified.

What sizes are available?

Small HVAC and automotive tubes range from about 4 × 0.5 mm to 16 × 1 mm and beyond, while larger multi-port sections reach about 120 mm wide. Port count ranges from 3 to more than 40.

Is microchannel tube supplied in coils?

Yes. Tubes can be supplied in coils for in-line straightening and cutting at the core plant, or as cut lengths.

How is extruded microchannel tube different from folded tube?

Extruded tube is seamless and suits very high pressures; folded tube is roll-formed from clad strip and can have thinner walls with corrosion protection built into the strip.
