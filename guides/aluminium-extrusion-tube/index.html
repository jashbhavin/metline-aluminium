Extruded tube guide

Extruded Aluminium Tube: Seamless and Porthole Tube Explained

Extruded aluminium tube is hollow stock formed by pushing a heated billet through a die around a mandrel, producing round, square, rectangular and shaped tubes in long lengths. The most important choice is the method: porthole (structural) tube is economical and fine for frames and general engineering, while seamless tube is required for pressure, hydraulic and high-strength applications. This guide explains both methods, the alloys, standards, sizes and weights, and how to order the right tube.

Overview

What is extruded aluminium tube?

An extruded tube is a hollow section made in a single extrusion pass. The die forms the outside of the tube and a mandrel forms the bore, so the wall thickness is the gap between them. The tube leaves the press as a continuous length, is quenched, stretched straight, cut and aged, exactly like a solid profile.

Extrusion is the most economical way to make aluminium tube in medium and large sizes and in non-round shapes. It produces round tube, square and rectangular hollow sections, oval and streamline tube, and hollows with internal webs, fins or slots, all from relatively inexpensive dies. Smaller, thin-walled and precision tubes are usually extruded first and then cold drawn to size; see the comparison later in this guide.

Tube and pipe are often used interchangeably, but in the aluminium trade "pipe" refers to the standard nominal pipe sizes (NPS) and schedules used in piping systems, while "tube" is specified by actual outside diameter and wall thickness. Both can be extruded, and both can be seamless or porthole, which is the distinction that matters most for performance.

Typical uses include structural frames and handrails, truck and trailer bodies, machine guarding, bicycle and sports equipment, hydraulic and pneumatic cylinders, heat-exchanger and condenser tubes, aerospace and defence components, and pipework for chemical, cryogenic and gas service.

Key decisions when buying extruded tube

  • Method: porthole (structural) or seamless, which depends on pressure and alloy
  • Alloy and temper: 6063 for appearance, 6061/6082 for strength, 7075/2024 for very high strength
  • Route: as-extruded or extruded and cold drawn for tighter tolerances and thinner walls
  • Standard: ASTM B221, B241 or B429, or EN 755-7 or -8, which sets tolerance and testing
Production methods

Seamless vs porthole extruded tube

There are two ways to put the hole in an extruded tube, and they produce materially different products.

Porthole (bridge die) tube

A solid billet is pushed into a porthole die, where bridges hold the mandrel. The metal splits into streams around the bridges and re-joins in a welding chamber before passing the mandrel and die. The result is a tube with longitudinal weld seams (typically two to four) running its full length. In 6xxx alloys these seams are solid-state welds made under high pressure and temperature and are sound for structural use.

  • Lower cost; faster to extrude
  • Any hollow shape, including multi-void sections
  • Thin walls and complex shapes
  • Not intended for pressure service

Seamless tube

A hollow billet (or a solid billet pierced in the press) is extruded over a mandrel that moves with or is fixed to the ram, so the metal never splits and re-welds. The tube has no longitudinal seams and uniform properties around the circumference. Hard alloys such as 2024 and 7075, which do not weld reliably in a porthole die, are made this way.

  • Required for pressure, hydraulic and gas service
  • Uniform properties for bending, flaring and expansion
  • Suits hard 2xxx and 7xxx alloys
  • Mainly round shapes; higher cost

ASTM B429 makes the point explicitly: structural pipe and tube made through a bridge or porthole die is not for fluid-carrying applications involving pressure; for those, the specification directs users to seamless products under ASTM B241. If your application involves internal pressure, bending to tight radii, flaring, swaging or expansion, specify seamless tube. Our seamless aluminium tube guide goes deeper into seamless production and testing.

Shapes

Extruded tube shapes and special tubes

Round tube

The most common shape, from small diameters to large-diameter tube over 300 mm. Specified by OD and wall, or by NPS and schedule for pipe.

Square tube

Square hollow sections with sharp or radiused corners for frames, furniture, construction and machine structures.

Rectangular tube

Rectangular hollow sections for frames, rails and beams where bending stiffness in one direction matters.

Oval and flat-oval tube

Oval, elliptical and flat-sided tube for handrails, furniture, ladders, heat exchangers and aerodynamic parts.

Streamline (airfoil) tube

Teardrop sections used for struts and fairings where low drag is wanted.

Hexagonal, octagonal and triangular tube

Less common hollow shapes for fittings, lighting, display systems and special structures.

Multi-void and microchannel tube

Flat tubes with many small channels, used in automotive and HVAC heat exchangers. See microchannel tooling.

Finned and ribbed tube

Tubes with integral external or internal fins or ribs for heat transfer or extra stiffness.

Slotted and cylinder tube

Tubes with T-slots for framing systems, and precision-bore tube for pneumatic cylinders.

Non-round tubes are almost always made in porthole dies, since a mandrel shaped like the bore is easier to hold with bridges. Custom hollow sections with internal webs, screw ports or attachment features are covered in our custom extrusion guide.

Large-diameter tube deserves a separate note. Round tube above roughly 300 mm OD needs a large press, and the very largest seamless tubes are made on specialised vertical presses or by alternative routes such as forging and boring, or rolling and welding plate. Lead time, minimum quantities and test requirements all grow with diameter, so it pays to confirm early whether a standard size from stock, a heavier-wall tube machined to size, or a fabricated alternative would serve. For heavy-wall cylinders and sleeves, bored bar or a seamless rolled ring can also be an economical substitute, depending on length.

EN 755-7 and EN 755-8 cover the same size ranges (round tube about 8 – 450 mm OD, and shaped tube within a circumscribing circle of about 10 – 350 mm), but each applies only to its own production method. If a European drawing calls up EN 755-7, porthole tube is not acceptable even if it meets the dimensions.

Extruded vs drawn

Extruded tube vs drawn tube

Much of the small-diameter tube on the market is extruded and then cold drawn over a mandrel or plug through a sizing die. The drawing step changes the product significantly.

A useful rule: if you need tight tolerances or thin walls on a tube under about 50 – 75 mm OD, ask for drawn tube; for larger diameters, heavy walls, non-round shapes or structural use, extruded tube is usually the right and cheaper answer. See our guide to drawn tube and wire.

Quality

Tolerances, testing and inspection

Extruded tube is supplied to the tolerance tables of its order standard: ANSI H35.2 for ASTM orders, EN 755-7 or EN 755-8 for EN orders. Tolerances are set on OD (or width across flats), wall thickness, ovality, straightness, twist (for shaped tube), corner radii and cut length.

  • Wall thickness and eccentricity: the most important tolerance for pressure tube; seamless tube is checked for wall variation around the circumference
  • Straightness: controlled by stretching; specify a tighter limit if the tube is used for shafts, rollers or cylinders
  • Hydrostatic or pneumatic testing: required by some pressure specifications; state the test pressure on the order
  • Eddy-current and ultrasonic testing: available to detect discontinuities in seamless tube for critical service
  • Flattening and flare tests: used to show ductility and the absence of weak seams
  • Certification: EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate with chemistry and mechanical properties; third-party inspection on request

The most frequent ordering error with tube is mixing up dimensions: specifying OD and ID and wall together over-defines the tube, and specifying ID tolerance on porthole tube when the standard controls OD and wall. Specify two of the three (normally OD and wall) and name the standard.

Working with tube

Bending, welding, joining and finishing

Bending

Seamless tube bends more predictably because it has no seams. 6063 bends best; 6061-T6 needs generous radii or bending in T4 followed by ageing. Mandrel bending prevents ovality and wrinkling on thin walls.

Welding

6xxx tube welds by TIG or MIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler; 5083 tube welds with ER5183 or ER5356. 2024 and 7075 tube are generally not fusion welded.

Mechanical joining

Flaring, swaging, compression fittings, threaded ends on heavy-wall pipe, clamps and adhesives are all used; seamless tube is preferred where ends are expanded or flared.

Machining

Tube can be cut, drilled, slotted, threaded and end-machined. For cylinder bores, honed or drawn tube gives the required finish.

Anodising and coating

6063 gives the most uniform decorative anodising; weld seams in porthole tube can show faintly after bright anodising. Powder coating hides seams completely.

Corrosion

Aluminium tube resists atmospheric and fresh-water corrosion well; 5xxx and 6061 perform well in marine air. Avoid direct contact with copper or carbon steel in wet conditions to prevent galvanic corrosion.

Selection

How to choose and order extruded tube

  1. Pressure or not?If the tube carries fluid or gas under pressure, or will be flared, expanded or tightly bent, specify seamless tube to ASTM B241 or EN 755-7. Otherwise porthole tube to B221, B429 or EN 755-8 is usually fine.
  2. Pick the alloy and temper6063-T5/T6 for appearance and light loads, 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 for structural and pressure tube, 3003 for heat exchangers, 5083 for marine and cryogenic, 7075/2024 for aerospace strength.
  3. Define the sizeOD and wall (or NPS and schedule for pipe), shape and corner radius for square and rectangular tube, and length.
  4. Choose route and toleranceExtruded for general and large tube; drawn for small, thin-walled and precision tube. Name the tolerance standard.
  5. Add tests and documentsHydrostatic, eddy-current or ultrasonic tests if required, EN 10204 3.1 certificate, export packing and delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP).

Common mistakes

  • Using porthole structural tube in a pressure line
  • Ordering 7075 or 2024 tube without stating seamless; these alloys should always be seamless
  • Over-specifying OD, ID and wall together, or tight ID tolerances on extruded tube
  • Expecting 6061-T6 to keep full strength after welding

FAQ

Extruded aluminium (aluminum) tube: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between seamless and structural aluminium tube?

Structural (porthole) tube is extruded through a bridge die, so the metal splits and re-welds, leaving longitudinal seams. Seamless tube is extruded from a hollow or pierced billet over a mandrel and has no seams. Seamless tube is required for pressure service and for hard alloys such as 2024 and 7075.

Can porthole extruded tube be used for pressure applications?

No. ASTM B429 states that structural tube made through a porthole or bridge die is not for fluid-carrying applications involving pressure. Use seamless pipe or tube to ASTM B241 or EN 755-7 instead.

How do I calculate the weight of an aluminium tube?

Weight per metre in kg = π × (OD − wall) × wall × 0.0027, with dimensions in mm. A 50 × 3 mm tube in 6061 weighs about 1.20 kg/m; NPS 2 schedule 40 pipe weighs about 1.87 kg/m.

Which alloy is best for extruded aluminium tube?

6061-T6 is the general-purpose choice for strength and pressure tube, 6063 for architectural and decorative tube, 6082 in Europe for structural hollows, 3003 for heat exchangers, 5083 for marine and cryogenic service and 7075 for maximum strength.

Is extruded tube as accurate as drawn tube?

No. Drawn tube has tighter OD, ID and wall tolerances and a smoother surface. Extruded tube is less expensive and is available in larger diameters, heavier walls and non-round shapes.

Can you supply square and rectangular extruded tube?

Yes. Square, rectangular, oval and custom hollow sections are produced in porthole dies in 6063, 6061, 6082 and 6005A, with sharp or radiused corners, mill or anodised finish.
