Aluminium Foil Tape for Duct Sealing and Insulation

Aluminium foil tape is a soft aluminium foil coated with a pressure-sensitive adhesive, used to seal HVAC duct joints, close insulation seams and protect surfaces from heat and moisture. The right tape depends on three choices: foil thickness, adhesive chemistry and, for ductwork, whether the job requires a UL 181 listed product. This guide covers each, with sizes, application practice and the mistakes that cause tape to peel.

Overview

What aluminium foil tape is and why it outperforms cloth duct tape

Foil tape has two layers: an annealed aluminium foil backing, usually an alloy such as 1235, 8011 or 1145 in the O temper, and an adhesive mass protected by a release liner until use. The foil gives a backing that does not stretch, rot, absorb water or soften with heat, and it reflects radiant heat. The adhesive does the sealing.

The name "duct tape" causes confusion. The grey cloth-backed tape sold under that name has a rubber adhesive that dries out and loses grip on warm metal ducts within months. Laboratory testing at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory in the late 1990s found cloth duct tape to be the least durable of the duct sealants it aged, while foil tapes and mastic held up far better. That is why most mechanical codes and energy codes now call for listed foil tape or mastic on duct joints, not cloth tape.

Beyond ductwork, foil tape is used to seal the facing of insulation boards and blankets, protect cables and hoses from radiant heat, mask parts during plasma spraying and powder coating, repair vapour barriers, and provide electromagnetic shielding where a conductive adhesive grade is used.

What foil tape does well

  • Seals air leaks at duct joints, which can account for a large share of heating and cooling losses in ducted systems
  • Keeps insulation vapour barriers continuous across seams and penetrations
  • Withstands temperatures that would melt or degrade plastic tapes
  • Resists water, UV and mould, so it ages slowly in roof spaces and plant rooms
  • Reflects radiant heat and is non-combustible as a backing material
Product types

Main types of aluminium foil tape

Plain foil tape (acrylic)

Soft foil with a solvent or water-based acrylic adhesive. The general-purpose choice for sheet-metal duct joints, insulation seams and repairs. Acrylic resists ageing and UV and keeps its bond across a wide temperature range.

Butyl foil tape

A thick, soft butyl rubber mass on foil. Very high tack and gap-filling ability, fully waterproof; used on roof flashings, outdoor duct, gutters, condensate lines and building envelope seams.

High-temperature foil tape (silicone)

Silicone adhesive that keeps its strength well above the range of acrylics. Used around exhausts, ovens, industrial dryers, masking for thermal processes and aerospace or automotive heat shields.

Reinforced foil tape (FSK / glass scrim)

Foil laminated to glass scrim and kraft paper (FSK) or to glass cloth for tear resistance. Matches FSK-faced insulation and resists puncture during installation.

Lined vs linerless

Most foil tapes use a paper or film release liner. Linerless tapes have a release coating on the foil itself; they are faster to apply but harder to handle in cold weather.

Conductive foil tape

Adhesive filled with conductive particles so the tape bonds electrically to the substrate. Used for EMI shielding of enclosures, grounding and cable wraps.

A useful rule is that acrylic is the default, butyl is chosen when the surface is wet, outdoors or irregular, and silicone is chosen only when temperature demands it, since silicone tapes cost more and can contaminate surfaces that will later be painted.

Foil gauge

Foil thickness: what each gauge is for

Foil thicknessImperial equivalentTypical use
25 – 30 µmAbout 1 – 1.2 milInsulation seams, light repairs, vapour-barrier patches; conforms easily to curves
35 – 50 µmAbout 1.4 – 2 milStandard HVAC duct tape gauge; the most common UL 181 listed construction is about 2 mil foil
60 – 80 µmAbout 2.4 – 3.2 milHeavy-duty duct work, dryer vents, abrasion-prone areas
100 µm and aboveAbout 4 mil and aboveHigh-temperature and industrial service, heat shields, masking that must survive grit blasting

Total tape thickness is the foil plus the adhesive, typically adding 20 – 60 µm for acrylic and much more for butyl. Thicker foil is stronger and resists puncture but is harder to press into corners and over rivet heads; on round flexible duct, a thinner foil often seals better because it conforms without creasing.

The foil itself is a soft, fully annealed product. Hard-temper foil would spring back, open creases and lift at the edges. Alloys 1235 and 8011 dominate because they combine softness with good resistance to pinholes and are readily available in the required gauges.

Standards

UL 181 ratings, fire performance and codes

For HVAC ductwork in North America and in many projects that follow US practice, the key standards are the UL 181 family. A tape listed and marked to these standards has passed tests for adhesion, shear, temperature, mould growth and surface burning characteristics.

MarkingStandardApplication
181A-PUL 181A, pressure-sensitive tapeClosure of rigid fibrous glass duct board
181B-FXUL 181B, pressure-sensitive tapeClosure of flexible air ducts and connectors
181A-M / 181B-MUL 181A / 181B, masticMastic closures; not tape, but the usual alternative
UL 723 / ASTM E84 ratingSurface burning characteristicsFlame-spread and smoke-developed indices; mechanical codes commonly limit duct materials to 25 / 50

Many building inspectors look for the printed listing mark on the tape backing, so tapes for duct work are usually printed with the marking at intervals along the roll. For sheet-metal duct, energy codes typically accept listed tape or mastic, and installers often apply mastic to longitudinal seams and listed foil tape to transverse joints.

In Europe and other regions, duct airtightness is specified at system level (for example EN 12237 for circular sheet-metal ducts and EN 1507 for rectangular ducts), and tapes are chosen by the duct supplier to meet the required airtightness class. Fire reaction of insulation systems including tapes is classified under EN 13501-1.

Tape converters buy the aluminium foil as wide coils, coat it with adhesive and slit it into finished rolls. For converters, the important foil properties are uniform gauge, a clean annealed surface that the adhesive primer wets reliably, and low pinhole count, because a pinhole becomes a leak path once the tape is on a pressurised duct.

Installation

How to apply foil tape so it stays stuck

  1. CleanRemove dust, oil and moisture with a solvent wipe such as isopropyl alcohol. New galvanised duct often carries mill oil that will stop any adhesive from bonding.
  2. Check temperatureApply within the tape’s application range, often above about 0 – 10 °C for acrylics. In cold conditions, warm the surface and the roll, or use a cold-weather grade.
  3. PositionCentre the tape over the joint with at least 25 mm of overlap on each side; on round duct, wrap fully around with an overlap at the end.
  4. Press hardRub the tape down firmly with a squeegee or plastic tool until the pattern of the surface shows through the foil. Pressure-sensitive adhesives need pressure, not just contact.
  5. Remove wrinklesWork from the centre outwards; creases form leak channels. Cut and patch rather than folding tape over a corner.
  6. Allow dwellAcrylic bond strength builds over the first day or more; avoid pressurising or flexing the joint immediately where possible.

Most tape failures come from skipping the first and fourth steps. Tape that peels from a duct a year after installation was usually applied to a dirty or oily surface or was laid on lightly without burnishing.

Applications

Where aluminium foil tape is used

  • HVAC ductwork: transverse joints, take-offs, access panels and flexible-to-rigid connections
  • Duct board and insulation: seams on fibreglass duct board, foil-faced and FSK-faced insulation, pipe insulation jackets
  • Dryer vents and exhausts: joints in metal vent duct, where codes often prohibit screws that protrude into the airstream
  • Refrigeration and cold rooms: sealing vapour barriers to stop condensation and ice in insulated panels
  • Building envelope: butyl foil tape on flashings, roof penetrations, gutters and window perimeters
  • Industrial: heat shielding of wiring and hoses, masking for powder coating and thermal spray, tank insulation
  • Electronics: conductive foil tape for EMI shielding and grounding
  • Automotive and appliance: heat reflection and sealing in engine bays, ovens and refrigerators
Common mistakes

Mistakes to avoid when buying and using foil tape

Using cloth duct tape on ducts

It degrades quickly on warm metal and is not accepted by most codes for duct closure.

Ignoring the listing

For duct board and flexible duct, an unlisted tape may fail inspection even if it performs well.

Choosing silicone by default

Silicone is for heat; it costs more, has lower tack, and can leave residue that stops paint adhering.

Taping over dirty galvanised steel

Mill oil and dust are the main causes of peeling; clean first.

Applying in the cold

Below the application range the adhesive cannot flow into the surface and never develops full bond.

Relying on tape for structural support

Tape seals joints; mechanical fasteners or clamps must hold ducts together.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) foil tape: frequently asked questions

Can aluminium foil tape be used on HVAC ductwork?

Yes, and it is the preferred tape for duct joints. For fibreglass duct board use a tape marked UL 181A-P, and for flexible duct a tape marked UL 181B-FX. On sheet-metal duct, listed foil tape or mastic is normally accepted by energy codes.

How much heat can foil tape take?

The foil itself is unaffected well beyond any HVAC temperature; the limit is the adhesive. Acrylic tapes typically handle around 100 – 150 °C depending on grade, while silicone-adhesive tapes are used up to about 250 °C. Check the data sheet for the exact product.

Is aluminium foil tape waterproof?

The foil is impermeable to water and vapour. Waterproofing depends on the adhesive and application: butyl foil tape is the usual choice outdoors and on wet or uneven surfaces, while acrylic tape is water resistant once fully bonded.

What is the difference between foil tape and duct tape?

Duct tape has a cloth and polyethylene backing with a rubber adhesive; it is versatile but dries out and loses grip on warm ducts. Foil tape has a metal backing that does not degrade and acrylic, butyl or silicone adhesives that hold for years.

Can foil tape be used on dryer vents?

Yes. Metal foil tape is commonly used to seal joints in metal dryer exhaust duct, partly because many codes prohibit screws that project into the duct and catch lint. Never use cloth duct tape there.

Which aluminium foil is used to make foil tape?

Soft, fully annealed foil, usually 1235, 8011 or 1145 in the O temper, in gauges from about 25 to 100 µm. Metline supplies this foil in wide coils or slit widths for tape converters, who coat, laminate and slit it into finished tape.
