Aluminium Washers: Alloys, Types, Sizes and Selection

Aluminium washers are used where a joint must be light, non-magnetic, electrically conductive, corrosion-resistant or able to seal by deforming. Soft 1050 and 1100 make sealing (crush) washers; 3003 and 5052 make general flat washers; 6061-T6 and 2024 are used when the washer must carry higher loads. This guide covers the types, alloys, standard sizes and the mistakes to avoid when specifying them.

Overview

Why choose an aluminium washer?

A washer spreads the clamping load of a bolt or screw, protects the surface underneath, seals a joint or locks it against loosening. Aluminium washers do those jobs at about one-third of the weight of steel, without rusting, and with good electrical and thermal conductivity.

They are the natural choice in aluminium assemblies, because matching metals avoid galvanic corrosion at the joint, and in sealing applications, because annealed aluminium flows under bolt load to fill surface irregularities. Their limitation is strength: an aluminium washer under a high-strength steel bolt can be crushed or embedded, so the alloy and thickness must match the clamp load.

Metline supplies the aluminium strip, sheet, coil, discs and bar from which washers are stamped or turned, and can cut or machine washers and blanks to your drawing. Mill test certificates accompany every order.

Washer types

Types of aluminium washer and what each does

Flat (plain) washer

Spreads load and protects the surface. Dimensions follow ISO 7089/7090, DIN 125 or ASME B18.22.1. Usually 3003, 5052 or 6061.

Sealing (crush) washer

Soft annealed 1050 or 1100 ring that deforms under torque to seal oil, fuel, coolant and hydraulic ports and drain plugs. Common form: DIN 7603 form A. Replace after each use.

Spring and wave washer

Provide preload against vibration and take up axial play. Aluminium has a low spring rate and relaxes, so these are used only for light duty; steel or stainless is normal for load-critical spring washers.

Tooth (serrated) lock washer

Teeth bite into the mating surface to resist rotation and can cut through oxide for electrical contact on aluminium busbars and earth points.

Countersunk and shoulder washer

Seat countersunk screws flush or guide and locate a screw; used in enclosures, instruments and electronics.

Custom and shaped washers

Square, D-hole, tab, multi-hole and stepped designs stamped or laser-cut from strip or sheet to drawing, often anodised.

Typical values from Aluminum Association / ASM data. Hardness for sealing washers is often specified as a maximum, because softer washers seal better.

Two rules simplify selection. For sealing, pick the softest material that will not extrude out of the joint: annealed 1050 or 1100. For load spreading, the washer should be at least as hard as the surface under it and thick enough not to dish; for steel property-class 8.8 bolts and above, an aluminium washer is suitable only if the bearing stress stays within the aluminium’s compressive yield strength.

How washers are made

How aluminium washers are made and finished

  1. MaterialStrip or coil slit to width for stamping; sheet for laser or waterjet cutting; bar or tube for turned thick washers and spacers.
  2. FormingHigh-volume washers are blanked and pierced in one press stroke. Low volumes and complex shapes are laser-cut or CNC-machined.
  3. Deburring and flatteningTumbling or vibratory finishing removes burrs; flattening keeps sealing faces true.
  4. Heat treatmentSealing washers are fully annealed after stamping so they are soft and seal reliably.
  5. FinishingNatural, clear or coloured anodising for corrosion and identification; chemical conversion coating where conductivity must be kept.
Selection advice

Common mistakes when specifying aluminium washers

  • Pairing with dissimilar metals in wet service: aluminium next to stainless steel or copper in salt spray corrodes; use an insulating washer or matching metals, or a copper-aluminium bimetallic washer for electrical joints.
  • Reusing crush washers: an annealed sealing washer work-hardens when tightened and will not seal reliably a second time.
  • Using soft washers under high clamp loads: 1xxx washers embed and relax, which loses bolt preload over time.
  • Ignoring temperature: aluminium loses strength progressively above about 150 °C; check service temperature for engine and exhaust joints.
  • Anodising sealing faces or contact washers: the anodic layer is hard and electrically insulating; mask the faces if the washer must seal or conduct.
Applications

Where aluminium washers are used

  • Automotive and motorsport: oil-drain and banjo-bolt sealing washers, light-weight fixings
  • Aerospace: 2024 washers to AN, NAS and MS standards in airframe fasteners
  • Electrical: busbar and earthing connections, terminal washers and heat-sink mounting
  • Solar and construction: washers on aluminium mounting rails and facade fixings to avoid galvanic corrosion
  • Electronics and instruments: spacers, countersunk and shoulder washers in enclosures
  • Marine: 5052 washers on aluminium deck hardware and boat structures

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) washers: FAQ

Are aluminium washers better than steel washers?

They are better where weight, corrosion resistance with aluminium parts, conductivity or sealing matter. Steel washers are better under high clamp loads and at high temperatures, because aluminium is softer and loses strength above about 150 °C.

Can aluminium sealing washers be reused?

It is not recommended. Annealed aluminium crush washers deform and work-harden when tightened, so a used washer will not seal as reliably. Fit a new one each time the joint is opened.

Which alloy is used for aluminium crush washers?

Soft, annealed commercially pure aluminium, usually 1050 (Al 99.5) or 1100 in O temper, because it flows under load to fill surface imperfections.

Do aluminium washers corrode?

They do not rust. The natural oxide film protects them in most environments, and anodising adds protection. Galvanic corrosion can occur when aluminium touches stainless steel or copper in wet or salty conditions, so isolate dissimilar metals.

Can I use aluminium washers with stainless steel bolts?

Indoors and in dry service, yes. Outdoors or in marine conditions, the aluminium washer can corrode sacrificially; use an insulating washer or sealant, or match fastener and washer materials.
