Filler metal guide

Aluminium Welding Wire: Grades, Filler Selection and MIG/TIG Practice

Aluminium welding wire is the filler metal used in MIG (GMAW) welding, where it is fed continuously as the electrode, and in TIG (GTAW) welding, where it is added as straight rod or by an automatic feeder. Two families cover almost every job: Al-Si fillers (4043, 4943, 4047, 4643) for crack resistance and fluidity, and Al-Mg fillers (5356, 5183, 5556, 5554) for strength, ductility and seawater resistance. This guide explains how to choose between them for each base alloy and how to get sound welds.

Overview

What makes aluminium welding wire different

Aluminium is harder to weld than steel for four reasons: it conducts heat away about five times faster, its tough oxide skin melts at over 2,000 °C while the metal melts near 600 °C, molten aluminium dissolves hydrogen that becomes porosity on freezing, and many high-strength alloys crack when they solidify. Filler wire is the main tool for managing the last two problems.

The filler is never just "aluminium". Its silicon or magnesium content is chosen so that, once mixed with melted base metal in the weld pool, the final weld chemistry avoids the composition range most prone to solidification (hot) cracking, gives the strength the joint needs and behaves well in service. That is why a 6061 structure is welded with 4043 or 5356 and never with 6061 wire: an autogenous or matching-composition 6061 weld sits right in the crack-sensitive range.

Wire quality matters as much as alloy. Aluminium wire is soft, so diameter consistency, a clean and smooth surface free of drawing lubricant and oxide, precise layer winding and moisture-proof packaging decide whether it feeds smoothly and welds without porosity. Metline supplies MIG wire and TIG filler rod in the standard AWS and ISO grades, with batch certificates.

Classification

How aluminium filler metals are classified

The two main specifications are AWS A5.10/A5.10M (used in the Americas and on many international projects) and ISO 18273 (used in Europe and much of Asia, published in Europe as EN ISO 18273). AWS uses the prefix ER for wire or rod that can be an electrode or a filler rod, and R for rod used only as filler. ISO uses S Al followed by the alloy number, and sometimes the chemical symbol, for example S Al 5183 (AlMg4,5Mn0,7(A)).

Shear strengths are typical published values for fillet welds and depend on joint, base metal and procedure; design to the governing code.

Weld metal from the 5xxx fillers is stronger and far more ductile, so it suits bending, impact and fatigue loads. Si-bearing fillers flow better, wet the joint edges, produce a flatter bead with fewer cracks and are less sensitive to cleanliness. Aluminium-magnesium fillers with more than about 3 % Mg should not be used where the weld will run continuously above 65 °C (150 °F), because magnesium-rich phases can form at grain boundaries and make the weld sensitive to stress-corrosion cracking. For hot service choose 5554 or a 4xxx filler.

MIG / GMAW

Feeding and welding with aluminium MIG wire

Drive system

Use U-groove drive rolls and the lowest tension that feeds without slipping. V-knurled rolls for steel shave aluminium wire and cause bird-nesting.

Liner and tip

Use a PTFE or nylon liner and keep the gun cable as straight and short as practical. Contact tips for aluminium are slightly oversize to allow for thermal expansion.

Spool gun or push-pull

Soft 4xxx wire in small diameters is best fed with a spool gun or push-pull system. Stiffer 5xxx wire feeds through standard push feeders over longer distances.

Transfer mode

Spray transfer or pulsed spray is normal for aluminium. Pulsed MIG controls heat input on thin sheet and in positional welding.

Shielding gas

Pure argon for most work. Argon-helium mixtures (typically 25 – 75 % helium) give hotter arcs and deeper penetration on thick sections. Never use CO2 or oxygen-bearing steel mixtures.

Technique

Push the torch (forehand angle about 10 – 15°) so gas shields ahead of the pool. Fill the crater at the end of each weld to avoid crater cracks.

As a starting point, 1.2 mm wire in spray transfer on 6 mm plate runs in the region of 180 – 230 A, rising with thickness and falling for vertical and overhead positions; 5xxx wires generally need slightly higher wire-feed speed and current than 4xxx wires of the same diameter. Develop and qualify the exact parameters on test pieces for your joint, equipment and position.

TIG / GTAW

TIG welding with aluminium filler rod

TIG welding aluminium uses alternating current. The electrode-positive half of each cycle breaks up the oxide film (cleaning action), and the electrode-negative half puts heat into the work. Modern inverter machines let you adjust the balance and frequency to narrow the arc and control cleaning width. Pure argon is the standard shielding gas; argon-helium raises heat input for thick sections.

  • Choose rod diameter close to the base-metal thickness on thin work, and not more than about half of it on thicker joints, so the rod melts without chilling the pool
  • Use the same filler logic as MIG: 4043 or 5356 for 6xxx, 5183 for 5083, 4047 for castings and leak-tight joints
  • Keep the hot end of the rod inside the gas shield between dips to avoid oxidising it
  • For automated TIG with cold- or hot-wire feeders, layer-wound spools of TIG-quality wire are used instead of cut rod

See aluminium TIG filler rod for rod sizes and packaging. Shielded metal arc (stick) welding of aluminium uses flux-coated electrodes instead of wire; see aluminium stick welding rod.

Store aluminium wire in its original sealed packaging in a dry room at a stable temperature, and bring it into the workshop well before opening so condensation does not form on a cold spool. Partly used spools should be resealed in a bag with desiccant or kept in a heated cabinet. Wire that has picked up a dull grey surface film can produce porosity even if it still feeds.

Applications

Where each filler is typically used

  • Shipbuilding and offshore: ER5183 and ER5356 on 5083 and 5086 hulls and superstructures; 6082 extrusions welded with 5183 or 5356
  • Road tankers and trailers: 5183 and 5356 on 5083, 5454 and 5182 plate, with 5554 on hot-product tankers
  • Rail and bus bodies: 5356 and 5087 on 6005A and 6082 extrusions, often robotic
  • Automotive and EV battery trays: 4043, 4943 and 5356 on 6xxx extrusions and castings
  • Structural and architectural: 4043 and 5356 on 6061 and 6063 frames, railings and handrails; 5356 where parts are anodised
  • Pressure vessels and cryogenic tanks: 5183 on 5083, 2319 on 2219
  • Castings and repair: 4043 and 4047 for cast housings, wheels and leak-tight joints
  • Additive manufacturing: wire-arc additive manufacturing uses high-quality 4043, 5183 and similar wires
Advice

Common mistakes when buying and using aluminium wire

  • Ordering "6061 wire" to weld 6061: matching-composition fillers crack; use 4043, 4943 or 5356.
  • Using 5356 on parts that run hot: engine parts, hot-product tanks and heat exchangers above 65 °C need 5554 or 4xxx filler.
  • Using 4043 on parts to be anodised for appearance: the weld turns grey; choose 5356.
  • Choosing 4043 for 5083 marine structures: Al-Si filler with high-Mg base metal forms brittle magnesium silicide; use 5183 or 5356.
  • Assuming a stronger wire makes a stronger joint in 6061-T6: the heat-affected zone governs; design to welded properties.
  • Buying large spools for a spool gun or slow consumption: opened spools absorb moisture; match spool size to usage.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) welding wire: FAQ

What is the most common aluminium welding wire?

ER4043 and ER5356 together cover most fabrication. 4043 is chosen for crack resistance, a smooth bead and hot service; 5356 for higher strength, ductility, seawater resistance and better colour match after anodising.

Which wire should I use to weld 6061?

ER4043 or ER5356. Use 4043 for crack-sensitive joints, parts in service above 65 °C and smoother beads; use 5356 for higher fillet shear strength and anodised parts. ER4943 offers higher strength than 4043, and ER4643 suits weldments that are heat-treated after welding.

Which wire is used for 5083 marine aluminium?

ER5183 is the usual first choice because it matches the strength of 5083 and resists seawater corrosion. ER5356 is also widely used; ER5556 gives the highest strength. Avoid 4xxx fillers on high-magnesium alloys.

Can I use the same wire for MIG and TIG?

The same alloy grades are used in both processes. MIG uses layer-wound spooled wire; manual TIG uses straight cut rod, although automatic TIG systems can feed spooled wire.

What shielding gas is used for aluminium MIG welding?

Pure argon for most work, and argon-helium mixtures for thicker sections where more heat and penetration are needed. Carbon dioxide and oxygen-containing mixtures used for steel must not be used on aluminium.

Why does my aluminium weld have porosity?

Almost always hydrogen from moisture or contamination: damp or oxidised wire, oil or hydrated oxide on the joint, or poor gas coverage. Store wire sealed and dry, clean the joint just before welding and check the gas system.

Can 7075 or 2024 be welded with aluminium wire?

They are generally considered unsuitable for arc welding because they crack and lose strength. Weldable 7xxx alloys such as 7005 and 7020 are welded with 5356 or 5183, and 2219 with 2319.
