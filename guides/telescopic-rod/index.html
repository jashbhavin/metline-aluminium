Telescopic Aluminium Tube: Sizing, Clearance & Alloy Selection

Telescopic tubing is a set of tubes sized so that each one slides inside the next, letting a pole, mast or support extend and collapse. Getting it right depends on three numbers: outside diameter, wall thickness and the clearance between them. This guide gives nesting charts for round and square aluminium tube, explains how to choose alloy, temper and locking method, and lists what to specify when ordering. Overview

How telescopic tubing works

In a telescoping set, the inside diameter of each tube is slightly larger than the outside diameter of the next smaller tube. The difference is the sliding clearance. Too little, and the tubes bind when dirty, cold or slightly bent; too much, and the assembly wobbles and the lock has to work harder to hold it rigid.

Aluminium is the natural material for most telescoping products because weight matters at full extension: every gram at the tip of a long pole is felt as bending moment at the user's hands or at the base clamp. Aluminium tube is about one third the weight of a steel tube of the same size, does not rust in the sliding joint, takes a hard anodised wear surface and is easily drilled, slotted and swaged for locks.

Telescoping tube is made in two ways. Cold drawn tube is pulled through a die over a plug, giving tight diameter and wall tolerances and a smooth bore, which is ideal for close-fitting round sets. Extruded tube is pushed through a die and is the economical route for larger sizes and for square, rectangular, oval and custom profiles, where matching dies are designed as a nesting family.

The three numbers that decide a telescoping set

  • Outside diameter (OD) of each stage
  • Wall thickness, which sets the inside diameter and the strength
  • Diametral clearance: inner tube OD versus outer tube ID
Round tube chart

Round telescoping tube: the 1/8 inch rule

The best-known round series uses outside diameters in 1/8 in (0.125 in) steps. The inside diameter of a tube is OD minus twice the wall, so the clearance to the next size down is 0.125 in minus twice the wall. With a 0.058 in wall the clearance is 0.009 in (about 0.23 mm) on diameter, a close sliding fit. With a 0.049 in wall it is 0.027 in (about 0.69 mm), a free fit that tolerates dirt and paint. Square tube

Square and rectangular telescoping tube

Square tube telescopes the same way, with one complication: the corners. The outside corner radius of the inner tube must be smaller than the inside corner radius of the outer tube, or the tubes touch at the corners while the flats are still loose. Square sets are therefore usually designed with more clearance than round sets, and the extrusion dies for a family are drawn together so corner radii step down consistently.

Stiffness, not strength, often limits a long telescoping pole. All aluminium alloys have nearly the same modulus (about 69 – 72 GPa), so a stronger alloy will not reduce deflection; a larger diameter or longer overlap will. Choose 7075 when you need thin walls to survive high bending stress, and 6061 when cost, machinability and corrosion resistance matter most.

Engineering

Design rules for telescoping assemblies

Overlap length

The engaged length of two stages carries the bending moment as a couple. A common starting point is an overlap of at least 2 – 3 times the inner tube OD, more for long, heavily loaded masts.

Wall thickness

Thin walls (about 1.0 – 1.65 mm) suit hand-held poles and light supports; 2.5 – 3.2 mm walls suit equipment stands and masts carrying loads at the tip.

Straightness

Even a small bow eats clearance and causes binding at full extension. Specify straightness for long lengths and avoid heavy machining on one side of thin tube.

Surface and wear

Hard anodising (Type III) gives a wear-resistant sliding surface; clear Type II anodising is enough for occasional adjustment. Remember the coating adds to diameter.

Thermal and corrosion

Outer tubes and inner tubes of the same alloy expand equally, so fits do not change with temperature. Avoid steel inner parts in wet service unless isolated.

Tolerance stack-up

Calculate the worst case: largest inner OD against smallest outer ID, including coating thickness. Drawn tube to ASTM B210 holds much tighter limits than extruded tube.

Spring-button systems give fixed positions and are the simplest; twist and lever locks give infinite adjustment but depend on consistent wall and ID. Lock hardware is normally sourced by the assembler; Metline supplies the aluminium tube cut to length with holes, slots and end forming done to drawing.

Worked example

Worked example: a three-stage 3 m pole

Suppose a hand-held inspection pole must reach about 3 m and collapse to about 1.1 m. Three stages of 1.15 m each, with 100 mm overlaps, give an extended length of roughly 3.25 m. Starting from a comfortable grip of 1.250 in (31.75 mm) OD for the bottom stage and using the 1/8 in rule with a 0.058 in wall, the middle stage is 1.125 in OD and the top stage 1.000 in OD. Each joint has 0.009 in diametral clearance, enough for a smooth slide with clean, clear-anodised tube.

Using the chart, the three tubes weigh about 0.38, 0.34 and 0.30 kg per metre, so the tube in the finished pole weighs roughly 1.2 kg before locks, grips and end caps. If testing shows too much tip deflection, the efficient fix is to move every stage up one or two sizes rather than to change from 6061-T6 to 7075-T6, because the stiffness of all aluminium alloys is almost identical. If the slide feels tight after anodising, switch the inner stages to the 0.049 in wall, which opens the clearance to 0.027 in at the cost of a little more play.

Masses calculated from geometry at 2.70 g/cm³. A short prototype set is the quickest way to confirm feel and lock function before ordering production quantities.

Processing

Processing and surface finishing

  • Cutting: precision saw or lathe cutting to length, with deburred ends and chamfers
  • Machining: drilling of button and pin holes, slotting for clamps, threading and tapping
  • End forming: swaging, flaring or reducing tube ends for stops and fittings
  • Anodising: clear, black or colour Type II, or hard Type III for wear surfaces
  • Powder coating and painting: for outer stages only, since coating thickness affects the fit
  • Sandblasting and brushing: for appearance and grip before anodising

Advice

Common mistakes when specifying telescoping tube

  • Specifying sizes from different standards (inch OD with metric walls) without checking that each stage still fits
  • Forgetting that anodising and powder coating add thickness to the sliding surfaces
  • Choosing a stronger alloy to cure excessive deflection, when a bigger diameter or longer overlap is the real fix
  • Using extruded tube where a close round fit needs drawn tube tolerances
  • Square sets with identical corner radii on every stage, which bind at the corners
  • Drilling button holes before anodising without allowing for the coating in the button fit

FAQ

Telescopic aluminium (aluminum) tube: FAQ

What wall thickness makes round aluminium tubes telescope?

With outside diameters in 1/8 in steps, a 0.058 in wall gives a close 0.009 in diametral clearance and a 0.049 in wall gives a freer 0.027 in clearance. Both are standard telescoping combinations.

How much clearance should a telescopic tube have?

For small round drawn tubes, about 0.2 – 0.4 mm on diameter gives a smooth, low-play slide. Larger or extruded tubes, square tubes and coated tubes need more, often 0.5 – 1.5 mm, with wear pads to take up play.

Which aluminium alloy is best for telescopic poles?

6061-T6 is the default for its balance of strength, cost and corrosion resistance. 7075-T6 is used for thin-wall, high-stress poles such as trekking and camera poles; 6063 suits square and decorative profiles.

Why does my square telescoping tube bind?

Usually because the corner radii of the inner and outer tubes conflict, or the tubes are bowed. The inner tube corners must be tighter than the outer tube inside corners, and long lengths need a straightness limit.

Can telescopic tubes be anodised after machining?

Yes, and it is recommended so holes and cut ends are protected. Allow for the coating thickness on both sliding surfaces when you set the clearance.

Does Metline supply complete telescopic poles?

No. We supply the aluminium tube stages cut to length and machined to drawing, with anodising on request. Lock hardware, grips and final assembly are done by the product maker.
