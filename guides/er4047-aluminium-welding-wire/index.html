ER4047 Aluminium Welding Wire (AlSi12)

ER4047 is a 12 % silicon aluminium filler metal with a melting range of only about 5 °C, which gives the most fluid pool, the least shrinkage and the lowest hot-crack risk of any common aluminium filler. It is chosen for leak-tight heat-exchanger and refrigeration joints, thin-wall assemblies, casting repair and as a brazing filler. This guide explains when to use it, when not to, and how to specify it.

Overview

What makes ER4047 different?

ER4047 contains close to the eutectic amount of silicon for the aluminium-silicon system (about 12.6 %). At that composition the alloy melts and freezes almost at a single temperature rather than over a wide mushy range, so the weld pool goes from fully liquid to fully solid very quickly.

That behaviour has three practical effects. First, the pool is extremely fluid and wets joint faces readily, which helps seal fine gaps in tube-to-header and fitting joints. Second, solidification shrinkage is lower than with ER4043, so there is less distortion and fewer shrinkage cracks in thin parts and castings. Third, there is almost no time for a liquid film to sit between grains and tear, which is why 4047 is the filler with the lowest hot-crack sensitivity on crack-prone 6xxx and casting alloys.

The same silicon makes the deposit harder and less ductile than a 4043 weld and turns it very dark when anodised. 4047 is also the base composition of many aluminium brazing fillers (AWS A5.8 BAlSi-4), which is why the alloy appears in both welding and brazing catalogues. The filler is the same; the process and temperature differ.

Use ER4047 when you need

  • Leak-tight welds on refrigeration, HVAC and heat-exchanger parts
  • Minimum distortion and shrinkage on thin-wall tubes and sheet
  • The best possible resistance to solidification cracking
  • A compatible filler for Al-Si castings and for torch or furnace brazing

Selection

ER4047 or ER4043: how to choose

Both are aluminium-silicon fillers suited to 6xxx alloys and castings. The choice usually comes down to whether sealing and crack resistance outweigh ductility and cost.

A practical rule many fabricators follow: use 4043 for general 6xxx structures and repairs, and switch to 4047 on parts that must hold refrigerant or pressure, on very thin walls where distortion matters, or where 4043 still shows cracking. Our ER4043 guide covers that filler in detail.

Joint properties

Strength and behaviour of ER4047 joints

As with any filler on heat-treatable alloys, the heat-affected zone governs the strength of a groove weld in 6xxx parent metal. AWS D1.2 and ASME IX set the same minimum as-welded tensile strength for 6061-T6 groove welds, 165 MPa (24 ksi), regardless of whether 4043 or 4047 is used; the filler mainly changes crack resistance, ductility and fillet capacity.

Fillet welds

The higher silicon gives a harder deposit and higher shear strength in fillets than 4043, though still below 5356.

Ductility

Bend and elongation values are lower than 4043. Avoid 4047 on joints that must bend in service or pass tight guided-bend tests without qualification.

Elevated temperature

Like other Al-Si fillers, 4047 has no magnesium and is suitable for service above 65 °C, where high-Mg 5xxx fillers risk sensitisation.

Heat treatment

The deposit itself does not age-harden. Dilution with 6xxx parent metal gives a limited response; for joints that will be solution treated and aged, 4643 or 4943 is the more usual choice.

Corrosion

Good general corrosion resistance in air and fresh water, comparable with 4043.

Conductivity

Electrical and thermal conductivity are close to those of 4043, adequate for heat-exchanger joints.

Technique

Welding and brazing tips for ER4047

  1. Prepare the jointDegrease, then brush with a dedicated stainless brush. On tube joints, keep fit-up tight: 4047 flows into small gaps but does not bridge large ones well.
  2. Set the gasPure argon for most MIG and TIG work; argon-helium mixes on thick sections or high-conductivity parts to improve penetration.
  3. Control heatBecause 4047 melts about 50 °C below 4043, use slightly less heat input and faster travel to avoid overheating thin walls.
  4. Mind the craterUse crater fill or a brief reverse at the stop. Even with 4047, crater cracks are the most common defect.
  5. For brazingHeat the parent metal, not the rod, to roughly 580 – 600 °C with suitable flux, and let capillary action draw the filler in. Parent alloys with low solidus temperatures, such as 6061, need careful temperature control.
  6. InspectLeak test (pressure decay or helium) on refrigeration parts; visual and dye-penetrant checks on structural welds.
Applications

Typical ER4047 applications

  • HVAC and refrigeration: condenser and evaporator headers, return bends, accumulators and receiver-dryers in 3003 and 1xxx alloys
  • Automotive heat exchangers: repair and assembly of radiators, charge-air coolers and oil coolers; 4047 is also the skin of most brazing sheet
  • Casting repair: porosity and crack repair in A356 and 413 die and sand castings, wheels and housings
  • Thin-wall tube and enclosure work: parts where distortion would spoil the fit
  • Pressure-tight containers: small tanks and manifolds in 3xxx and 6xxx alloys that must pass leak testing
  • Root passes: some procedures use 4047 for a crack-free root and a 5356 or 4943 cap where strength or colour matters
Ordering

Specifying and ordering ER4047

State the classification (AWS A5.10 ER4047, ISO 18273 S Al 4047A, or a brazing designation such as BAlSi-4 or AMS 4185), the form and diameter, packaging, quantity and required documents. Metline supplies a certificate of conformance or heat-lot analysis with each consignment, packs spools in sealed moisture-barrier bags and ships worldwide on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.

Common ordering mistakes

  • Ordering a brazing filler when the procedure calls for an AWS A5.10 welding classification, or the reverse
  • Choosing 4047 for a 5083 or 5086 marine structure, where 5183 or 5356 is required
  • Using 4047 on visible parts that will be anodised for appearance
  • Ignoring spool size and wire stiffness for the feeding system in use

FAQ

ER4047 aluminium (aluminum) welding wire: FAQ

Is ER4047 a welding wire or a brazing filler?

Both. The 12 % silicon alloy is classified as a welding filler under AWS A5.10 (ER4047) and has a brazing counterpart under AWS A5.8 (BAlSi-4). For fusion welding the parent metal melts; for brazing only the filler melts at about 580 – 600 °C.

Can ER4047 be used on 5083 or 5086?

No. High-magnesium 5xxx alloys react with the silicon to form brittle Mg2Si. Use ER5183, ER5356 or ER5556 on those alloys.

Why is ER4047 preferred for refrigeration and HVAC joints?

Its near-eutectic composition gives a very fluid pool that wets and seals joint faces, with minimal shrinkage and very low crack risk. That combination produces leak-tight joints on thin 3003 and 1xxx tube and fittings.

Can the dark colour after anodising be avoided?

Not with 4047 itself, because the silicon stays in the anodic film. Options are to place welds out of sight, paint or powder coat after anodising, or cap a 4047 root with a 5356 pass.

Is ER4047 stronger than ER4043?

In fillet welds it usually gives higher shear strength. In 6061-T6 groove welds both reach the same code minimum of 165 MPa because the heat-affected zone controls strength. 4047 is less ductile.

How should 4047 wire be stored?

Keep spools sealed in their bags in a dry, heated store above the dew point, and reseal part-used spools. Moisture on the wire causes porosity.
