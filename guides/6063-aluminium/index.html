6063 Aluminium Alloy: Composition, Tempers, Properties and Uses

6063 is the architectural extrusion alloy: a lean aluminium-magnesium-silicon grade that flows easily through complex dies, leaves a smooth surface and anodises to a bright, even colour. In the common T5 and T6 tempers it offers 150 – 240 MPa tensile strength, enough for frames, trims, tube and light structures. This guide covers its chemistry, tempers, guaranteed properties, standards and where it beats 6061.

Overview

What is 6063 aluminium?

6063 belongs to the heat-treatable 6xxx family, like 6061 and 6082, but carries less magnesium and silicon and almost no copper, chromium or manganese. That lean chemistry is deliberate. It lowers the force needed to push hot billet through a die, allows very thin walls and fine detail, and gives a clean surface with few die lines.

The trade-off is strength. 6063-T6 reaches roughly three quarters of the strength of 6061-T6, which is ample for windows, curtain-wall mullions, shopfronts, railings, furniture, LED channels, heat sinks and irrigation tube, but not for heavily loaded machine parts. Because so much of the world's extruded aluminium goes into building products, 6063 is the single most extruded alloy and is often simply called "architectural alloy".

Another reason 6063 dominates extrusion is its low quench sensitivity. It reaches T5 properties when cooled by fans or a light water mist at the press exit, so a separate solution-treatment furnace is not needed. That keeps processing cost down and makes thin, wide or asymmetric sections practical without distortion from a hard water quench.

Why designers choose 6063

  • Excellent extrudability: thin walls, hollow sections and fine detail at high press speeds
  • The best anodising response of the common structural alloys, with consistent colour from batch to batch
  • Good corrosion resistance in urban, industrial and coastal atmospheres
  • Lower cost and wider availability than 6061 in most extruded shapes

Compared with 6061, 6063 has roughly half the magnesium and copper and a quarter of the chromium. Fewer second-phase particles means fewer sites that disturb the anodic film, which is the metallurgical reason 6063 gives a more uniform anodised appearance.

On drawings, T5 and T6 are the two tempers to know. T5 is cheaper and perfectly adequate for most building profiles; T6 adds about 25 – 30 % yield strength and is worth specifying where wind load, span or impact governs the section size.

Finishing

Why 6063 anodises so well

Low alloy content

Little copper, chromium and iron means few intermetallic particles in the surface, so the oxide film grows evenly and stays clear.

Fine, uniform grain

Well-controlled extrusion gives a consistent grain structure, so etching and anodising produce the same tone along the length.

Smooth extruded surface

Lower extrusion pressure means fewer die lines and pick-up marks, reducing the polishing or etching needed before anodising.

Even dye uptake

The porous anodic film absorbs dyes and electrolytic colours uniformly: silver, champagne, bronze, black and gold are routine.

Architectural film classes

Clear anodising to AA 15 – AA 25 (15 – 25 µm) is common for exterior work under the Qualanod and AAMA 611 schemes.

Powder coating

6063 profiles are also the standard substrate for polyester powder coating to Qualicoat and AAMA 2603/2604/2605 classes.

Colour consistency depends on chemistry and process control as much as alloy. For a large façade, order all profiles from one source and one alloy window, ask for colour limit samples, and avoid mixing 6063 with 6061 or 6082 parts that will sit side by side, since they anodise to visibly different tones. See our guide to aluminium anodising for more.

Working with 6063

Machining, welding and bending 6063

Machining

Fair. 6063 is softer than 6061 and tends to form long, stringy chips. Use sharp, polished high-rake tools, high speeds and good lubrication. T6 machines noticeably better than T4 or T5.

Welding

Readily welded by TIG and MIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. Strength in the heat-affected zone drops to about the O to T4 level, so welded frames are sized for the reduced value.

Bending

Good in T4 and T52, acceptable in T5, limited in T6. Use mandrels for tube, and bend before anodising: anodic films crack on tight bends.

Joining

Most window and façade frames are mechanically joined with corner cleats, crimping and screw ports designed into the profile, which avoids weld softening and finish damage.

Applications

Where 6063 is used

  • Building envelope: window and door profiles, thermal-break sections, curtain-wall mullions and transoms, shopfronts, louvres
  • Interiors: partition systems, ceiling grids, skirting, stair nosings, furniture and shopfitting frames
  • Lighting and electronics: LED channels, extruded heat sinks, enclosures and rack rails
  • Railings and outdoor structures: balustrades, fences, pergolas, greenhouse frames and solar mounting rails
  • Tube and pipe: irrigation pipe, handrail tube, schedule 40 pipe and substation bus pipe
  • Transport: interior trims, luggage racks and window frames in buses, rail coaches and boats

FAQ

6063 aluminium alloy: frequently asked questions

What is 6063 aluminium used for?

Mainly extruded building products: window, door and curtain-wall profiles, railings and trims. It is also used for furniture, LED and heat-sink profiles, irrigation and handrail tube, and substation bus pipe.

Is 6063 stronger than 6061?

No. 6063-T6 is typically 241 MPa tensile against 310 MPa for 6061-T6. 6063 trades strength for easier extrusion and a better anodised finish.

What is the difference between 6063-T5 and 6063-T6?

T5 is cooled at the press and artificially aged; T6 is solution heat-treated (or effectively press-quenched) and then aged. T6 is roughly 25 – 30 % stronger in yield, while T5 is cheaper and sufficient for most building profiles.

Can 6063 aluminum be welded?

Yes, by TIG or MIG with 4043 or 5356 filler. The heat-affected zone loses much of its temper strength, so welded structures must be designed for the lower value.

Is 6063 good for outdoor and coastal use?

Yes. Its corrosion resistance is very good, and anodising or powder coating extends service life further, which is why it is the standard alloy for façades and windows.

What is the European equivalent of 6063?

EN AW-6063 (AlMg0.7Si), formerly DIN AlMgSi0.5 / Werkstoff 3.3206. The old British designation was HE9.
