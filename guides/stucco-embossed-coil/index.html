Embossed coil guide

Stucco Embossed Aluminium Coil & Sheet

Stucco embossed aluminium coil carries a random, orange-peel texture rolled into the surface. The pattern hides scratches and dents, cuts glare and stiffens thin sheet, which is why it is the standard skin for pipe and tank insulation jacketing, cold-room panels, truck bodies and appliance liners. Metline supplies stucco and other embossed patterns in 1xxx, 3xxx and 5xxx alloys, plain, coated or with a moisture barrier, worldwide.

Overview

What is stucco embossed aluminium?

Stucco embossing is a cold-rolling operation. Flat coil passes between a hard steel roll engraved with an irregular stucco texture and a softer backing roll, which presses the pattern through the sheet so it shows on both faces. No metal is removed, so the mass per square metre stays the same as plain sheet of the same gauge.

The texture does three useful jobs. It scatters light, so a large jacketed pipe rack or tank does not glare in the sun. It disguises the small scratches and handling marks that would show on mill-finish sheet. And the slight three-dimensional ripple raises the bending stiffness of thin gauges, so jacketing and liners resist oil-canning and look flatter after installation.

Embossing also work-hardens the metal a little, which is why suppliers usually emboss from a slightly softer temper (for example H14 or H24) so the finished sheet still bends around pipe and forms into lock seams without cracking.

Why specifiers choose stucco embossed coil

  • Hides scratches, fingerprints and minor dents during installation and service
  • Low-glare, uniform appearance on large surfaces
  • Stiffer feel than smooth sheet of the same thickness
  • Same corrosion resistance and recyclability as plain aluminium

Main application

Stucco embossed aluminium for insulation jacketing

The biggest use of stucco embossed coil is metal jacketing over thermal insulation on pipework, vessels and tanks in refineries, power plants, chemical plants, LNG terminals and HVAC systems. The jacket keeps rain, washdown and mechanical damage out of the insulation; wet insulation loses much of its thermal performance and can cause corrosion under insulation on the steel underneath.

In North America, aluminium jacketing is specified to ASTM C1729, which sets requirements for the alloy, temper, thickness, finish and moisture retarder, with the sheet itself to ASTM B209. A typical pipe specification calls for 0.016 in (0.40 mm) stucco embossed 3003 or 1100 for smaller lines and 0.020 – 0.024 in for larger pipe and equipment, with corrugated sheet for large vertical tanks.

  • Moisture barrier: a polysurlyn (polyethylene / ionomer) film, typically around 3 mil (76 µm), or kraft paper, heat-laminated to the inner face to stop galvanic and crevice corrosion between the jacket and wet insulation or the carbon-steel pipe
  • Overlaps: longitudinal laps of 50 – 75 mm placed to shed water, with Z-lock or modified Pittsburgh seams on larger equipment
  • Fastening: aluminium or stainless bands and seals; stainless bands need an isolation strip under them in wet service
  • Fire service: aluminium melts at about 660 °C, so projects that require jacketing to survive a fire specify stainless steel jacketing instead

Process

How it is produced and what to inspect

  1. Cold rollingCoil is rolled to just over the target gauge and to the temper required before embossing.
  2. Cleaning and levellingRolling oil is removed and the strip is tension-levelled for a flat, uniform base.
  3. EmbossingPatterned rolls press the stucco texture into the strip at controlled pressure for an even depth.
  4. Coating or laminationOptional colour coating, anodising, or lamination of a polysurlyn or kraft moisture barrier.
  5. Slitting and packingSlit to width, cut to sheet or cut-and-rolled, then packed eye-to-wall or eye-to-sky on export pallets.

On delivery, check that the pattern depth is uniform across the width, that there are no oil stains, white corrosion spots or roll marks, and that the moisture barrier is fully bonded at the edges. Each lot should come with an EN 10204 3.1 mill certificate confirming alloy, temper and mechanical results.

FAQ

Stucco embossed aluminium (aluminum) coil: FAQ

What is stucco embossed aluminium used for?

Mainly as metal jacketing over pipe, tank and equipment insulation, and as facing for cold-room panels, refrigerator liners, truck bodies and appliance panels where a scratch-hiding, low-glare surface is wanted.

Which alloy is best for stucco embossed jacketing?

3003 in H14 or H24 temper is the most common because it combines good formability with better strength than 1100. 5052 is used for marine and high-abuse areas; 1100 and 1060 for lighter-duty work.

Does embossing make aluminium sheet thicker or heavier?

It makes the sheet measure thicker across the pattern peaks, but it does not add metal, so weight per square metre is the same as flat sheet of the original gauge.

What is the moisture barrier on aluminium jacketing?

A thin polysurlyn film or kraft paper laminated to the inside face. It separates the aluminium from wet insulation and steel pipe, reducing galvanic and crevice corrosion.

What thickness of aluminium jacketing should I use?

For most pipe insulation 0.40 mm (0.016 in) is standard; larger pipes, vessels and areas with foot traffic or mechanical risk use 0.50 – 0.80 mm (0.020 – 0.032 in). Follow your project specification or ASTM C1729.

Can stucco embossed coil be colour coated?

Yes. PE and PVDF coatings are applied before or after embossing, and anodised embossed sheet is also available for appliance and decorative uses.
