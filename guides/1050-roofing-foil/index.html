1050 Aluminium Foil for Roofing

1050 roofing foil is 99.5 % pure aluminium used in three ways on roofs: as a bare, low-emissivity radiant barrier, as a colour-coated weathering layer, and as the facing of laminated insulation such as foil-bubble and foil-scrim-kraft. A clean, bare face reflects about 95 % of radiant heat, but coatings change that figure, so the type you choose decides the performance. This guide explains the types, coatings, standards and checks.

Overview

What is 1050 roofing foil and how does it work?

Heat crosses a roof in three ways: conduction through solid materials, convection in air, and radiation across air gaps. Bulk insulation such as mineral wool slows conduction. Aluminium foil tackles radiation, which in a sunlit roof is often the largest heat path in summer.

A clean aluminium surface has a thermal emittance of roughly 0.03 – 0.05, which means it emits only 3 – 5 % as much long-wave infrared radiation as an ideal black surface and reflects around 95 % of what falls on it. When the foil faces an air gap under hot roof sheeting, very little of the sheeting's heat reaches the ceiling below. ASTM C1313 defines a sheet radiant barrier as a material with at least one surface of emittance 0.1 or less, a level only bright metal surfaces reach.

1050 is a popular choice because its 99.5 % purity gives a bright, corrosion-resistant surface and excellent ductility for rolling to thin gauges, laminating and embossing. It costs less than higher-purity 1070 and has more consistent surface quality than recycled-content foil.

The key design rule

  • Foil only reduces radiant heat flow across an air space of at least about 20 mm (¾ in)
  • The low-emittance (bright) face must face that air space
  • Paint, dust or condensation on the bright face raises its emittance and cuts performance
  • A coloured coating is for weathering and looks; it is not a radiant barrier surface
Product types

Main types of 1050 roofing foil

Bare reflective foil

Plain, bright 1050 foil, usually 7 – 40 µm, laminated or loose-laid under roof sheeting as a radiant barrier and vapour control layer.

Laminated insulation facings

Foil bonded to kraft paper and glass scrim (FSK), woven polyethylene, PE bubble film or foam. The foil provides the low-e face; the backing provides strength and puncture resistance.

Colour-coated roofing foil

Thicker foil, typically 0.05 – 0.15 mm, coated with polyester or PVDF paint for use as a visible, self-adhesive or bonded weathering layer on membranes and panels.

Perforated foil

Micro-perforated so the layer breathes, used where a vapour-open radiant barrier is needed.

Foil tape

Foil with pressure-sensitive adhesive for sealing laps and penetrations in foil-faced insulation.

Wide rolls and light gauge strip

Strip of about 0.3 – 3 mm up to very wide widths for roofing sheet and composite panels, where fewer seams are wanted.

Performance

Benefits and limitations

  • Reflects about 95 % of radiant heat when the bright face meets an air gap
  • Practically zero water vapour transmission when unperforated, so it acts as a vapour control layer
  • Excellent natural corrosion resistance as a 99.5 % pure grade
  • Very light: 25 µm foil adds under 0.1 kg/m² to the roof
  • Fully recyclable
  • Gives little benefit without an air gap: foil pressed against a hot surface conducts heat straight through
  • Dust and condensation on upward-facing foil reduce its reflectivity over time
  • Unperforated foil on the wrong side of insulation can trap moisture in cold climates
  • Thin foil tears easily; laminated facings or careful handling are needed on site
  • Coloured coatings raise emittance, so coated faces do not act as radiant barriers

Applications

Uses of 1050 roofing foil

  • Radiant barriers under metal and tiled roofs, facing a ventilated air space
  • Insulation facings on glass wool, rock wool and foam boards, and in foil-bubble products
  • Self-adhesive and bituminous membranes with a coloured foil face for UV protection
  • Composite and honeycomb panels for roofs and walls
  • Ceilings and façades where coloured or embossed foil laminates add a finished surface
  • Under corrugated sheet: combined with corrugated aluminium sheet and an air gap, the foil keeps radiant heat out of the building

FAQ

1050 roofing foil: frequently asked questions

How much heat does aluminium roofing foil reflect?

A clean, bare aluminium face reflects about 95 % of long-wave radiant heat (emittance about 0.03 – 0.05). It only works when that face looks onto an air space; foil sandwiched tight between two materials gives little radiant benefit.

Does a coloured coating reduce the insulation effect?

Yes. Paints have emittance around 0.8 – 0.9, so a coated face does not act as a radiant barrier. Use coloured foil for weathering and appearance, and keep a bare or low-e lacquered face towards the air gap.

Why 1050 rather than 8011 or 1235 foil?

1050 gives a bright, corrosion-resistant surface and good strength in the gauges used for roofing. 8011 is stronger and common in packaging; 1235 is used for very thin foil. All three can work as radiant barriers when laminated.

What thickness of foil is used for roof insulation?

Facings on laminated insulation are usually about 7 – 25 µm. Self-supporting or colour-coated roofing foils are thicker, typically 0.05 – 0.15 mm.

Is aluminum foil a vapour barrier?

Unperforated foil is practically impermeable to water vapour. Place it on the warm side of insulation in cold climates, or use perforated foil where the assembly must breathe.
