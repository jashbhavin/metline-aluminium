Aluminium Threaded Rod and Studs: Alloys, Sizes and Load Capacity

Aluminium threaded rod (all-thread) is a headless rod threaded along its full length, or at the ends only, used where a fastener must be light, non-magnetic, corrosion-resistant or galvanically compatible with aluminium structures. It is normally made from 6061-T6, 2024-T4 or 7075 bar to ASTM B211, threaded to ISO metric or Unified inch standards. This guide gives thread sizes, calculated tensile loads by alloy, and the practical rules for using aluminium threads without galling or corrosion.

Overview

What is aluminium threaded rod?

Threaded rod comes in three basic forms: all-thread rod, threaded end to end and cut to length with nuts used at any point; double-end studs, threaded at both ends with a plain centre; and single-end or tap-end studs, threaded at one end for screwing into a tapped hole. Aluminium versions do the same jobs as steel rod but at about one third of the weight.

Aluminium rod is chosen not for raw strength, where steel and stainless steel are far ahead, but for what it adds to an assembly: low mass in aircraft, vehicles, drones and portable equipment; no magnetism for instruments, MRI rooms and electronics; good electrical and thermal conductivity for busbar clamps and grounding; and, most often, compatibility with aluminium structures, because an aluminium stud in an aluminium frame avoids the galvanic corrosion that steel or copper fasteners can cause in wet conditions.

The rod is made from extruded or cold-finished bar. Threads are either cut (by die-head, lathe or CNC) or rolled. Rolling displaces rather than removes metal, so the grain flows around the thread form, the root is smooth and work-hardened, and fatigue strength is noticeably better. Rolled threads are preferred for production quantities and fatigue-loaded joints; cut threads suit short runs, large diameters and special forms.

When aluminium threaded rod makes sense

  • Fastening aluminium panels, frames and housings where dissimilar-metal corrosion must be avoided
  • Weight-critical assemblies in aerospace, motorsport, marine and robotics
  • Non-magnetic or conductive fixings in electrical and scientific equipment
  • Tie rods, spacers and adjusters carrying light to moderate loads

Metric threads

Metric aluminium threaded rod: sizes and tensile loads

ISO metric coarse threads to ISO 261/262 with tolerance class 6g are standard; fine pitches (for example M10 × 1.25, M12 × 1.5) are available for adjustment and vibration resistance. Threaded rod dimensions follow DIN 976-1, which has replaced DIN 975. Engineering

Design rules for aluminium threaded joints

  • Galling: aluminium threads, especially aluminium on aluminium, can gall and seize under high torque. Use a thread lubricant or anti-seize free of copper and graphite (both promote galvanic corrosion of aluminium), or anodise one part.
  • Thread engagement: when a stud screws into a tapped aluminium part, use a longer engagement than in steel, commonly 1.5 – 2 × diameter, or fit helical thread inserts for repeated assembly.
  • Preload and torque: base tightening torque on the rod alloy and lubricant; aluminium needs lower torque than steel of the same size. Use washers to spread bearing load on soft parts.
  • Galvanic corrosion: keep aluminium rod away from bare copper, brass and carbon steel in wet service. Stainless steel nuts on aluminium rod need isolating washers and sealant in marine conditions.
  • Creep and temperature: 6xxx and 7xxx alloys lose strength with long exposure above about 100 – 150 °C, and preload relaxes. Choose steel or titanium for hot joints.
  • Thermal expansion: aluminium expands about twice as much as steel; an aluminium stud clamping aluminium parts keeps preload steadier through temperature cycles than a steel one.
Finishes and inspection

Finishes, inspection and documentation

Anodising

Clear or coloured sulphuric anodising for corrosion resistance and identification; hard anodising for wear. Allow for coating build-up on thread fits.

Conversion coating

Chromate-free conversion coatings protect 2024 and 7075 and act as a paint base.

Thread inspection

GO/NO-GO ring and plug gauges for the specified class (6g/6H or 2A/2B); pitch and major diameter checks.

Mechanical testing

Tensile and hardness tests on the rod lot; proof-load testing of studs where required.

Material certification

Mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1 with chemistry and properties of the bar.

Traceability

Lot marking and packing labels linking each bundle to its certificate.

Buyer advice

Common mistakes with aluminium threaded rod

Treating it like steel rod

Swapping a steel rod for the same size in aluminium without checking load can halve the capacity. Size up or choose 7075 where strength matters.

7075-T6 in wet service

T6 is prone to stress-corrosion cracking under sustained tension in moist environments. Specify 7075-T73 or 6061-T6 instead.

Copper-based anti-seize

Common copper or graphite anti-seize compounds accelerate corrosion of aluminium. Use a nickel, zinc or aluminium-compatible product.

Ignoring coating build-up

Anodising adds thickness to both flanks of the thread; specify whether the thread class applies before or after coating.

Over-torquing

Aluminium threads strip or gall at torques that are normal for steel. Use calibrated tools and published torque values for the alloy.

Unclear stud lengths

For double-end and tap-end studs, give overall length and each thread length on the drawing, not just "M12 × 100".

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) threaded rod: frequently asked questions

How strong is aluminum threaded rod?

It depends on the alloy. Using specified minimum tensile strengths, an M10 rod breaks at about 17 kN in 6061-T6, 25 kN in 2024-T4 and 31 kN in 7075-T6. Apply a safety factor for working loads.

Which aluminium alloy is best for threaded rod?

6061-T6 for general and marine use thanks to its corrosion resistance; 2024-T4 or 7075-T73 for high-strength aerospace studs; 7075-T6 for maximum strength in dry, protected conditions.

How do I stop aluminium threads from galling?

Use a copper-free and graphite-free anti-seize or thread lubricant, avoid over-torquing, consider anodising one of the mating parts, and use helical inserts in tapped aluminium holes that are assembled repeatedly.

Can I use stainless steel nuts on aluminium rod?

Yes in dry conditions. In wet or marine service, isolate the metals with nylon or neoprene washers and sealant, or use aluminium nuts, to limit galvanic corrosion.

Are rolled threads better than cut threads?

For fatigue-loaded joints, yes: rolling work-hardens the root and keeps the grain continuous, which improves fatigue life. Cut threads are fine for static loads and short runs.

Do you supply studs to drawing?

Yes. Metline supplies aluminium threaded rod and studs to drawing, in metric and inch threads, with mill certificates for the bar.
