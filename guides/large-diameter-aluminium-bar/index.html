Large Diameter Aluminium Bar and Billet

Large diameter aluminium bar is round stock above roughly 300 mm in diameter, supplied as forged bar, homogenised cast billet or heavy extruded rod for machining big shafts, discs, rollers, flanges, mould bases and aerospace fittings. Sizes of 600 – 700 mm are common in heavy machining, and larger diameters are possible as forged or cast stock. This guide explains production routes, alloys, weights and how to specify the right bar.

Overview

What counts as large diameter aluminium bar?

Standard extruded and drawn round bar covers diameters from a few millimetres to about 300 mm. Above that, the product changes: the bar is either extruded on very large presses, forged from a cast ingot, or supplied as a direct-chill cast and homogenised billet.

The route matters because it decides the internal quality and the properties you can rely on. An extruded bar has a worked structure throughout; a forged bar has had its cast structure broken down and porosity closed by heavy deformation; a cast billet keeps a cast structure, homogenised to even out segregation but not worked. All three are machinable, but they are not interchangeable for highly stressed parts.

Buyers usually need large bar for parts that would otherwise be machined from thick plate or forged to shape: big turned rollers and drums, wheel and turbine discs, large flanges and rings, shafts, mould and die bases, vacuum chamber parts, sputtering target backing plates and aerospace fittings.

Why large bar instead of thick plate?

  • Round parts machined from bar waste far less material than if cut from plate
  • Forged bar has more uniform properties around the circumference
  • Sawn discs give near-net blanks for rings, wheels and flanges
  • Very large diameters can exceed the thickness of any stretched plate

When ordering, state whether properties are required in the longitudinal, transverse or short-transverse direction and at what location in the section. Large forgings and bars show some directionality, and an unspecified test location leads to disputes later.

Working with large bar

Machining and handling tips

  • Rough machine first, then finish: removing material relieves residual stress; leave stock on critical faces and finish after a rest period or stress-relief ageing.
  • Choose stress-relieved tempers: T651, T7351 and T7451 have been stretched or compressed to reduce quench stress, which limits movement during machining.
  • Watch the centre: the core of a large bar is where porosity or segregation would sit; specify UT if the bore or centre stays in the finished part.
  • Saw with care: large-diameter band saws with coolant prevent heat build-up and smearing; specify flatness if discs will be used without facing.
  • Lift safely: use slings or cradles rated for the weight, and avoid point loads that dent the surface of softer alloys.
Ordering

How to order large diameter bar

  1. Define the partSend the finished part drawing or the blank diameter and length, alloy, temper and governing standard.
  2. Choose the routeWe advise forged, extruded or cast billet depending on stress level, inspection needs and budget.
  3. Agree testingUltrasonic class, test directions, and any third-party inspection.
  4. ProcessingSaw cutting to length or into discs, rough turning or boring on request.
  5. Packing and deliveryBars fixed to timber skids with straps or steel bands and blocked against rolling; shipped on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms worldwide.

FAQ

Large diameter aluminium (aluminum) bar: FAQ

What is the largest diameter aluminium bar available?

Extruded bar usually stops at about 300 – 500 mm. Forged bar and cast billet are produced in much larger diameters, with 600 – 700 mm common and around 1,000 mm and above possible for some alloys. Send your size and we confirm feasibility.

Is forged bar better than cast billet for machining?

For stressed parts, yes: forging closes porosity and refines the grain, giving higher ductility and fatigue strength. Cast billet is cheaper and acceptable for moulds, fixtures and lightly loaded parts.

How much does a 600 mm aluminium bar weigh?

About 763 kg per metre in 6061 (density 2.70 g/cm³). A 2xxx or 7xxx alloy bar of the same size weighs about 3 – 4 % more.

Can large diameter aluminum bar be cut into discs?

Yes. Bars are saw-cut into discs or blanks to your thickness, with allowance for facing, and can be rough-turned or bored on request.

Which temper should I order for a large machined part?

Choose stress-relieved tempers such as 6061-T651, 7075-T7351 or 7050-T7451 so the part stays stable during heavy machining. 5083 is supplied in O or H112.

Do you test large bar ultrasonically?

Ultrasonic testing to ASTM B594 or AMS-STD-2154 can be specified on the order, with the acceptance class you need, together with the EN 10204 3.1 certificate.
