Aluminium Slab: High-Integrity Cast Slab for Rolling, Forging & Machining

Aluminium slab is a large rectangular ingot, direct-chill (DC) cast and usually homogenised, that is hot rolled into plate and sheet, forged, or sawn into thick blocks for machining. Its internal quality, meaning freedom from cracks, porosity, oxide inclusions and segregation, decides the yield and reliability of everything made from it. This guide covers the alloys, the defects that matter, the inspections to specify and the sizes and weights to expect when you buy slab.

Overview

What aluminium slab is and who buys it

Slab, also called rolling ingot, sheet ingot or flat ingot, is the starting point for rolled aluminium. Molten metal of a controlled composition is cast vertically into a water-cooled rectangular mould; as the solid shell forms, the starter block descends and water sprays directly onto the emerging ingot. The result is a rectangular slab several metres long with a fine, relatively uniform cast structure.

Three groups of buyers use slab. Rolling mills hot roll it into plate, sheet and coil. Forging shops use sections of thick slab as stock for open-die and closed-die forgings, especially in 2xxx and 7xxx alloys where large cross-sections are needed. Machining and mould makers buy sawn slab blocks when they need a section thicker than rolled plate can provide, or when they accept cast properties in exchange for size.

For standard sheet and plate production the commercial specification of a rolling slab is well established; our rolling slab and sheet ingot specification guide covers that side. This page focuses on high-integrity slab: material for high-strength alloys, thick plate, forgings and fatigue-critical parts, where internal soundness and traceability matter as much as chemistry.

Why slab quality is worth paying for

  • A crack or inclusion found after hot rolling, forging, heat treatment or precision machining costs far more than one rejected at the slab stage
  • Segregation and coarse grains cause batch-to-batch scatter in strength, toughness and machinability
  • Porosity and oxide films become fatigue crack starters in highly stressed parts
  • Thick plate and forgings retain more of the original cast structure in their centres, so slab defects show up there first

High-strength 7xxx and 2xxx alloys are the most demanding to cast. 7050 and 7075 have a long freezing range and contract strongly, so they are prone to hot tearing during solidification and to cold cracking from thermal stress after casting. Large 7xxx slabs are therefore cast at controlled speeds, often stress-relieved or homogenised promptly after casting, and inspected more thoroughly than 5xxx or 6xxx slabs.

High-magnesium 5xxx alloys such as 5083 oxidise readily in the melt, so melt cleanliness and filtration matter. 4032 and 2618 are often used as forging stock, where a fine, uniform cast structure improves forgeability and final properties.

Process

From melt to slab: the steps that decide internal quality

  1. Melting and alloyingPrimary metal, master alloys and controlled scrap are melted and adjusted to the target analysis, usually aimed well inside the standard limits so that properties are consistent.
  2. Melt treatmentThe melt is fluxed and degassed (often by inline rotary degassing with argon or argon-chlorine mixtures) to remove hydrogen, which otherwise forms porosity, and alkali impurities.
  3. FiltrationCeramic foam or deep-bed filters remove oxide films and inclusions. Two-stage filtration is common for aerospace and high-integrity slab.
  4. Grain refinementAn Al-Ti-B or Al-Ti-C grain refiner is added inline to give fine, equiaxed grains and reduce hot-tearing susceptibility.
  5. DC castingMetal is cast into a water-cooled mould at a controlled speed and temperature. Start-up and end of cast are the most defect-prone phases, so butt and head ends are cut off.
  6. HomogenisationThe slab is soaked at high temperature for many hours to even out microsegregation, dissolve coarse constituents and precipitate dispersoids that control grain structure later.
  7. Scalping and sawingThe rolling faces are milled to remove the cast surface layer, which carries segregation and oxides. Ends are sawn square; slabs for forging or machining are cut into blocks.
  8. Inspection and releaseChemistry, dimensions, surface and, where required, ultrasonic, macro-etch and grain size tests are completed before the slab is released with its traceability records.

No single test finds all of these. Chemistry confirms the alloy and impurity levels, ultrasonic testing finds internal voids, cracks and large inclusions, macro-etching of a cross-section reveals grain structure and segregation patterns, and metallographic sections show porosity and constituent particle size. High-integrity orders typically combine several of them.

Inspection & documentation

What to specify for inspection and traceability

Chemical analysis

Spectrometer analysis of the melt (and optionally of the slab), reported against the Aluminum Association, EN 573-3 or GB/T 3190 limits, plus any tighter customer limits.

Ultrasonic testing

Immersion or contact UT of the slab or of sawn blocks, with acceptance classes agreed with the buyer. Aerospace orders often reference AMS-STD-2154 or ASTM B594 class definitions used for the wrought product.

Macro-etch and grain size

Etched transverse slices show grain size, grain shape and any feathery grains or segregation bands. Grain size can be rated against an agreed chart.

Porosity and cleanliness

Metallographic or density-based porosity checks, and melt cleanliness measurements such as hydrogen content, where specified.

Dimensions and surface

Thickness, width, length, flatness of scalped faces, squareness of sawn ends and visual surface inspection.

Traceability package

Cast number, furnace and casting records, homogenisation chart and an EN 10204 3.1 certificate tying every slab or block to its cast.

State the inspection plan in the enquiry. Testing adds cost and time, and the right level depends on the downstream product: commercial sheet ingot needs much less than slab destined for aerospace plate or forgings.

A useful rule: if your part fits within the thickness of available rolled plate or a forged block, those wrought products offer better and more consistent properties than cast slab, because hot working closes porosity and breaks up the cast structure. Choose sawn slab when the section is larger than plate or forgings allow, or when the part is lightly stressed and the lower cost of cast material is attractive.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) slab: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between aluminium slab and aluminium plate?

Slab is a cast ingot, typically 300 – 700 mm thick, with a cast structure. Plate is made by hot rolling slab and then heat treating it, which closes porosity and refines the structure, so plate has better and more consistent properties. Slab is bought by mills, forgers and machinists who need very large sections.

Why is 7xxx slab harder to produce than 5xxx or 6xxx slab?

7050 and 7075 freeze over a wide temperature range and contract strongly, which makes them prone to hot tearing during casting and to cold cracking from thermal stress afterwards. Casting speed, grain refinement and prompt stress relief or homogenisation are used to control this.

Is aluminium slab supplied homogenised and scalped?

Rolling slab is normally homogenised and scalped before hot rolling, and slab for forging or machining is usually homogenised. Condition can be as cast, homogenised, scalped or sawn to blocks; specify it in the order.

Can slab be ultrasonically tested?

Yes. Slabs and sawn blocks can be ultrasonically inspected to an acceptance class agreed with the buyer. UT finds internal voids, cracks and large inclusions; macro-etch and grain size tests complement it.

How heavy is an aluminium slab?

Multiply volume by density. A 480 × 1,660 × 6,000 mm slab is 4.78 m³, so about 12.9 t at 2.70 g/cm³ or 13.4 t in 7075. Large slabs regularly exceed ten tonnes.

Can I buy slab in Chinese GB grades such as 2A12 or 7A04?

Yes, slab can be supplied to GB/T 3190 compositions as well as Aluminum Association and EN grades. Give the standard and grade on the enquiry.
