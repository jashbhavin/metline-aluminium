6061 Aluminium Busbar and Electrical Flat Bar

6061 busbar is chosen when a conductor must also carry mechanical load: it is about 40 % stronger than 6101-T6 and far stronger than pure 1350, while still conducting around 40 – 43 % IACS. That makes it suited to self-supporting bars, battery and rectifier connections under vibration, and parts that double as brackets. This guide covers conductivity, resistance tables, sizes, tolerances, bending, plating and how to choose between 6061 and the high-conductivity grades.

Overview

When does 6061 make sense as a busbar?

Most aluminium busbar is made from high-conductivity grades: 1350 (EC aluminium, about 61 % IACS) for maximum conductivity, or 6101 and 6063 (about 52 – 57 % IACS) when a little more strength is needed. 6061 gives up some conductivity in exchange for much higher strength, hardness and machinability.

That trade is worthwhile when the busbar is not just a conductor. Typical examples are bars that span between widely spaced supports, connections in battery packs and traction equipment exposed to vibration, rectifier and electroplating bus that also carries anode weight, and machined terminals where threads must hold torque. For a simple panel busbar with short supports, a high-conductivity grade is usually more economical because a smaller section carries the same current.

Because 6061 has roughly 40 % higher resistivity than 1350, a 6061 bar needs a correspondingly larger cross-section to run at the same temperature. Always check temperature rise first, then confirm the stronger alloy is genuinely needed.

6061 busbar strengths

  • Minimum yield of 240 MPa in T6, so bars stay straight and resist short-circuit forces
  • Hard surface that holds bolted joints and machined threads
  • Machines cleanly for terminals and complex connectors
  • Good corrosion resistance and anodising for insulated or visible parts

Tolerances

Dimensional tolerances for flat busbar

Extruded 6061 flat bar is supplied to EN 755-5 (rectangular bar) or ANSI H35.2 tolerances unless the drawing is tighter. As a guide, thickness and width tolerances widen with size: a few tenths of a millimetre for bars under about 35 mm wide, rising to around ±1 mm for bars over 100 mm wide. Busbar for laminated or stacked assemblies often needs tighter thickness control and flatness, which can be agreed or achieved by machining.

  • State tolerance class or values for thickness, width and flatness
  • Specify edge form: square, radius (for example R1 or R2) or full round
  • Ask for surface free of dents and scratches in contact areas
Fabrication

Bending, drilling, joints and plating

Bending

T6 bars need generous radii, typically 2 – 3 × thickness flatwise, more for thick or edgewise bends. For tight bends, order T4, bend, then age to T6, or use a high-conductivity grade.

Drilling and punching

6061 drills and punches cleanly. Deburr holes so contact faces sit flat and joint resistance stays low.

Bolted joints

Use Belleville washers to keep pressure constant through thermal cycles, clean the oxide with a wire brush and apply contact compound.

Plating

Tin plating is the usual finish for contact areas; silver for high-current or high-temperature joints; nickel for corrosive atmospheres.

Copper interfaces

Plate the aluminium or use bimetallic transition pieces to prevent galvanic corrosion where aluminium meets copper.

Insulation

Powder coat, epoxy, heat-shrink sleeving or anodising insulate bars in compact assemblies.

Applications

Where 6061 busbar is used

  • Battery systems: module and pack interconnects in EV, bus and energy-storage systems exposed to vibration
  • Rectifiers and electroplating: bus that also supports anode and cathode bars
  • Rail and traction: conductor and earthing bars that must resist shock
  • Power electronics: machined terminals and heat-sinking bus in inverters and drives
  • Switchgear supports: long spans where 1350 or 6101 would sag
  • Earthing: ground bars in outdoor installations, often tin plated

FAQ

6061 aluminium (aluminum) busbar: frequently asked questions

Is 6061 a good busbar material?

Yes, when strength matters. It conducts about 43 % IACS in T6, lower than 1350 or 6101, but is much stronger. Where strength is not needed, a high-conductivity grade carries the same current in a smaller section.

What is the resistance of a 10 × 100 mm 6061 busbar?

About 40 µΩ per metre at 20 °C for 6061-T6, compared with about 28 µΩ per metre for 1350.

Can 6061-T6 busbar be bent?

Yes, with generous radii of about 2 – 3 × thickness flatwise. For tighter bends, bend in T4 and age afterwards.

Should aluminium busbar be plated?

Plating is recommended at joints, especially where aluminium meets copper. Tin is the usual choice; silver suits high-current joints.

How does 6061 busbar compare with copper?

Copper conducts about 2.3 times better than 6061-T6 by volume, but 6061 weighs less than a third as much, is cheaper and is stronger than soft copper.
