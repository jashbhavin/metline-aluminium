Radiator tube guide

Aluminium Hourglass Tube for Radiator & Heat Exchanger Cores

An aluminium hourglass tube is a thin-wall, high-frequency (HF) welded flat tube made from brazing-clad strip, with a waisted, hourglass-style profile and formed dimples or internal channels that strengthen the tube and stir the coolant. It is used in brazed radiator, heater and machinery cooling cores. This guide covers the configurations, clad materials, typical sizes and the details a core designer must confirm before ordering.

Overview

What is an aluminium hourglass tube?

In a brazed aluminium radiator, coolant flows through rows of flat tubes while air passes over corrugated fins brazed between them. The tube must be thin for heat transfer, strong enough to hold pressure and resist vibration, and clad with filler metal so it brazes to the fins and headers in one furnace pass. The hourglass tube is one answer to these demands.

The tube is roll-formed from coil of brazing sheet and closed with a longitudinal HF weld. During forming, the broad faces are drawn inwards so the profile narrows towards the middle, and dimples or internal webs are pressed into the walls. Where opposite walls touch, they braze together in the furnace, tying the faces to each other. This prevents the flat faces from ballooning under pressure, lets the wall be thinner than in a plain flat tube, and breaks up the coolant boundary layer to raise heat transfer.

The exact geometry differs from one core maker to another, which is why hourglass tube is always made to a drawing: width, height, wall thickness, dimple pattern and end configuration are all defined by the core design and header slot.

Why core designers use hourglass tube

  • Internal contact points brazed together give high burst and pressure-cycle resistance for a thin wall
  • Dimples act as built-in turbulators, so no separate insert is needed
  • HF welding from coil is fast and economical for long production runs
  • Clad strip brazes to fins and headers in the same controlled-atmosphere brazing (CAB) cycle
Configurations

Hourglass tube configurations

Ordinary hourglass tube

The basic waisted profile without additional dimpling. Used where the core design needs the profile shape but turbulence is supplied in another way or is not required.

Dimple hourglass tube

Dimples are pressed into the broad faces in a defined pattern and area. They stiffen the faces, braze to the opposite wall and stir the coolant. Dimple pitch, depth and position follow the drawing.

Free-end hourglass tube

A plain, undimpled zone is left at each tube end, commonly about 5 – 6 mm, so the tube slides cleanly into the header slots and seals well when brazed. Other free-end lengths are made to suit the header design.

Two-channel tube

The profile is formed with an internal division that creates two parallel coolant channels. The central contact line brazes shut, which roughly halves the unsupported span of each face.

Three-channel tube

Two internal divisions give three channels, used on wider tubes where a single central support is not enough to control face deflection under pressure.

Custom profiles

Where an existing profile is close but not identical to the drawing, roll tooling can be adapted. A tube from a different design family, such as a folded B-type tube, is not a direct substitute.

Materials

Clad materials for hourglass tube

Hourglass tube is made from brazing sheet: a 3xxx-series core alloy with a thin layer of 4343 aluminium-silicon filler on one or both sides. Clad on both sides is normal for hourglass tube because the inside surfaces must braze together at the dimples and channel walls, and the outside must braze to the fins and header.

LayerAlloyKey composition (wt %)Function
Clad layer4343Si 6.8 – 8.2Filler metal; melts at about 577 – 613 °C and flows into joints during brazing
Core3003Mn 1.0 – 1.5, Cu 0.05 – 0.20Standard core: good formability, weldability and corrosion resistance
Core3005Mn 1.0 – 1.5, Mg 0.20 – 0.6Magnesium raises post-braze strength; Mg content must suit the CAB flux
Other coresLong-life 3xxx variantsTo customer specificationImproved post-braze strength or internal corrosion resistance

Material thickness for this tube family is typically 0.22 – 0.5 mm. After brazing, the core alloy is effectively in the annealed condition, so the tube is designed on annealed strength; for 3003-O, EN 485-2 specifies a tensile strength of 95 – 135 MPa. Clad ratio (cladding thickness as a percentage of total) is set by the strip supplier and confirmed on the mill certificate. For background on clad materials, see our guide to brazing clad strip and coil.

Magnesium above roughly 0.3 % can react with standard potassium fluoroaluminate flux and impair CAB brazing. If you specify a 3005 core, confirm that the brazing process is set up for it.

Selection

How to select the right tube

Tube typeConstructionStrengthsTypical use
Hourglass / dimple welded tubeHF welded clad strip with formed supportsEconomical, high pressure resistance, built-in turbulenceRadiators, heater cores
B-type folded tubeFolded clad strip with a central brazed web, no weldVery thin walls, strong central webCar radiators, heater cores
Flat-oval tubeHF welded plain flat ovalSimple, low pressure dropRadiators with inserts or low pressure
Microchannel (MPE) tubeExtruded multi-portVery high pressure capacityRefrigerant condensers, evaporators
  • Start from the core drawing and header slot. Tube width, height and nose shape must match the header punching exactly.
  • Choose channels by width. Wider tubes need more internal supports to limit face deflection and meet burst targets.
  • Check clad on both sides. Internal brazing of dimples and channel walls needs inside cladding.
  • Define test requirements. Burst, leak, pressure-cycle and thermal-cycle targets drive wall thickness and material choice.
  • Avoid direct substitution. A tube from another family (for example B-type) changes the internal structure and end design, so the core must be revalidated.
Quality and testing

Inspection and testing

  • Dimensions: width, height, wall, straightness and cut length checked against the drawing; dimple pattern verified with gauges or optical inspection
  • Weld integrity: continuous eddy-current testing of the HF weld seam during production
  • Pressure tests: leak and burst testing on samples from each run
  • Metallography: weld zone and clad layer checked in cross-section
  • Brazeability: trial brazing on sample cores when a new material or profile is introduced
  • Traceability: strip coil number and mill certificate linked to each tube lot

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) hourglass tube: frequently asked questions

What is an hourglass tube in a radiator?

It is a flat, HF-welded aluminium tube with a waisted profile and formed dimples or internal channels. The internal contact points braze together in the furnace, making the thin-wall tube strong and creating turbulence in the coolant.

Which material is used for hourglass tube?

Usually brazing sheet clad on both sides, such as 4343/3003/4343 or 4343/3005/4343, about 0.22 – 0.5 mm thick. The 4343 layer is the filler metal; the 3xxx core provides strength and corrosion resistance.

What does free-end mean?

A plain, undimpled length at each tube end, commonly about 5 – 6 mm, so the tube fits and seals correctly in the header slot. The length is set by the header design.

Should I choose two-channel or three-channel tube?

Follow the core design. As a rule, wider tubes need more internal supports, so three-channel tubes are used on wider profiles to control face deflection and meet burst requirements.

Can an hourglass tube replace a B-type tube?

Not directly. The two have different internal structures and end designs, so substituting one for the other requires a new core design and revalidation.

Can you make tubes to our drawing?

Yes. Send the cross-section and specification; existing profiles are checked first and roll tooling can be adapted where the drawing differs.

Need hourglass tube for your radiator cores?

Send the drawing, material, quantity and destination. We will review the profile and reply with samples and a quote.
