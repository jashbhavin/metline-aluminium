7075 Aluminium Alloy: Properties, Tempers, Forms and Uses

7075 is a zinc-based, heat-treatable aluminium alloy with one of the highest strengths of any commercial aluminium grade: about 572 MPa typical tensile strength in the T6 temper. It machines superbly but cannot be fusion-welded and needs protection against corrosion. This guide gives the data, explains the tempers and helps you decide when 7075 is the right choice and when another grade is better.

Overview

What is 7075 aluminium?

7075 belongs to the 7xxx series, where zinc is the main alloying element. With roughly 5.6 % zinc, 2.5 % magnesium and 1.6 % copper, it forms fine MgZn2-type precipitates during ageing that block dislocation movement and give the alloy its high strength. In the T6 temper its yield strength is about 503 MPa, close to twice that of 6061-T6.

That strength comes with trade-offs that every designer should know. 7075 has lower general corrosion resistance than 5xxx and 6xxx alloys because of its copper content, and in the peak-aged T6 temper it is susceptible to stress-corrosion cracking. It is not suitable for arc welding. It also costs more than the general-purpose grades. Where those limitations are acceptable, it offers a strength-to-weight ratio that few metals can match at its price.

Strengths and limits of 7075

  • Strengths: very high strength, excellent machinability, good fatigue strength, good anodising response, stable at cryogenic temperatures
  • Limits: not fusion-weldable, lower corrosion resistance, SCC risk in T6, limited cold formability, loses strength above about 120 – 150 °C

Working with 7075

Machining, welding, forming and finishing

Machining

Among the best-machining aluminium alloys in T6 and T651: short chips, low built-up edge and fine finishes. Use carbide tools, high speeds and coolant. Choose T651 or T7351 for heavy material removal to limit distortion.

Welding

Not recommended for MIG or TIG welding: hot cracking and a weak heat-affected zone result. Use mechanical fastening, adhesive bonding or, with qualification, friction stir welding.

Forming

Poor cold formability in T6; minimum bend radii are large. Form in the O temper or freshly quenched, then heat-treat. Small bends in T6 need generous radii and testing.

Anodising

Anodises well for protection and hard coatings. Clear anodising gives a slightly yellow-grey tone because of copper; colour matching with 6xxx parts is difficult.

Corrosion protection

Anodise, conversion-coat or paint parts used outdoors or near the sea. Avoid direct contact with carbon fibre or steel without insulation.

Temperature

Prolonged service above about 120 °C over-ages the alloy and reduces strength. Strength and toughness are retained well at low temperatures.

Choose 6061 when you need welding, corrosion resistance and low cost more than strength. Choose 2024 for fatigue-critical sheet structures where crack growth resistance matters. Choose 7075 for the highest strength in machined parts, moulds and sports equipment. Choose 7050 for thick aerospace parts where 7075 would lose strength in the centre or risk stress corrosion. Applications

Where 7075 is used

  • Aerospace: wing and fuselage structure, fittings, landing-gear parts and fasteners (see our 7075 aircraft aluminium guide)
  • Moulds and tooling: blow moulds, injection-mould prototypes and jigs that benefit from fast machining and good hardness
  • Motorsport and automotive: suspension parts, uprights, gears and high-stress brackets
  • Sports and outdoor: bicycle components, climbing hardware, archery and firearm parts, tent poles
  • Defence: structural parts for vehicles and weapon systems
  • Electronics: premium phone frames and device housings where strength allows thinner walls
Selection advice

Common mistakes with 7075

  • Designing a welded structure in 7075; choose 6061, 6082 or 5083 instead
  • Using 7075-T6 in thick parts under sustained stress across the thickness, where T7351 is the safer temper
  • Ordering T6 plate for heavy machining rather than stress-relieved T651, then fighting distortion
  • Leaving 7075 bare in marine or outdoor service without anodising or paint
  • Assuming typical values are guaranteed; design to the specified minimums for the actual thickness

FAQ

7075 aluminium (aluminum): frequently asked questions

How strong is 7075 aluminium?

In the T6 temper, typical tensile strength is about 572 MPa (83 ksi) and yield about 503 MPa (73 ksi). Guaranteed minimums for T651 plate range from 538 MPa down to about 462 MPa as thickness increases to 100 mm.

Is 7075 stronger than 6061?

Yes. 7075-T6 has roughly 1.8 times the yield strength of 6061-T6, but 6061 is weldable, more corrosion resistant and cheaper.

Can you weld 7075?

Not reliably with MIG or TIG; it is prone to hot cracking and loses much of its strength near the weld. Use mechanical fasteners, bonding or qualified friction stir welding.

What is the difference between 7075-T6 and T651?

Strength is the same. T651 is stretched 1.5 – 3 % after quenching to relieve residual stress, so it stays flat during machining. Plate is normally supplied as T651.

Is 7075 corrosion resistant?

Moderately. It is less resistant than 5xxx and 6xxx alloys and should be anodised or painted for outdoor or marine use. The T73 temper greatly improves resistance to stress-corrosion cracking.

What is the European equivalent of 7075?

EN AW-7075 (AlZn5.5MgCu), Werkstoff 3.4365. In Japan it is A7075 (JIS H4000 for sheet and plate), and China uses the designation 7075 in GB/T 3190.
