Honeycomb core guide

5056 Aluminium Honeycomb Core

5056 aluminium honeycomb core is the highest-strength standard aluminium core: expanded from 5% magnesium foil, it carries roughly 15 – 25 % more compression and shear than 5052 core of the same density and resists salt spray better. It is the usual choice for aerospace sandwich panels, naval interiors and energy-absorbing parts. Metline supplies 5056 core in a wide range of cell sizes, densities and forms, unexpanded, expanded or cut to drawing, to customers worldwide.

Overview

What is 5056 honeycomb core?

Honeycomb core is the lightweight middle layer of a sandwich panel. Two thin skins carry bending loads as tension and compression, while the core keeps them apart and carries shear between them. Doubling core thickness roughly quadruples stiffness for a very small weight increase, which is why honeycomb panels are found wherever stiffness per kilogram matters.

5056 core is made from 5056 aluminium foil, an alloy with 4.5 – 5.6 % magnesium. The foil is supplied in a fully hard condition, typically 5056-H39, to give the cell walls maximum strength. Compared with core made from 5052 foil (2.5 % Mg) or 3003 foil, 5056 core offers the highest compression and shear strength of the commercial aluminium cores and noticeably better resistance to salt spray and humid tropical air.

The trade-off is cost and formability. 5056 foil is harder to roll thin and the finished core is slightly less forgiving to form over tight curves, so designers use it where the extra strength or corrosion margin is needed, and choose 5052 or 3003 core for less demanding panels.

When 5056 core is the right choice

  • Panel design is limited by core shear or flatwise compression, not skin strength
  • Service in salt spray, on board ship or in hot, humid climates
  • Aerospace parts that need anodised, primed core and qualification to AMS-C-7438
  • Crash and energy-absorbing parts that need a high, steady crush strength
Manufacture

How 5056 honeycomb core is produced

  1. Foil preparationThin 5056 foil, commonly 0.0007 – 0.004 in (about 18 – 100 µm), is cleaned and given a corrosion-protective treatment such as phosphoric acid anodising or a conversion coating.
  2. Node linesParallel lines of adhesive are printed on the foil. Their spacing sets the cell size.
  3. Stacking and curingSheets are stacked with the adhesive lines offset by half a pitch and cured under heat and pressure to form a solid block (the unexpanded "HOBE" block).
  4. SlicingThe block is sliced to the core thickness (T direction) required.
  5. ExpansionEach slice is pulled open in the W direction, forming hexagonal cells. Over-expanding gives rectangular OX cells for easier curving.
  6. FinishingThe expanded core is trimmed, inspected for cell size and density and cut or machined to drawing.

High-density cores made from thick foil can also be produced by corrugating each sheet before bonding, which avoids the very high force needed to expand thick foil. Either way, the result has three principal directions: L (the ribbon direction, along the continuous foil), W (the expansion direction) and T (the thickness). Shear strength in L is typically about 1.5 – 1.8 times that in W, so panels should be laid out with the L direction along the main shear load.

Configurations

5056 honeycomb core configurations

Beyond plain hexagonal core, 5056 foil can be formed into several cell geometries and part forms. Match the type to the panel shape and load direction.

Hexagonal core

The standard geometry with the most predictable properties. Suits flat or gently curved panels such as floors, bulkheads and equipment decks.

Over-expanded (OX) core

Cells pulled into an elongated, near-rectangular shape. Curves easily in the W direction, with higher W shear and lower L shear than hexagonal core.

Flexible core

A special cell shape that bends to tight radii and compound curves without anticlastic distortion, for fairings, ducts and leading edges.

Reinforced core

Extra foil bonded along the ribbons (double-wall or bonded strips) to raise local strength for hard points, insert zones and heavy loads.

Micro-cell core

Very small cells (1/16 in class) support thin skins and prevent dimpling, giving a smooth surface for precision panels and mirrors.

Perforated core

Small holes in the cell walls let air escape during bonding and allow venting or pressure equalisation in service.

Pre-crushed core

Core compressed slightly in the T direction to remove the initial strength peak, giving a flat crush curve for energy absorbers.

Block and machined core

Thick expanded blocks milled to contour, chamfered or pocketed to drawing, ready for bonding.

Preformed core

Core heat-formed or shaped to a specific curvature before lay-up, used for cabin walls, radomes and shells.

Corrosion protection

Surface treatments and corrosion protection

Honeycomb panels fail far more often from corrosion at the bond line than from overload, so foil treatment matters. 5056 already resists corrosion better than 5052, but aerospace and marine panels normally add one of these treatments:

Phosphoric acid anodising

Creates a porous oxide that adhesives key into strongly and that resists moisture. The usual treatment for structural aerospace core, often followed by a corrosion-inhibiting primer.

Conversion coating

Chromate or chromium-free conversion coatings give good corrosion protection and paint adhesion at lower cost for industrial and marine panels.

Primer and coatings

Corrosion-inhibiting primer applied over anodised or converted foil, for panels in wet or salt-laden service.

Also design out moisture paths: seal panel edges, use potted inserts, and add a glass-fibre isolation ply between aluminium core and carbon-fibre skins to prevent galvanic corrosion.

If weight is critical and loads are moderate, 5052 core of slightly higher density may match a lighter 5056 core at lower cost; compare on strength per kilogram, not grade alone. Non-metallic aramid core is the alternative where electrical insulation, low thermal conductivity or very tight curvature matter more than strength.

Selection guide

How to specify 5056 honeycomb core

  1. Define the loadsCalculate the core shear stress and flatwise compression from panel span, load and skin thickness, with a safety factor.
  2. Pick densityChoose the lightest density whose minimum shear and compression (not typical values) exceed the design stresses.
  3. Pick cell sizeSmaller cells support thin skins and prevent dimpling; larger cells reduce cost at the same density.
  4. Orient the corePut the L direction along the higher shear load; specify OX or flexible core for curved panels.
  5. Specify treatment and testsAnodising or coating, perforation, and the test standard (AMS-C-7438, ASTM C273, C365, C297).

Common mistakes

  • Designing to typical values instead of specified minimums
  • Ignoring the 40 % or so lower shear strength in the W direction
  • Bonding aluminium core directly to carbon-fibre skins without an isolation ply
  • Leaving panel edges unsealed in wet service

FAQ

5056 aluminium (aluminum) honeycomb core: FAQ

How much stronger is 5056 core than 5052 core?

At the same cell size and density, 5056 core is typically about 15 – 25 % stronger in compression and shear, because its foil contains roughly twice the magnesium. It also resists salt spray better.

What do the L, W and T directions mean?

L is the ribbon direction along the continuous foil, W is the direction in which the block was expanded, and T is the core thickness. Shear strength is highest in L, roughly 1.5 – 1.8 times that in W.

Can 5056 honeycomb core be curved?

Hexagonal core bends in one direction but forms a saddle shape over compound curves. For curved panels, order over-expanded (OX) core, flexible core or preformed core.

Which skins can be bonded to 5056 core?

Aluminium, titanium, stainless steel, glass-fibre and carbon-fibre skins are all used. With carbon-fibre skins add a glass-fibre isolation ply to avoid galvanic corrosion of the aluminium core.

Why buy unexpanded core?

Unexpanded blocks or slices take up a small fraction of the volume of expanded core, which cuts freight cost for large orders. You then expand the core in your own facility.

Which test reports can be supplied?

Density, cell size and thickness reports, plus compression and shear test results to ASTM C365 and C273 where specified, with lot traceability. Tell us the specification, for example AMS-C-7438, when you enquire.
