Electrical wire rod guide

1080, 1070 and 1050 Aluminium Wire Rod for Electrical Use

1080, 1070 and 1050 wire rod are high-purity aluminium coils, usually 9.5 – 25 mm in diameter, that wire drawers reduce to conductors, winding wire and thermal spray wire. Purity rises from 99.5 % (1050) to 99.8 % (1080), and conductivity rises with it, from about 61 % to about 62 – 63 % IACS, while strength falls slightly. This guide explains the differences and what to specify.

Overview

What is high-purity electrical wire rod?

Wire rod is the semi-finished feedstock for wire drawing. Most electrical aluminium rod is made by continuous casting and rolling: molten metal is cast into a bar on a wheel and immediately hot-rolled to a round rod, typically 9.5 mm, which is coiled in one continuous length. Larger diameters are also produced by extrusion.

The electrical cable industry mainly uses 1350 (EC grade) rod, whose chemistry is tuned for conductivity. The plain high-purity grades 1050, 1070 and 1080 are used where a buyer's standard calls for them, for products that are not covered by conductor specifications, or where very high purity is wanted, for example in fine winding wire, thermal spray wire and some cable and conductor standards in Europe and Asia.

Because conductivity rises with purity, 1080 is the most conductive of the three and 1050 the least. The reverse is true of strength: impurities such as iron and silicon add a little strength, so 1080 is the softest. A common misconception is that 1080 is stronger than 1050; in the same temper, it is not.

Applications

What the rod is drawn into

  • Conductors: wires for stranded conductors (AAC and, with steel or alloy cores, ACSR and ACAR) where the buyer's standard allows these grades
  • Insulated cables: low- and medium-voltage cable cores and aerial bundled cable
  • Winding wire: round and flat enamelled or paper-covered wire for transformers and motors
  • Thermal spray wire: 99.5 – 99.8 % aluminium wire for arc and flame spraying of corrosion-protective coatings on steel
  • Earthing and connectors: earthing wire, terminals and cold-formed connector parts

FAQ

1080, 1070 and 1050 wire rod: FAQ

Which is more conductive, 1080 or 1050?

1080. With at least 99.80 % aluminium it reaches about 62 – 63 % IACS annealed, against about 61 % for 1050.

Is 1080 aluminium stronger than 1050?

No. Higher purity makes aluminium softer, so in the same temper 1080 has slightly lower strength than 1050.

What diameter is electrical aluminum wire rod?

The standard continuously cast and rolled size is 9.5 mm. Rod from about 7.5 mm to 25 mm is available for different drawing lines and products.

Should I use 1350 or 1070 for cable?

1350 is the standard electrical conductor grade and is named in most conductor specifications. 1070 has similar conductivity and is used where a buyer or national standard specifies it.

Can the rod be used for thermal spray wire?

Yes. 1050, 1070 and 1080 are drawn into spray wire for aluminium coatings that protect steel from corrosion; higher purity gives a cleaner coating.
