Precision tube guide

High-Precision, High-Strength Rectangular Aluminium Tube

A high-precision rectangular tube is a hollow aluminium section held to tighter size, wall, twist and corner-radius limits than ordinary structural tube, usually in a strong heat-treatable or high-magnesium alloy. This guide explains how such tube is made, which alloys and tempers to specify, what "precision" actually means in tolerance terms, and how to order it. Overview

What makes a rectangular tube "high precision"?

Most rectangular aluminium tube is bought for frames and handrails, where a millimetre of variation does not matter. Precision tube is different: it is specified when the outside or inside faces locate other parts, slide in guides, carry seals, or must be stacked and joined without shimming.

Precision is not one property but a bundle of controlled characteristics: outside width and height, wall thickness and its uniformity around the section, corner radii (inside and outside), flatness of each face (convexity and concavity), squareness of adjacent sides, twist along the length, straightness, and length after cutting. Ordinary extruded tube meets the standard tolerance tables for these; precision tube is held to a fraction of them, either by cold drawing after extrusion, by straightening and stretching more carefully, or by machining critical faces.

"High strength" usually means the tube is made in a heat-treatable alloy (2024, 6061, 7020, 7075) or a high-magnesium alloy (5083, or 5A06 in Chinese GB designations), rather than the soft 6063 used for architectural sections. The two demands pull in opposite directions, because the harder the alloy, the more difficult it is to extrude thin walls and sharp corners. That trade-off is the central engineering decision when you specify this product.

Typical reasons to specify precision rectangular tube

  • Telescoping or nested sections that must slide with a controlled clearance
  • Guide rails, linear-motion housings and machine frames where faces act as datums
  • Waveguide-like enclosures, battery and electronics housings with sealed lids
  • Aerospace and defence structures that need high strength-to-weight and repeatable fit
  • Hydraulic or pneumatic bodies with a rectangular bore
How it is made

Seamless, porthole and cold-drawn rectangular tube

The way a rectangular tube is produced decides how strong, how accurate and how expensive it is. There are three main routes, and many precision tubes combine two of them.

RouteHow it worksPrecisionAlloysBest for
Porthole (bridge-die) extrusionBillet splits around a mandrel bridge and re-welds inside the dieStandard extrusion tolerances6063, 6061, 6082, 6005A, 3003, some 7020Most commercial tube; long lengths at low cost
Seamless extrusionHollow billet extruded over a mandrel; no weld seamsStandard, with better wall uniformity in strong alloys2024, 5083, 7075, 6061 and all othersPressure-bearing and high-strength tube; required by aerospace specs
Cold drawingExtruded tube pulled through a die over a plug or mandrel, often in several passesTight: typically several times tighter than extruded1100, 3003, 5052, 6061, 2024, 7075Small and medium sections where size and surface matter
Machined from extrusion or plateFaces milled or bore machined after extrusionVery tight, local to machined facesAnyShort parts with datum faces or very thick walls

Porthole tube contains longitudinal weld lines where the metal re-joins. In 6xxx alloys these welds are sound and invisible after anodising in most cases, but some aerospace and pressure specifications (for example ASTM B241 for seamless pipe and tube) require seamless material. The high-strength 2xxx, 7075 and high-magnesium 5xxx alloys generally do not weld well in a porthole die, so they are produced seamless.

Cold drawing work-hardens the metal and sizes it accurately. For heat-treatable alloys the tube is then solution treated and aged (for example to T6) and straightened, so the final tolerances depend on how well distortion from quenching is controlled. Very large sections, such as the 300 × 120 mm and larger tubes used for launcher supports and military shelters, are extruded seamless on large presses and cannot be drawn.

7020-T6 (EN AW-AlZn4,5Mg1) is a weldable Al-Zn-Mg alloy common in European structural and defence tube; its EN 755-2 minimums sit well above 6082-T6. Chinese GB grades sometimes appear on drawings: 2A12 is the near equivalent of 2024, 7A04 belongs to the 7075-type Al-Zn-Mg-Cu family, and 5A06 is an Al-Mg alloy with about 6 % magnesium that has no exact ASTM or EN counterpart. Need strength and welding

5083 or 7020. 5083 keeps most of its strength in the weld zone; 7020 partly recovers strength by natural ageing after welding.

Need strength and machining

6061-T6 for general parts; 2024-T3 or 7075-T6 where weight is critical and the tube is mechanically fastened.

Need surface and accuracy

6063-T6 drawn or extruded tube gives the cleanest anodised finish and the sharpest corners.

Tolerances

Which tolerances define a precision tube?

In North America, extruded and drawn tube tolerances come from ANSI H35.2 (published by the Aluminum Association) and are invoked by ASTM B221, B210 and B241. In Europe, EN 755-7 and EN 755-8 cover seamless and porthole extruded tube, and EN 754-7 and EN 754-8 cover the cold-drawn equivalents; ISO 6362 and ISO 6363 follow the same structure. A precision order states which limits must be tighter than these tables, and by how much.

CharacteristicWhat it controlsHow precision is achieved
Width and heightFit into mating parts and fixturesCold drawing, or tight die design and press control
Wall thickness and eccentricityStrength, weight and bore sizeSeamless extrusion over a fixed mandrel, then drawing
Corner radiiClearance for nested parts and sealingDie design; sharp inside corners are easier in soft alloys
Convexity / concavityFlatness of each faceDrawing, controlled quench, roller straightening
TwistRotation of the section along its lengthStretching after quench; measured over a set length
Straightness (bow)Deviation along the lengthStretch straightening, roller straightening
Squareness of sides and cut endsAssembly accuracyDie accuracy; precision saw or CNC cutting
LengthReady-to-assemble partsCNC sawing to ±0.1 – 0.5 mm, or machining of ends

Drawn rectangular tube is typically held to a small fraction of the extruded size tolerance, and its surface is smoother because the die burnishes the outside. As a rule of thumb, ask for drawn tube when outside dimensions must be held within a few hundredths of a millimetre on small sections, and for seamless extruded tube with machined faces when the section is large or the alloy is too strong to draw.

Write precision limits on the drawing as numbers, not as words such as "high precision". State which faces are critical, how twist and straightness will be measured (over the full length or per metre), and whether the inside dimension or the outside dimension is the controlled one. That avoids rejected deliveries and keeps cost down, because only the features that matter are tightened.

Fabrication

Machining, joining and surface finishing

Machining

2024, 6061 and 7075 in T-tempers cut cleanly with sharp carbide tools. Clamp thin-wall tube with internal support to stop chatter and distortion.

Welding

5083 and 6061 weld by MIG and TIG; 7020 is weldable with 5xxx filler. 2024 and 7075 are not considered weldable for structures and should be bolted, riveted or bonded.

Bending

Rectangular tube wrinkles on the inside of a bend. Bend in soft temper (O or T4) with mandrels and age afterwards where the alloy allows.

Anodising

6063 gives the most uniform appearance. 2024 and 7075 anodise to a darker, less even colour; hard anodising suits wear faces.

Coating

Powder, PVDF and electrophoretic coatings give colour and UV resistance for outdoor and architectural use.

Corrosion

5083 and 6xxx tube resist marine and industrial atmospheres; 2024 and 7075 need anodising, primer or sealing in humid service.

Applications

Where precision rectangular tube is used

  • Aerospace and defence: airframe secondary structure, seat tracks, launcher and antenna supports, shelter frames
  • Automotive and rail: crash-management members, battery enclosure frames, roof rails, seat structures
  • Machinery and automation: gantry beams, linear guide housings, telescopic arms, measurement frames
  • Hydraulics and pneumatics: rectangular-bore cylinder bodies and manifolds machined from thick-wall tube
  • Electrical and electronics: enclosures, rack frames, heat-spreading housings
  • Optics and cameras: lightweight rigid supports and lens-mount bodies
  • Marine: 5083 brackets, rails and frames that stay in salt-laden air
  • Building systems: reinforcement profiles and high-load mullion sections supplied as tube to fabricators
Ordering

How to specify and order

  1. Define the sectionOutside dimensions, wall, corner radii inside and outside, and which dimension (inside or outside) is controlled.
  2. Choose alloy, temper and routeFor example 6061-T6 drawn, 2024-T3 seamless, or 5083-O extruded. State if porthole tube is acceptable.
  3. List critical tolerancesSize, wall, twist, straightness, flatness and length, with the measuring method.
  4. Name the standardASTM B221, B210, B241, EN 755 or EN 754, plus any customer or aerospace specification.
  5. Agree inspection and documentsDimensional report, EN 10204 3.1 mill certificate, ultrasonic or eddy-current testing where needed.
  6. Confirm deliveryCut lengths, protective film or interleaving, export cases, and EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.

Common mistakes include asking for sharp inside corners in 7075 (a radius of at least the wall thickness is far easier and stronger), over-specifying tolerances on non-functional faces, and forgetting that heat treatment after drawing can move dimensions unless straightening is included.

FAQ

Precision rectangular aluminium tube: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between drawn and extruded rectangular tube?

Extruded tube is pushed through a die once and meets the standard extrusion tolerance tables. Drawn tube is extruded first and then pulled through a sizing die over a mandrel, which tightens size and wall tolerances, improves the surface and adds strength through cold work.

Which alloy gives the strongest rectangular tube?

7075-T6 has the highest common strength (typical tensile about 570 MPa), followed by 2024-T3. Both are hard to weld, so 7020 or 5083 is chosen when the structure must be welded.

Is seamless rectangular tube better than porthole tube?

Seamless tube has no extrusion weld lines, so it is required for many pressure, aerospace and high-strength applications and is the only route for alloys such as 2024 and 7075. For 6063 and 6061 in general structural use, porthole tube is sound and cheaper.

How tight can rectangular tube tolerances be?

Drawn small sections can be held to a few hundredths of a millimetre on outside size. Large or high-strength sections are extruded to standard tolerances and critical faces are machined. Specify numeric limits for each critical feature.

Can you supply large rectangular aluminium tube?

Yes. Large seamless square and rectangular sections with thick walls are produced to order for defence, rail and machinery projects; feasibility depends on alloy, wall and section size, so send the drawing for review.

Do you supply GB grades such as 2A12 or 5A06?

We can supply to the drawing grade or offer the nearest ASTM/EN grade (for example 2024 for 2A12) with a mill certificate showing the actual chemistry and properties.
