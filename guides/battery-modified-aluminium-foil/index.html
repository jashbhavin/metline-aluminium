Modified Aluminium Foil for Batteries

Modified battery foil is aluminium current-collector foil whose surface has been coated, perforated or etched to lower interface resistance, improve electrode adhesion or protect the foil from corrosion. It is used on the cathode side of lithium-ion cells and in supercapacitors. This guide compares the main types, explains which suits LFP, NCM, high-rate and capacitor designs, and lists what to specify when buying.

Overview

What is modified aluminium foil for batteries?

In a lithium-ion cell, the cathode active material (LFP, NCM, NCA, LCO or LMO) is coated as a slurry onto a thin aluminium foil, the current collector. The foil carries electrons in and out of the electrode and supports the coating mechanically. Plain rolled foil works, but its natural oxide film and smooth surface create contact resistance and limit how well the electrode sticks. "Modified" foil changes that surface.

The most common modification is a thin conductive carbon primer, usually a few hundred nanometres to a few micrometres thick per side, made from carbon black, graphite, carbon nanotubes or graphene in a binder. Other routes perforate the foil to make it lighter and allow ions to pass through, or etch it chemically or electrochemically to increase the real surface area. Each one addresses a different limitation, so the right choice depends on the cell chemistry and the performance target.

Why aluminium on the cathode? It is light, conductive and cheap, and in common carbonate electrolytes with LiPF6 it forms a stable passive film at high cathode potentials. On the anode side of a graphite cell aluminium would alloy with lithium at low potential, so copper foil is used there instead. The exception is cells with lithium titanate (LTO) anodes, which operate at about 1.55 V versus lithium, where aluminium foil can be used on both electrodes.

What modification aims to achieve

  • Lower contact (interface) resistance between active material and foil, for better rate capability and less heat
  • Stronger adhesion of the electrode coating, allowing less binder and fewer delamination defects
  • Protection of the aluminium surface against corrosion by electrolyte at high voltage or by aggressive salts
  • Lower collector weight, or better ion transport, in perforated and porous designs

Substrate foil

Choosing the substrate alloy and thickness

The coating is only as good as the foil underneath. Battery foil is rolled from high-purity 1xxx aluminium to the full-hard H18 temper so that it is strong enough to survive coating, calendering and winding at high line speeds.

AlloyAluminium contentCharacteristicsWhere it fits
1235≥ 99.35 %Economical, good conductivity, widely rolled to thin gaugesConsumer and general-purpose cells
1060≥ 99.60 %Higher conductivity and purityPower and storage cells
1070≥ 99.70 %Higher purity still, low resistivityHigh-rate and power cells
1100≥ 99.00 %Small copper addition gives slightly higher strengthCells where foil strength during processing matters
1085 / higher purity≥ 99.85 %Very high purity for low resistance and clean surfacesSupercapacitors and specialised cells

Typical cathode foil thicknesses run from about 10 to 20 µm, with 12 – 15 µm common in electric-vehicle cells; thinner foil saves weight and cost but needs higher strength and cleaner rolling to avoid breaks. Key substrate properties to specify are thickness tolerance, tensile strength and elongation in H18, pinhole count, surface wettability (dyne level), flatness and edge-wave, and freedom from oil residues that would spoil coating adhesion. Selection

Which modified foil for which battery?

LFP cells

LFP has low electronic conductivity and small particles that are harder to bond, so carbon-coated foil is widely used to reduce interface resistance and improve adhesion, especially in power and fast-charge cells.

NCM / NCA cells

High cathode voltages make aluminium corrosion and interface stability more important. Carbon or functional primer coatings are used for high-rate and high-voltage designs; plain foil is common in energy-optimised cells.

High-rate and fast-charge cells

The aim is the lowest possible internal resistance and heat, so thin, highly conductive carbon, graphene or CNT primers are the usual choice.

LTO anodes

Aluminium can serve as the anode collector in LTO cells, saving weight and cost compared with copper, often with a carbon primer.

Supercapacitors (EDLC)

Etched or carbon-coated high-purity foil gives good contact with activated-carbon electrodes and low equivalent series resistance.

Lithium-ion capacitors

Perforated foil allows lithium pre-doping through the collector stack; carbon priming improves contact.

Electrolyte salt matters too. LiPF6 forms a protective fluoride-rich layer on aluminium, but imide salts such as LiTFSI and, to a lesser degree, LiFSI can corrode bare aluminium at high potential. Cells that use these salts, or that run above about 4.3 V, are where protective coatings earn their cost.

Specification

What to put in a modified foil specification

Specification checklist

Substrate
Alloy, temper (H18), thickness and tolerance, tensile strength and elongation, pinholes
Coating
Type (carbon black, graphite, graphene, CNT, primer), single or double side, coat weight per side (g/m²) and tolerance
Coated width
Coated width and position, uncoated margins for tab welding, and total slit width
Performance
Surface or through-thickness resistance, peel strength with your electrode, and electrolyte soak stability
Surface
Coverage, absence of streaks, pinholes and agglomerates; wettability of uncoated areas
Roll format
Core inner diameter (76 mm is common), outer diameter or roll length, joints allowed per roll
Packing
Vacuum or moisture-barrier bags with desiccant, clean-room compatible wrapping, pallet orientation

Agree the test methods along with the values. Resistance results depend strongly on probe type and pressure, and peel strength depends on the electrode used in the test. The most useful acceptance test is a short run on your own coating line followed by cell-level resistance and cycling checks.

Handling

Storage and handling of coated foil

  • Keep it dry: store sealed rolls in a cool, dry room and open them only in the coating area; many carbon primers absorb moisture.
  • Acclimatise before opening: let rolls reach room temperature in their packaging to avoid condensation.
  • Avoid dust and contact damage: unpack in a clean environment and lift by the core, never by the foil surface.
  • Use first-in, first-out: coated foil has a recommended use-by period; confirm it with the supplier.
  • Protect the uncoated margins: the bare strips are later used for tab welding and must stay clean and oxide-free.
Pitfalls

Common mistakes when buying modified foil

  • Comparing datasheets, not cells: conductivity and peel figures measured by different methods cannot be compared; test candidates on the same electrode and cell format.
  • Ignoring the substrate: a good coating on foil with oil residue, pinholes or poor flatness still causes coating defects and breaks.
  • Over-specifying coat weight: a heavier carbon layer adds cost and thickness without always lowering resistance further.
  • Forgetting the tab margin: coated lanes must leave clean bare aluminium where tabs are welded, or weld quality suffers.
  • Poor storage on site: opening rolls in humid air or leaving them unsealed between shifts can degrade adhesion.

FAQ

Modified aluminium (aluminum) battery foil: FAQ

Why is carbon-coated aluminium foil used in lithium-ion batteries?

The carbon layer lowers the contact resistance between the cathode coating and the foil, improves adhesion so less binder is needed, and shields the aluminium from electrolyte attack. The result is lower internal resistance, less heat at high current and more consistent cells.

Why is aluminium used for the cathode and copper for the anode?

Aluminium is stable at the high potential of the cathode because it forms a passive film, but at the low potential of a graphite anode it alloys with lithium. Copper does not, so it is used on the anode. With LTO anodes, which work at a higher potential, aluminium can be used on both sides.

Which modified foil suits LFP and which suits NCM?

LFP cells benefit most from carbon-coated foil because LFP has low electronic conductivity and fine particles. NCM and NCA cells use carbon or functional primer coatings mainly for high-rate or high-voltage designs; energy-focused NCM cells often use plain foil.

What thickness is battery current-collector foil?

Cathode foil is typically 10 – 20 µm thick, with 12 – 15 µm common in electric-vehicle cells. The coating adds well under a few micrometres per side.

Can you supply single-side or patterned coatings?

Yes. Coatings can be applied to one or both sides, and coated widths can be arranged in lanes with uncoated margins for tab welding. Specify the pattern on a drawing.

What packing is used for coated foil?

Rolls are sealed in moisture-barrier or vacuum bags with desiccant, supported on their cores in cartons or wooden cases, and labelled with lot data for traceability.
