Unexpanded Aluminium Honeycomb Core

Unexpanded aluminium honeycomb is the bonded stack of foil, supplied as blocks or thin slices before it is pulled open into hexagonal cells. It takes a small fraction of the volume of expanded core, so it ships and stores far more cheaply, and panel makers expand it on their own lines to the exact size they need. This guide covers alloys, specifications, the expansion step and when unexpanded supply makes sense.

Overview

What is unexpanded honeycomb core?

Most aluminium honeycomb is made by the expansion process. Sheets of foil are printed with parallel lines of adhesive, called node lines, offset by half a pitch on alternate sheets. The sheets are stacked and cured under heat and pressure into a solid-looking block. When the block is later pulled apart at right angles to the sheets, the unbonded sections open up and form hexagonal cells.

The block in its bonded but unopened state is called unexpanded core, sometimes abbreviated HOBE (honeycomb before expansion). It can be supplied as a full block or sliced across the node lines into thin pieces whose thickness equals the final core thickness of the panel. Because the cells are still closed, an unexpanded slice is a compact, flat stack of foil that occupies only a small part of the volume of the same core once expanded.

Unexpanded core suits manufacturers who have their own expansion and panel lines: sandwich-panel producers, rail and vehicle interior makers, aerospace subcontractors and companies that make filters, air-flow straighteners and energy absorbers.

Why buyers choose unexpanded core

  • Much lower freight and warehouse cost, because air is not being shipped
  • Expansion to the exact length and cell geometry needed for each job
  • Less damage in transit compared with fragile expanded core
  • Better control over core preparation inside the buyer's own process
Forms

Unexpanded supply forms

Unexpanded blocks

Full cured blocks for buyers who slice and expand in-house. Blocks give the most flexibility to cut different core thicknesses from one stock.

Unexpanded slices

Blocks pre-cut to the core thickness required, ready to expand. This is the most common form for panel producers, since no slicing equipment is needed.

Custom-cut core

Slices cut to agreed length, width and thickness, in a chosen alloy, foil gauge and cell size, with perforated foil where venting is required.

Perforated foil is specified when the core will be used in vacuum bagging, aerospace or space structures, or bonded panels that must vent trapped air and moisture during curing and in service. Non-perforated core is standard for most building and transport panels.

Core is usually identified by alloy, cell size, foil thickness and density, for example "5052, 6.35 mm cell, 0.05 mm foil". Mechanical properties such as flatwise compressive strength and plate shear strength in the L (ribbon) and W directions are tested on expanded core to ASTM C365 and C273, density to ASTM C271 and node bond strength to ASTM C363.

Processing

How unexpanded core is expanded

  1. Prepare the sliceCheck the ribbon (L) direction, which runs parallel to the foil sheets, and the expansion (W) direction at right angles to them.
  2. Grip the edgesClamp or pin both long edges of the slice on an expansion frame or table, keeping the grip even along the full length.
  3. Pull to sizeExpand steadily in the W direction until cells reach the specified shape. Over-expansion turns cells rectangular; under-expansion leaves them elongated.
  4. Set the shapeHold the core at size; some processes use a light stretch-set or a frame so the core does not spring back before bonding.
  5. Trim and bondTrim the expanded core to panel size and bond it to the face sheets with film or liquid adhesive under pressure.

Even cell geometry is the main quality target, because distorted cells change the shear and compressive properties of the core. The expansion ratio and final cell shape should match the design values the panel was engineered for.

If you do not have expansion equipment, buy expanded core or finished panels from a panel fabricator. If you produce panels in volume, unexpanded supply usually gives the lowest delivered cost per square metre of core.

Applications

Where unexpanded core ends up

  • Sandwich panels: aluminium-faced panels for building facades, ceilings, partitions and clean rooms
  • Aerospace: floor panels, interiors, flaps and fairings, often in 5052 or 5056 to AMS-C-7438
  • Rail and road transport: lightweight floors, walls and doors for trains, buses and trucks
  • Marine: bulkheads, decks and furniture panels in yachts and ferries
  • Energy absorbers: crash pads and impact attenuators using controlled crushing of the core
  • Air-flow products: flow straighteners, vent panels and filters

FAQ

Unexpanded aluminium (aluminum) honeycomb core: frequently asked questions

What is an unexpanded aluminium honeycomb core?

It is a cured block or slice of foil sheets bonded along offset node lines, supplied before being pulled open into hexagonal cells. The buyer expands it to the required size.

What is the difference between unexpanded and expanded core?

Unexpanded core is compact and cheap to ship but must be expanded before use; expanded core is ready to bond but bulky and more easily damaged in transit.

Do I need special equipment to expand the core?

Yes. You need an expansion frame or table that grips the slice evenly and pulls it to the correct cell shape. Without one, buy expanded core instead.

What are common specifications?

Cell sizes of about 3 – 25 mm, foil of 0.03 – 0.1 mm, expanded densities of about 20 – 130 kg/m³ and slice thicknesses of about 5 – 200 mm, in 3003, 5052 or 5056 alloy.

Can unexpanded core be customised?

Yes. Alloy, foil gauge, cell size, perforation and slice dimensions can be specified to match your panel design and expansion line.

Which alloy should I choose?

3003 for general building and decorative panels, 5052 for transport, marine and most structural uses, and 5056 where the highest core strength is needed, such as aerospace.
