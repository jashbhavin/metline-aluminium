6061 Aluminium: Product Forms, Specifications and Selection

6061 is the most widely used heat-treatable aluminium alloy, supplied as plate, sheet, bar, tube, extrusions, forgings, coil and wire. Most buyers order it in T6 or one of its stress-relieved variants (T651 for plate and rolled bar, T6511 for extrusions), with a typical tensile strength of 310 MPa and yield of 276 MPa. This guide maps each product form to its standards and tempers, summarises the properties, and explains when 6061 is the right choice and when another alloy will do better.

Overview

What is 6061 aluminium?

6061 is an aluminium-magnesium-silicon alloy with small additions of copper and chromium. Heat treatment dissolves magnesium and silicon and then precipitates them as fine Mg2Si particles, which gives the alloy its strength. It was introduced in the mid-1930s and has been the general-engineering aluminium ever since.

Its popularity comes from balance rather than any single property. It is strong enough for structures and machine parts, machines well, welds with ordinary MIG and TIG procedures, resists atmospheric corrosion, anodises evenly and is produced in every major product form by mills around the world. That availability matters: a designer who specifies 6061 can buy plate, bar, tube and extrusion in the same alloy and temper family from many sources, with consistent certification.

Its limits are just as clear. It is not as strong as 2xxx or 7xxx aerospace alloys, it loses a large part of its T6 strength in the heat-affected zone of a weld, and for permanent seawater immersion the 5xxx marine grades corrode less. Knowing those limits is the key to choosing it well.

If a part will be machined heavily, order a stress-relieved temper (T651, T6511 or T652). Ordinary T6 bar or plate can move as internal quench stresses are released when material is removed.

Fabrication

Machining, welding, forming and finishing 6061

Machining

Good in T6/T651: short chips and a bright finish. Use carbide tools, high speeds and coolant. Annealed material is gummy and machines poorly.

Welding

MIG/TIG with ER4043 (crack resistance, post-weld heat treatment) or ER5356 (higher fillet strength, better anodised colour match). Design codes take welded 6061-T6 at about 165 MPa tensile in the heat-affected zone.

Forming

Bend T6 to about 1.5 – 3 × thickness; form tighter in O or T4 and then age.

Anodising

Clear, dyed and hard anodising all work well, giving an even appearance.

Corrosion

Good in atmospheric and fresh-water service; anodise or paint for marine exposure, or use 5083 for immersed parts.

Temperature

Useful strength to about 100 – 150 °C; long exposure above that over-ages the alloy. Good toughness at cryogenic temperatures.

Applications

Where 6061 is used

  • Machinery and automation: frames, base plates, fixtures, linear-motion parts
  • Transport: truck and trailer parts, rail interiors, EV battery trays and brackets, bicycle frames
  • Marine: deck fittings, masts, brackets and superstructure parts above the waterline
  • Aerospace and defence: fittings, ground-support equipment and secondary structure to AMS specifications
  • Construction: structural shapes, handrails, bridge components and connection plates
  • Electronics: housings, heat sinks and semiconductor-equipment chambers
  • Energy: solar mounting structures and pressure-vessel components

FAQ

6061 aluminium (aluminum): frequently asked questions

What is 6061 aluminium used for?

Machine parts, frames and fixtures, vehicle and trailer components, marine fittings, structural shapes, bicycle frames, electronics housings and aerospace fittings. It is the general-purpose structural aluminium.

What is the difference between 6061-T6, T651 and T6511?

All three have the same strength. T651 (plate and rolled bar) and T6511 (extrusions) are stretched after quenching to relieve internal stress, so they stay stable when machined.

Is 6061 stronger than 6063?

Yes. 6061-T6 has a typical yield of 276 MPa against 214 MPa for 6063-T6. 6063 extrudes more easily and gives a better surface finish.

Can 6061 aluminum be welded?

Yes, by MIG or TIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. The weld zone loses part of its T6 strength, which design codes account for, unless the assembly is re-heat-treated.

What is the European equivalent of 6061?

EN AW-6061 (AlMg1SiCu, 3.3211). Many European designs use 6082, which has similar properties and wider local availability.

Does 6061 rust or corrode?

Aluminium does not rust. 6061 forms a protective oxide and resists atmospheric corrosion well; anodising or painting is recommended for marine or industrial atmospheres.
