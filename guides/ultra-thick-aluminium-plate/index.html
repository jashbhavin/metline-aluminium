Ultra-Thick Aluminium Plate: 50 to 600 mm

Ultra-thick aluminium plate covers sections from about 50 mm up to 600 mm, used for moulds, machine bases, vacuum chambers and aerospace parts machined from solid. Above roughly 150 – 200 mm the choice of production route (rolled, cast or forged) and of stress-relieved temper matters as much as the alloy. This guide explains how to choose and what to check.

Overview

What is ultra-thick aluminium plate?

Most rolled plate is ordered between 6 and 100 mm. "Ultra-thick" or "heavy gauge" plate starts where standard stock ranges thin out, around 50 – 100 mm, and continues into blocks several hundred millimetres thick. At these sizes, a single slab replaces bolted or welded stacks, and a large part can be machined in one setup from one piece of metal.

The challenge is metallurgical. A thick plate must be rolled from a very large ingot with enough reduction to give wrought properties at its centre, then quenched fast enough that the core still reaches the required strength, then stress-relieved so it does not move when machined. Each of these steps gets harder as thickness increases, which is why properties, available alloys and prices all change across the thickness range.

Typical reasons to buy ultra-thick plate

  • Large injection, blow and rubber moulds machined from a single block
  • Machine beds, gantries and vacuum-chamber bodies where flatness and stability are critical
  • Aerospace frames, bulkheads and fittings hogged out of thick 7050 or 7075 plate
  • Fixture plates, semiconductor equipment bases and heavy tooling

If your part needs strength and fatigue resistance, specify rolled plate as far as the alloy's standard thickness range allows and move to a forged block beyond it. If the priority is that a mould or jig stays flat, cast plate is often the better and cheaper choice. Our large forgings guide and forged plate page cover the forged route in detail.

Two physical constants stay the same at any thickness: 6061 and other 6xxx plate weighs 2.70 kg per m² per millimetre, while 7075 is denser at about 2.81. Thermal conductivity of thick 6061-T651 is around 167 W/m·K, which is why it is popular for temperature-controlled moulds and platens.

Stress relief

Why the temper matters more in thick plate

T651 / T7451 / T351

Stretched 1.5 – 3 % after quenching to relieve residual stress. The standard condition for rolled heat-treated plate that will be machined. Stretching becomes difficult in very large cross-sections.

T652 / T7452

Stress-relieved by compression instead of stretching. Used for forged slabs and some very thick products where a stretcher cannot pull the section.

T6 (no stress relief)

Same strength as T651 but with high locked-in quench stress. Thick T6 plate can bow or twist when machined, so avoid it for precision parts.

H112

As-fabricated temper for non-heat-treatable grades such as 5083 and 5052; low residual stress, moderate strength.

O (annealed)

Softest condition with the lowest internal stress; used where the block will be heat-treated after rough machining or where formability matters.

Cast plate

No temper stretching needed: the casting and stress-relieving route gives very low stress, at the cost of strength.

Quality

Inspection of ultra-thick plate

Internal soundness is the main risk in heavy plate, because porosity or inclusions at the centre are invisible until a cutter opens them up, often after many hours of machining. That is why ultrasonic testing is standard practice above about 50 mm for aerospace, vacuum and mould applications.

  • Ultrasonic testing to ASTM B594 or AMS-STD-2154, with the acceptance class (for example A or AA) stated on the order
  • Mechanical testing from the specified location and direction; short-transverse tests for thick aerospace plate
  • Chemical analysis confirming the alloy limits of EN 573-3 / Aluminum Association registrations
  • Conductivity and hardness to confirm temper, particularly for over-aged 7xxx tempers
  • Dimensional and flatness check against the order standard or drawing
  • EN 10204 3.1 certificate covering the lot, with third-party inspection on request
Applications

Where ultra-thick plate is used

Moulds and tooling

7075-T651 and 6061-T651 blocks for injection, blow, foam and rubber moulds; cast plate for jigs and fixtures that must stay flat.

Machinery and automation

6061 and 6082 plates for machine beds, gantries, CNC and milling-machine tables and large robot bases.

Semiconductor and vacuum

6061-T651 and 5083 blocks machined into chamber bodies and wafer-handling platforms.

Aerospace

7050-T7451 and 7075 plate for frames, spars and bulkheads hogged from solid; 2024-T351 for fatigue-critical parts.

Marine and cryogenic

5083 blocks for welded LNG and cryogenic fittings and marine structural parts.

Electronics and thermal

Thick 6061 for cooling plates, platens and heat-spreading bases.

Practical advice

Ordering tips and common mistakes

  1. Specify the finished partTell us what the block becomes. A mould, a vacuum chamber and an aircraft frame need different routes even at the same thickness.
  2. Choose a stress-relieved temperOrder T651, T7451 or T652 rather than T6 for anything machined deeply.
  3. State the standard and test classASTM B209, EN 485 or an AMS specification, plus the ultrasonic class if required.
  4. Allow for saw cut and skimSaw-cut faces need a machining skim; order a few millimetres oversize on each face.
  5. Plan packing and handlingHeavy plate ships in reinforced crates or on skids by sea; confirm crane and forklift capacity at delivery (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP).

FAQ

Ultra-thick aluminium (aluminum) plate: frequently asked questions

How thick can aluminium plate be?

Rolled heat-treated plate is normally available up to about 150 – 200 mm, depending on alloy and temper. Cast tooling plate and forged slabs extend the range to several hundred millimetres, and blocks up to about 600 mm can be supplied by those routes.

Is thick aluminium plate weaker than thin plate?

Yes, guaranteed minimum strength falls as thickness increases, because the centre cools more slowly during quenching and receives less rolling reduction. Low-quench-sensitivity alloys such as 7050 lose less strength than 7075 or 2024.

Which is better for a mould: 7075-T651 or 6061-T651?

7075-T651 is harder and stronger, which helps with wear and polish on high-volume moulds. 6061-T651 is cheaper, more weldable for repairs and adequate for prototype and short-run tooling.

Why do thick plates need ultrasonic testing?

Internal porosity or inclusions are invisible from the surface and may only appear after expensive machining. Ultrasonic testing to ASTM B594 or AMS-STD-2154 screens the plate before it is cut.

What is the difference between T651 and T652?

Both are stress-relieved after quenching: T651 by stretching, T652 by compression. T652 is used for forgings and very large sections that cannot be stretched.

Can you cut ultra-thick plate to size?

Yes. Thick plate can be saw-cut into blocks to your dimensions, with milled faces on request, which reduces freight weight and machining time.
