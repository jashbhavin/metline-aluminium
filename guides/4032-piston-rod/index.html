4032 and 4A11 Aluminium Extruded Rod for Pistons

4032 is a 12 % silicon aluminium alloy with copper, magnesium and nickel, designed for forged pistons: it expands less with heat than other wrought alloys and resists wear well. It is supplied as large-diameter extruded rod in F or H112 condition, cut into slugs, forged and then heat-treated to T6. 4A11 is the Chinese equivalent.

Overview

Why pistons are forged from 4032

A piston runs hot, cycles millions of times and slides against a cylinder bore. It needs low thermal expansion so clearances stay tight, good strength at elevated temperature, and a wear-resistant surface.

Most production pistons are cast in hypereutectic or eutectic Al-Si alloys. Forged pistons, used in performance, racing, diesel and aircraft engines, are stronger and tougher because forging closes porosity and gives a fine grain flow. 4032 is the forging alloy that comes closest to cast piston alloys in thermal expansion: silicon at 11 – 13.5 % lowers the expansion coefficient to about 19.4 µm/m·°C, versus about 22 – 23 for 2618 and 6061. That lets engine builders run tighter piston-to-bore clearances, with less noise and blow-by when cold.

Copper and magnesium allow precipitation hardening to T6, and nickel forms stable intermetallic particles that help retain strength at operating temperature. The hard silicon particles give wear and scuff resistance in the skirt and ring grooves.

Properties

Properties of 4032 in T6 condition

The extruded rod is supplied unheat-treated, so these are the properties of the finished, forged and T6-treated part. Typical values from Aluminum Association and ASM data:

PropertyTypical 4032-T6
Tensile strengthabout 360 – 380 MPa (52 – 55 ksi)
Yield strength (0.2 %)about 315 MPa (46 ksi)
Elongationabout 5 – 9 %
Brinell hardnessabout 120 HB
Fatigue strength (5 × 10⁸ cycles)about 110 MPa (16 ksi)
Modulus of elasticityabout 79 GPa (11,400 ksi)

The high modulus, about 15 % above most aluminium alloys, comes from the silicon particles and helps piston crowns resist deflection under combustion pressure. Strength falls at sustained temperatures above about 150 – 200 °C, as with all age-hardened aluminium alloys.

Processing

From extruded rod to finished piston

  1. Cut slugsThe rod is sawn into slugs of the forging weight.
  2. Preheat and forgeSlugs are heated and forged in closed dies, giving a dense structure and grain flow following the crown and pin bosses.
  3. Heat treatSolution treatment, quench and artificial ageing to T6 (or a stabilised variant for high-temperature service).
  4. MachineRing grooves, pin bore, skirt profile and crown are machined. Use sharp carbide or PCD tools: silicon particles are abrasive.
  5. FinishSkirt coatings, hard anodising of the top ring groove, or thermal barrier crown coatings are applied as needed.

FAQ

4032 piston rod: frequently asked questions

Why is 4032 used for pistons?

Its high silicon content gives lower thermal expansion and better wear resistance than other forging alloys, so pistons can run tighter clearances.

What temper is 4032 rod supplied in?

F or H112, unheat-treated. The forger heat-treats the finished forging to T6.

Is 4A11 the same as 4032?

They are essentially equivalent; 4A11 has slightly different silicon and small Mn and Ti limits.

Is 4032 or 2618 better for racing pistons?

2618 for high boost and extreme temperatures because it is tougher when hot; 4032 for street and moderate racing engines where low expansion and quiet running matter.

Is 4032 hard to machine?

It machines cleanly but the silicon is abrasive, so carbide or diamond tooling gives much longer tool life.
