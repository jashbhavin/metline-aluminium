Aluminium Angle Extrusion Dies: Standard Sizes and Custom Tooling

Every extruded aluminium angle is pushed through a steel die cut to its exact L-shape, so the die decides which sizes are quick and economical to buy. Standard metric angles from 5 × 5 mm to 200 × 200 mm already have tooling in the extrusion supply chain, so they can be ordered without a tooling charge. This guide lists those standard sizes, explains how angle dies are designed, and shows when a custom die is worth it.

Overview

How an aluminium angle die works

An angle is an open, solid profile: it has no enclosed hollow. That means it is made on a solid die (also called a flat die), a hardened steel disc with an L-shaped aperture machined through it, rather than the more complex porthole or bridge dies used for tubes.

In the press, a billet heated to roughly 450 – 500 °C is forced through the aperture. The metal leaves the die as a continuous angle, is cooled on the run-out table, stretched by a small percentage to straighten it and relieve stress, cut into lengths and aged to temper (T5 or T6). The die itself is supported by a backer and bolster, and is usually made from H13 hot-work tool steel that is hardened and often nitrided to resist wear.

The critical feature inside the die is the bearing: the short land around the aperture that controls the speed of the metal. Thick sections flow faster than thin ones, so the die maker uses longer bearings where the angle is thick (the corner) and shorter bearings at the thin toes. When these are balanced correctly, both legs come out straight, flat and at a true 90°. Poorly balanced dies give twisted angles, wavy toes or an angle that is open or closed beyond tolerance.

Why standard die sizes matter to buyers

  • No tooling charge and no die trial, because the die already exists and is proven
  • Faster supply, because only extrusion and finishing are needed
  • Repeatable dimensions from order to order, as the same die geometry is reused
  • Easier substitution: a near-standard size often meets the design and avoids custom tooling

Special shapes

Round-corner, lipped and irregular angle dies

Inside root radius

A fillet at the inner corner (for example R = t on structural angles) reduces stress concentration, improves metal flow and extends die life. Sizes in inch dimensions such as 50.8 × 50.8 × 6.35 mm usually carry R ≈ t.

Outside rounded corner

A radius on the outer heel for safety on edge guards and handrails, or to match a design language. Adds a second radius to specify.

Lipped or flanged angle

A return lip on one or both toes increases stiffness of the free edge and creates a seat for panels or gaskets. Needs a custom die but is still a solid profile.

Bulb angle

A thickened, rounded toe raises the section modulus with little extra weight; common in marine stiffeners.

Non-90° angles

Angles at 60°, 100°, 120° or 135° for faceted enclosures and cladding. The die is simple, but tolerances on the included angle must be agreed.

Stepped or reinforced angles

Different leg thicknesses, grooves for screws or serrations for grip. Every extra feature adds die complexity, so keep only the features you need.

Design for extrusion

Design rules for a new angle die

Angles are among the easiest shapes to extrude, but a few limits decide whether a profile is practical and economical:

ParameterPractical guidanceWhy it matters
Circumscribing circle (CCD)Most presses handle angles up to about 200 – 300 mm CCD economically; larger needs big pressesDie size, press size and cost all scale with CCD
Minimum wallAround 0.6 – 1.0 mm for small 6060/6063 angles; 1.5 – 3 mm for 6061/6082 or larger CCDsThin walls need lower speeds and are harder to keep flat
Leg-to-thickness ratioUp to roughly 20 – 30:1 for 6063; lower for harder alloysVery thin long legs wave and twist on the run-out table
Thickness variationKeep adjacent thickness ratios below about 2 – 3:1Uneven sections cool and flow unevenly, causing distortion
Corner radiiAdd a small radius (0.5 – 1 mm minimum) to every cornerSharp internal corners crack dies and concentrate stress
Included angleState the nominal angle and tolerance on the drawingOpen/closed angle is a common rejection cause

These are general industry guidelines. The extruder's die designer confirms final limits for each alloy, press and profile.

Alloy matters too. 6060 and 6063 flow easily and suit thin, sharp, decorative angles. 6061 and 6082 are stiffer to push, so they need slightly thicker walls and generous radii. 7075 and 2024 angles are extruded at much lower speeds, which is why they cost more and why fewer die sizes exist for them.

Tolerances

Tolerances for extruded angles

Governing standards

EN 755-9
Dimensional and form tolerances for extruded profiles in most alloys: leg length, thickness, angle, straightness, twist and flatness
EN 12020-2
Tighter tolerances for precision profiles in 6060 and 6063, often used for architectural angles
ANSI H35.2 / ASTM B221
Standard dimensional tolerances for extrusions supplied to American specifications
ASTM B308
Dimensions and tolerances for 6061-T6 standard structural angles and other shapes

Under these standards the allowed deviation grows with the size of the section and its CCD. For buyers, the points that cause most problems are the included angle (squareness), twist over the length, and straightness of the toes. If an angle will be used as a precision stop, a datum or a visible trim, call out tighter tolerances on the drawing, and ask for the inspection method to be agreed before production.

Custom tooling

When to order a custom angle die, and how it works

A new die is justified when a standard size does not meet the function, when a lip, groove or radius saves later machining, or when the annual volume is large enough that a lighter, optimised section saves more in metal than the die costs. Extrusion dies are relatively low-cost tooling compared with casting or stamping tools, so even modest volumes can justify them.

  1. Drawing reviewSend a dimensioned drawing (DWG, DXF or PDF), alloy, temper, finish and expected volume. The die designer checks wall, radii and CCD.
  2. Design adjustmentSmall changes are suggested if needed, such as adding radii or evening out thicknesses, to improve yield and straightness.
  3. Die manufactureThe die is machined from H13 steel, heat-treated and finished with wire EDM for the bearing surfaces.
  4. Trial and sampleA first extrusion run produces samples that are measured against the drawing and approved by the buyer.
  5. ProductionOnce approved, the same die is reused for future orders. Dies wear over time and are refurbished or replaced as part of normal tooling maintenance.
Ordering

How to order angle from standard or custom dies

  • Profile size A × B × t, root and toe radii, and included angle (or a drawing)
  • Alloy and temper, for example EN AW-6063-T5, 6061-T6 or 6082-T6, and the standard (EN 755, ASTM B221 or B308)
  • Length: mill length or cut length, and tolerance on length
  • Finish: mill, anodised, powder coated or brushed, and which faces are visible
  • Quantity, packing (interleaved, filmed, bundled or crated) and delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP)
  • Documents: mill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1, dimensional report or first-article report if required

FAQ

Aluminium angle extrusion dies: frequently asked questions

Do I pay a die charge for a standard aluminium angle size?

Usually not. Standard metric and inch angle sizes are already tooled across the extrusion industry, so you pay only for the metal, extrusion and any finishing or cutting. A tooling charge applies only when a new die must be made.

What kind of die is used to extrude aluminum angle?

A solid (flat) die, because an angle has no hollow. It is machined from hardened H13 hot-work tool steel, with bearing lengths adjusted so the thick corner and thin toes leave the die at the same speed.

What is the smallest aluminium angle that can be extruded?

Small 6063 trim angles around 5 × 5 × 0.6 mm are routinely extruded. Below that, metal flow and handling become difficult and roll-formed strip may be a better route.

Can a custom angle be extruded in 6061 or 7075?

Yes, but harder alloys need thicker walls, larger radii and slower extrusion. Expect fewer standard sizes and a higher price per kilogram in 7075 than in 6063 or 6061.

How long does a custom angle die last?

Die life depends on alloy, section and press conditions. Dies are routinely nitrided and refurbished to extend their life, and a worn die is replaced as part of normal tooling maintenance rather than charged to each order.

What drawing information is needed for a new angle die?

Leg lengths, thickness of each leg, root and toe radii, included angle, tolerances, alloy and temper, finish, visible faces and the expected annual volume.

Need an angle size or a custom angle die?

Send your size list or drawing with alloy, finish, quantity and destination, and we will confirm the tooling route and quote.
