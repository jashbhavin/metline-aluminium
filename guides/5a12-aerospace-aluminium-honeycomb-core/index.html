Honeycomb core guide

5A12 Aerospace Aluminium Honeycomb Core

5A12 honeycomb core is a hexagonal cell structure made from foil of 5A12, a high-magnesium (about 9 % Mg) aluminium alloy registered in Chinese standards. Bonded between two thin skins it forms a sandwich panel that is very stiff for its weight, and the high-magnesium foil gives more compressive and shear strength per unit density than ordinary 3003 or 5052 core. This guide explains the alloy, how to choose cell size, foil and density, which standards apply and when 5052 or 5056 is the better choice.

Overview

What is 5A12 honeycomb core?

Aluminium honeycomb is made by printing lines of adhesive on stacked sheets of thin foil, curing the stack into a solid block, slicing the block to the required thickness and then pulling the slices open so the bonded and unbonded zones form hexagonal cells. The result is a core that is mostly air, yet when bonded between face sheets it keeps the skins apart and carries shear, much like the web of an I-beam.

The foil alloy sets the strength of every cell wall. 5A12, formerly LF12, is one of the most highly alloyed Al-Mg grades in the GB/T 3190 standard, with roughly twice the magnesium of 5056. In foil form it is strong and springy, so a 5A12 core reaches a given compressive or shear strength at lower density than 5052 core. That is why it is specified on Chinese aerospace and space programmes where weight is critical: satellite structural panels, equipment decks, launch-vehicle fairings and aircraft secondary structure.

Like other high-magnesium alloys, 5A12 is not used where the core will see sustained elevated temperatures in a corrosive setting, because it can sensitise. In practice aerospace sandwich panels are sealed, the foil is given a corrosion-resistant surface treatment and the panel is protected by its skins, but the service temperature should still be part of the selection.

Selection

How cell size, foil thickness and density interact

Three numbers describe any honeycomb: the cell size (the distance across flats of a hexagon), the foil thickness and the resulting density. They are linked: for a given cell size, thicker foil means higher density; for a given foil, smaller cells mean higher density. Strength rises faster than density, roughly with density to the power 1.5, so a modest increase in density buys a large increase in strength.

The Chinese specification code reads as cell size, alloy and density. For example, 5-5A12-52 is a 5 mm cell in 5A12 foil at 52 kg/m³. The table shows common combinations with indicative stabilised flatwise compressive strength.

Small cells (3 mm)

Support thin skins without dimpling, give the best surface finish and the highest strength at a given foil. Heavier and more costly.

Medium cells (5 mm)

The general choice for aerospace panels, balancing skin support, strength and weight.

Large cells (7 – 8 mm)

Lowest density for lightly loaded panels and large flat areas; require thicker skins to avoid intra-cell buckling.

Mechanical behaviour

Strength directions and what to test

Honeycomb is anisotropic. The L direction runs along the ribbon (the direction of the continuous foil), and the W direction is the expansion direction. Because the double-thickness bonded walls run in L, shear strength and modulus in L are typically about 1.5 – 2 times those in W. Panels should be laid out with the L direction aligned to the main shear load.

Standards

Standards for 5A12 and other aerospace cores

Relevant specifications

HB 5443
Chinese aviation industry standard for aluminium honeycomb core; the usual basis for 5A12 core
SAE AMS-C-7438
Aluminium core material for sandwich construction (successor to MIL-C-7438); covers 5052, 5056 and 2024 cores
SAE AMS-A-81596
Uncoated aluminium foil for honeycomb core
ASTM C271, C273, C297, C365
Test methods for sandwich core density, shear, flatwise tension and compression

An important practical point: AMS-C-7438 does not list 5A12. If a programme is qualified to AMS-C-7438, the equivalent high-strength choice is 5056 core; 5A12 is ordered to HB 5443 or to a purchaser specification that defines the alloy, geometry and acceptance tests.

For most commercial sandwich panels 5052 honeycomb core is the default; 5056 core is the step up when an internationally recognised high-strength core is needed. Choose 5A12 when the drawing calls for it or the weight target cannot be met otherwise.

Applications

Where 5A12 honeycomb is used

  • Spacecraft: satellite structural panels, equipment decks and solar-array substrates where stiffness, flatness and low mass matter
  • Launch vehicles: fairing panels, interstage structures and crushable energy absorbers for landing loads
  • Aircraft: floor panels, fairings, access doors, control-surface and nacelle sandwich parts
  • Defence and marine: lightweight bulkheads, shelters and radar structures needing a metallic core

FAQ

5A12 honeycomb core: frequently asked questions

What is 5A12 aluminium honeycomb made of?

Foil of 5A12, a high-magnesium Al-Mg alloy with about 8.3 – 9.6 % magnesium and some manganese, bonded into hexagonal cells.

Is 5A12 honeycomb stronger than 5052 aluminum honeycomb?

At equal density, yes: the higher-magnesium foil gives more compressive and shear strength. That lets designers reach a strength target with a lighter core.

Can 5A12 core be supplied to AMS-C-7438?

No. AMS-C-7438 covers 5052, 5056 and 2024 cores. 5A12 is normally supplied to HB 5443 or a purchaser specification; use 5056 if AMS-C-7438 compliance is required.

What do the codes like 5-5A12-52 mean?

The first number is the cell size in millimetres, then the alloy, then the nominal density in kg/m³. 5-5A12-52 is a 5 mm cell in 5A12 foil at about 52 kg/m³.

Which direction of honeycomb is stronger?

The L (ribbon) direction, where the double-thickness bonded walls run. Shear strength in L is typically about 1.5 – 2 times that in W.

Can the core be perforated?

Yes. Perforated core lets trapped air escape during cure and equalises pressure in vacuum, which is common for space hardware.
