Aluminium Square Tube: Sizes, Weights, Alloys and Selection

Aluminium square tube is an extruded hollow section with four equal sides, used wherever a frame needs stiffness in both directions at a third of the weight of steel. Most square tube is 6063-T5/T52 for architectural and light framing work or 6061-T6 and 6082-T6 for load-bearing structures. This guide gives the common metric and inch sizes with calculated weights and section properties, explains how to choose alloy and wall thickness, and lists what to put in an enquiry.

Overview

What is aluminium square tube?

Square tube (also called square tubing, square hollow section, SHS or box section) is a closed profile with equal outside width and height. Because the material sits far from the neutral axis on all four faces, it resists bending and twisting much better than a solid bar or an open angle of the same weight.

Almost all aluminium square tube is extruded: a heated billet is pushed through a porthole or bridge die, the metal splits around a mandrel and rejoins inside the die to form the hollow. This produces fine longitudinal seam welds that are metallurgically sound and fully acceptable for structural use under ASTM B221 and EN 755. Where a design or code calls for a tube with no seam, for example some pressure or hydraulic applications, seamless tube is extruded over a piercing mandrel or drawn, which costs more and limits the size range.

Square tube is described by outside width × outside width × wall thickness, for example 50 × 50 × 3 mm or 2 × 2 × 1/8 in. Corners may be sharp (a small outside radius only) or radiused. Sharp-corner tube is common in architectural and furniture work; generous radii improve metal flow, reduce stress concentration and are typical of heavier structural sections.

Why designers specify aluminium square tube

  • About one third the weight of steel tube of the same size, with no rust and no need for galvanising
  • Equal stiffness about both axes, so frames behave the same whichever way they are loaded
  • Flat faces make drilling, bolting, riveting, welding and joining with corner keys simple
  • Accepts anodising, powder coating and brushed or polished finishes for visible work
  • Fully recyclable without loss of properties
Alloy selection

Which alloy and temper for square tube?

Six alloys cover nearly every square tube requirement. The first decision is whether the tube is mainly structural (choose 6061, 6082 or 6005A) or mainly appearance-led (choose 6063 or 6060). Specified minimums for 6061-T6 extruded tube in ASTM B221 are 260 MPa (38 ksi) tensile and 240 MPa (35 ksi) yield, which is why some catalogues quote 35 ksi rather than the typical 40 ksi.

Stronger aerospace alloys are also extruded as square tube. 2024-T3/T351 offers high fatigue strength and 7075-T6 the highest static strength, but both are poor for welding and less corrosion-resistant, so they are used only where a specification calls for them. 5052 and 3003 square tube exist in small sizes for marine trim and general fabrication where formability and corrosion resistance matter more than strength.

Engineering

How to choose size and wall thickness

For the same weight, a larger tube with a thinner wall is stiffer than a smaller tube with a thick wall, because stiffness rises with the cube of the width. That is why 50 × 50 × 2 mm (1.04 kg/m, I = 14.8 cm⁴) is about 45 % stiffer in bending than 40 × 40 × 3 mm (1.20 kg/m, I = 10.2 cm⁴), even though it is lighter. The limit is local buckling: very thin walls crumple or dent before the tube reaches its yield strength.

  1. Start from the load caseWork out the bending moment, axial load and allowable deflection. Aluminium has about one third of the elastic modulus of steel (about 69 GPa), so deflection usually governs before strength.
  2. Pick a width for stiffnessUse the I values above to find the smallest section that meets the deflection limit, then check stress using Z and the design strength of the alloy.
  3. Check wall slendernessKeep the face width-to-thickness ratio (b/t) moderate for members in compression or bending. Design codes such as the Aluminum Design Manual and EN 1999-1-1 (Eurocode 9) classify sections by b/t and reduce allowable stress for slender walls.
  4. Allow for weldingWelding 6xxx tube softens a band about 25 mm wide around the weld. Eurocode 9 and the Aluminum Design Manual give reduced heat-affected-zone strengths; place welds away from the highest-stress point where possible.
  5. Think about jointsThicker walls hold threads, rivnuts and self-tapping screws better. If the frame uses bolted joints through the tube, check bearing and crushing at the holes or add sleeves.

Thin wall (≤ 1.5 mm)

Light frames, shelving, displays, furniture and trim. Easy to cut and bend; prone to denting and limited for threaded fixings.

Medium wall (2 – 4 mm)

The general-purpose range: machine guards, workstations, gates, trailers, solar frames and platforms.

Heavy wall (5 mm and above)

Load-bearing columns, structural frames and parts that are heavily machined, tapped or welded.

Tolerances & quality

Tolerances, straightness and inspection

Square tube is supplied to the tolerance tables of the ordering standard unless the drawing tightens them. Outside width tolerance depends on size and on whether it is measured at the corners or mid-face; wall thickness tolerance is usually a percentage of nominal wall, and seamless tube is held tighter than porthole tube.

Finishes and processing

Surface finishes and fabrication

Mill finish

The as-extruded surface. Lowest cost and suitable for structures that are hidden, painted later or used outdoors where a natural grey patina is acceptable.

Anodised

Clear, bronze, black or other colours, typically 10 – 25 µm for architectural work. Use 6063 or 6060 for the most even colour; 6061 anodises well but can look slightly less uniform.

Powder coated

Any RAL colour, including black square tubing for frames, gates and furniture. Gives a durable, thicker coating that hides minor extrusion lines.

Brushed or polished

Decorative finishes for visible interior work, display systems and furniture, usually protected by clear anodising or lacquer.

Cutting and machining

Saw cutting to length, mitre cuts, drilling, punching, slotting, tapping and CNC machining to drawing.

Bending and welding

Square tube can be bent with mandrel support (form 6063 or 6061 in T4 or O temper for tight radii) and is readily TIG or MIG welded with ER4043 or ER5356 filler.

Applications

Where aluminium square tube is used

  • Building and architecture: balustrade posts, partitions, sub-frames, pergola members and support structures for cladding, using 6063 or 6060 profiles supplied to fabricators
  • Machinery and automation: machine bases, guards, conveyor frames and workbenches, mainly 6061-T6 and 6082-T6
  • Transport: trailer and truck body frames, bus and rail interior structures, roof racks and ladders
  • Marine: boat frames, T-tops, rails and deck furniture, anodised or painted, often in 6061-T6
  • Solar and outdoor: mounting frames, equipment platforms and enclosures where corrosion resistance and low weight cut installation cost
  • Furniture, retail and exhibitions: shelving, display racks, stands and frames in thin-wall 6063

A typical light-industrial workstation, for example, is built from 40 × 40 × 3 mm 6061-T6 tube with bolted corner brackets, while a balustrade might use 50 × 50 × 2 mm 6063-T5 posts that are anodised after cutting.

Because aluminium is about three times less stiff, a straight swap of the same size usually deflects about three times as much. Designers normally choose a larger or thicker aluminium section, which still ends up roughly half the weight of the steel member it replaces. The full life-cycle cost, including no painting or galvanising and lower transport and handling cost, is often lower than steel even though aluminium costs more per kilogram.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) square tube: frequently asked questions

What is the strongest aluminum square tubing?

Among widely available grades, 7075-T6 is the strongest, followed by 2024-T3. For welded or general structural frames, 6061-T6 and 6082-T6 give the best balance of strength, weldability and corrosion resistance.

How much does 2 × 2 aluminium square tube weigh?

A 2 × 2 in tube with a 1/8 in wall weighs about 1.64 kg/m (1.10 lb/ft) with sharp corners. A metric 50 × 50 × 3 mm tube weighs about 1.52 kg/m.

Should I choose 6061 or 6063 square tube?

Choose 6061-T6 when the tube carries significant load, is welded into a structure or is machined. Choose 6063-T5 or T52 when appearance, anodising quality or easy bending matter more than strength, such as railings, trim and furniture.

What is the difference between square tube and square pipe?

In aluminium the terms are used loosely. Square tube is ordered by outside size and wall thickness; "pipe" strictly refers to round pipe sized by nominal pipe size and schedule. Box section and square hollow section mean the same as square tube.

Can aluminium square tube be bent?

Yes. Use mandrel or roll bending with internal support to stop the faces collapsing, and keep the centreline radius generous; T6 tube needs a much larger radius than O or T4, so trial-bend a sample before production. For tight bends, form in O or T4 temper and age to T6 afterwards.

Can you cut square tube to length and finish it?

Yes. Metline can supply exact cut lengths, mitres, drilled holes and anodised or powder-coated finishes on request, with mill certificates for the material.
