Extrusion selection guide

Aluminium Channel: Types, Sizes, Weights and Selection

Aluminium channel is an extruded profile with a web and two flanges, made mostly in 6061-T6 for structural work and 6063-T5 or T6 for architectural and light-duty uses. This guide explains every common channel shape, compares the alloys, gives calculated size, weight and stiffness tables in metric and inch sizes, and sets out the standards, tolerances and ordering details you need to choose the right channel.

Overview

What is aluminium channel?

A channel is a profile made of a flat web with a flange along each edge, giving a U or C cross-section. It is one of the most useful shapes in engineering: it carries bending loads efficiently about its strong axis, provides a flat back for bolting to walls and frames, and its open side can hold a panel, a sheet of glass, a cable or a sliding part.

Aluminium channel is almost always extruded. Heated billet is pushed through a steel die shaped like the channel cross-section, then quenched, stretched to straighten it and aged to the required temper. Extrusion produces sharp or radiused corners, thin or thick walls and features such as screw ports and grooves that would be impossible to roll in steel. A smaller share of light-gauge channel is roll-formed from coil, which suits thin, uniform-wall shapes made in very long runs.

Compared with steel channel of the same size, aluminium channel weighs about one third as much, does not rust and needs no painting, and can be cut and drilled with woodworking-grade tools. It is about one third as stiff, so a direct swap of section sizes increases deflection; where stiffness governs, a deeper aluminium channel is used.

Quick selection rules

  • Load-bearing frames, supports and trailers: 6061-T6 (or 6082-T6 in Europe)
  • Trims, edging, glazing and visible architectural work: 6063-T5 or T6
  • Sharp-corner channel for neat fits; radiused (structural) channel for strength and fatigue
  • Check deflection, not just strength, when replacing steel channel
Shapes

Types of aluminium channel

U channel

Flanges usually equal in height to or taller than the width; square inside corners. Used for edging panels and glass, cable runs and light frames.

C channel

Wider web with shorter flanges, often with radiused inside corners and sometimes tapered flanges. The standard structural channel for beams, frames and trailers.

J channel

One flange longer than the other. Caps panel edges and trims siding, cladding and soffits.

F channel

A channel with an extra leg, used for soffit and fascia trims and to support panels at corners.

H channel

Two channels back to back sharing one web. Joins two panels edge to edge in partitions, signs and glazing.

Hat channel

A raised channel with outward flanges; used as furring for cladding, stiffeners on panels and roof battens.

Lipped / strut channel

Channel with inturned lips that hold spring nuts, for adjustable support of pipes, cable trays and equipment.

Z and L sections

Not true channels but often bought alongside them for flashing, girts and panel connections.

Custom channel

Any of the above with added screw ports, gasket grooves, snap features or uneven walls, extruded from a dedicated die.

U versus C: in everyday use the names overlap. Most suppliers call a channel "U" when it has square inside corners and relatively tall flanges, and "C" when it has a broad web, shorter flanges and radiused corners intended for structural loads. Always confirm with a drawing rather than relying on the name.

6061 and 6063 minimums from ASTM B221 / B308; 6082 and 6060 from EN 755-2, where values depend on wall thickness. Typical properties are higher: about 310 MPa tensile for 6061-T6 and 186 MPa for 6063-T5.

Other alloys appear for specific needs: 5083 and 5086 extruded channel for marine structures where welded strength and seawater resistance matter; 2024 and 7075 channel for aerospace parts and highly loaded fittings, accepting poorer corrosion resistance and weldability. These are less widely stocked and usually ordered to a specification.

Tolerances cover not only depth, width and wall thickness but also straightness, twist, flatness of the web and the angle between web and flange. For long channels used as tracks or guides, straightness and twist often matter more than cross-section size, so state them on the order. Precision profiles to EN 12020-2 have roughly half the tolerance of EN 755-9 on many dimensions and are the usual choice for 6063 architectural channel.

Working with channel

Cutting, machining, welding, bending and finishing

Cutting

Carbide-tipped circular saws with a negative hook angle give clean square cuts; mitre saws suit frame corners. Deburr cut ends before anodising or assembly.

Drilling and machining

6061-T6 machines cleanly with sharp tools and coolant; 6063 is softer and can be gummy, so use high rake angles. CNC machining adds slots, holes and notches to drawing.

Welding

MIG and TIG with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. Welding reduces strength next to the weld (6061-T6 heat-affected zone drops towards T4-level), so place welds away from peak stress or design to reduced values.

Bending

Channels can be bent on a profile bender. Bending about the weak axis or in O or T4 temper is easiest; tight radii in T6 risk cracking or flange buckling.

Anodising

Clear, black, bronze or champagne. 6063 gives the most even appearance; specify 15 – 25 µm for outdoor exposure.

Powder coating and other finishes

Any RAL colour, wood-grain transfer, brushing and polishing. Mill finish is normal for structural channel that will not be seen.

Joining aluminium channel to steel needs isolation (neoprene pads, plastic sleeves, coated or stainless fasteners) to prevent galvanic corrosion in damp conditions.

Selection

How to choose and order the right channel

  1. Define the jobStructural (carrying load), guiding (sliding or tracking) or finishing (edging, trims). This sets the alloy family.
  2. Pick the shapeU, C, J, H, hat or strut, and sharp or radiused corners. Check how the channel connects to the parts around it.
  3. Size itCheck strength and deflection with the section properties; for replacement of steel, increase depth to recover stiffness.
  4. State the specificationAlloy and temper, standard (ASTM B221 / B308 or EN 755), tolerance standard and any special straightness or twist limits.
  5. Length, finish and processingMill lengths or cut-to-length; anodising, powder coating; drilling, punching or machining.
  6. Documents and deliveryEN 10204 3.1 mill certificate, protective packing and delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP).

Common mistakes

  • Ordering by name ("C channel") without a drawing or dimensions
  • Using 6063-T5 for load-bearing frames that need 6061-T6
  • Swapping steel channel for aluminium of the same size and getting three times the deflection
  • Ignoring twist and straightness on long tracks
  • Bolting directly to steel without galvanic isolation

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) channel: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between U channel and C channel?

U channel usually has square inside corners and flanges as tall as or taller than the web is wide; C channel has a broad web, shorter flanges and often radiused corners for structural use. The terms overlap between suppliers, so specify dimensions or a drawing.

Which is stronger, 6061 or 6063 aluminium channel?

6061-T6. Its minimum yield strength is 240 MPa against 110 MPa for 6063-T5 and 170 MPa for 6063-T6. 6063 is chosen for better surface finish and anodising when loads are modest.

How much does aluminium channel weigh?

Weight equals cross-section area times 2.70 kg per metre for each 1,000 mm² of area. For example, a 50 × 25 × 3 mm sharp-corner channel weighs about 0.76 kg per metre; a 2 × 1 × 0.125 in channel about 0.55 lb per foot.

Can aluminium channel replace steel channel?

Often, with a weight saving of around 50 – 60 % for an equivalent design. Because aluminium is about one third as stiff, the aluminium channel normally needs to be deeper or thicker to keep deflection within limits.

What lengths does aluminium channel come in?

Common mill lengths are around 3 to 6 metres (or 12, 20 and 24 feet in inch sizes), with longer lengths possible from the extrusion line. Cut-to-length supply is available on request.

Can I order a custom channel profile?

Yes. A custom channel needs an extrusion die made to your drawing; after that, the profile can be produced in the alloy, temper, length and finish you specify.

What finish should I choose for outdoor aluminium channel?

Anodising of 15 – 25 µm or a durable architectural powder coating. 6063 gives the most uniform anodised appearance.
