8011 Aluminium Foil for Air Conditioners

8011 is the most widely used alloy for air-conditioner fin foil: an aluminium-iron-silicon alloy that forms well, conducts heat efficiently and costs less than manganese- or magnesium-bearing alternatives. It is supplied 0.08 – 0.2 mm thick, bare or with hydrophilic, hydrophobic or corrosion-resistant coatings. This guide explains each type, the specifications, and how to choose the right 8011 foil for the climate and the coil design.

Overview

Why 8011 is the standard AC fin alloy

An air-conditioner coil is a bundle of copper or aluminium tubes threaded through thousands of thin aluminium fins. The fins provide most of the heat-transfer area on the air side, so their material, thickness and surface treatment strongly affect cooling capacity, noise, energy use and service life.

8011 contains about 0.5 – 0.9 % silicon and 0.6 – 1.0 % iron. These elements form fine intermetallic particles that give the foil more strength and a finer grain than pure 1100 or 1200 aluminium, while keeping excellent ductility for drawing fin collars. It is produced in very large volumes for foil and closures, which keeps it economical and widely available.

For most residential and light commercial units, 8011 in an H22 or H24 temper with a hydrophilic coating is the default choice. Where the design needs thinner or stiffer fins, 8006 fin stock is the usual upgrade.

Bare foil

Rolled and annealed with no surface treatment. Mature, low-cost and with good base heat transfer. Limitations: condensate forms droplets that bridge the fins and the bare surface corrodes faster in humid or salty air.

Hydrophilic foil

The coating makes water spread into a thin film that drains, preventing droplet bridging. Air resistance and fan noise fall and capacity is maintained in wet conditions. Costs more than bare foil.

Hydrophobic foil

A high contact-angle surface makes condensate bead and run off fast, helping defrost and limiting water carry-over. Used in refrigeration and some precision cooling units.

Coated foil

Epoxy, polyester, PE or PVDF-type coatings in colours such as gold, blue or black protect against salt, pollutants and chemicals. Heavier coatings (for example 10 – 14 µm) are used on duct and pipe applications in harsh environments.

How to choose the right 8011 foil

Indoor unit, low humidity, cost-drivenBare or hydrophilicLittle condensate or corrosion risk
Evaporator in humid climateHydrophilicDrains condensate, keeps airflow and capacity
Refrigeration evaporator, frostingHydrophobicWater sheds fast, easier defrost
Outdoor condenser near the coastGold or blue corrosion-resistant coatedResists salt deposits
Industrial or polluted atmosphereHeavier epoxy-type coatingResists acids and pollutants
Need thinner or stiffer finsConsider 8006 instead of 8011Higher strength at the same gauge

Temper choice. H22 gives the best collar-forming ability for standard designs; H24 and H26 add stiffness for thin or narrow-pitch fins but tolerate less collar height. O temper is used for bare foil formed into deep shapes.

Total cost. Coated foil costs more per tonne, but it can extend coil life considerably in coastal and humid regions and keep capacity from falling as the unit ages. Evaluate it over the expected service life, not the purchase price alone.

Common mistakes

  • Using bare foil on outdoor coils in coastal regions
  • Choosing a harder temper to save thickness without a press trial
  • Skipping coating durability tests after changing foil or coating supplier
Quality

Quality checks for 8011 AC foil

  • Chemistry: spectrometer analysis confirming 8011 limits for each cast
  • Mechanical properties: tensile strength and elongation to the agreed standard for the temper
  • Thickness: gauge checks across width and length to keep fin weight and heat transfer consistent
  • Surface: no scratches, pinholes, oil stains, blisters or coating voids
  • Coating: weight, adhesion, uniformity and contact angle
  • Corrosion: salt-spray testing of coated foil for agreed hours
  • Certification: EN 10204 3.1 certificate with results per lot
Related HVAC products

8011 foil tape and flexible duct foil

8011 foil is also the base for two common HVAC accessories. Aluminium foil tape combines thin foil with a pressure-sensitive adhesive to seal duct joints, insulation seams and pipe connections, giving a vapour barrier that reflects heat. Flexible duct foil is laminated and spiral-wound with a wire helix into flexible air ducts that are easy to cut and install without special tools. Both use soft O-temper foil for conformability. FAQ

8011 air-conditioner aluminium (aluminum) foil: frequently asked questions

Why is 8011 used for air-conditioner fins?

It forms tall fin collars easily, conducts heat well and is cheaper than manganese- or magnesium-bearing alloys. Its iron and silicon content gives a little more strength and a finer grain than pure aluminium foil.

What thickness of 8011 foil is used for AC fins?

Typically 0.08 – 0.2 mm, with about 0.095 – 0.15 mm most common for residential units.

Hydrophilic or hydrophobic: which is better?

Neither is better in general. Hydrophilic foil spreads condensate into a draining film and is standard for AC evaporators; hydrophobic foil beads water so it runs off, which suits refrigeration and frosting conditions.

What do gold and blue coated fins do?

They are corrosion-resistant coatings that protect outdoor coils from salt and pollutants. The colour helps identify the coating and check coverage.

What is the difference between 8011 and 8006 fin foil?

8006 contains more iron plus manganese, so it is stronger at the same thickness and allows thinner fins or taller collars. 8011 is more economical and adequate for most standard coils.

Which temper of 8011 should I order?

H22 for standard collar heights, H24 or H26 for stiffer, thinner fins, and O for bare foil formed into deep shapes. Confirm with a press trial when changing temper.
