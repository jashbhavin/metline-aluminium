ER4043 Welding Wire (AlSi5): A Practical Guide

ER4043 is a 5 % silicon aluminium filler metal, supplied as MIG wire and TIG rod, that flows well and strongly resists hot cracking. It is the usual first choice for welding 6xxx extrusions, 3xxx sheet and aluminium-silicon castings, and the wrong choice for high-magnesium 5xxx plate or parts that must be colour-matched after anodising. This guide covers chemistry, compatible base metals, weld strength, settings and buying details.

Overview

What is ER4043 welding wire?

ER4043 is an aluminium-silicon filler metal classified under AWS A5.10 (ASME SFA-5.10) and ISO 18273 as S Al 4043, chemical symbol AlSi5. "ER" means it can be used as an electrode (MIG/GMAW wire) or as a rod (TIG/GTAW); the same alloy sold only as cut rod is designated R4043.

Silicon is the working ingredient. Adding about 5 % silicon lowers the melting point of the filler below that of most parent alloys and narrows the freezing range of the weld pool. The pool stays fluid longer, wets the joint edges well and, most importantly, the last liquid to freeze can feed shrinkage between grains instead of tearing apart. That is why 4043 is the classic remedy for solidification (hot) cracking in crack-sensitive alloys such as 6061, 6063 and 6082.

The trade-off is that the deposit is softer and less ductile than a 5xxx-series weld, it turns grey-black when anodised, and it reacts with high-magnesium parent metals to form brittle magnesium silicide. Knowing those three limits covers most filler selection mistakes made with 4043.

ER4043 in one minute

  • Best for: 6xxx alloys, 1xxx and 3xxx alloys, low-Mg 5xxx (up to about 2.5 % Mg) and Al-Si castings such as A356 and 319
  • Strengths: high fluidity, low crack sensitivity, bright low-spatter beads, suits service above 65 °C
  • Limits: lower shear strength than 5356, poor anodised colour match, not for 5083, 5086 or 5456
  • Forms: layer-wound MIG spools, drums and straight TIG rods in 1 m lengths

Mechanical properties

How strong is an ER4043 weld?

With non-heat-treatable parent metals, the weld is usually as strong as the annealed parent. With heat-treatable 6xxx alloys, the heat-affected zone (HAZ) softens, and the joint fails there rather than in the filler. The design figure therefore depends on the parent alloy, not just on the wire.

JointConditionTensile strengthBasis
6061-T6 groove weld, 4043As welded165 MPa (24 ksi) minimumAWS D1.2 / ASME IX qualification minimum
6061-T6 groove weld, 4043As weldedAbout 185 MPa (27 ksi) typicalPublished filler-maker test data
6061-T6 welded with 4043, then solution treated and agedPost-weld heat treatedAbout 300 MPa (44 ksi) typicalPossible because 4043 picks up Mg from the parent
6063-T6 groove weld, 4043As welded117 MPa (17 ksi) minimumAWS D1.2 qualification minimum
3003-H14 groove weld, 4043As welded97 MPa (14 ksi) minimumEquals the annealed parent

Shear strength is where 4043 lags. Published data give minimum fillet-weld shear strengths of about 80 MPa (11.5 ksi) longitudinal and 103 MPa (15 ksi) transverse for 4043, against about 117 MPa (17 ksi) and 179 MPa (26 ksi) for 5356. A fillet joint designed for 5356 needs bigger welds if it is switched to 4043, so never substitute fillers on a structural drawing without the engineer's agreement.

Aluminium wire is soft compared with steel and is easily shaved or kinked in the feeder. Smaller diameters (0.8 – 0.9 mm) feed reliably only with a push-pull or spool gun; 1.0 mm and above can be pushed over short conduit with U-groove drive rolls, a PTFE or carbon liner and a contact tip about 0.1 mm oversize. Diameter tolerance is controlled to the AWS A5.10 and ISO 544 limits, and good wire has a consistent cast and helix so it leaves the tip straight.

Clean first

Remove oil with solvent, then brush with a stainless-steel brush used only on aluminium. Weld within a few hours, before the oxide re-forms thickly.

Push, do not drag

Use a push angle of about 10 – 15° so the gas shield runs ahead of the pool and the arc cleaning action works on the joint.

Fill the crater

Aluminium crater cracks easily. Use a crater-fill function or pause and reverse briefly at the end of each weld.

TIG technique

AC with balance set toward penetration, pure argon, and a 2.4 mm rod for 3 mm sheet is a common starting point.

Preheat thick work

Sections over about 25 mm, or castings, benefit from preheat to around 100 °C; keep heat-treatable alloys below about 120 °C to protect strength.

Store dry

Keep spools sealed until use and store indoors above the dew point. Moisture and hydrated oxide on the wire cause porosity.

ER4943 was registered in 2010 as a 4043 with a small magnesium addition: it keeps the fluidity and crack resistance but gives noticeably higher as-welded and shear strength. ER4047 has twice the silicon, a near-eutectic narrow melting range and the least shrinkage, which suits leak-tight and thin-wall heat-exchanger work. Applications

Where ER4043 is used

  • 6xxx extrusion fabrication: frames, handrails, truck bodies, trailer cross-members, bicycle frames and machine guards in 6061 and 6063
  • Casting repair and assembly: filling porosity or cracks in A356 and 319 castings, and joining castings to wrought parts
  • Heat exchangers and HVAC: headers, manifolds and housings in 3003 and 6063, including parts running warm
  • Automotive and EV: brackets, sub-frames and battery enclosures in 6xxx where the procedure specifies an Al-Si filler
  • Tanks and vessels: 3003 and 5052 tanks, ducting and covers that are not anodised
  • General repair work: a forgiving filler for mixed or unknown low-Mg alloys in maintenance shops

Avoid 4043 on marine hull plate in 5083 or 5086, on welded parts that will be bright-anodised for appearance, and wherever the design calls for the shear capacity of a 5xxx filler.

FAQ

ER4043 aluminium (aluminum) welding wire: FAQ

Should I use ER4043 or ER5356 on 6061?

Both are approved for 6061. Choose 4043 when crack resistance, smooth bead appearance, service above 65 °C or post-weld heat treatment matter. Choose 5356 when the joint needs higher shear strength, more ductility or a better colour match after anodising.

Can ER4043 weld 5083 or other high-magnesium alloys?

No. On parent alloys with more than about 2.5 % magnesium, the silicon in 4043 combines with magnesium to form brittle Mg2Si in the weld, giving poor ductility and crack-prone joints. Use 5356, 5183 or 5556.

Why does an ER4043 weld turn dark after anodising?

The silicon in the deposit does not anodise; it stays in the oxide as fine particles that look grey to black. The darkness increases with silicon content, which is why 4047 looks darker still. For a close colour match, use a 5xxx filler.

Can ER4043 welds be heat treated?

The filler cannot be hardened by itself, but when it dilutes with a 6xxx parent metal it picks up enough magnesium to respond to solution treatment and ageing. Small 6061 parts welded with 4043 and then fully heat treated can approach T6 strength.

How should ER4043 wire be stored?

Keep it in the sealed bag until use, in a dry, heated store above the dew point. Return part-used spools to a sealed bag or a heated cabinet. Moist or contaminated wire is a leading cause of porosity.

Is ER4043 the same as AlSi5 or S Al 4043?

Yes. AlSi5 is the chemical symbol and S Al 4043 is the ISO 18273 designation for the same alloy. Older European documents use SG-AlSi5 (DIN 1732).
