7075 Aluminium Sheet for Phone Middle Frames and Mid-Plates

7075-T6 and T651 sheet and plate are used for smartphone middle frames, mid-plates and small structural parts because they give the highest strength of the common aluminium alloys in a thin, CNC-machinable section. The challenge is not strength but consistency: flatness, low residual stress and a fine, even grain that anodises without streaks. This guide covers specifications, properties, the anodising trade-offs and what to check when buying.

Overview

Why phone makers use 7075

A phone middle frame is the metal skeleton between the display and the back glass. It carries the screen, battery, boards and buttons, acts as part of the antenna system and must resist bending when the phone is sat on or dropped. Mid-plates are the thin internal plates that stiffen the assembly and spread heat from the processor.

Most aluminium frames are CNC-machined from flat stock, with 60 – 90 % of the metal removed. 7075-T6 has roughly twice the yield strength of 6063 and about 1.8 times that of 6061, so the frame can be thinner and narrower for the same bend resistance. That frees space for a larger battery and keeps weight down.

The cost of that strength is cosmetic. Zinc and copper make 7075 anodise to a slightly darker, more yellow-grey shade than 6xxx alloys, and grain or segregation defects show up more readily as streaks. That is why material for visible frames is bought to tighter internal specifications than general engineering 7075, and why some brands use modified 7xxx alloys with lower copper for bright colours.

What a good phone-frame sheet must deliver

  • Flatness and low residual stress, so thin machined frames do not warp
  • Fine, uniform grain through the thickness for even anodised colour
  • Clean metal with few inclusions that could appear as pits after polishing
  • Consistent properties coil to coil, so CNC programs and anodising recipes stay stable

Finishing

Anodising 7075 frames: what to expect

Colour range

Black, grey, dark blue and other deep colours work well. Bright silver, light gold and pastel shades are harder because of the alloy's natural yellow-grey tint.

Film growth

Sulphuric acid anodising works on 7075, but film quality and dye uptake differ from 6xxx alloys, so bath temperature, current density, sealing and dye control must be qualified for this alloy.

Grain-related defects

Coarse or mixed grain shows as streaks, "orange peel" or bands after chemical polishing. Uniform fine grain in the sheet is the main prevention.

Inclusions and pits

Oxide films and large intermetallic particles pull out during polishing and leave pits. Clean melt practice and filtration upstream reduce them.

Pretreatment

Keep degreasing, etching and chemical polishing times consistent. 7075 etches faster than 6xxx alloys, so recipes cannot simply be copied.

Sample first

Run anodised colour trials on frames machined from the actual lot before releasing production.

Machining

Machining thin 7075 frames without distortion

  1. Start with stress-relieved stockUse T651 for plate thicker than about 6 mm. Stretching removes most quench stress, which is the main cause of frames bowing after the centre is milled out.
  2. Rough symmetricallyRemove material evenly from both faces and leave a small allowance, then release clamps before finishing.
  3. Use aluminium toolingSharp carbide or PCD cutters with high helix and polished flutes, high spindle speeds and flood coolant or MQL give the fine finish needed before anodising.
  4. Control temperatureMachining heat and fixturing can move thin walls; finish passes should be light and consistent.
  5. Inspect flatnessCheck frames on a flat reference or CMM after machining and again after anodising, since film growth slightly changes dimensions.
Applications

Parts made from 7075 in phones and devices

  • Middle frames: the unibody structural ring that supports display, battery and boards
  • Mid-plates: thin internal stiffening and heat-spreading plates
  • SIM trays and side buttons: small parts that must resist bending and wear
  • Camera rings and brackets: precise, stiff parts with cosmetic finishes
  • Tablets, laptops and wearables: frames, hinges and watch cases where strength per gram matters

For back covers and visible housings that need bright colours rather than maximum strength, 5xxx and 6xxx sheet are often a better fit; see our guides to 6013, 6063 and 7021 electronics sheet.

FAQ

7075 phone-frame aluminium (aluminum): frequently asked questions

Why is 7075 used for phone middle frames instead of 6061?

7075-T6 has about 1.8 times the yield strength of 6061-T6, so the frame can be thinner and lighter while resisting bending. 6061 anodises to brighter colours, so it is still chosen for frames where appearance matters more than strength.

Can 7075 be anodised in bright colours?

Dark and deep colours are easy. Bright silver and pale shades are harder because zinc and copper give the oxide a slight yellow-grey tint. Low-copper 7xxx alloys or 6xxx alloys are used where bright colours are essential.

Should I order 7075-T6 or T651 for frames?

Thin sheet is supplied as T6. For plate thicker than about 6 mm that will be heavily machined, order T651, which is stretched to relieve stress and distorts far less.

What thickness of 7075 is used for phone frames?

Frame blanks are commonly machined from roughly 6 – 12 mm plate, depending on frame depth, while mid-plates and small brackets use thinner sheet from about 1.5 mm.

What causes streaks after anodising 7075 frames?

Usually non-uniform grain structure or segregation in the sheet, sometimes combined with inconsistent etching. Specify fine, uniform grain and run colour trials on each new material lot.
