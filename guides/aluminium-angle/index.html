Aluminium Angle Bar: Sizes, Alloys and How to Specify It

Aluminium angle bar is an L-shaped extrusion with two legs at 90°, used for frames, brackets, edge trims and light structures. Most angle is extruded in 6061-T6 for load-bearing work or 6063-T52 for architectural and trim work, in equal or unequal legs. This guide explains how to choose the alloy, leg size, thickness, corner type and finish, and how to estimate weight before you order.

Overview

What is aluminium angle bar?

An aluminium angle is a long section shaped like the letter L. It is described by three numbers: leg A, leg B and thickness, for example 50 × 50 × 5 mm or 2 × 2 × 1/4 in. When both legs are the same length it is an equal angle; when they differ it is an unequal angle.

Almost all aluminium angle is made by hot extrusion: a heated billet is pushed through a steel die with an L-shaped opening, cooled, stretched straight and then aged to its final temper. Thin, light angles can also be roll-formed or press-braked from sheet, but those have a large inside radius and lower strength than extruded sections, and are rarely used for structural work.

Angle is popular because it is the simplest open section that resists bending in two directions. Bolted or welded into a frame it stiffens panels, carries shelves, supports equipment and protects edges, while weighing about a third as much as a steel angle of the same size. Aluminium also needs no galvanising or painting for most indoor and many outdoor uses, because its natural oxide film protects it.

The four decisions that define an angle order

  • Alloy and temper: 6061-T6 for strength, 6063-T52 or T5 for finish and economy, 5xxx or 7xxx for special cases
  • Geometry: equal or unequal legs, leg length, thickness and corner type (structural radius or sharp corner)
  • Length and processing: mill lengths, cut-to-length, drilled, slotted or bent
  • Finish: mill, anodised, powder coated, brushed or polished
Types

Equal, unequal and special angle profiles

Equal leg angle

Both legs the same length. The default for frames, cross-bracing, shelving supports and general fabrication, because it behaves the same whichever leg is fixed and is the most widely available shape.

Unequal leg angle

One leg longer than the other, for example 75 × 50 mm or 3 × 2 in. Used when a long leg must reach a fixing line while a short leg carries a panel, or when bending stiffness is needed mainly in one direction.

Structural angle (radius corners)

Has a fillet radius at the inside root and slightly rounded toes. The root radius reduces stress concentration and makes the section stronger and stiffer. Aluminum Association and ASTM B308 standard angles are this type.

Sharp-corner architectural angle

Nearly square inside and outside corners for a crisp look on trims, edge guards and display work. Usually 6063-T52; slightly weaker at the root, so best for light duty.

Bulb, lipped and stepped angles

Angles with a thickened toe, return lip or step, made on custom dies. They add stiffness to a free edge or create a seat for glass, panels or gaskets.

Double and non-90° angles

Back-to-back double angles and angles at 60°, 120° or 135° for enclosures and facade details. These are custom extrusions; see our double angle extrusions.

As a rule of thumb, choose equal angle unless a fixing detail forces unequal legs, because equal sizes are stocked in more alloys and lengths and usually cost less per kilogram.

6061-T6 vs 6063-T52: 6061-T6 has about twice the yield strength of 6063-T52, so it is the choice whenever the angle carries real load. 6063 extrudes at higher speeds into sharper, thinner shapes with a smoother surface, anodises more evenly and costs less, so it wins for trim, cladding edges, shopfitting and anything where appearance matters more than strength. If you need more strength in 6063, ask for T6 rather than T52.

Engineering

How strong is an aluminium angle?

There is no single "load rating" for an angle, because capacity depends on how it is loaded, how long the span is and how the ends are fixed. Three checks normally govern:

  1. TensionCapacity is roughly section area × allowable stress. A 2 × 2 × 1/4 in 6061-T6 angle has about 0.94 in² of area, so it would yield in pure tension at around 33,000 lb before any safety factor. Bolt holes reduce the net area and must be deducted.
  2. BendingAngles are weak in bending compared with channels or tubes of the same weight, and they twist under load because the shear centre sits at the corner. For a short cantilever, the load at first yield is the yield stress times the elastic section modulus divided by the lever arm. For a 1 × 1 × 1/8 in angle this is in the order of 80 lb at a 12 in arm; for 2 × 2 × 1/4 in it is in the order of 700 lb. Apply your code's safety factors to these figures.
  3. Compression and bucklingLong angles in compression fail by flexural-torsional buckling well below the yield stress. Thin legs (high leg-to-thickness ratio) can also buckle locally. This is why the Aluminum Design Manual and Eurocode 9 (EN 1999-1-1) treat angles with specific slenderness rules.

Two practical consequences follow. First, aluminium's elastic modulus is about one third of steel's (69 GPa vs 200 GPa), so an aluminium angle of the same size deflects about three times as much; stiffness, not strength, often controls the size you need. Second, welding 6061-T6 or 6063-T6 softens the metal near the weld, so welded joints are designed to reduced strength. For critical structures, size the angle with a qualified engineer using the Aluminum Design Manual or EN 1999.

Fabrication

Cut, drilled, slotted, bent and perforated angle

Cut to length

Saw cutting to your list, with typical tolerances of about ±0.5 – 1 mm on short pieces. Mitre cuts are possible for frames. Ordering cut lengths saves freight and offcut waste.

Pre-drilled and slotted

Holes and slots punched or CNC-drilled to drawing, for adjustable racking, solar mounts and modular frames. Keep holes at least 1.5 – 2 hole diameters from the toe and ends to protect edge distance.

Bent and rolled

Angles can be rolled into arcs or bent to a corner. 6063 bends more easily than 6061-T6; tight bends are made in a softer temper (O or T4) and aged afterwards where strength matters.

Perforated

Hole patterns for ventilation, drainage, cable routing or weight saving. Perforation reduces section strength, so check capacity if the angle carries load.

Welding

MIG or TIG welding with ER4043 or ER5356 filler. Expect the heat-affected zone of 6061-T6 to drop to roughly 165 MPa tensile, and allow for it in design.

Machining

6061-T6 and 6082-T6 machine cleanly; 6063-T52 is softer and can be gummy, so use sharp, polished carbide tools.

Applications

Where aluminium angle is used

  • Construction and framing: secondary frames, lintel supports, brackets, partition framing and cladding sub-structures (6061 or 6063)
  • Architectural trim: wall corners, stair nosings, tile trims, edge protection and shopfitting details (6063, anodised or powder coated)
  • Industrial equipment: machine guards, racks, conveyor side frames and equipment bases (6061-T6, 6082-T6)
  • Solar and outdoor structures: panel mounting rails, cross-bracing and enclosure frames, where corrosion resistance and light weight cut installation time
  • Transport: truck bodies, trailer floors and side rails, caravan and bus frames, where weight saving pays back in fuel or payload
  • Marine: deck fittings, hatch frames and stiffeners (5083 or 6061, often anodised)
  • Aerospace: stringers, clips and stiffeners in 2024 and 7075 to AMS specifications
Ordering

How to specify and order aluminium angle

  1. Define the profileEqual or unequal, A × B × t, and corner type (structural radius or sharp corner). Attach a drawing for anything non-standard.
  2. Choose alloy and standardFor example 6061-T6 to ASTM B308, 6063-T52 to ASTM B221, or EN AW-6082-T6 to EN 755-2 with tolerances to EN 755-9.
  3. Set length and processingMill lengths or cut lengths, plus drilling, slotting, bending or machining.
  4. Select finish and packingMill, anodised or coated; interleaved paper or film for finished surfaces; bundles, crates or wooden cases.
  5. Confirm documents and deliveryMill test certificate to EN 10204 3.1, packing list, and terms such as EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP to your port or site.

FAQ

Aluminium (aluminum) angle bar: frequently asked questions

Which is better for aluminum angle, 6061 or 6063?

Use 6061-T6 when the angle carries load: its minimum yield strength (240 MPa / 35 ksi) is roughly double that of 6063-T52. Use 6063 for trims, edges and decorative work, where its smoother surface, sharper corners, better anodising and lower cost matter more than strength.

How do I calculate the weight of an aluminium angle?

Multiply the cross-section area by density. For an angle, area ≈ (A + B − t) × t. In millimetres, kg/m = area × 0.0027; for example 50 × 50 × 5 mm gives 475 mm² and about 1.28 kg/m. In inches, lb/ft ≈ area (in²) × 1.17.

What is the difference between structural and architectural angle?

Structural angle has a radius at the inside corner and is normally 6061-T6 to ASTM B308, so it is stronger at the root. Architectural angle has sharp corners for appearance and is usually 6063-T52, best for light-duty trim and edging.

Can aluminium angle be welded and bent?

Yes. It welds by MIG or TIG with 4043 or 5356 filler, though the area next to the weld loses part of its heat-treated strength. 6063 bends more easily than 6061-T6; for tight radii, bend in a soft temper and age afterwards.

Is aluminium angle strong enough to replace steel angle?

Often, if you size it correctly. 6061-T6 has a yield strength similar to mild steel, but its stiffness is one third of steel's, so a same-size aluminium angle deflects about three times as much. Designers usually pick a deeper or thicker aluminium section and still save around half the weight.

Can you supply angle cut to size with holes?

Yes. Metline supplies angle cut to length, drilled, slotted, bent or perforated to drawing, in mill finish or anodised and powder-coated finishes, with mill test certificates and export packing.
