Thin sheet guide

High-Strength, High-Toughness 7-Series Aluminium Thin Sheet for 3C Electronics

7-series (Al-Zn-Mg and Al-Zn-Mg-Cu) thin sheet in gauges of roughly 0.20 – 0.50 mm gives phones, tablets, laptops and wearables more strength per gram than any 5xxx or 6xxx sheet, so internal plates and frames can be thinner without bending or denting. The difficulty is getting high strength, enough elongation to bend without cracking, and flatness all at once in such thin gauges. This guide explains the alloys, the trade-offs and what to specify; Metline supplies 7-series sheet, strip and plate worldwide with certified test data.

Overview

Why 3C devices use 7-series thin sheet

Computers, communication devices and consumer electronics ("3C") keep getting thinner while battery size and screen area grow. The structure left to hold everything together is a set of thin internal plates, brackets and frames, and those parts must resist bending in a pocket, survive drops and keep their shape under heat.

The 7xxx series contains zinc and magnesium (and in the strongest grades, copper) and responds strongly to precipitation hardening. In the T6 temper, 7075 sheet is roughly twice as strong as 6061-T6 and about two and a half times as strong as 5052-H32, at a density of only about 2.8 g/cm³. For a plate that must not yield when a device is flexed, that strength allows a large reduction in thickness, or more stiffness and dent resistance at the same thickness.

Aluminium also has properties that matter specifically in electronics. It is non-magnetic, so it does not disturb compasses, wireless charging coils or magnetic attachments. It conducts heat several times better than stainless steel, helping spread heat from processors and batteries. And it is far lighter than steel for the same stiffness-to-weight design.

Requirements typically set for 3C thin sheet

  • Lighter: low density to cut device weight
  • Stronger: high yield strength so thin parts do not take a permanent set
  • Tougher: enough elongation to bend and form without cracking
  • Thinner: reliable supply in gauges of about 0.20 – 0.50 mm
  • Stable: flat sheet and consistent properties from coil to coil

7075 composition limits (Aluminum Association) are zinc 5.1 – 6.1 %, magnesium 2.1 – 2.9 %, copper 1.2 – 2.0 % and chromium 0.18 – 0.28 %, with silicon ≤ 0.40 % and iron ≤ 0.50 %. Lower-copper 7xxx grades trade some strength for brighter, more uniform anodising and better resistance to some forms of corrosion.

Properties

Strength, elongation and the thin-gauge challenge

Published typical values for 7075-T6 are about 572 MPa tensile and 503 MPa yield with around 11 % elongation, measured on thicker sheet. In very thin gauges, the specified minimums in ASTM B209 are lower and elongation falls: thin 7075-T6 sheet is guaranteed at roughly 510 – 525 MPa tensile and 430 – 460 MPa yield, with minimum elongation in the single figures. Check the thickness band in the standard for your exact gauge.

Many 3C specifications ask for a combination such as tensile strength above about 520 MPa, yield above about 460 MPa, elongation above 10 % and a 90° bend without cracking at 0.2 – 0.5 mm. That is achievable only with tight control of composition, rolling and heat treatment, and it sits at or beyond the minimums of the general standards. Treat such figures as a customer specification: write them on the order and require tensile and bend test results for every coil, rather than assuming a standard 7075-T6 certificate covers them.

Higher strength usually means lower ductility and toughness. Over-aged tempers such as T76 or T73 give up some strength for better toughness and resistance to stress-corrosion cracking; they are worth considering where parts are bent or stay under stress.

Production

Why thin high-strength 7-series sheet is hard to make

Rolling

7xxx alloys work-harden quickly and are prone to edge cracking, so rolling down to 0.2 mm needs more passes and intermediate anneals than soft alloys.

Solution heat treatment

Thin sheet heats quickly, but must be quenched fast and evenly; uneven cooling causes distortion and property variation.

Flatness

Quench distortion is severe in thin gauges. Tension levelling or stretching is needed, and it must not use up too much of the elongation.

Grain control

Zirconium or chromium additions and controlled processing keep grains fine, which supports both bendability and cosmetic quality.

Ageing

Artificial ageing must be uniform along the coil, or strength varies from one end to the other.

Consistency

Small shifts in zinc, magnesium or copper move strength and elongation noticeably, so tight chemistry and lot control matter.

Fabrication

Forming, machining and finishing thin 7-series sheet

7075-T6 has limited room-temperature formability. Bend radii of several times the thickness are normally needed, and bends across the rolling direction perform better than bends along it. Where parts need deeper forms, three approaches are common:

  1. Form in the O temper, then heat treatMaximum formability, but parts must be solution treated, quenched and aged afterwards, with fixtures to control distortion.
  2. Form in the W temperForm shortly after solution treatment, while the alloy is still soft, then age. The W temper hardens naturally at room temperature, so timing or refrigeration is needed.
  3. Form in T6 with generous radiiSimplest route for gentle bends and shallow features; follow the minimum bend radius for the gauge.

Stamping and blanking

Use sharp, tight-clearance tools; 7075 shears cleanly but burrs harden. Fine blanking gives the best edges.

CNC machining

Excellent machinability in T6; clamp thin sheet on vacuum fixtures to avoid vibration and distortion.

Joining

Not suitable for fusion welding. Use rivets, screws, clinching, adhesives or laser-welded steel inserts in designs that allow it.

Anodising

7075 anodises to a slightly yellowish tone; choose 7021 or 7003 where cosmetic anodising matters.

Applications

Typical 3C applications

  • Smartphones: internal support plates, display back plates, camera and battery brackets
  • Laptops and tablets: keyboard support plates, lid stiffeners, hinge brackets, internal frames
  • Wearables: small high-strength structural parts where every gram counts
  • Cameras and drones: stiff, light internal plates and frames
  • Thermal parts: plates that also spread heat from chips and batteries
  • Shielding and covers: thin, non-magnetic covers that resist denting

Metline supplies the 7-series sheet, strip and plate; stamping, machining and finishing of the parts are carried out by the device maker or its component suppliers. For machined middle frames, thicker 7xxx plate or extrusion is normally used rather than thin sheet.

Ordering

How to specify 7-series thin sheet

  1. Alloy and temperFor example 7075-T6, 7075-O for forming then heat treatment, or a low-copper 7xxx for cosmetic parts.
  2. Gauge and tolerancesThickness (e.g. 0.30 mm) with tolerance, width, coil ID or sheet size, and flatness.
  3. Mechanical requirementsMinimum tensile, yield and elongation, and the bend test (angle, radius, direction).
  4. SurfaceSurface class, protective film, and anodising quality if the part is cosmetic.
  5. Standard and documentsASTM B209, EN 485 or AMS 4045 as the base, plus your specification; EN 10204 3.1 certificate with test results per coil.
  6. Packing and deliveryInterleaved sheets or coils in sealed export cases; EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.

Common mistakes include specifying 7075-T6 and then designing bends that only an O-temper part could make, relying on typical values from thick sheet for a 0.25 mm gauge, and choosing 7075 for a cosmetic anodised face.

FAQ

7-series aluminium (aluminum) thin sheet: frequently asked questions

How thin can 7075 aluminium sheet be supplied?

Thin 7xxx sheet and strip are produced down to about 0.2 mm. Availability of very thin gauges depends on alloy, temper and width, and properties should be confirmed by test for each gauge.

What strength does thin 7075-T6 sheet have?

Typical 7075-T6 values are about 572 MPa tensile and 503 MPa yield. Specified minimums for thin gauges in ASTM B209 are lower, roughly 510 – 525 MPa tensile and 430 – 460 MPa yield, and elongation is reduced.

Can 7075-T6 thin sheet be bent 90 degrees?

Yes, with an adequate bend radius, which for 7075-T6 is several times the thickness. For tighter bends, form in the O or W temper and age afterwards, and verify with a bend test on the actual gauge.

Why use 7-series aluminium instead of stainless steel in phones?

For the same weight, aluminium parts can be much thicker and stiffer, and 7-series aluminium is non-magnetic and conducts heat better. Stainless steel is denser and is chosen mainly where its higher hardness or modulus outweighs the weight.

Is 7021 better than 7075 for 3C parts?

7021 is less strong than 7075 but anodises more uniformly and with less colour tint, so it suits cosmetic frames and housings. 7075 is preferred for hidden structural parts that need maximum strength.

Do you supply 7-series sheet worldwide?

Yes. Metline supplies 7075 and other 7xxx sheet, strip and plate worldwide, cut to size, with EN 10204 3.1 certificates and export packing.
