Carbon Coated Aluminium Foil: How It Works and How to Specify It

Carbon coated aluminium foil is battery-grade aluminium foil, typically 12 – 20 µm thick, with a conductive layer of carbon black, graphite or graphene about 0.5 – 3 µm thick on one or both sides. Used as the cathode current collector in lithium-ion cells and in some capacitors, it lowers the contact resistance between foil and electrode coating, improves adhesion and protects the aluminium from electrolyte attack. This guide explains the benefits, substrate and coating options, test methods and buying checklist; Metline supplies bare and carbon coated battery foil to cell and electrode makers worldwide.

Overview

What carbon coated foil is and why cells use it

In a lithium-ion cell the cathode active material, such as lithium iron phosphate (LFP) or a nickel-manganese-cobalt oxide (NCM), is mixed with a conductive additive and binder and spread onto aluminium foil. The foil carries electrons in and out of the electrode. Aluminium is used on the cathode side because it is light, cheap, highly conductive and protected by a passive film at the high potentials of the positive electrode.

That same passive film is also a problem. Aluminium oxide is an electrical insulator, and the contact between hard oxide particles and a smooth, oxidised foil is made at relatively few points. The result is a contact resistance at the foil-electrode interface that adds to cell impedance, generates heat at high current and can grow as the cell ages and the electrode loosens.

A thin carbon primer solves much of this. The carbon particles bond to the foil through a binder, penetrate the thin oxide at many points and present a rough, conductive surface that the electrode coating grips firmly. Peer-reviewed studies have shown a clear reduction in contact resistance for LFP cathodes on carbon coated current collectors, which is why the coated foil is now standard in many LFP and high-power cell designs.

Benefits reported for carbon coated current collectors

  • Lower interfacial contact resistance and lower cell internal resistance
  • Reduced polarisation, so better rate capability and less heat at high current
  • Stronger adhesion of the electrode coating, allowing less binder or thicker electrodes
  • Protection of the aluminium from corrosion by electrolyte, particularly at higher voltages
  • More consistent cells and longer cycle life, especially for LFP and lithium titanate systems
Base foil

The aluminium substrate

The base is a high-purity 1xxx-series foil in full-hard H18 temper. The hard temper gives the tensile strength needed to run the foil through coating, drying, calendering and slitting lines at speed without breaks or wrinkles. Typical alloys are 1235, 1060, 1070 and 1100; 1100 and certain modified 1235 grades give higher strength for very thin gauges.

Coating materials

Carbon black, graphite and graphene coatings

CoatingCharacterTypical use
Conductive carbon blackFine particles with high surface area; low cost; forms a uniform, thin layerGeneral LFP and NCM cathodes, mainstream coated foil
Graphite (flake)Plate-like particles lie flat and give good in-plane conduction and adhesionHigh-power cells, thicker coatings

Carbon nanotube blendsFibrous conductive networkHigh-rate and specialty cells

Typical coating parameters

Coating thickness
About 0.3 – 3 µm per side; 1 – 2 µm is common
Coating areal density
Roughly 0.5 – 2.0 g/m² per side
Sides
One side or both sides
Pattern
Continuous, or intermittent (skip) coating leaving bare tab areas
Edge margin
Uncoated edges for tab welding, for example a 230 mm coat on 260 mm foil
Total thickness
For example 16 µm foil + 1 µm per side ≈ 18 µm

Thicker carbon layers lower contact resistance further but add mass and volume that store no energy. Most cell makers choose the thinnest coating that reliably covers the foil and passes adhesion tests.

Production

How carbon coated foil is produced

  1. Casting and rollingHigh-purity aluminium is cast into coil and cold-rolled, with intermediate annealing, then foil-rolled to the final gauge in H18 temper.
  2. CleaningRolling oil is removed and the surface is treated so the dyne level meets the specification.
  3. Slurry preparationCarbon, binder and solvent (usually water) are dispersed into a stable, fine slurry.
  4. CoatingGravure, micro-gravure or slot-die coating applies a thin, even film, continuous or patterned.
  5. Drying and curingThe coated foil passes through an oven so the binder cures and the coating bonds to the foil.
  6. Inspection and slittingOnline thickness and defect inspection, then slitting to width and rewinding on clean cores.

The main production challenges are keeping the coating uniform across the width, avoiding pinholes and streaks, preventing foil breaks at very thin gauges, and making sure the binder will not swell or dissolve when the cathode slurry (often based on NMP) is applied on top.

The real proof is in the cell. Ask for, or run, impedance (EIS) and rate-capability comparisons between electrodes on bare and coated foil made from the same slurry. Gains vary with the active material and electrode design, so published laboratory figures should be read as indicative only.

Applications

Where carbon coated foil is used

LFP cells

LFP has low intrinsic conductivity, so reducing contact resistance gives clear gains. Coated foil is widely used in LFP for energy storage, buses and passenger EVs.

High-power NCM and LMO cells

Power tools, hybrid vehicles and start-stop batteries benefit from lower impedance at high current.

Lithium titanate (LTO) cells

Fast-charging, long-life cells where low resistance is essential.

Energy storage systems

Lower resistance means less heat and higher round-trip efficiency in stationary batteries.

Supercapacitors and solid polymer capacitors

Carbon coated foil can lower equivalent series resistance and improve adhesion of activated-carbon electrodes.

Emerging chemistries

Lithium-sulphur and sodium-ion research cells use coated aluminium collectors to protect the foil and improve contact.

For energy-optimised NCM cells at moderate rates, bare foil is often adequate. For LFP, high-power and long-life designs, the coated foil usually pays for itself through lower resistance and better yield.

Ordering

What to specify when buying

  • Substrate alloy, temper and thickness, with tolerance
  • Width, coated width and edge margins, or the intermittent coating pattern
  • Coating material, thickness or areal density per side, and one- or two-sided
  • Resistance target and test method
  • Adhesion, solvent and electrolyte resistance tests and acceptance levels
  • Roll length, core size, outer diameter and splice rules
  • Packing: vacuum-sealed or nitrogen-flushed bags with desiccant, in clean wooden cases
  • Documentation: mill certificate for the substrate and coating test report for each lot

FAQ

Carbon coated aluminium (aluminum) foil: FAQ

What is carbon coated aluminium foil used for?

Mainly as the cathode current collector in lithium-ion batteries, especially LFP and high-power cells, and in some supercapacitors. The carbon layer lowers contact resistance and improves adhesion of the electrode.

How thick is the carbon coating?

Usually about 0.5 – 3 µm per side, with 1 – 2 µm most common. That corresponds to roughly 0.5 – 2 g/m² per side, depending on the carbon type and binder.

Which aluminium alloy is used as the substrate?

High-purity 1235, 1060, 1070 or 1100 in H18 temper, typically 12 – 20 µm thick. Higher-strength grades are used for the thinnest gauges.

Does the coating reduce energy density?

Slightly, because it adds a few micrometres and grams per square metre that store no energy. In practice the lower resistance and possible reduction in binder often offset this, especially in power-oriented cells.

Can tabs be welded to coated foil?

Tabs are normally welded to uncoated areas. Specify uncoated edge margins or an intermittent coating pattern that leaves bare foil where tabs will be attached.

How should carbon coated foil be stored?

In its sealed packaging, in a dry, clean room at moderate temperature. Moisture and dust can affect the coating and the subsequent electrode coating step, so open rolls only just before use.

Qualifying coated foil for a new cell?

Send your substrate and coating specification. Metline will reply with matching foil, sample rolls and test data.
