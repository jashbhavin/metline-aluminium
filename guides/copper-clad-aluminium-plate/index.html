Copper Clad Aluminium Plate & Sheet

Copper clad aluminium plate is a laminate in which copper is bonded to one or both faces of an aluminium plate, giving a copper surface for electrical contact or soldering and an aluminium body for low weight. It is supplied as one-side (Cu/Al), two-side (Cu/Al/Cu) and reverse (Al/Cu/Al) constructions, typically 0.5 – 15 mm thick, in sheets, blanks and stamped parts. Metline supplies copper clad aluminium plate to agreed layer ratios with bond and conductivity test certificates to buyers worldwide.

Overview

What copper clad aluminium plate is used for

Copper and aluminium are the two metals of electrical engineering, and they are often in the same assembly: copper terminals on equipment, aluminium conductors and housings for weight. Joining them directly causes trouble. They cannot be fusion welded without brittle intermetallics, and a bare bolted joint between them corrodes in damp air. A clad plate solves the problem by doing the difficult joint once, in a controlled rolling mill, so that every part cut from it has a copper face and an aluminium face that can each be joined by ordinary methods.

Copper clad aluminium plate therefore has two main jobs. The first is as a transition material: a washer, block, tab or connector that sits between copper and aluminium conductors. The second is as a lightweight conductor or heat spreader with a copper working surface, where solid copper would be heavier and costlier than necessary.

This page covers plate and sheet. For long rectangular conductors with copper around all four sides, see copper clad aluminium bus bar; for thin coils and slit strip, see copper clad aluminium strip.

Typical uses

  • Battery cell and module connectors joining aluminium and copper cell terminals
  • Copper-aluminium transition plates, washers and earthing blocks in substations and switchgear
  • Connectors and terminals for aluminium cable and busbar systems
  • Heat spreaders and bases where a copper face meets the heat source and aluminium spreads and sheds heat
  • Electrolysis and electroplating equipment where large currents pass between copper and aluminium

The ratio is stated as a percentage of total thickness. It sets the conductivity, weight and cost of the plate, and it controls what each face can tolerate: a thin copper layer can be broken through by deep stamping, heavy machining or grinding, so the copper thickness should be chosen with the forming process in mind. Two-side constructions are symmetrical, so they stay flat when heated, whereas a one-side laminate bows slightly with temperature because aluminium expands about 40 % more than copper.

For electrical work the combination of C11000 copper with 1060 or 1070 aluminium is standard: both are soft, conductive and bond well in roll bonding. Alloyed aluminium grades are stronger but bond less readily and conduct less, so they are reserved for parts where mechanical performance comes first.

Mechanical properties depend on the temper of each layer and on the ratio. Annealed (O) laminates are soft and very formable; hard-rolled laminates (H14 – H18 range for the aluminium) are stiffer and suited to flat parts. Because there is no single product standard that sets mechanical minimums for every combination, the agreed values for tensile strength, elongation and bond strength should be written into the order and reported on the certificate.

Manufacturing

How the plate is bonded, and how much heat it tolerates

  1. Surface preparationCopper and aluminium strips are degreased and wire-brushed to break the oxide film and roughen the surface.
  2. Roll bondingThe layers are stacked and rolled with a large single-pass reduction, often 50 % or more, so fresh metal is forced into contact and cold-welds.
  3. Diffusion annealA controlled anneal strengthens the bond by short-range diffusion and restores ductility, without growing a thick intermetallic layer.
  4. Finish rollingFurther rolling brings the laminate to final thickness and temper; flattening and cutting follow.
  5. Explosion bonding (thick plate)For very thick or large plates, explosion bonding joins the layers in one event with a characteristic wavy interface.

Copper and aluminium react above roughly 200 – 250 °C to form intermetallic compounds such as Al2Cu. Research on roll-bonded Cu/Al shows that a thin diffusion layer gives the best bond, while long heating, especially above about 400 °C, produces a thick brittle layer and a sharp loss of peel strength. For users this means: soldering is fine, short laser or ultrasonic welds on one face are fine if heat input is controlled, but brazing, furnace treatments and long service above about 200 °C need to be discussed before ordering.

Thickness tolerance is normally agreed on the total thickness and on each layer, for example ±0.05 mm on a 2 mm laminate and a layer ratio tolerance of a few percentage points. Flatness and camber are agreed for blanked parts. When a drawing calls for a copper face of minimum thickness after machining, state it explicitly so the laminate is built with enough copper.

Fabrication

Cutting, stamping, bending and joining

Shearing and blanking

Clean shear edges with sharp tools and small clearance. Blank with the copper face toward the punch if burr on the copper side must be minimal.

Stamping and bending

Annealed laminates bend to 90° and beyond without cracking or separation at sensible radii. Bend with the copper on the outside of the bend only after checking that the copper layer will not thin excessively.

Machining

Machines like soft copper and pure aluminium: sharp, polished tools, high rake. Remember the copper layer is thin; facing cuts can remove it.

Joining the copper face

Solder, bolt, crimp, ultrasonic- or laser-weld to copper. Tin plating improves solderability and long-term contact resistance.

Joining the aluminium face

Ultrasonic, laser or resistance-weld to aluminium tabs or bars; TIG/MIG only where heat input is low and far from the interface.

Cut edges

Edges expose the copper-aluminium interface. In damp service, coat or seal edges to stop galvanic corrosion at the boundary.

FAQ

Copper clad aluminium (aluminum) plate: FAQ

What is the difference between Cu/Al and Cu/Al/Cu plate?

Cu/Al has copper on one face and aluminium on the other, ideal for transition parts. Cu/Al/Cu has copper on both faces, so both sides can be soldered or bolted to copper, and it stays flat when heated because the structure is symmetrical.

What copper ratio is most common?

For one-side plate, 10 – 20 % copper by thickness is common; for two-side plate, 10/80/10. Higher copper fractions are used for thicker contact faces or higher conductivity.

Can copper clad aluminum plate be bent without delaminating?

Yes. Properly bonded annealed laminate bends to 90° and more without cracking or separation at sensible radii. A bend test is part of normal inspection.

Can the plate be welded?

Each face can be joined to its own metal by ultrasonic, laser or resistance welding with controlled heat. Avoid brazing or long heating, which grows brittle intermetallic compounds at the interface.

Is there a standard for copper clad aluminium plate?

There is no single international product standard for roll-bonded Cu/Al plate. Orders are placed to an agreed specification covering layer ratio, thickness tolerance, temper, bond and bend tests and conductivity, with the component metals referenced to ASTM B152 and ASTM B209 or their EN equivalents.

How much does copper clad aluminium plate weigh?

Multiply thickness in mm by the laminate density: about 3.32 kg/m² per mm at 10 % copper, 3.63 at 15 % and 3.94 at 20 %, compared with 8.89 kg/m² per mm for solid copper.
