6951 Aluminium Alloy for Brazed Heat Exchangers

6951 is a heat-treatable Al-Mg-Si-Cu alloy designed to survive brazing. Its low silicon keeps the solidus near 616 °C, above the melting range of Al-Si filler alloys, and because it age-hardens it can be strengthened after the brazing cycle. That makes it a standard core alloy for clad brazing sheet and a fin alloy for radiators, condensers, evaporators and charge-air coolers.

Overview

Why 6951 exists

Most 6xxx alloys are built for extrusion and structural strength. 6951 is built for one process: furnace brazing, where an assembly is heated to around 590 – 605 °C so that a thin Al-Si cladding melts and flows into every joint.

To come through that cycle the core alloy must not melt or be heavily eroded by the liquid filler. 6951 keeps silicon at 0.20 – 0.50 %, well below 6061 or 6063, which raises its solidus to about 616 °C and leaves a working margin above the filler's melting range. Its copper addition makes the core electrochemically slightly more noble than the clad layer, which helps the filler-side surface corrode sacrificially rather than the core.

The other advantage is heat treatment. The brazing cycle itself acts as a solution treatment, and when the assembly is cooled reasonably quickly and then aged (naturally or in an oven), Mg2Si precipitates and the core gains strength. Non-heat-treatable cores such as 3003 cannot do this, so 6951 is chosen where a thinner, lighter or more pressure-resistant brazed part is wanted.

Where 6951 is specified

  • Core of clad brazing sheet for radiators, condensers, evaporators and oil coolers
  • Heat-treatable fin stock that gains strength after brazing
  • Headers, tanks and plates in vacuum- or flux-brazed exchangers
  • Bar and machined fittings that are brazed into exchanger assemblies

Product forms

6951 brazing sheet configurations

Brazing sheet is a roll-bonded composite: a core alloy with a thin Al-Si filler layer on one or both sides, sometimes with an additional water-side liner. Clad thickness is usually a small percentage of total thickness per side.

LayoutTypical layersUse

Multi-layerFiller / interlayer / 6951 / linerDesigns needing extra erosion or corrosion protection

Filler choice sets the brazing window: 4343 (about 7 – 8 % Si) and 4045 (about 9 – 11 % Si) are used in flux brazing, 4004 and 4104 in vacuum brazing. 4047 (about 12 % Si) has the lowest melting range and is often used for wire or paste rather than cladding. Heat-treatable fins resist crushing and fin-collapse better than 3003 fins of the same gauge, so they allow thinner fins and lighter cores. The trade-off is that Mg in the fin can interfere with flux brazing, so 6951 fins are most common in vacuum-brazed exchangers or with flux systems adapted to Mg-bearing alloys.

Other forms

Bar, plate and machined parts

6951 is also supplied as bare sheet, plate and bar for headers, side plates, manifolds, fittings and machined blocks that are brazed into an exchanger. Using the same alloy family as the core simplifies the brazing cycle and the post-braze heat treatment. For bar and rod, see 6951 bar; for clad core material in sheet, coil and fin, see 6951 brazing core.

Processing

Brazing and post-braze ageing tips

  1. CleanDegrease formed parts thoroughly; residual oil causes porosity and poor fillets.
  2. Assemble and fixtureKeep clearances small and consistent so the filler can flow by capillary action.
  3. BrazeHeat to the filler's brazing temperature (typically around 590 – 605 °C) for the shortest time that gives full fillets; long times increase core erosion.
  4. CoolFaster cooling after brazing retains more Mg and Si in solution and gives a stronger aged part.
  5. AgeNatural ageing at room temperature gives a modest rise; an oven ageing step gives the highest strength. Validate the cycle on sample parts.

Common mistakes include using a 6951 core with a high-Mg design in a flux brazing line without adapting the flux, brazing too hot or too long (core erosion), and assuming as-brazed strength equals T6 strength without ageing.

FAQ

6951 aluminium (aluminum): FAQ

What is 6951 aluminium used for?

Mainly as the core of clad brazing sheet and as fin stock in brazed heat exchangers such as radiators, condensers, evaporators and oil coolers, plus bar and plate for brazed fittings.

Why is 6951 used instead of 6061 for brazing?

6061 has more silicon and a lower solidus, close to brazing temperature, so it risks melting. 6951's low silicon keeps its solidus about 616 °C, giving a safe margin.

Can 6951 be heat-treated after brazing?

Yes. The brazing cycle acts as a solution treatment; after reasonably fast cooling, natural or artificial ageing raises strength toward T6 levels.

Which filler alloys are clad on 6951?

4343 or 4045 for flux brazing and 4004 or 4104 for vacuum brazing, sometimes with a 7072 water-side liner.

What is the EN designation of 6951?

EN AW-6951, chemical symbol EN AW-AlMgSi0.3Cu, under EN 573-3.
