8079 Colour Coated Aluminium Foil for Roof Waterproofing

8079 roofing foil is a soft, ductile aluminium-iron alloy foil, typically 0.04 – 0.15 mm thick, coated with polyester (PE) or PVDF paint and laminated as the exposed top surface of bituminous waterproofing membranes, self-adhesive flashing tapes and insulation facings. The foil protects the bitumen from UV, reflects solar heat and gives the roof its colour, while 8079's high elongation lets the membrane flex with the building. This guide covers coatings, specifications, applications, quality checks and how to buy.

Overview

What 8079 coated roofing foil is used for

Bitumen is an excellent waterproofing material but it ages quickly in sunlight. Ultraviolet radiation oxidises the surface, which hardens and cracks, and a dark membrane can exceed 70 °C on a summer roof. A thin, factory-bonded aluminium foil on top solves both problems: it blocks UV completely and, when bright or light-coloured, reflects much of the solar heat.

8079 is the alloy most often chosen for that foil. It contains about 0.7 – 1.3 % iron and a little silicon, which give a fine grain and high elongation in soft tempers. A membrane is rolled, unrolled, folded at upstands and moved by thermal cycling and building movement for decades; a foil that cracks on folding would let water and UV reach the bitumen. 8079 stretches and creases without cracking better than most packaging foils at the same thickness, which is why membrane makers specify it.

The foil is almost always coated. The coating protects the aluminium from alkaline run-off, acid rain and salt, gives a durable colour, and on the underside provides a primer that bonds to the bitumen compound. On self-adhesive flashing tapes and vapour control layers the same foil is used, sometimes uncoated on the inside face.

Why membrane makers choose 8079

  • High elongation and fold resistance at 0.04 – 0.15 mm
  • Complete UV and moisture barrier for the bitumen beneath
  • Takes PE and PVDF coatings with good adhesion and flexibility
  • Light weight: a 0.08 mm foil weighs about 0.22 kg/m² before coating

Coating systems

PE, PVDF and lacquer coatings compared

The coating decides how the foil weathers, so it should be chosen for the climate and the design life of the roof rather than on price alone.

Coating suppliers also offer modified formulations, such as high-durability polyesters and nano-modified PVDF finishes with improved dirt resistance. Their performance claims should be backed by test data on the actual foil system, not on thicker coil.

Applications

Applications in building waterproofing and insulation

Torch-on and mop-applied membranes

Coated foil laminated as the cap-sheet surface of SBS or APP modified bitumen membranes on flat and low-slope roofs.

Self-adhesive membranes and tapes

Foil-faced self-adhesive bitumen or butyl tapes for flashing, gutters, skylights, upstands and roof repairs.

Basements, tunnels and bridges

Foil-faced membranes used where a robust moisture and vapour barrier is needed, often protected by backfill or screed.

Roof insulation facings

Coated foil on PIR, phenolic or mineral wool boards and blankets as a weathering and vapour control face.

External wall systems

Weather-resistant facings and flashing details around openings and parapets.

Ducts and service areas

Coated foil on duct insulation and heat shields in plant rooms and kitchens, where the foil reflects radiant heat and protects the insulation.

In membrane systems, typical laying practice is to overlap sheets by about 75 – 100 mm on side laps and more on end laps, staggered between layers, following the membrane maker's instructions. The foil surface should not be walked on heavily or punctured during installation.

How the foil improves roof performance

  • UV protection: aluminium is opaque to UV, so the bitumen beneath does not oxidise and crack
  • Lower surface temperature: a bright or white surface reflects much more sunlight than black bitumen, reducing heat flow into the building and thermal stress in the membrane
  • Moisture barrier: the foil is effectively impermeable to water vapour, which helps when the membrane also serves as a vapour control layer
  • Weather and chemical resistance: PVDF and PE coatings resist acid rain, salt and airborne pollution
  • Fire behaviour: a metal surface does not burn and can improve the fire performance of a membrane system; the system classification (for example EN 13501-5) must be tested on the complete product
  • Appearance: colours can match tiles, metal roofing or a brand palette
Process

From 8079 foil stock to coated roofing foil

  1. Foil rolling and annealing8079 is rolled to final gauge and annealed to O, or partly annealed to H22/H24; annealing also burns off rolling oil.
  2. Pre-treatmentThe surface is cleaned and given a chemical conversion treatment so coatings bond reliably.
  3. CoatingPrimer and top coats are applied by roll coating, one or both sides, at controlled film thickness.
  4. CuringThe coated foil passes through an oven; PE systems typically cure at lower temperatures than PVDF, which needs around 230 – 250 °C peak metal temperature in coil-coating practice.
  5. Inspection and slittingFilm thickness, colour, gloss and adhesion are checked; the foil is slit to width and rewound.
  6. PackingRolls are wrapped against moisture, packed with edge protection on pallets or in wooden cases.

8079 compared with other roofing foil alloys

The choice is about how the foil will be stressed. A cap sheet is unrolled, cut, folded into corners and upstands, and then moved by daily temperature swings for decades, so elongation and fold resistance matter more than tensile strength. An insulation facing lies flat inside a roof, where tear resistance during laminating is more important. That is why membrane makers lean towards 8079 and insulation laminators often use 8011 or 1xxx foil.

Handling

Storage and handling of coated roofing foil

  • Store rolls indoors, dry, off the floor and away from direct sunlight and heat sources
  • Keep rolls in their original moisture-barrier wrap until use; condensation between wraps causes white corrosion spots on uncoated faces
  • Allow rolls shipped from a cold hold to reach room temperature before unwrapping, to avoid condensation
  • Handle by the core, never by the outer wraps, and store eye-to-sky on pallets or hung on core supports
  • Use coated foil within the period recommended by the coating supplier, typically checking adhesion if stock is old
  • Record coil numbers on finished membrane batches so any issue can be traced back to the foil lot

On the laminating line, keep unwind tension low and even. Thin soft foil necks or wrinkles under high tension, which spoils the appearance of the finished membrane and can create stress points where cracks begin later.

FAQ

8079 roofing foil: frequently asked questions

Why is 8079 used for roofing membranes instead of 8011?

8079 has a finer grain and higher elongation in soft tempers, so it folds and stretches with the membrane without cracking. 8011 is stronger but less tolerant of repeated folding at thin gauges.

Should I choose PE or PVDF coated aluminum foil?

Choose PVDF where the roof is exposed for many years to strong sun, salt or pollution, because it keeps its colour and gloss far longer. PE is a cost-effective choice for moderate climates and shorter design lives.

What thickness of foil is used on bitumen membranes?

Most foil-faced cap sheets use roughly 0.06 – 0.08 mm foil. Self-adhesive flashing tapes and heavier-duty products use up to about 0.15 mm.

Does the foil make the roof cooler?

A bright or light-coloured foil reflects much more solar radiation than black bitumen, lowering the surface temperature and the heat entering the building. Dark coated colours give less benefit.

Can you match an existing colour?

Yes. Colours can be matched to RAL or Pantone references or to a physical sample, with an agreed colour-difference tolerance.

What documents come with 8079 foil?

Each lot ships with an EN 10204 3.1 mill certificate for chemistry and properties, and coating test results such as film thickness, T-bend and colour can be included.

Can uncoated 8079 foil be used on an exposed roof?

It is not recommended. Bare foil exposed to rain, alkaline run-off from concrete or masonry, and pollution will corrode and lose its brightness. Use at least a clear lacquer, or PE or PVDF where the surface stays exposed for many years.
