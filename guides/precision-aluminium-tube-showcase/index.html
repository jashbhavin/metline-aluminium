7 Precision Drawn Aluminium Tube Specifications, Explained

Precision drawn aluminium tube is extruded and then cold drawn over a mandrel to tight diameter and wall tolerances with a smooth, bright surface. The clearest way to see how it is specified is through real-world specification types. Below are seven typical orders, from thin-wall 2024-T3 aerospace tube to large-diameter 5083 marine tube, with what each specification line means and why it was chosen.

Overview

What makes a tube "precision drawn"?

A drawn tube starts as an extruded or pierced hollow. It is then pulled through a die while a mandrel or plug supports the bore, reducing diameter and wall in one or more passes. Cold drawing tightens dimensions, improves surface finish and, in non-heat-treatable alloys, raises strength by cold work.

The result is tube that is rounder, straighter and more uniform in wall than extruded tube, with a surface that often needs no further finishing. It is the right material when the tube is a precision component in its own right: a telescopic section, a hydraulic or pneumatic barrel, an instrument housing, an aircraft fluid line or a part that will be machined with minimal stock removal.

Specification examples

Seven typical precision tube specifications

Each example below is a typical specification pattern for its application, not a particular customer order. Weights are calculated from nominal dimensions at the density of each alloy.

1. 2024-T3, 25 × 2.5 mm, aerospace

Seamless drawn tube to ASTM B210 and AMS-WW-T-700/3 for aircraft structures and fittings. T3 (solution treated, cold worked, naturally aged) gives high strength and good fatigue resistance. Cut to short lengths for machining.

2. 2024-T4, 34 × 1.0 mm, drone frames

Thin-wall seamless tube with a diameter-to-wall ratio of 34. Used for UAV arms and frames where stiffness per gram matters. Wall tolerance is the key line: thin walls must stay uniform for consistent stiffness.

3. 2017-T4, 42 × 1.0 mm, CNC parts

Free-machining Al-Cu tube for turned sleeves and housings. Close OD and wall tolerances reduce the stock that must be removed and make automated bar-fed machining reliable.

4. 5083, 60 × 5.0 mm, marine

Heavy-wall cold drawn seamless tube for marine fittings, railings and offshore equipment. 5083 resists seawater and keeps strength after welding; long 6 m lengths reduce joints.

5. 6005A-T6, 64 – 115 × 3.5 mm, machinery

A family of three diameters with the same 3.5 mm wall, drawn to EN 754 for mechanical components. 6005A combines good strength with excellent surface finish after drawing.

6. 5052-O, 90 × 1.5 mm, aerospace ducting

Annealed thin-wall tube for formed ducts and fluid lines. The O temper allows bending, flaring and swaging without cracking.

7. 5083-O, 166 – 219 × 3.0 mm, defence

Large-diameter thin-wall seamless tube for housings and structural shells. Roundness and straightness over long lengths are the hardest requirements at this size.

Process

How precision drawn tube is made

  1. Melting and castingAlloy is melted, degassed and filtered, and cast into billets with a fine, uniform structure.
  2. Extrusion or piercingBillets are extruded into a hollow; for seamless tube, a pierced billet or mandrel extrusion avoids weld seams.
  3. Cold drawingSeveral passes through dies over a mandrel or plug reduce OD and wall, improve tolerance and polish the surface.
  4. Annealing and heat treatmentIntermediate anneals restore ductility between passes; heat-treatable alloys are solution treated and aged to final temper.
  5. Straightening and cuttingRoller or stretch straightening, then cutting to length and deburring.
  6. Inspection and packingDimensions, surface and mechanical properties are checked; tubes are protected against scratches and packed for export.
Applications

Where precision aluminium tube is used

  • Aerospace and defence: fluid lines, radar and antenna parts, seat structures and housings
  • Medical equipment: wheelchairs, stretchers, instrument parts with a clean, smooth finish
  • Automotive: shock-absorber and suspension tubes, pneumatic and hydraulic parts
  • CNC machining: close-tolerance tube reduces boring and makes automated machining easier
  • Telescopic products: uniform walls and straightness for smooth, play-free telescoping
  • Heat transfer: HVAC, automotive cooling and solar thermal systems

FAQ

Precision drawn aluminium (aluminum) tube: FAQ

What is the difference between drawn and extruded aluminium tube?

Extruded tube is formed hot through a die and has wider tolerances. Drawn tube is extruded and then cold drawn over a mandrel, giving tighter diameter and wall tolerances, a smoother surface and, in non-heat-treatable alloys, higher strength.

What tolerances can precision drawn tube achieve?

Standard tolerances follow ASTM B210 or EN 754-7. By agreement, small-diameter tube can be held to a few hundredths of a millimetre on OD, with wall tolerance around ±0.05 mm or tighter, depending on alloy and size.

Should I specify seamless tube?

Yes for pressure lines, hydraulic or pneumatic barrels and fatigue-critical parts. Seamless tube has no extrusion weld seams, so properties are uniform around the circumference.

Can precision tube be anodised?

Yes. The smooth drawn surface anodises evenly. 6060, 6063 and 6061 give the best decorative results; 2024 and 7075 anodise but with a less uniform colour.
