Seamless Rolled Aluminium Rings for Bearing, Flange and Gear-Ring Blanks

A seamless rolled ring is a forged aluminium ring made by piercing a billet and rolling the wall out to size, so it has no weld and a grain flow that runs continuously around the circumference. This guide is for engineers and buyers who need ring blanks for bearing housings, slewing rings, flanges, gear-ring carriers and aerospace structural rings: which alloys to choose, how the process affects properties, what to put in the specification and how to order rough-machined rings.

Overview

What is a seamless rolled ring?

A seamless rolled ring starts as a solid aluminium billet. The billet is upset into a pancake, pierced to make a doughnut-shaped preform, and then rolled between a driven main roll and an inner mandrel roll while conical axial rolls control the height. The wall gets thinner and the diameter grows until the ring reaches its target size. Because the ring is never cut open and rewelded, the word "seamless" describes it exactly.

The rolling action does more than shape the part. It kneads the metal in the circumferential direction, closing any internal porosity from casting and aligning the grain along the ring. That tangential grain flow is the reason rolled rings outperform rings cut from plate, rings bent from bar and welded, or cast rings, wherever a part sees hoop stress, bolt loads or cyclic rotation.

Ring rolling is also efficient. A plate-cut ring of 1,500 mm outside diameter throws away the entire centre disc as scrap; a rolled ring uses almost all of the billet, and a profiled (contoured) ring can be rolled close to the final cross-section so that only a modest machining allowance remains. For large rings in costly aerospace alloys, that saving in material and machining time is often decisive.

Typical ring blank uses

  • Bearing housings, wire-race slewing ring bodies and turntable rings for lightweight rotating equipment
  • Flanges, collars and connector rings for aluminium piping, vacuum vessels and cryogenic systems
  • Gear-ring carriers, pulley rims and timing rings where a steel tooth insert or light-duty teeth are used
  • Aerospace and launch-vehicle rings: tank joint rings, interstage and adapter rings, engine casing rings
  • Semiconductor and vacuum-chamber rings, lids and spacers machined to a fine finish
Production route

How aluminium rings are rolled, step by step

  1. Billet selection and cuttingHomogenised cast or pre-forged billet is chosen in the ordered alloy and cut to the weight of the finished ring plus losses. For critical aerospace rings, wrought (pre-forged) billet is preferred over as-cast stock.
  2. HeatingThe billet is heated uniformly to the forging range for the alloy, typically around 400 – 480 °C. Aluminium has a narrow working window: too cold and it cracks, too hot and it risks incipient melting or coarse grain.
  3. Upsetting and piercingOn an open-die press the billet is upset to reduce its height, then punched through the centre to form the ring preform. The punched slug is the main material loss of the route.
  4. Radial-axial ring rollingThe preform is placed over the mandrel. The main roll squeezes the wall radially while axial rolls control the height; the ring grows steadily in diameter. Modern CNC mills follow a programmed growth curve and measure the ring continuously.
  5. Heat treatmentHeat-treatable alloys are solution treated, quenched (water or polymer for thick sections), and aged to T6, T73, T7452 or similar tempers. Many rings are mechanically stress relieved by cold expansion or compression after quenching (tempers ending in 52) to reduce distortion during machining.
  6. Machining and inspectionRings are rough or finish turned, then inspected: dimensions, hardness or conductivity, tensile tests from extensions or sacrificial rings, and ultrasonic testing of the full volume.

Two details matter for buyers. First, quench sensitivity: thick ring walls cool slowly in the centre, so alloys such as 7050 and 2219, which hold strength in thick sections, are preferred over 7075 or 2014 for heavy rings. Second, distortion: a ring quenched without stress relief carries high residual stress and can move out of round when machined. Specifying a stress-relieved temper, or leaving extra allowance for semi-finish and finish passes, avoids scrapped parts.

Properties by direction

Grain direction, test locations and inspection

A rolled ring has three principal directions. The tangential (circumferential) direction follows the grain and gives the highest strength and elongation. The axial direction runs along the ring height, and the radial direction runs through the wall; these are the weaker, short-transverse-type directions, especially in 2xxx and 7xxx alloys. Specifications therefore state where tensile specimens are taken, and designers should orient the main loads of a part along the circumference wherever possible.

Typical inspection and documentation

Ultrasonic testing
ASTM B594 for wrought aluminium products, or AMS-STD-2154 for aerospace, with the acceptance class stated on the order
Mechanical tests
Tensile tests in the tangential direction (and axial or radial when required) from ring extensions or a sacrificial ring from the same lot
Hardness and conductivity
Checked on every ring of heat-treated alloys to confirm uniform temper
Metallography
Grain size and macro-etch for grain flow on request, common for aerospace first-article inspection
Dimensional report
OD, ID, height, flatness and roundness recorded for each ring
Certificate
EN 10204 3.1 mill certificate with chemistry, heat treatment and test results; third-party witnessing (3.2) when the project requires it

Design and ordering

How to specify a rolled ring blank

Give finished and blank sizes

Send the finished part drawing as well as the ring size you want. The producer adds machining allowance on OD, ID and height that suits the alloy, diameter and heat-treatment distortion.

Consider a profiled ring

Stepped, flanged or tapered cross-sections can be rolled close to shape, saving material on large rings. They need special tooling, so they pay off on repeat quantities.

Choose the delivery condition

As-rolled (F), annealed, or heat treated and stress relieved; rough turned or finish machined. Heat-treated, rough-turned rings are the usual compromise.

Mind the proportions

Very thin, tall rings and very thick, small-bore rings are hardest to roll and to quench evenly. Ask for advice before fixing extreme height-to-wall ratios.

State test direction and class

Tangential-only testing is cheaper; axial and radial tests and higher UT classes add cost and need extra material.

Plan for distortion

Rough machine, let the ring relax, then finish machine, especially for thin rings in 7xxx alloys or rings without stress relief.

FAQ

Seamless rolled aluminium (aluminum) rings: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a forged ring and a seamless rolled ring?

Both are forged and seamless. A forged ring in the narrow sense is punched and then expanded on a press over a saddle mandrel; a rolled ring is finished on a ring rolling mill, which gives closer dimensions, better roundness and less machining allowance, especially for large, thin-walled rings.

Why is grain flow important in a ring?

Rolling aligns the grain around the circumference, which is the direction of hoop stress and bolt-circle loads in most rings. Strength, ductility, fatigue life and resistance to stress-corrosion cracking are all best in that tangential direction.

Which aluminium alloy is best for a large structural ring?

For aerospace, 7050-T7452 is often preferred for thick rings because it is less quench sensitive than 7075. For general machinery and flanges, 6061-T6 or 6082-T6 is the usual choice; 5083 suits marine and cryogenic service; 2219 is used where welding and cryogenic strength are both needed.

Can aluminium rolled rings be used for bearings and gears?

Aluminium is used for bearing housings, wire-race slewing ring bodies and lightweight gear-ring carriers, where steel raceways or tooth inserts carry the contact stress. It is not a substitute for hardened bearing steel in rolling-contact surfaces.

How are rolled rings inspected?

Typically by ultrasonic testing to ASTM B594 or AMS-STD-2154, tensile tests in the tangential direction, hardness or conductivity checks and a dimensional report, all recorded on an EN 10204 3.1 certificate.

Do you supply rolled rings worldwide?
