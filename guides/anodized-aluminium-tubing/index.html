Anodized Aluminium Tubing: Alloys, Finishes and Sizes

Anodized aluminium tubing is extruded or drawn tube with an electrochemically grown oxide layer that adds corrosion and wear resistance and can be coloured clear, black, bronze, gold or bright shades. 6063 gives the best cosmetic finish, 6061 adds strength, and 7075 serves high-load sports and aerospace parts. This guide covers alloy choice, coating types, sizes and the practical points that decide whether a tube looks right after finishing.

Basics

What is anodized aluminium tubing?

Anodised tube starts as ordinary extruded or cold-drawn aluminium tube. It is cleaned, etched or brightened, made the anode in a sulphuric acid bath to grow a porous oxide film, optionally coloured, and sealed. The oxide is converted from the tube wall itself, so it does not chip or peel the way paint or powder coat can on a tube that is knocked or flexed in service.

Tube is a slightly harder shape to anodise well than flat sheet. The outside surface is fully exposed to the electrolyte and current, but the bore is shielded: the further the solution is from the tube ends, the less current reaches it. In long, small-diameter tubes the inside film is therefore thinner than the outside, and sometimes only partly formed. Where a uniform internal coating matters, for example for fluid contact or internal wear, the anodiser can use internal cathodes or a pumped electrolyte, and the drawing should state the minimum film required inside.

Tubes are hung on racks during anodising and each contact point leaves a small bare mark. Good practice is to place contacts at the ends or in areas that will be cut off or hidden in assembly. When ordering tube that will be cut into short pieces after anodising, remember that every cut exposes bare metal at the ends.

6063 is the default for anything visible. Its lean chemistry and fine grain give a smooth, bright surface and even dye uptake, which is why most anodised furniture, shopfitting and architectural tube is 6063. 6061 is chosen when the tube carries real load, at the cost of a slightly less lustrous clear finish. 7075 is reserved for parts where strength-to-weight is the whole point; it anodises acceptably in black and in hard coat but gives a darker, less clean clear finish because of its zinc and copper content.

Anodising types

Anodising types used on tube

Type II decorative

Sulphuric anodising, usually 5 – 15 µm for indoor tube and furniture. Takes dyes for black, gold, red, blue and other colours.

Architectural (Class I / AA20 – AA25)

18 – 25 µm films for handrails, balustrades and exterior tube. Clear or electrolytically coloured champagne and bronze give the best outdoor colour stability.

Type III hard anodising

Low-temperature, high-current process giving films of about 25 – 100 µm. Very wear resistant; natural colour is grey to bronze, or dyed black. Used on pistons, guide tubes and rollers.

Type I chromic acid

Thin, ductile films with good fatigue behaviour, used on some aerospace parts. Being phased down because of hexavalent chromium; thin-film sulphuric or tartaric-sulphuric processes replace it.

Bright dip + anodise

Chemical brightening before a thin film for a near-chrome look on 6063 trim and decorative tube.

Sealing

Hot-water, mid-temperature or cold seal closes the pores for corrosion resistance and dye retention. Unsealed films stain and fade.

For tube that will be bent after finishing, keep the film thin (around 5 – 10 µm) and use generous bend radii; thick and hard films crack on the outside of a bend. For tube that will be welded, anodise after welding, because the oxide insulates and contaminates the weld.

Applications

Where anodised aluminium tube is used

  • Building interiors and exteriors: handrails, balustrade posts and rails, sunshade tubes and decorative columns
  • Furniture and shopfitting: chair and table frames, clothes rails, display stands and shelving uprights
  • Sports and outdoor: tent and trekking poles, kayak paddles, bicycle parts, fitness equipment
  • Medical and care: walking aids, rehabilitation frames and equipment supports that are wiped and disinfected often
  • Machinery and automation: guide tubes, rollers, guarding frames and pneumatic cylinder barrels (hard anodised)
  • Lighting and electronics: luminaire bodies, LED tube housings and antenna masts
  • Marine: deck rails, canopy frames and fittings above the waterline

Metline supplies the anodised tube or anodising-quality bare tube; frames, rails and assemblies are fabricated by the customer. Tube can be cut to length before dispatch.

Specification advice

Specification checklist for anodised tube

  1. Choose the alloy for the job6063 for appearance, 6061 for load, 7075 only where strength justifies cost and a darker finish.
  2. State the coatingType and minimum thickness (for example ISO 7599 AA20, AAMA 611 Class I, or MIL-A-8625 Type III 50 µm), plus colour and sealing.
  3. Define the boreSay whether the inside must be coated and to what thickness, or whether outside only is acceptable.
  4. Agree rack marksMark on the drawing where contact points may be placed, normally at the ends.
  5. Approve colour samplesAgree light and dark limit samples; dyed colours vary more between lots than clear finishes.

FAQ

Anodized aluminium (aluminum) tubing: frequently asked questions

Is 6061 or 6063 better for anodized aluminum tubing?

6063 gives the brighter, more uniform anodised finish and is the choice for visible tube. 6061-T6 is about 30 % stronger and is used where the tube carries load, accepting a slightly less lustrous clear finish.

Is the inside of anodised tube also anodised?

Partly. The electrolyte reaches the bore, but current density falls away from the ends, so inside films are thinner, especially in long, small-diameter tube. If the bore needs a specified coating, say so on the order so the anodiser can use internal cathodes.

Can anodised aluminium tube be bent?

Thin decorative films on 6063 tolerate moderate bends with generous radii. Thick architectural and hard-anodised films crack on the outside of the bend, so bend first and anodise afterwards for tight radii.

Will black anodised tube fade outdoors?

Organic black dyes are reasonably light-fast, but for long outdoor exposure an electrolytic black or dark bronze, sealed properly and at 20 µm or more, keeps its colour far better.

Can anodised tube be welded?

The oxide must be removed at the joint before welding, and the heat will discolour the surrounding finish. Weld bare tube and anodise the finished assembly when appearance matters.

What lengths can anodised tube be supplied in?

Standard lengths are 6 m or 20 ft. Longer lengths depend on the anodising tank available for the order, so confirm the length you need at enquiry.
