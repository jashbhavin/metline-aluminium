Retortable Aluminium Foil for Peel-Off Ends

Retortable peel-off end foil is a laminated or coated aluminium membrane that is heat-sealed to a metal ring, seamed onto a food can, and must then survive steam sterilisation at around 121 °C or higher before the consumer peels it open by hand. It is usually based on 8011 foil with a polypropylene-compatible heat-seal layer. This guide explains the structure, performance demands and how to specify reels for lid makers; Metline supplies the aluminium lidding foil worldwide.

Overview

What a peel-off end is and why the foil matters

A peel-off end (POE) replaces the familiar ring-pull easy-open end on a food can. Instead of a scored metal panel, it uses a flexible aluminium membrane sealed to a narrow metal ring. The ring is double-seamed onto the can body on a standard seamer, and the consumer opens the can by pulling a tab that peels the membrane away.

POEs are lighter than full metal ends, leave no sharp edge when opened, and give a wide, clean opening that suits fish, pâté, pet food and premium products. They also allow attractive printing and embossing on the lid.

Everything depends on the membrane. It must be a complete barrier for the life of the product, bond strongly enough to stay sealed through retort pressure and transport, and yet peel cleanly with a comfortable force. Aluminium foil supplies the barrier; the coatings and laminates supply the seal and peel behaviour.

What the foil has to survive

  • Heat sealing to the ring at high temperature and pressure
  • Retort sterilisation in steam or water, typically 121 °C and above, often for an hour or more
  • Pressure differences between the can and the retort that flex the membrane
  • Contact with oily, acidic or salty foods for a shelf life measured in years

Exact gauges depend on the lid design, ring coating, sealing equipment and food. Lid makers usually specify the foil gauge and temper, the coating or film type and weight, and the sealing window. Present your existing structure or lid drawing when enquiring so the foil can be matched to it.

The peel mechanism is designed in. In a well-made structure the seal to the ring is stronger than the internal bond that fails when the lid is peeled, so opening is clean and predictable instead of tearing the foil or leaving fragments on the ring.

Alloy

Why 8011 is the standard alloy

8011 is an Al-Fe-Si foil alloy. Its iron and silicon form fine particles that refine the grain, which gives higher strength and better puncture resistance than pure 1xxx foil at the same gauge, along with fewer pinholes. In the annealed O temper it embosses and forms well; partial-hard tempers are chosen where more stiffness is wanted. 8079 and 1235 are occasionally used for specific structures.

The ring itself is usually tinplate or aluminium, and the can body may be tinplate or aluminium (3003 and 5xxx sheet are common for drawn aluminium bodies). The ring coating and the foil sealant must be designed as a pair.

Applications

Foods packed under retortable peel-off ends

Fish and seafood

Tuna, sardines and mackerel in oil or brine; oily, salty contents test the sealant and adhesive.

Meat and ready meals

Pâté, luncheon meat and meals; long retort cycles at elevated temperature.

Vegetables and fruit

Acidic contents demand corrosion-resistant inner layers.

Pet food

Wet cat and dog food in shallow trays and cans, a major user of peel-off ends.

Speciality and premium foods

Products such as bird's nest drinks and dairy desserts where opening experience and printing matter.

Infant and medical nutrition

Powder and liquid products where clean opening and hygiene matter.

Retort conditions vary: a fish cannery may run a different temperature and time from a pet food plant. Always qualify a new foil on your own sealing line and retort cycle before production, and keep retained samples from each lot.

Metline supplies the aluminium lidding foil in reels to lid makers and converters. The lids themselves are made by the lid maker, which cuts, seals and forms the membrane onto rings.

Three mistakes cause most problems with new lid foil: changing the ring coating without re-qualifying the sealant, judging peel force only before retort (it often changes afterwards), and specifying foil by gauge alone while ignoring temper and coating weights. Write all three into the specification and test them together on your own line.

FAQ

Retortable aluminium (aluminum) peel-off end foil: FAQs

What is retortable lidding foil?

It is aluminium foil laminated or coated with heat-seal and protective layers that withstand steam sterilisation, used as the peelable membrane in peel-off ends for food cans.

How is it different from ordinary aluminium foil?

Plain foil has no sealant and no retort-resistant coatings. Retortable lid foil adds a heat-seal layer matched to the ring, adhesives that resist hydrolysis, and print coatings that survive 121 °C or higher.

Which alloy is used?

Usually 8011, for its strength, low pinhole count and good embossing. 8079 and 1235 are used in some structures.

Do you supply finished peel-off lids?

No. Metline supplies the aluminium lidding foil in reels; lid makers cut, seal and form it onto rings to make the finished ends.

What retort temperature must the foil withstand?

Low-acid foods are typically sterilised at about 121 °C or higher. Give us your actual temperature, time and retort type so the structure can be matched and tested.

Can the foil be printed and embossed?

Yes. Printed graphics, colours and embossed patterns are common, using inks and overlacquers designed to survive retort without fading.
