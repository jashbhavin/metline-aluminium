Packaging foil guide

Aluminium Foil for Candy and Confectionery Wrapping

Candy wrapping foil is very thin, soft (O temper) aluminium foil, usually 8011 or 1235, that keeps sweets dry, fresh and free from odours while holding a twist or fold without springing open. It is used plain, printed, colour-lacquered or laminated to paper or film. This guide explains which alloy, gauge, temper and construction suit each candy format; Metline supplies foil in jumbo and slit rolls to converters and confectionery packers worldwide.

Overview

What foil does for a sweet

Sugar confectionery is hygroscopic: hard candies, toffees and caramels pull moisture from the air, turn sticky and lose their shape. Fat-based sweets go rancid when exposed to light and oxygen, and all confectionery picks up odours from its surroundings. Aluminium foil is one of the few flexible materials that blocks moisture, oxygen, light and aroma almost completely, and it does so at a thickness of a few hundredths of a millimetre.

The second reason confectioners choose foil is dead-fold. Fully annealed foil stays exactly where it is folded or twisted. A twist-wrapped toffee does not unwind, a folded chocolate coin stays closed, and a foil-wrapped praline keeps its crisp edges, all without glue or heat sealing. Plastic films spring back and need an extra seal to do the same job.

Foil also prints and embosses well, accepts gold, silver and coloured lacquers, and gives seasonal and premium sweets the shine buyers associate with quality. Chocolate bars, which have their own requirements, are covered in our chocolate foil guide; this page focuses on candies, toffees, lollipops and mixed confectionery.

Foil properties that matter for candy

  • Barrier against moisture, oxygen, light and odours, extending shelf life
  • Dead-fold so twists and folds stay closed without adhesive
  • Clean, pinhole-controlled surface that is safe for direct food contact
  • Good printability and lacquer adhesion for brand colours
Chemical composition

8011 and 1235 foil alloys

8011 contains iron and silicon additions that give a finer grain and higher strength than pure aluminium, so thin foil has fewer breaks during rolling and converting. 1235 is 99.35 % pure aluminium; it is softer, has slightly better dead-fold and is widely used for the thinnest gauges and paper laminates. Tempers and thickness

Tempers and gauges for each candy format

Almost all candy foil is supplied in O temper (fully annealed). The final anneal softens the metal and burns off rolling oil, leaving a dry, clean surface that is ready for printing and food contact. Harder tempers such as H18 are used only when the foil is laminated to a film that provides the shape, or for lids and trays.

Product forms

Plain, coated and laminated foil options

Plain foil

Bare O-temper foil for direct wrapping. Lowest cost and full dead-fold, but thin plain foil can tear on high-speed machines and will show any pinholes.

Colour-lacquered foil

Gold, silver and coloured transparent or opaque lacquers applied on one or both sides for festive and premium sweets. The lacquer must be food-safe on the product side.

Printed and embossed foil

Gravure or flexo printed with brand designs and often embossed with a pattern that adds stiffness and hides creases.

Foil/paper laminate

Foil bonded to light paper, typically with wax or adhesive. The paper adds strength and a printable surface; the foil supplies barrier and fold. Common for twist wraps and gum.

Foil/film laminate

Foil laminated to PET, BOPP or PE for flow-wrap and pouch packs. Gives heat-sealability; dead-fold is reduced.

Heat-seal lacquered foil

A lacquer that seals to itself or to a tray, used where packs must be sealed rather than folded.

Metallised film (a vapour-deposited aluminium layer on plastic film) is a cheaper alternative for flow-wraps, but it does not dead-fold and its barrier is lower than a continuous foil. For gifts and premium lines, solid foil remains the preferred material.

Quality

Quality points to specify

  • Pinholes: unavoidable in the thinnest foil but must be controlled; specify an acceptable count per m² for the gauge
  • Wettability: annealed foil should pass a water-brush test (often grade A or B) so inks, lacquers and adhesives wet evenly
  • Tensile and elongation: high enough for the wrapping machine; very thin foil benefits from 8011's strength
  • Surface: free from oil stains, streaks, creases, corrosion spots and roll marks
  • Winding: tight, even rolls without telescoping, with joints marked and limited in number
  • Food contact: declarations of compliance with EN 602 and the relevant regulations (for example EU 1935/2004, US FDA)

Each delivery is supplied with a mill certificate showing alloy, temper, thickness and mechanical test results, and a food-contact declaration where required.

Cost

What drives the price of candy foil

Foil is priced as the metal value plus a conversion charge. The metal part follows the aluminium market (LME or a regional reference price on the day of order). The conversion charge depends on how much rolling and annealing the gauge needs, and it rises steeply as foil gets thinner: 7 µm foil costs far more per tonne to make than 20 µm foil, although it covers almost three times the area.

  • Gauge and width, and whether the foil is single- or double-rolled
  • Alloy and temper; 8011 and 1235 are similar in cost
  • Coating, printing, embossing or lamination
  • Roll size, core type and packing
  • Quantity and delivery terms (EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP)

FAQ

Candy wrapping aluminium (aluminum) foil: FAQ

Which aluminium alloy is used for candy wrappers?

8011 and 1235 in O temper. 8011 is a little stronger and runs well on fast machines; 1235 is softer with excellent dead-fold and is common in foil/paper laminates.

How thick is candy wrapping foil?

Most twist wraps use about 7 – 12 µm foil, often laminated to paper. Chocolate novelties and pralines use about 9 – 20 µm, and box liners and lids are heavier.

Why does foil stay twisted when plastic film unwinds?

Annealed aluminium deforms plastically and has almost no elastic spring-back, so it holds the shape it is folded into. This "dead-fold" is the main reason foil is used for twist-wrapped sweets.

Is aluminium foil safe for direct contact with sweets?

Yes. Food-contact foil meets the composition limits of EN 602, and coatings on the food side must comply with the food-contact rules of the market. Annealing also removes rolling oil from the surface.

What is the difference between foil and metallised film for candy?

Foil is a solid aluminium layer with a near-total barrier and dead-fold. Metallised film is plastic with a microscopic aluminium coating; it is cheaper and heat-sealable but does not hold a fold and has a lower barrier.

Can the foil be supplied coloured or printed?

Yes. Foil can be supplied with gold, silver or coloured lacquers, printed and embossed, or as a laminate with paper or film, depending on your wrapping process.
