Sandwich core guide

5052 Aluminium Honeycomb Core

5052 aluminium honeycomb core is a hexagonal cellular material made from hard-temper 5052 foil, bonded at the nodes and expanded, that gives sandwich panels very high stiffness and strength for their weight. It is the general-purpose grade for aerospace, rail, marine and industrial panels: stronger and more corrosion-resistant than 3003 core, and more economical than 5056. Densities run from about 1.6 to 12 lb/ft³ (26 – 190 kg/m³) depending on cell size and foil gauge. This guide gives the property data, explains how to select a core and lists what to specify.

Overview

What is 5052 honeycomb core and how is it made?

A honeycomb sandwich panel works like an I-beam spread over an area. Two thin, stiff facings carry bending loads as tension and compression, and the lightweight core between them holds the facings apart and carries shear. Doubling the core thickness roughly quadruples panel stiffness for only a small weight increase, which is why honeycomb panels are used wherever stiffness per kilogram matters.

Most aluminium honeycomb is made by the expansion process. Thin 5052 foil, typically 0.0007 – 0.004 in (18 – 100 µm) thick, is printed with parallel lines of adhesive, stacked sheet on sheet with the lines offset by half a pitch, and cured under heat and pressure into a solid block. Slices are cut from this block to the required core thickness, then pulled apart so the unbonded areas open into hexagonal cells. The bonded lines become the double-thickness cell walls, called nodes.

For very high densities and thick foils, the corrugation process is used instead: foil is corrugated into half-hexagons, stacked and bonded or brazed. Either way the result has clear directional properties, and knowing the directions is essential before you design with it.

Core directions

L (ribbon) direction
Parallel to the continuous foil ribbons and the double-wall nodes. Strongest and stiffest in shear.
W (transverse) direction
Perpendicular to the ribbons, the direction of expansion. Shear strength about 60 % and stiffness about 50 % of L.
T (thickness) direction
Along the cell axis. Carries flatwise compression.

Why 5052 is the default aluminium core

  • Higher strength than 3003 core of the same geometry, with better corrosion resistance
  • Covered by the aerospace core specification AMS-C-7438, so it is accepted on qualified programmes
  • Wide range of cell sizes, foil gauges and densities available from stock geometries
  • Compatible with aluminium, CFRP, GFRP, laminate and stainless facings
Nomenclature

How 5052 core is specified: cell, foil and density

Aluminium honeycomb is described by four parameters, usually written in this order: alloy, cell size, foil gauge and density, for example 5052 – 1/4 – .0015 – 3.4. That means 5052 foil, 1/4 in cell measured across the flats of the hexagon, 0.0015 in foil, and a nominal density of 3.4 lb/ft³. Coating, perforation and whether the core is supplied expanded or unexpanded are added as separate callouts.

Density follows the ratio of foil thickness to cell size: for the same cell, doubling foil gauge roughly doubles density, and for the same foil, halving cell size roughly doubles it. Strength rises faster than density, roughly with density to the power 1.5 – 1.7 for compression, so a modest increase in density buys a significant gain in strength.

Configurations

Core variants and special forms

Standard hexagonal

Regular hexagon cells, the most efficient shape for flat panels and the basis for published data.

Over-expanded

Cells stretched in W into a rectangular shape. Easier to bend around the L axis for single-curvature parts, with higher W shear and lower L shear.

Flexible-geometry core

Modified cell shapes that drape onto compound curves without anticlastic bending; used for fairings and radomes.

Reinforced (double-wall)

Extra foil layers in the L direction for local high shear, such as around inserts and hinge lines.

Perforated

Micro-perforated cell walls let air escape during autoclave or vacuum bonding and in space structures.

Microcell

Cells under about 1/16 in (1.6 mm) for very thin facings, precision parts and high-density energy absorbers.

Pre-crushed and energy-absorbing

Core with a pre-initiated crush front to avoid the peak load at the start of crushing; used in crash attenuators and landing systems.

Thick blocks and stacked core

Slices of 150 mm and more, or bonded stacks, for impact pads and large stiff platforms.

Pre-formed core

Core heat-formed or machined to a contour so it fits curved moulds without stress.

Durability

Corrosion resistance, coatings and bonding

5052 is one of the more corrosion-resistant aluminium alloys, but a honeycomb core has enormous surface area and foils only a few hundredths of a millimetre thick, so even light pitting can consume a cell wall. For this reason aerospace and marine cores use treated foil. Common options are chromate or chromate-free conversion coatings, and phosphoric-acid anodising followed by a corrosion-inhibiting primer, which also gives the most durable adhesive bond.

AMS-C-7438 sets salt-fog limits for coated core, measured as weight loss after exposure in ASTM B117 salt spray. Producers publish results well inside the limit for their coated grades. Uncoated (bare) core is acceptable for dry interior use and many industrial panels, and costs less.

  • Seal panel edges with closeouts, edge filler or tape so water cannot enter the cells.
  • Isolate aluminium core from carbon fibre facings with a glass ply or film adhesive scrim to prevent galvanic corrosion.
  • Avoid trapped moisture during lay-up; dry core before bonding if it has been stored in humid conditions.
  • Use perforated core or venting where panels see pressure or temperature cycles.

Selection guide

How to select the right 5052 core

  1. Define the loadsPanel span, support conditions, distributed and point loads, and allowable deflection. These give required bending stiffness and core shear stress.
  2. Set the facing and thicknessChoose facing material and thickness, then core thickness for stiffness. Remember stiffness rises with the square of facing separation.
  3. Check core shearCompare the maximum transverse shear stress with core shear strength. Orient the L direction along the main span where possible.
  4. Check local failure modesFace wrinkling, intra-cell dimpling (thin facings over large cells), core crushing under point loads and insert pull-out.
  5. Choose cell size and foilPick the lowest density that passes all checks; reduce cell size if dimpling or bond area limits the design.
  6. Add environment requirementsCoating, perforation, edge sealing, temperature and fire requirements.

Working with core

Cutting, forming, bonding and inserts

Cutting

Slice with a band saw or reciprocating knife; profile on CNC with ultrasonic or rotary honeycomb cutters. Holding core on a vacuum table or freezing it with a temporary filler gives clean edges.

Expansion

Unexpanded slices are stretched evenly on a frame to nominal cell size. Over-stretching changes density and properties, so check cell count per length after expansion.

Forming

Bend around the L axis, preferably with over-expanded core; compound curves need flexible-geometry core or pre-formed parts.

Bonding

Film adhesives with good fillet-forming are standard for aerospace; epoxy or polyurethane for industrial panels. Clean, dry, treated core gives the best bond.

Inserts and edges

Threaded inserts are potted into the core with a filled epoxy compound. Use higher-density or reinforced core in insert zones; close out edges with extrusions or edge fill.

Splicing

Join core pieces with expanding foam adhesive along the splice line, keeping L directions aligned.

Applications

Where 5052 honeycomb core is used

  • Aircraft interiors and floors: floor panels, galley and lavatory structures, overhead bins and sidewalls bonded with aluminium or glass-fibre facings.
  • Aircraft secondary structure: flaps, spoilers, fairings, access doors and control surfaces where weight and stiffness are critical.
  • Space structures: satellite equipment platforms, instrument panels and solar-array substrates, usually with perforated core.
  • Rail and metro: floors, doors, ceilings and partitions requiring low weight and fire performance.
  • Marine: bulkheads, decks and furniture on fast ferries and yachts, using coated core and sealed edges.
  • Industrial: machine covers, clean-room walls, optical tables, vacuum chucks and precision fixtures.
  • Energy absorption: crash attenuators, landing pads, drop-test fixtures and protective packaging.
  • Airflow: flow straighteners in wind tunnels and HVAC, and EMI-shielding vents.

FAQ

5052 aluminium (aluminum) honeycomb core: FAQs

What does "5052 – 1/8 – .0010 – 4.5" mean?

5052 foil alloy, 1/8 in (3.2 mm) cell size across the flats, 0.0010 in (25 µm) foil, and a nominal density of 4.5 lb/ft³ (72 kg/m³). That geometry has a typical stabilised compressive strength of about 580 psi (4.0 MPa).

Is 5052 honeycomb stronger than 3003 honeycomb?

Yes. At the same cell size and foil gauge, 5052 core is stronger in compression and shear and more corrosion-resistant. 3003 is chosen mainly for cost in non-structural or architectural panels.

Which direction should the ribbon (L) run in a panel?

Along the main span or the direction of highest shear, because L shear strength is about 1.6 times and L shear stiffness about twice the W values.

Can 5052 honeycomb be supplied unexpanded?

Yes. Unexpanded slices take a fraction of the shipping volume and are expanded on a stretching frame at your plant. Expanded and cut-to-size core is also available.

Does aluminium honeycomb need a corrosion coating?

For aerospace, marine and humid service, yes: coated or anodised foil with sealed panel edges. Bare core is acceptable for dry interior and many industrial panels.

Can 5052 core be bonded to carbon fibre facings?

Yes, but isolate the aluminium from the carbon with a glass ply or scrim-supported adhesive to prevent galvanic corrosion.

What core thickness tolerance can be held?

Typically ±0.005 in (±0.13 mm) for slices up to 4 in thick and ±0.062 in (±1.6 mm) above that, with tighter tolerances possible by sanding.
