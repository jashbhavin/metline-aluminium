Aluminium Foil and Strip for Lids, EOE and POE Can Ends

Lids and can ends use two very different aluminium products. Heat-sealed lids on cups, trays and capsules are made from coated foil, typically 8011, 8079 or 1235 at 30 – 90 µm. Rigid easy-open ends (EOE) and the rings of peel-off ends (POE) are stamped from much thicker, harder strip, mainly 5182 and 5052. This guide explains which alloy, temper, gauge and coating suits each closure, and what to specify when you buy.

Overview

Three closure types, three material families

Before choosing a grade, be clear which closure you are supplying. The term "lid" covers everything from a 35 µm yogurt seal to a 0.25 mm beverage can end, and the materials have almost nothing in common apart from the base metal.

Lidding foil

Aluminium foil for heat-sealed lids

Lidding foil must seal reliably to the container, peel cleanly when the consumer opens it, form an absolute barrier to light, oxygen and moisture, and print or emboss attractively. The foil itself provides the barrier; the coatings provide sealing, printability and protection.

8011

The workhorse lidding alloy. Iron and silicon give good strength and pinhole resistance at thin gauges. Used soft (O) for embossed and deep-drawn lids, or in H14 – H18 for stiffer flat lids that must be die-cut cleanly.

8079

Higher iron and very low silicon give excellent ductility and puncture resistance in the soft temper. Preferred for peelable and laminated structures and for lids that must resist puncture in transit.

1235

Commercial-purity foil with excellent ductility and a bright surface; used for flexible seals and laminated lid stock where strength is less important.

The heat-seal coating must match the container material, such as polystyrene, polypropylene, PET, PVC or aluminium, and the filling conditions: cold fill, hot fill, pasteurisation or retort. A coating that seals well to polystyrene may not seal to polypropylene at all, so always state the container material and process when ordering. Read why heat-sealed foil fails to adhere for typical sealing faults.

EOE and POE strip

Aluminium strip for easy-open and peel-off ends

Can ends must withstand internal pressure (beverage) or vacuum and retort cycles (food) without buckling, be scored precisely so they open with a predictable force, and form tight double seams. That needs a hard, strong strip with good formability and a fully protective internal lacquer.

5182-H19 / H48

An Al-Mg alloy with about 4 – 5 % magnesium and the highest strength of the common end alloys. The standard for beverage can ends and tabs, and widely used for food EOE. H48 is the coated-and-cured equivalent of H19, accounting for softening in the lacquer oven.

5052-H19 / H48

Lower magnesium (2.2 – 2.8 %) and good corrosion resistance. Used for food-can EOE, aerosol and specialty ends, and the rings of peel-off ends, where very high strength is not required.

3104 / 3004

Al-Mn-Mg alloys used mainly for can bodies; also used for some shallow lids, closures and POE rings where formability matters more than end-panel strength.

Peel-off ends combine both worlds: a rigid ring stamped from strip is seamed to the can, and a coated foil membrane is heat-sealed across the aperture. The membrane foil must withstand retort sterilisation while remaining peelable, so its heat-seal system is carefully matched to the ring coating.

Food-contact compliance covers both the metal and the coatings. The aluminium composition should meet EN 602 for food contact in Europe, and coatings must comply with the regulations of the destination market, such as EU Regulation 1935/2004 and the relevant US FDA rules for coatings. Ask for compliance declarations alongside the mill certificate.

Applications

Typical lid and end applications

  • Dairy: yogurt, dessert and cream cups sealed with coated 8011 or 8079 lids
  • Coffee and beverages: capsule lids and portion-pack seals that must resist pressure during brewing
  • Ready meals and pet food: tray lids and peel-off ends that survive retort sterilisation
  • Beverage cans: 5182 ends and tabs for beer, soft drinks and energy drinks
  • Food cans: EOE for fish, meat, fruit and vegetables; POE for powdered milk, nuts and infant formula
  • Pharmaceutical and nutraceutical: induction and heat-seal lids for jars and containers
Ordering

How to specify and order

  1. Closure and processDescribe the closure (cup lid, EOE, POE), container material, filling and sterilisation process.
  2. MaterialAlloy, temper, gauge and tolerance, coil width, inner diameter and maximum outer diameter or coil weight.
  3. CoatingsHeat-seal and protective lacquer types and weights, colour or print primer, or laminate structure.
  4. ComplianceFood-contact declarations required for the destination market; mill certificate to EN 10204 3.1.
  5. Packing and termsCoils packed eye-to-sky or eye-to-wall with desiccant in sealed wooden cases; terms EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP.

FAQ

Lid foil and can end strip: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between EOE and POE?

An easy-open end (EOE) is a rigid stamped end with a scored panel and a tab or ring that tears the panel open. A peel-off end (POE) has a rigid ring seamed to the can and a foil membrane heat-sealed across the opening, which the consumer peels away.

Which aluminium alloy is used for beverage can ends?

5182 in H19 or the coated H48 temper is the standard end and tab alloy because of its high strength. 3004 and 3104 are used for can bodies, not normally for beverage ends.

Which foil is best for yogurt and dessert lids?

8011 and 8079 foil, usually 30 – 50 µm, with a heat-seal lacquer matched to the cup material on one side and a printable lacquer on the other. 8079 is chosen where extra ductility and puncture resistance help.

Why must the heat-seal coating match the container?

Heat-seal lacquers are formulated to bond to specific polymers. A lacquer designed for polystyrene may not seal to polypropylene or PET, so the container material and filling process must be specified.

What does H48 mean for can end stock?

H48 describes strain-hardened strip that has been lacquered and stoved; it accounts for the slight softening during coating cure and delivers properties comparable to H19 in the finished end.

Do you supply coated foil and strip?

Yes. Metline supplies lidding foil and end strip bare or coated to your specification, slit to width, with food-contact declarations and mill certificates, for delivery worldwide.
