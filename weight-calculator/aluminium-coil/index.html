How it works

How the weight is calculated

A coil is treated as a hollow cylinder of metal. Its volume is the ring area between the outer and inner diameters × the coil width. Gaps between wraps are negligible in tightly wound coil.

Coil weight formula

Weight (kg)
π/4 × (OD² − ID²) × width × density
Common core IDs
150, 200, 300, 405, 505 and 508 mm
Coil length
Weight ÷ (width × thickness × density)

Worked example: 3003 coil, OD 1400, ID 508, width 1250 mm: π/4 × (1.4² − 0.508²) × 1.25 × 2730 = 4,561.48 kg.

Getting it right

Why the delivered weight differs from the theoretical weight

Dimensional tolerance

Mills supply to a tolerance band, not the exact nominal size. Plate on the plus side of its thickness tolerance can weigh 2–5% more than the calculated figure.

Alloy and temper

Density varies with the alloying elements, from about 2.64 g/cm³ for 5A06 to 2.83 g/cm³ for 7050 and 2011. Temper has no practical effect on density.

Cladding and coatings

Alclad layers, anodising, paint, PVC film and interleaving paper add small amounts of weight. Coated coil and sheet are usually quoted by net metal weight.

Packing and pallets

Freight is charged on gross weight. Wooden cases, pallets, steel strapping and coil cores typically add 3–8% on top of the metal.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the length of strip in a coil?

Divide the coil weight by (width × thickness × density). A 3,000 kg coil of 1 mm × 1250 mm 3003 contains about 3000 ÷ (1.25 × 0.001 × 2730) ≈ 879 m of strip.

Does the weight include the core?

No. The calculator gives net metal weight only. Steel or paper cores, eye-to-sky skids and packing are added to the gross weight on the packing list.

How accurate is this aluminium weight calculator?

It gives the theoretical weight from nominal dimensions and the typical density of the alloy. Real material is within a tolerance band, so delivered weight usually differs by a few percent. Use the result for budgeting, handling and freight planning, and rely on the weighbridge or packing list for invoicing.

What density does the calculator use for aluminium?

Each alloy has its own typical density, from 2.64 g/cm³ (5A06) to 2.84 g/cm³ (2219). If your alloy is not listed, choose "Other alloy" and enter the density from its data sheet. Pure aluminium is 2.70 g/cm³.

Can I use inches or feet?

Yes. Every dimension has its own unit selector (mm, cm, m, in or ft), so you can mix units, such as a width in inches and a length in feet. The result is always shown in kilograms and pounds.

Is the weight per piece or for the whole order?

Both. Enter the number of pieces and the calculator shows the total weight, the weight of each piece and the weight per running metre.
