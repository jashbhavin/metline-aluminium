5083 Aluminium Circle, Disc and Round Plate

5083 aluminium circles are round blanks cut from 5083 sheet or plate, the aluminium-magnesium alloy specified for ships, pressure vessels and cryogenic tanks because it combines high non-heat-treatable strength with excellent seawater resistance and weldability. Metline supplies 5083 discs and round plates in O/H111, H116 and H321 tempers, cut to diameter from thin sheet up to heavy plate and certified to ASTM and EN standards, worldwide.

Overview

What 5083 aluminium circles are used for

A 5083 circle is not a separate product so much as 5083 plate in a round shape. Buyers order discs when the finished part is round, such as a flange, tank head, hatch, valve body or machined end plate, so they pay for less scrap and less machining.

5083 contains about 4.5 % magnesium with manganese and chromium. It gains its strength from solid-solution hardening and cold work rather than heat treatment, so it keeps most of its strength after welding: in the O/H111 condition the weld zone is essentially as strong as the parent metal. It also resists seawater, marine atmospheres and many industrial chemicals, and it becomes stronger and tougher at cryogenic temperatures, which is why it is used for LNG tanks.

The main limitation is temperature. Alloys with more than about 3 % magnesium can become sensitised to intergranular corrosion and stress-corrosion cracking if held for long periods above roughly 65 °C. 5083 is therefore not recommended for parts that run hot in service, such as heater housings or cooking surfaces; 5754 or 5052 are safer choices there.

Why choose 5083 discs

  • Highest strength of the commonly used non-heat-treatable alloys
  • Excellent resistance to seawater and marine atmospheres, especially in H116 and H321
  • Keeps strength after welding; no post-weld heat treatment needed
  • Tough at cryogenic temperatures down to −196 °C
  • Cutting to round reduces scrap and machining time on round parts

Tempers

Which temper to order

O / H111

Annealed (H111 has a slight strain from flattening). The most formable condition, used for discs that will be spun, pressed or dished into tank heads, and for parts that will be welded heavily.

H116

Strain-hardened and tested for resistance to exfoliation and intergranular corrosion under ASTM G66 and G67, as ASTM B928 requires for marine hulls. Order H116 for discs going into ship structures.

H321

Strain-hardened and thermally stabilised. Similar strength to H116, also covered by ASTM B928 with the same corrosion tests when ordered for marine use.

H32 / H34

Quarter- and half-hard tempers for general engineering where a little more strength than H111 is wanted and marine corrosion testing is not required.

Working with 5083

Machining, forming, welding and finishing

Machining

Softer tempers are gummy; use sharp, polished, high-rake carbide tools, high speeds and plenty of coolant. H116/H321 and stress-relieved cast 5083 tooling plate machine more cleanly.

Spinning and dishing

O/H111 discs can be spun or pressed into tank heads and domes. Allow for springback and work-hardening between stages.

Welding

MIG and TIG with ER5183 filler for the highest weld strength, or ER5356. The weld zone keeps close to O-temper strength, so welded structures are designed on annealed values.

Finishing

Anodises to a protective but slightly grey finish; paints and powder-coats well after proper pre-treatment. Often left bare in marine service.

Applications

Where 5083 discs are used

  • Marine: hatch covers, deck fittings, flanges, portlight frames and bulkhead penetrations
  • Pressure vessels and tanks: dished heads and end plates for road tankers, LNG and cryogenic vessels
  • Process equipment: flanges and blind flanges for seawater and chemical piping
  • Vacuum and semiconductor: chamber lids and end plates machined from stress-relieved 5083
  • Defence and transport: welded structures where strength after welding is critical

FAQ

5083 aluminium (aluminum) circles: frequently asked questions

Is 5083 good for cookware discs?

No. 5083 is too strong to deep-draw easily and is not recommended for parts held above about 65 °C, where high-magnesium alloys can become sensitised. Use 1050, 1100 or 3003 circles for cookware.

What is the difference between 5083-H111 and 5083-H116 discs?

H111 is essentially annealed and best for forming and welding. H116 is strain-hardened for higher strength and is corrosion-tested under ASTM B928 for marine structures.

How heavy is a 5083 aluminium disc?

Weight = π/4 × diameter² × thickness × 2.66 g/cm³. A 500 mm disc 10 mm thick weighs about 5.2 kg; a 1000 mm disc 20 mm thick about 41.8 kg.

Can you cut 5083 rings with a centre hole?

Yes. Annular blanks save material on flanges and rings; send the outside and inside diameters and the tolerance on each.

Is 5083 weldable?

Yes, very. Use ER5183 or ER5356 filler. Strength in the weld zone stays close to the annealed value, so no post-weld heat treatment is needed.

Do you ship 5083 circles internationally?

Yes. Metline ships from Mumbai worldwide with mill certificates and export packing on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
