1235 Aluminium Foil

1235 aluminium foil is a 99.35 % pure, unalloyed foil that rolls down to the thinnest gauges with few pinholes, conducts heat and electricity very well and forms easily in the annealed condition. It is the standard alloy for thin packaging foil (6 – 9 µm), lithium-ion battery current collectors, cable shielding and capacitor foil. Metline supplies 1235 foil in coils and sheets, plain, coated or laminated, to converters worldwide.

Overview

What is 1235 aluminium foil?

1235 is a 1xxx-series, commercially pure aluminium with at least 99.35 % Al. Unlike most alloys it has no single limit for silicon or iron; instead the combined Si + Fe is capped at 0.65 %, which gives mills flexibility to balance the two for the best rolling behaviour.

That high purity is what makes 1235 roll so well. With few hard intermetallic particles in the metal, foil can be reduced to 6 – 7 µm (about 0.00025 in) with a low pinhole count, which is why 1235 is the alloy of choice for the "double-zero" foil used in aseptic drink cartons, cigarette inner liners and laminated pouches. At these gauges, foil is rolled two sheets at a time: the sides that touch the rolls come out bright, and the sides that touch each other come out matte.

Heavier gauges of 1235 serve technical markets. Hard H18 foil of 10 – 20 µm is coated with cathode paste in lithium-ion cells; 0.1 – 0.2 mm foil laminated with plastic film shields data and power cables; and high-purity, clean-surface foil is etched to make electrolytic capacitor electrodes. Across all of these, buyers care most about gauge consistency, pinholes, surface cleanliness (wettability) and coil shape, more than raw strength.

Choose 1235 foil when you need

  • Very thin gauges (6 – 9 µm) with low pinhole counts for barrier packaging
  • High electrical and thermal conductivity (around 60 % IACS)
  • Soft, dead-fold foil that wraps and laminates without springing back
  • A widely available, lower-cost alternative to 1145 foil

Product types

Types of 1235 foil we supply

Plain (bare) foil

Mill-finish foil with a clean, degreased surface, ready for printing, lacquering or lamination by the converter.

Double-zero foil

6 – 9 µm foil, one side bright and one matte, for aseptic cartons, pouches and tobacco liners. Supplied on cores for high-speed laminators.

Battery foil

H18 foil around 10 – 20 µm with tight gauge tolerance and high surface tension for cathode current collectors. Carbon-coated versions improve adhesion and lower contact resistance.

Cable foil

Heavier O-temper foil laminated with PE or copolymer film, used as a screen and moisture barrier in communication and power cables.

Capacitor foil

High-purity, clean-surface foil for etching into electrolytic capacitor anodes and cathodes.

Coated and laminated foil

Heat-seal lacquered, colour-coated, embossed, hydrophilic-coated or laminated to PET, PE and paper for packaging and insulation.

Tolerances & quality

What to specify and inspect on 1235 foil

Key quality parameters

Gauge tolerance
Usually ±5 – 8 % of nominal for packaging foil; tighter (around ±3 – 4 %) for battery and capacitor foil. EN 546-3 gives the standard tolerances
Pinholes
Count per m² by light-table inspection; critical below 9 µm
Wettability
Water brush test (class A or B) to show the surface is free of rolling oil; essential before coating and lamination
Coil shape
Flatness, telescoping, edge wave and winding tightness; layer misalignment is commonly held within ±1 mm
Mechanical
Tensile strength and elongation per temper and thickness
Certification
Mill test certificate (EN 10204 3.1); food-contact declarations and third-party reports on request

Most problems on converting lines come from the coil rather than the alloy: residual oil causing poor adhesion, loose winding leading to creases, or splices in the wrong place. Put your wettability class, splice allowance, maximum joins per coil and winding direction on the order, and inspect them at goods-in.

Applications

Where 1235 aluminium foil is used

  • Aseptic and flexible packaging: drink cartons, pouches, sachets and stick packs laminated with paper and polymer films
  • Lithium-ion batteries: cathode current collectors, bare or carbon-coated
  • Cables: aluminium-polymer laminated tape for screening and moisture barriers
  • Capacitors: etched anode and cathode foil for electrolytic capacitors
  • Tobacco and confectionery: paper-backed inner liners and wrappers
  • Household and food service: kitchen foil and wrapping foil in O temper
  • Insulation: foil facings on glass wool, foam and reflective building membranes
  • Heat exchangers: hydrophilic-coated fin foil for some air-conditioning units

Choose 8011 foil when you need more strength and puncture resistance, for example in food containers, pharmaceutical blister lids and bottle caps.

FAQ

1235 aluminium (aluminum) foil: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 1235 and 1145 foil?

Both are commercially pure foil alloys. 1145 requires at least 99.45 % aluminium and 1235 at least 99.35 %, with slightly higher combined Si + Fe allowed in 1235. For most packaging and cable uses they perform the same, and 1235 is more widely available.

Is 1235 aluminium foil food safe?

Yes. Its composition complies with EN 602 for food-contact aluminium, and annealed 1235 foil is widely used for household foil and food laminates. Ask for the declaration of compliance your market requires.

What thickness is 1235 battery foil?

Cathode current collector foil is usually H18 temper between about 10 and 20 µm, with tight gauge tolerance and a clean, wettable surface. Carbon-coated variants are available.

Why is 1235 used for very thin foil?

Its high purity means fewer hard particles that cause pinholes and breaks during rolling, so it can be rolled to 6 – 7 µm reliably.

What does O temper mean for foil?

O means fully annealed: the foil is soft, has its highest elongation and folds without springing back. The final anneal also burns off rolling oil, improving wettability.

How many square metres are in a kilogram of 1235 foil?

About 370 divided by the thickness in microns. A kilogram of 9 µm foil covers about 41 m²; a kilogram of 20 µm foil about 18 m².
