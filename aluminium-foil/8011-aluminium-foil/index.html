8011 Aluminium Foil

8011 aluminium foil is an aluminium-iron-silicon foil alloy that is noticeably stronger and stiffer than pure 1xxx foils while staying easy to form, emboss and lacquer. That combination makes it the default alloy for foil food containers, pharmaceutical blister lidding, household foil and air-conditioner fins. Metline supplies 8011 foil in O, H14, H18, H22 and H24 tempers, bare, lacquered or coated, to converters worldwide.

Overview

What is 8011 aluminium foil?

8011 is an 8xxx-series alloy in which iron (0.6 – 1.0 %) and silicon (0.5 – 0.9 %) are added on purpose rather than tolerated as impurities. They form a fine dispersion of Al-Fe-Si particles that strengthen the foil, keep the grain size small after annealing and give it better puncture and tear resistance than 1235 or 1100 foil of the same gauge.

The fine grain is the real advantage. In fully annealed (O) condition 8011 is still soft enough to fold and wrap, but it has more elongation at heavier gauges and draws into deep trays without tearing or orange-peel. In the partially annealed H22 and H24 tempers it keeps enough stiffness for a container to hold its shape when filled and lifted. In the hard H18 temper it is strong enough to act as a push-through lidding that tears cleanly when a tablet is pressed out.

The price of that strength is slightly lower conductivity and a practical lower gauge limit: 8011 can be rolled to about 6 – 7 µm, but the very thinnest laminating foils are more often made in 1235. Above about 9 µm, 8011 is the workhorse foil for food service, pharmaceutical and building products.

Choose 8011 foil when you need

  • More strength and puncture resistance than pure aluminium foil
  • Good deep-drawing behaviour for trays and containers (O, H22, H24)
  • A stiff, printable, heat-seal-lacquerable lidding foil (H18)
  • A food-contact alloy that is widely available and economical

H2x tempers are worth understanding. "H22" and "H24" foil is rolled harder than needed and then partially annealed back, rather than rolled directly to quarter or half hard (H12/H14). The partial anneal gives more elongation at the same strength, which is exactly what a container press needs: the walls form without splitting, and the finished tray still resists denting.

Product types

Types of 8011 foil we supply

Container foil

O, H22 or H24 foil around 30 – 120 µm, bare or lacquered, for smooth-wall and wrinkle-wall food containers and baking trays.

Blister (PTP) lidding foil

H18 foil around 20 – 25 µm, primer-printed on one side and heat-seal lacquered on the other, for sealing to PVC, PVDC or PP blister trays.

Household foil

O-temper foil around 10 – 25 µm in jumbo rolls for rewinding into kitchen and catering rolls.

Fin stock

H22, H24 or H26 foil around 0.1 – 0.2 mm, bare or with hydrophilic coating, for air-conditioner and heat-exchanger fins.

Lacquered and colour-coated foil

Epoxy, polyester or vinyl lacquers, gold and colour coatings for lids, containers and decorative packaging.

Laminated and embossed foil

Laminated to paper or film for labels, insulation facings and roofing membranes; embossed patterns add stiffness and grip.

For container foil, the most common cause of rejects is temper drift: foil that is too hard splits at the corners of deep trays, and foil that is too soft produces flimsy containers. Agree the tensile and elongation window with your container press supplier, write it on the order, and check it on every lot. For blister lidding, peel and burst strength depend as much on the heat-seal lacquer as on the foil, so specify the lacquer system and the tray material together.

Applications

Where 8011 aluminium foil is used

  • Food containers: takeaway and airline trays, baking pans and lids that go from freezer to oven
  • Pharmaceutical packaging: push-through blister lidding and strip packs; thicker soft foil for cold-form laminates
  • Household and catering foil: kitchen rolls, catering rolls and pop-up sheets
  • Air conditioning: bare and hydrophilic-coated fin stock for evaporators and condensers
  • Closures: heat-seal lids and induction seal liners; heavier 8011 strip is used for ROPP bottle caps
  • Building: foil facings for insulation, vapour barriers and roofing laminates
  • Labels and decorative packaging: laminated and colour-coated foil for beverage labels and confectionery

FAQ

8011 aluminium (aluminum) foil: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 8011 and 1235 foil?

8011 contains deliberate iron and silicon additions that make it stronger, stiffer and more puncture resistant. 1235 is 99.35 % pure aluminium, softer and more conductive, and rolls more easily to 6 µm. Use 8011 for containers, lidding and household foil; 1235 for thin laminates, battery and cable foil.

Which 8011 temper is used for food containers?

Usually O, H22 or H24 depending on how deep the container is and how rigid it must be. Deep trays favour softer tempers; shallow, rigid trays favour H24. Your container press supplier will usually specify a tensile and elongation window.

Is 8011 foil food grade?

Yes. Its composition complies with EN 602 for food contact, and it is widely used for containers, household foil and pharmaceutical packaging. We can supply food-contact declarations on request.

What thickness is 8011 blister lidding foil?

Push-through blister lidding is typically 20 – 25 µm in H18 temper, printed on one side and coated with heat-seal lacquer on the other.

Can 8011 foil be coated or coloured?

Yes. It takes epoxy, polyester and vinyl lacquers, heat-seal coatings, colour coatings and hydrophilic coatings for fin stock, as well as printing, lamination and embossing.

Do you export 8011 foil?

Yes. Metline supplies 8011 foil worldwide from Mumbai with mill certificates and moisture-proof export packing, on EXW, FOB, CIF or DAP terms.
